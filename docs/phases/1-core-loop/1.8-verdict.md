# 1.8: The core-loop journey and the owner's verdict

**Goal.** The thread is played as one loop: by both runners until green, by a soak, and then by the owner,
who decides whether this host makes the game they want.

**Starting state.** Written by the agent that closes the last of 1.1–1.7.

**Read first.** The Part 1 README; decision 0001 (the fallback); the 0.2 G2 latency record; every Part 1
brief's Outcome.

## Deliverables
- **The core-loop journey** (`tests/journeys/core-loop.yaml`), built from the chunk journeys. It runs: arrive at
  Claywater Station, talk and learn a topic, buy and equip, take the quest, fight the ambush, die, wake,
  retrieve the vastei, climb the cliff, swim, sail to the mooring, complete the quest, rest, spend at the
  sitting, then load the rest save and read every family.
  - It must be green on both runners twice in a row.
  - It is also looped for 60 minutes as a **soak**: no crash, Papyrus queue length flat, co-save size flat
    after the first loop, frame time within the G2 bar.
- **Immersion counts:** the inventory's reachable rows for the block, with verdicts so far. Any Nordic element
  the owner will meet is fixed before the check (a blocker, never polish).
- **The owner packet** (via `owner_inbox.py`), plain English, one check per bullet:
  1. In the studio pick **Scenes → 1.8 core loop**. Play it through: talk, trade, fight, die, recover, rest,
     level, follow the quest's directions, climb, swim and sail. About 30 minutes, on the phone and once on the
     Mac.
  2. **Scenes → 1.4 duel ring:** duel at light, medium and heavy load.
  3. The measured stream latency for each device and network is printed on the packet. Your feel verdict is
     read against it.
  4. Reply with four answers (yes / no / not yet, and why):
     - does combat feel like Dark Souls;
     - does dialogue feel like Morrowind;
     - is the UI one coherent thing;
     - is this the game you want?

     Add anything that jarred. Use **Report here** in the studio to mark places.
  - Also in the packet: the wiring-spec draft (1.4) for review, the costs, the re-estimate of Parts 2–4, and the
    O-2 (20x for Part 2's two tracks, with measured numbers), O-4, O-9, O-11 and O-16 asks.
- **Triage:** every finding is a blocker (fixed before Part 2 opens) or polish with its tag and the Part 2 chunk
  that owns it.

## The decision this chunk makes
- **Yes on all four:** Part 2 opens.
- **Not yet:** the planner names what would change the answer, fixes it inside Part 1, and asks again (at most
  twice before a re-plan).
- **No on combat or the host:** decision 0001's re-plan. The planner writes a costed comparison of fixing it on
  Skyrim against the fallback, and the owner chooses.

## Loops (docs/loops.md)
- **Journey:** core loop on both runners; the soak.
- **Joins owned:** any join between two Part 1 chunks that no single chunk owned, found by the journey. Each is
  fixed in the earlier chunk's code with a test beside it.

## Risks and fallbacks
- **The stream feels poor over a long session** (fatigue, drops, bitrate): revisit 0007's options (bitrate, codec,
  AV1 on L4, a local PC) before judging the game.
- **Integration surprises between Part 1 chunks** (combat × dialogue interception, boats × save, climbing ×
  navmesh): the journey and soak exist to catch them; each is fixed at source, once, with a test.

## Deliberately out of scope
New features. Thin content is expected and is not part of the verdict.
