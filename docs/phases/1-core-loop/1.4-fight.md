# 1.4: Fight: the combat map and the DS1 first rung

**Goal.** Map every DS1 combat behaviour to a tuned mod or to our own build of DS1's system, so that how much of
DS1 we rebuild is an informed decision (decision 0019). Then build the first rung on that map: one weapon class
against one archetype, final stack, wired to our sheet, so the owner can say whether it feels like Dark Souls
before anything else is built on it. This is the single largest
quality risk in the plan, so it comes first.

**Starting state.** Written by the agent that closes 1.3. Expected:
- the 0.0 souls-like stack (`infra/gpu-box/combat-test-mods.json`) and the owner's feel notes (0.0 §d):
  - it "felt reasonably good";
  - missing: stagger, poise and interrupts; a riposte animation; stamina and damage tuning; kicks and the
    running, roll and backstep attacks;
  - the backstab needs a block first, where DS1 takes a single press;
- the gamepad already in DS1's layout (0.0 §d); still missing: a separate heavy, use-item and two-hand;
- G6 runs when a map row needs DSR numbers, G9 when a set window cannot be met by settings (owner ruling
  2026-10-28);
- the sheet exists (1.3).

**Re-check before starting:** versions of every stack mod on the pinned runtime; newer souls-like mods (BFCO,
SCAR, CPR, Ultimate Combat, kick and backstab mods, DS-style movesets) on Nexus.

**Read first.** Decisions 0004, 0010, 0014 §2, 0019, 0020; [2.7](../2-slice/2.7-combat.md); the 0.0 §d stack and
feel notes; [stream-and-combat-stack](../../research/2026-10-28/stream-and-combat-stack.md),
[parry-riposte-backstab](../../research/2026-10-29/parry-riposte-backstab.md) and
[skyrim-combat-traversal](../../research/2026-10-27/skyrim-combat-traversal.md); the 0.2 G2 latency record.

## Deliverables
- **The combat map** (decision 0019 §2): `docs/research/<date>/combat-map.md`. One row per DS1 behaviour:
  - the action states;
  - rolls and backstep by load;
  - guard, chip damage and guard break;
  - parry, the parried state and riposte;
  - backstab;
  - poise, stagger and interrupts;
  - stamina rhythm;
  - running, rolling, backstep and plunging attacks; kicks;
  - lock-on;
  - enemy AI shape;
  - the controls (heavy on its own button, use-item, two-hand).

  Each row gives:
  - the candidate mods, tried in game;
  - how close they get once tuned, measured against DSR where a number decides it (decision 0004);
  - what a small tweak would add;
  - the cost and risk of building it from DS1 instead;
  - the verdict: **mod, tuned**, **mod, tweaked** or **build from DS1**.

  The owner sees the map, and rules on the verdicts, before any "build" row starts.
- **The stack tuned for the thread:**
  - mod settings for one weapon class (straight sword), rolls at three burden tiers, a medium shield's guard and
    parry, riposte and backstab, stamina and damage;
  - the settings pinned in the dependency manifest, with every new mod recorded in the sourcing register.
- **The "mod, tweaked" rows**, each by the cheapest route (0010): first of all the single-press backstab, then
  the rest the map lists. Config patches and SPID or Papyrus shims come before any SKSE code.
- **The "build from DS1" rows the thread touches** (2.7 §d), built once and final:
  - the golden table for those rows (2.7 §b), every row with provenance and zero `GUESS:`;
  - the combat engine: DS1's state machine for those behaviours in a pure C++ core, with exhaustive L2 tests
    generated from the table;
  - the adapter over the mods' APIs (Precision's pre-hit callback, TDM, OAR graph variables, the dodge and
    attack frameworks), with G9's timing control where a clip must meet a window.
- **The wiring spec, drafted** (2.7 §a): how the sheet reaches the mods' damage, stamina, burden-tier rolls,
  armour, poise and guard, and the archetype's HP, poise and damage → a D2 band. The owner reviews the draft in
  1.8.
- **One archetype:** a DS1 Hollow Soldier-shaped human with sword and shield, mapped onto a Claywater hostile record.
  Its AI is configured with the stack's AI mods (SCAR, CPR or the map's pick) to approach, space, combo and
  punish like the DSR reference.
- **Feel, side by side:** the same scripted inputs in DSR and in Argonia, shown side by side and read by readers.
  This is **L5, reason: feel**. Where a window is set or tuned, it is within one DS1 frame of DSR (decision 0004
  §6).
- The HUD's health, stamina and lock-on states join here (1.2 components).

## Acceptance
- Every map row has a verdict with its evidence. Every "build from DS1" row has an owner nod before its code
  starts; each built row's state-machine transitions are 100% covered.
- The tuned thread is checked in game:
  - roll through a swing;
  - parry and riposte;
  - a single-press backstab;
  - guard break;
  - poise holding through a light hit and breaking under a heavy one.
- Each tuned window is within one DS1 frame (33 ms, aim 16 ms) at 30 and 60 fps.
- The archetype's duel reproduces the DSR capture's approach distance, combo length and punish reaction within the
  stated tolerances.
- The reader panel reports no "obvious" feel difference.
- A five-minute duel loop on the game runner shows no crash and no growing script lag.

## Loops (docs/loops.md)
- **Subsets:**
  - the map (L4 eyes and timestamps per row);
  - the tuned windows (L4 timestamps);
  - the built rows' core (L2);
  - the wiring (L2 over five builds);
  - the AI duel (L4);
  - feel (L5, reason above).
- **Joins owned:**
  - the sheet → damage, stamina and poise (L2, L4);
  - mod windows ↔ clip timing (L4);
  - combat → HUD bars (L3, L4);
  - combat state → lock-on camera (L4).
- **Journey:** draw, lock on, trade blows, roll through a swing, parry and riposte, backstab, kill, loot. It runs on
  both runners, with real inputs for every action, never `activate`.

## Owner check
In 1.7, the heart of the verdict: duel the archetype at the three burden tiers in **Scenes → 1.4 duel ring** (proving
ground) and **Scenes → 1.4 Claywater ambush**, with the map's verdicts listed. The stream latency is
shown on the check.

## Risks and fallbacks
- **A tuned mod that turns out not close enough** in play: move its row to "build from DS1" and tell the owner.
- **A built row that costs far more than expected:** keep the tuned mod for now and record the deviation.
- **Mod conflicts:** the 0.3 manifest's conflict list; re-test after each addition.
- **No permitted moveset close enough** for the straight sword: try the next class with a close moveset and tell the
  owner; never ship a poor one.
- **Precision tunnelling** on fast swings: tune its collision step or capsules.

## Deliberately out of scope
Other weapon classes, ranged and thrown weapons, more archetypes, bosses (2.7); death and respawn (1.5); building
a row the map gives to a mod (decision 0019).
