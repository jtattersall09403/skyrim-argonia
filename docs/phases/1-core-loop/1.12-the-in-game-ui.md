# 1.12: The in-game UI: Wind UI under our Argonian patch

**Goal.** Every screen the player meets in play reads as one Argonian design system and works on every device:
- **Wind UI** (Nexus 196534) is the in-game base for everything it covers: Morrowind's windows open together,
  draggable, resizable and remembered.
- **Our patch on top** retints its frames to Black Marsh. It puts our parchment chart in the map pane, our paper book
  in the journal window and our Morrowind dialogue window in the same frame style, and adds a minimap to our HUD.
- **The front door stays:** the parchment main menu and the 3D-model loading screens, as 1.9 left them.
- **One designer** looks at every screen, off game first and then in game, until the whole reads as one design.
- **Every menu is reachable** by keyboard and mouse, by touch on the phone, and by the GameSir pad, folded and
  unfolded.

## The owner's rulings this chunk carries (2026-10-31)

**Wind UI and the front door**
- Wind UI is the base for every in-game menu it covers. Its "hold Shift for Skyrim's menu" is switched off.
- The parchment main menu and the loading screens stay. The designer may tweak them lightly (shared type sizes, ornament
  colour, button and focus states, the title) but never restructure them.
- **Small viewports** use Wind UI's full-screen one-pane mode.

**Perks**
- Perks are visible and taken in Wind UI's perk list. Each level banked by rest grants one perk point at the sitting.
- Skyrim's perk names and descriptions are reworded only, to pass the Nordic review (0012); the numbers don't change.
- Skills with no tree show a placeholder tree; 2.1 gives them real ones.

**The chart and the minimap**
- The map pane holds only our parchment chart. It zooms from the whole province down to the player, so there is no
  separate local view.
- The chart shows the player marker, the land revealed around where the player has been, and each place's marker
  once discovered (unmarked before, marked after). There are no quest or other markers.
- **No fast travel from the map, ever.** Fast travel is only through in-world services and Mark/Recall.
- **Our HUD has a pinned minimap at the top right,** drawn from the chart, with a small compass that turns as the
  player turns. Skyrim's compass bar stays gone.

**The journal and the dialogue window**
- **The journal:** our paper book inside Wind UI's journal window, with no "show on map":
  - ribbons for *Current*, *Finished* (failed quests there, marked) and *All entries* (the dated diary), and a
    *Topics* ribbon that 2.12 fills;
  - current quests grouped by questline or place, and each quest opening on its latest direction, then its dated
    entries;
  - search by keyboard; no statistics page.
- **The dialogue window:** Morrowind's layout in the same frame style as the panes (no longer parchment). Barter from
  it opens Wind UI's barter window.

**The remaining screens**
- **Other in-game screens** use the frame style: message boxes, wait and rest, the sitting, lockpicking and notices.
- **Book reading** stays paper, like the journal.
- **This chunk owns the HUD's styling** from here on. The designer's loop includes it.

**The delivering agent's latitude**
- The designer may give the journal a different form if it reads better, as long as every device and input reaches
  it.
- The button map below may be changed per device if anything is clunky, looks worse than it could, or is hard to
  navigate. The report says what changed and why.
- Favourites versus Wind UI's quick keys on the pad are the agent's call.

## Input on every device (the starting map)

| Action | Pad (GameSir X2s) | Keyboard and mouse | Touch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open all windows (menu mode) | Start | Tab | an on-screen menu button |
| Move between panes | the bumpers (the System pane last in the cycle) | the mouse | each pane's tab |
| Move within a pane | the d-pad or left stick | the mouse | tap |
| Zoom the map | the triggers | the wheel | pinch |
| Pan the map | the right stick, whichever pane is selected | drag | drag |
| The system menu | Start, then the bumpers to System | Esc | the System tab |
| The journal, the map directly | through the panes | J, M | the tabs |
| Wait and rest | Select | T | an on-screen button |
| Close, back to play | B or Start | Esc or Tab | Close |

The pad uses Wind UI's own pad support if it has any. If it has none, we build focus navigation as above.

## Work

The order is de-risk first (0023, 0029). §1–§2 prove the two classes of work that could change the plan, on a real
sample, before the rest is built.

### 1. Wind UI in the stack, and its facts
- **Record first:**
  - copy the Nexus page's description, requirements and permissions text into the private archive;
  - write the permissions in our words in `windui.md`;
  - add a sourcing-register row for 1.3.0 Beta, pinned (0002), with the archive kept privately (Nothing lives only on a

- **Lay it** through `infra/gpu-box/stack.py`, above SkyUI. Dear Diary's inventory, container and barter SWFs give way
  to it; Dear Diary keeps the main menu and books.
- **Find on the agents' machine (L4, one batch):**
  - every menu it replaces, and whether it touches dialogue, the HUD, message boxes or the tween menu;
  - whether it has pad navigation;
  - where its frames, colours and fonts come from (the page says it reads them from the game's own menus);
  - how its map and journal windows are drawn;
  - how Shift-for-vanilla, fast travel and "show on map" are switched off.
- **Acceptance:** windui.md answers each, with stills.

### 2. The probe: our chart and book inside Wind UI's windows
- **Route order** (0010): Wind UI's own settings and data first (its Morrowind map style draws prebuilt map data:
  can our chart be that data?), then overriding its files from our patch. After that, reading its plugin (decompiling
  is allowed, 0003 §8) for the window's rectangle, focus and full-screen state. Last, a PrismaUI view laid exactly
  over the window and following its moves, resizes and full-screen mode.
- **One sample each:** the chart in the map window and a page of the book in the journal window. Each must move,
  resize and go full screen with the window, and take the pad's focus when its pane is selected.
- **Gate:** the chosen route for each, or the stand-in fallback above, written into this brief before §4–§6 start.
- **Chosen (2026-10-31, the probe: windui.md "In game").** Wind UI is one closed ImGui DLL: no route places our
  pixels inside its windows, and its licence forbids integrating its code. So both take the stand-in:
  - **the chart:** Wind UI's map style is set to Skyrim (`settings.json`, laid by the `windui` row), so M and the
    taskbar's Map open Skyrim's MapMenu, which our chart already stands in for: our window in the frame style.
    Wind UI's own map and its click fast travel never show;
  - **the book:** J opens our journal over Wind UI's windows. The taskbar's Journal still toggles Wind UI's own
    journal window, since no setting hides it; that is a known limit, left to the owner's look.

### 3. The Argonian patch
- **The retint:** Wind UI's frames, borders, buttons, tabs and highlights, recoloured toward `tmp/image.png`
  (dark green-black panes, gold-green filigree, warm serif text), Pelagiad throughout.
- **Built at install:** anything derived from Wind UI's, SkyUI's or Skyrim's files is a recipe run on the player's
  machine (0014), extending `tools/ui_theme/` beside the Dear Diary retheme. Only our own art and code are committed.
- **Light touch:** colour and trim only; Wind UI's layout and behaviour stay its own.

### 4. Stats, perks and the sitting
- **Our sheet in Wind UI's stats pane:** the seven attributes, 27 skills and vastei (C2). Where its pane can't show
  them, our patch fills it.
- **The perk point:** each level taken at the sitting (1.5) grants the engine's perk point.
- **Wind UI's level-up window is suppressed:** the sitting is the level-up.
- **The perk text:** every perk name and description reworded for the Nordic review, as data through the pipeline,
  with each verdict in the immersion inventory.
- **Placeholder trees** for the skills without one.

### 5. The chart and the minimap
- **The art:** confirm it is the old repo's hand-drawn map (the owner liked it); if not, use that map's design, in our
  own or permitted art.
- **Zoom and bounds:** the zoom runs from the whole province, top to bottom with nothing cut off, down to the land
  around the player.
- **The marks:**
  - the player marker in our style;
  - the reveal of the land around where the player has been, through a native mechanism (say which), holding after a
    save and load;
  - place markers drawn once Skyrim's discovery event fires for a place.
- **No fast travel:** map fast travel is off in Wind UI and the engine.
- **The minimap:**
  - top right of our HUD, from the chart's data: the player, the revealed land and discovered markers;
  - a small compass rose that turns with the player;
  - the effects stack moves to make room, clear of the phone's touch controls.

### 6. The journal and the dialogue window
- **The journal:**
  - the book in the journal window, to the rulings above, over ArgoniaCore's log (the message schema grows, the rules

### 7. The System pane, quit, and the remaining screens

  set.
- **The remaining screens:** message boxes, wait and rest, the sitting, lockpicking, notices and book reading,
  restyled to their look in the table.

### 8. Every device

### 9. The designer's loop (one design agent, docs/loops.md)
- **The UI map:** every screen the player can meet anywhere in the game, now or as later chunks land, is listed in
  `ui/DESIGN.md` with who draws it. The list covers the front door, loading, each Wind UI pane, the map, the journal,
  the dialogue window, the System pane, the HUD and minimap, message boxes, books, the sitting and notices.
- **Off game:**
  - a render of each screen at the four viewports (1920×1080, 1280×720, the V5 folded and unfolded);
  - our PrismaUI views through the Playwright harness;
  - Wind UI's panes and the Scaleform screens from in-game captures, re-rendered with the patch's textures and tokens;
  - Ruffle for SWFs if it renders them faithfully.
- **The loop:** the agent reads the whole set side by side and changes the tokens and the patch, with small,
  simple-to-get-right tweaks by preference and creative freedom over the whole. It re-renders and repeats until
  satisfied, then records the tokens in `ui/DESIGN.md`.
- **Then in game:** deploy, take stills of every screen from scenes, and run a reader's pass (one design, Pelagiad, no
  Nordic tells, nothing vanilla showing). Then one L5 clip of the open and close hand-offs (reason: a hand-off shows only
