## The owner's words: the detectives' quests, and a rule for every faction

> Detectives faction quest structure: establish this structure early, then gradually increasingly break it in
> later quests: you are identifying who, means, motive, opportunity. Gather evidence and present case to
> handler/choose from one of a few suspects, but you have to have found the evidence first for the dialogue
> options to be available. In dialogue with your handler, choose the suspect, then next bit of conversation choose
> the motive (if you've found the evidence of it for them), then means and opportunity (same). Each possible
> subject for each case should have some motive and possible means so there are red herrings, but each one except
> the actual culprit has some alibi or conflicting evidence that you can discover - so it's possible to form a
> wrong conclusion but also possible to be thorough and eliminate leads and find the right suspect. You can get it
> right or wrong: after choosing your suspect in dialogue with your handler, you "take the person before the
> magistrate" or elder or judge or whatever (choose them in dialogue with your handler then fade to black then
> appear before magistrate with them and watch dialogue play out), magistrate goes through case and reveals either
> that you are right, or the key evidence that you missed and shows that you're wrong. If right, you complete the
> quest and get rewarded and progress through faction. If wrong, pay some kind of penality, and have to do another
> mission at the level you're at. If you fail 3 at the same level you're kicked out of the faction. Don't have
> *every* lamp mission in this format establish the structure early and use it for first act and most of second,
> with escalating drama and stakes and difficulty and seriousness of crimes, then start to break the structure
> later e.g. some of the later ones should be more like you confront the murderer and either pass a hard speech
> check (convince them to come quietly) or they attack you if you fail; or they find and attack you partway
> through the investigation if you're getting close; some other exciting/interesting different structures.
>
>
> Ruling for every faction's questline: quests should escalate in difficulty over the course of their story (I.e.
> objective difficulty - skill levels required, danger bands of areas visited, etc. I don't mean they should level
> to the player, I mean they should escalate up an objective hardness ladder in terms of what they *require*,
> regardless of the player's level). Rewards should get progressively better in proportion (and be hand authored,
> not random)
