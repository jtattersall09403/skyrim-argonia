# The faction names: your call

On 2026-11-16 you said you did not like "the Free Marque" as the pirates' name, and asked for every faction to
have a well researched name grounded in the lore of its region, its people's culture, its role in society and its
aims. You asked for the pirates to be renamed now and for alternatives for all the factions, with nothing else
changed until you choose.

Below, for each of the eleven bodies, is the name it has now, what that name means and where it comes from, and
three alternatives. Each alternative gives its meaning, the lore it rests on (with its source) and why it fits the
faction's people, place and aim. My recommendation is marked. Where the present name is already well grounded I
say so and recommend keeping it.

Two words used throughout. *Jel* is the Argonians' own language. *UESP* is the Unofficial Elder Scrolls Pages, the
wiki of the games' lore; "UESP Lore:Jel" means its page of attested Jel words. Every Jel word below appears on
that page with the meaning given. A name I have put together from two attested words says so.

## 1. The pirates (the pirates' plot, Skeith Isle and Soulrest)

**Applied now: Bramman's Brethren** ("the Brethren" for short). Red Bramman was the pirate-king of Topal Bay. In
1E 1033 the Imperial Navy found his escape route, a hidden river behind the mangroves near Soulrest, and followed
it up to his bandit kingdom near Blackrose (UESP Lore:Red Bramman; `story/lore/topics/history-timeline.md:31`,
`story/lore/soulrest.md:13-15`). Our lore already calls the bay's pirates "Bramman's heirs"
(`story/lore/extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md:491`). *Brethren* is the old sailors' word for the members of a
pirate crew, who share the prizes as equals. It fits a crew of mixed races (Khajiit, Argonian, Dunmer, Imperial)
whose bay crew sits at the mouth of Bramman's own river and whose aim, like his, is to hold a city of their own.
The camp below their keep is still called Marque Landing, after the letters of marque the Archon coast's pirates
were given in 1E 2833 (`story/lore/archon.md:43-47`). The camp is already in the built world, so renaming it means
rebuilding its cell; say if you want it changed too.

Alternatives, if you prefer another:

- **a) The Stormwidow's Crew.** Named for their betrayed ship. The *Stormwidow* was the best ship of the Skeith
  captains; the Company sold her course to the Archwardens and her crew were killed on the beach of Skeith Isle
  (our story, `story/design/quests/lines/pirates.md`, section 2; the wreck is a place in our world, Wreck of the
  Stormwidow). Pirates naming themselves after a wrong done to them suits a crew whose whole war is a grudge.
- **b) The All-Flags.** After the All Flags Navy, the fleet of every nation that sailed against Thras in 1E 2200,
  which the Argonian freebooters of Alten Corimont joined and never came back from
  (`story/lore/topics/history-timeline.md:32`, UESP Lore:All Flags Navy). It suits a crew of every race that
  flies whatever flag a prize calls for.
- **c) The Tsona-Mota**, "the swim-hunters". Put together from two attested Jel words, *tsona* (swim) and *mota*
  (hunt, hunter) (UESP Lore:Jel). Argonians who fish the coast would use it for raiders who come out of the
  water, and it suits a line whose fights are dives to wrecks and boardings from the sea.

**Recommendation: keep Bramman's Brethren.** It is the one name that rests on the province's own pirate history,
and on both halves of the crew.

## 2. The Veiled Reed (the main quest's hidden order)

**Now:** a name in plain Tamrielic for the hidden watchers of the marsh, our own
(`story/lore/topics/guilds-and-orders.md:340`, `story/design/quests/lines/main-plot.md`). It is your name and the only one that may
use "Reed".

- **a) The Xal-Toh**, "the sacred secret". *Xal-toh* is an attested Jel phrase (UESP Lore:Jel, from Jee-Lar in
  The Elder Scrolls Online). It names an order whose whole being is a secret kept for the Hist.
- **b) The Gloor-Wardens.** *Gloor* is the Jel word for the pervasive will of the Hist (UESP Lore:Jel;
  `story/lore/topics/hist-and-sap.md:52`). It names watchers who keep the trees' will in check.
- **c) The Deelith**, "those who pass on wisdom" (UESP Lore:Jel; `story/lore/topics/material-culture.md`, the
  offices). It suits dreamers who pass on visions, but it hints at the late twist.

**Recommendation: keep the Veiled Reed.** It is sayable, it is yours and it already carries the story.

## 3. The Unbound (the main quest's cult of tribeless Naga who drain the Hist's sap)

**Now:** a plain Tamrielic word for people with no tribe and no bond (`main-plot.md`, sections 1 and 2). It is
vague, and "Unbound" is also the name of Skyrim's opening quest, which players know well.

- **a) The Rootless.** The Argonians call themselves *Saxhleel*, "the people of the Root" (UESP Lore:Jel); a Naga
  with no tribe is cut from the Root (tribeless Naga: `story/lore/tribes.md:96`). It is the name the tribes
  would give them, and one their leader could wear with pride.
- **b) The Norg-Leel**, "the forbidden people". Put together from *norg* (forbidden, a thing not spoken of) and
  *leel* (tribe, people) (UESP Lore:Jel). It is what the tribes would call those who bled a Hist.
- **c) The Knot-Cutters.** Translated from their leader's name, Cuts-the-Old-Knot (`main-plot.md`). It names
  their aim: to cut the trees free of the old bond.

**Recommendation: a) the Rootless.**

## 4. The Lamps (the detectives, seated at Soulrest)

**Now:** lamps as the light thrown on a crime (`story/design/quests/lines/lamps.md`, section 1). It clashes with
canon: the Twin Lamps were Morrowind's secret society that smuggled escaped Argonian and Khajiit slaves to
freedom, with two lamps in a safe house's window as their sign (UESP Lore:Twin Lamps;
`story/lore/topics/loading-facts.md:724-725`). An Argonian hearing "the Lamps" would think of them, not of
detectives.

- **a) The Wasseek-Mota**, "the bright hunters". Put together from *wasseek* (bright) and *mota* (hunt, hunter)
  (UESP Lore:Jel; the Bright-Throat tribe is Wasseek-Haleel). It keeps the light of the present name and adds the
  hunt for proof.
- **b) The Soulrest Inquest.** Plain Imperial civil wording, from the old Imperial office in Soulrest whose
  powers the three rivals of the city now hire out (`lamps.md`, sections 1 and 2). It suits a body that sells
  inquiries to every side of a divided city.
- **c) The Haj-Mota**, "the hidden hunters", an attested Jel phrase (UESP Lore:Jel). But it is also the name of a
  giant turtle in canon and in our ecology (`story/design/world/74-ecology.md:109`), so I would not use it.

**Recommendation: a) the Wasseek-Mota,** "the Bright Hunters" in Tamrielic mouths.

## 5. The Eelskins (the thieves, under Lilmoth)

**Now:** ripper eels leave alone anyone rubbed with eel slime, and the thieves who cross the eel canals took the
name (`story/design/quests/lines/thieves.md`, section 1; UESP Lore:Ripper Eel, via
`story/lore/topics/guilds-and-orders.md:198-201`). It is regional, grounded and says the trick of the trade.

- **a) The Tsona-Thuxis**, "the swimming snakes". Put together from *tsona* (swim) and *thuxis* (snake, and also one
  who deceives) (UESP Lore:Jel). It is what Argonians would call swimmers who cross the canals to steal.
- **b) The Slime-Rubbed.** The same eel fact, worn as a badge by a guild that lives in the canals.
- **c) The Haj-Thuxis**, "the hidden deceivers", from *haj* (hidden) and *thuxis* (UESP Lore:Jel). A plain thieves'
  name.

**Recommendation: keep the Eelskins.**

## 6. The Shadowscales (the assassins, at Archon)

**Now:** canon. Argonians born under the sign of the Shadow were given to the Dark Brotherhood and trained as its
assassins (UESP Lore:Shadowscales; `story/lore/topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md`). A canon body is not renamed.
If you want a Jel name for what they call themselves among themselves:

- **a) Ku-Vastei**, "the catalysts of change", the canon title of their judges (UESP Lore:Jel;
  `sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:21`, `:64`).
- **b) The Xul-Mota**, "the death-hunt", an attested phrase for a dangerous hunt (*xul* death, *mota* hunt; UESP
  Lore:Jel). It names their work.
- **c) The Haj-Xul**, "the hidden death", put together from *haj* (hidden) and *xul* (death) (UESP Lore:Jel).

**Recommendation: keep the Shadowscales.**

## 7. The Sisei-Xul (the Argonian order of magic and alchemy, at Helstrom)

**Now:** Jel, "sprout and death": *sisei* (sprout, and the name of the third month) and *xul* (death) (UESP
Lore:Jel; `story/lore/topics/sky-moons-calendar.md:21`). The pair spans hatching to grave, as the Hist calendar does,
and fits an order of medicine and the rites of the dead (`sisei-xul.md`, section 1).

- **a) The Xal-Fek**, "the sacred plants", an attested phrase for the fourteen plants sacred to the Black-Tongue
  tribe's alchemy (UESP Lore:Jel). It names an order of brewers.
- **b) The Vastei-Sei**, "the ritual of change", attested (UESP Lore:Jel). It suits the order that sees the dying
  through their passage.
- **c) The Hist-Deek**, "the Hist sapling", the fourth month (UESP Lore:Jel; `sky-moons-calendar.md`). It names a
  young order growing towards the trees.

**Recommendation: keep the Sisei-Xul.** Both parts are attested and it already says what the order is.

## 8. The Rothalen house (the Dunmer necromancers behind a trading house, at Thorn)

**Now:** a Dunmer family name in the canon pattern, our own. The Great Houses and Ashlander tribes are named for
founding families (UESP Lore:Great Houses). No canon house is called Rothalen, so there is no clash.

- **a) House Cardruhn**, "the ancestral tomb". *Cardruhn* is attested Dunmeri for an ancestral tomb, built on
  *ruhn*, home or hearth-hall (UESP Lore:Dark Elvish). It names a house that lives off the old tombs.
- **b) House Huulen**, "the halls" (UESP Lore:Dark Elvish). It points at the barred halls the house hides.
- **c) House Dunruhn**, "the cursed hearth", put together from *dun-* (dark, cursed, ill-favoured by fate) and
  *ruhn* (home, hearth-hall) (UESP Lore:Dark Elvish). A darker name, for a house the player comes to fear.

**Recommendation: keep the Rothalen house.** A family name is how Dunmer houses are named, and it gives nothing
away.

## 9. The Nibenese Company (the Imperial merchants, at Gideon)

**Now:** Cyrodilic; "Nibenese" names the Imperial people of the Niben valley who settled Gideon
(`story/lore/gideon.md`; `merchants.md`, section 1). It says who the merchants are, but not where they trade. Canon's
Imperial companies are named for their charter or their ground: the East Empire Company, the Cyrodilic
Collections (UESP Lore:East Empire Company; `story/lore/topics/foreign-powers.md`).

- **a) The Topal Bay Company.** Named for the water whose trade is its whole business, as the East Empire Company
  is named for its ground (UESP Lore:Topal Bay). It says plainly what the pirates are fighting over.
- **b) The Gideon Charter Company.** Named for its city and the Imperial charter it trades under, as Gideon's
  Lukiul do business the Imperial way (`story/lore/gideon.md:68-71`). It suits a chartered frontier firm.
- **c) The Cape and Bay Company.** For Cape Percar and the bay, the ground of its posts (`merchants.md`, section 1).

**Recommendation: a) the Topal Bay Company,** if you want a change. Keeping the Nibenese Company is also sound.

## 10. The Chainbreakers (who free people from Owings gone wrong, under Stormhold)

**Now:** plain Tamrielic, from the chains of Dunmer slavery (the Dres slave-coffles of Thorn:
`story/lore/topics/history-timeline.md:29`). But the bondage they fight in Black Marsh is a written work roll, not a
chain (`story/lore/topics/labour-and-bondage.md`, section 2; `owing-house.md:7-9`).

- **a) The Rollbreakers.** Named for the thing they take and tear up: the roll of an Owing. It says their aim in
  the province's own terms.
- **b) The Tum-Haj**, "the hidden houses". Put together from *tum* (house) and *haj* (hidden) (UESP Lore:Jel). It
  names their chain of safe houses, as the Twin Lamps' safe houses were in Morrowind.
- **c) The Well-Wardens.** For their seat, the Well-Room under Stormhold (`story/design/quests/lines/chainbreakers-newworld.md:28`, an Ayleid chamber under
  Stormhold).

**Recommendation: a) the Rollbreakers.**

## 11. The Ulvarys (the Dunmer house that profits from the Owing, at Thorn)

**Now:** an invented Dunmer family name in the canon pattern, a family of former Dres overseers (`owing-house.md`,
section 1). No canon clash.

- **a) House Julan**, "benefit" (UESP Lore:Dark Elvish; *Gah-Julan*, "great benefit"). A house that profits by its
  rolls.
- **b) House Isra**, "the row" (UESP Lore:Dark Elvish; *Bal Isra*, "row of stones"). The rows of bonded workers, and
  the rows of the ledger.
- **c) House Sunna**, "blessed" (UESP Lore:Dark Elvish). An ironic name for a house of slavers that calls its trade
  a kindness.

**Recommendation: keep the Ulvarys.** A family name is right for a Dunmer house.

## How to reply

Reply on this issue with one entry per faction, by its number above and a letter: for example "1 keep, 3a, 4b,
9 keep", or write a name of your own for any of them. A faction you do not mention keeps its present name.

**Cost.** Every faction exists only as plot pages so far. A rename now is a quick text change across those pages
and the story explorer, an hour or less of agent time in all. Once quests, dialogue and records are built, the same
change costs a rebuild of every quest that names the faction.
