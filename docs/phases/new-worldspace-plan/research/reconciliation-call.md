# Reconciling the stories with the lore: your call

On 2026-11-17 you asked for the main quest and every faction to be reconciled against the province's lore, and
for options and a recommendation on each thing that does not fit, before anything else is changed. This page is
that list. The nine plot pages and the Owing house page are not changed until you rule.

Some words used throughout. *The lore* is our researched description of the province in 4E 201,
`story/lore/extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`, built on the published games and books (cited as *UESP*, the
Unofficial Elder Scrolls Pages, the wiki of the games' lore). *The Owing* is the marsh's sentence: months of owed
work in place of a prison. *The Hist* are the great thinking trees every Argonian village is built round, and
*sap* is what they bleed. A *plot page* is one of the nine pages that tell a line of quests: the main quest and
the eight faction lines (Chainbreakers, Lamps, thieves, Shadowscales, pirates, the Argonian order of magic, the
necromancers' house at Thorn and the Imperial merchants at Gideon), plus the page on the Dunmer house that profits
from the Owing.

## Already decided: you are not asked these again

- **The An-Xileel fell after Umbriel.** They called the flying city down in 4E 48 through Lilmoth's mad Hist, and
  no party of that name survived it.
- **The Xeech-Leel rule**, "the People of the New Beginning". They rose by being not the An-Xileel, and they are
  becoming like them by the means they use to make sure no tree brings another Umbriel. Their seat is Helstrom.
  They kept the An-Xileel's office of *Archwarden* for the wardens who hold the forts, roads, crossings and tolls;
  the city magistrates called *Vicecanons* stay a city office. The An-Xileel's council, *the Organism*, is gone.
- **The Toh-Reel-Ka**, "the Secret Warriors", are the Xeech-Leel's elite intelligence and security organisation,
  in place of the Veiled Reed. They keep the dreamers of the main quest. I propose their five ranks in the rename
  map (`docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/research/rulers-rename-map.md`): Voh-Vastei (Apprentice), Toh-Wo
  (Secret-Speaker), Reel-Ka (Warrior), Ka-Deelith (War-Teacher), Toh-Nassa (Leader of the Secret). Say if you want
  others.
- **The Chainbreakers are the militant successors of Morrowind's Twin Lamps**, the secret society that smuggled
  escaped slaves to freedom.
- **The Shadowscales are back in secret**; the death of "the last one" in 4E 201 was a cover (your ruling of
  2026-11-16).
- **The faction-names call** (`faction-names-call.md`) is still open for every name but the hidden order's. Item 4
  below touches it.

The lore now says all of the above. Everything below is still open, ordered from the change that would alter the
stories most to the least.

## 1. The Owing: who sets it, who enforces it, and whether there is any court

**What the plots say.** The Owing house page says "Black Marsh has no court and no written code"
(`owing-house.md:27`). The Chainbreakers' page has the Vicecanons of Stormhold and Thorn hold a hearing that bans
the selling of Owings across "the north and east" (`chainbreakers-newworld.md:352-364`), and has Archwarden
garrisons set Owings on carters and sell them (`:222-228`, `:277-279`). The thieves' page has an Archwarden fort
hold runaways for the Owing house (`thieves.md:206`). Three pages sentence a person to "the Owing for life"
(`lamps.md:340`, `shadowscales.md:274`, `:298`), and the Argonian order's page has a Vicecanon put a Dunmer to the
Owing (`sisei-xul.md:273`).

**What the lore says.** An Owing is a counted number of months that ends, which no prison does
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:72`). It is set by an arbitrator, or, where that has drifted, by whoever holds the
crossing, the estate or the hiring office (`:85-86`). Gideon has a courthouse that still works, and an Owing there
is filed and can be contested (`:133`); Vicecanons are city magistrates (lore §2.2). The lore says nothing on the
Xeech-Leel and the Owing.

**Options.**
- **a) The Owing stays a native custom; the Xeech-Leel make no law about it.** Their Archwardens hold crossings,
  so they set Owings there as any crossing-holder does, and some sell them. A Vicecanon's ruling binds only her
  city. "No court" becomes "no court outside Gideon". "For life" becomes a long counted Owing (say twenty years).
- **b) The Xeech-Leel license the Owing.** Their Archwardens register Owings at the crossings, and the Owing
  house pays for its licence. The state is then complicit in the drift into bondage, which gives the Chainbreakers
  the ruling party as an enemy and fits the irony you asked for.
- **c) An Owing for life is real** as the worst sentence for murder, and the Chainbreakers' fight is against
  everything short of that being stretched to it.

**Recommendation: a), with one line of b).** The Archwardens set and sell Owings at their crossings because they
hold them, and the party looks away because the tolls pay its wardens. This changes the fewest stories and keeps
the state thin. Cost: one paragraph in the lore, and about ten lines across five plot pages (the "no court" line,
the Vicecanon's reach, the three life sentences). Under an hour of agent time.

## 2. Slavery and the Dunmer houses

**What the plots say.** A Dunmer house of Thorn, the Ulvarys, descended from House Dres's overseers, buys Owings
and works the people (`owing-house.md:3`, `:13-22`); a Chainbreakers' page calls it "slavery in all but name"
(`chainbreakers-newworld.md:18`, `:36`). The Shadowscales' page has a bandit who "sells" carters whose kin cannot
pay, and calls people "slavers" in 4E 201 (`shadowscales.md:21`, `:42`, `:230`). The necromancers' page has House
Telvanni "keep taking Argonians" and buy Argonian dead now (`rothalen-house.md:49-50`), and an Archein who "sold
his village's people" to the necromancers, with no date (`rothalen-house.md:200`, `sisei-xul.md:67-69`).

**What the lore says.** Slavery in the province ended (lore §6); the Pact formally ended it in 2E 572, but House
Telvanni refused and kept taking Argonians (`regions/thornmarsh-and-east.md:65`); the Dres slavers on the Thorn
coast are late Third Era at the latest (lore §10.1). The Owing's drift into sold, inherited debt is the province's
bondage now (`labour-and-bondage.md`).

**Options.**
- **a) Every sale in 4E 201 is a sale of Owings.** "Slavers" becomes "Owing-buyers"; the bandit sells the carters'
  Owings; the Archein's sale is dated before the secession and told as history.
- **b) Keep a little open slaving at the edges**: Telvanni buyers over the border taking people the Owing has
  already made helpless, which the Xeech-Leel's frontier screen does not stop.
- **c) Both.**

**Recommendation: c).** The Owing is the trade inside the province; the Telvanni are the market over the border
for the dead and, rarely, the living. Cost: about eight lines across three pages.

## 3. The Shadowscales, the Dark Brotherhood and the Toh-Reel-Ka

**What the plots say.** The Shadowscales' page has secret survivors; the thieves' page goes further, with
hatchlings training "in the sealed school at Archon" and an Eldest holding trials for novices at Ra'tal Den
(`thieves.md:53`, `:247-248`). The Shadowscales' page calls the Dark Brotherhood "the assassins of the Empire"
(`shadowscales.md:14-15`). No page says what the Toh-Reel-Ka make of the Shadowscales.

**What the lore says.** The Archon school closed in 4E 187 and is sealed and watched; the Brotherhood's Listener at
Cheydinhal wants it reopened (lore §2.4). The Toh-Reel-Ka's dreamers began after 4E 187, when the province lost its
Shadowscale arbiters (lore §2.5). The Brotherhood is a foreign order, not the Empire's arm in 4E 201.

**Options.**
- **a) The Toh-Reel-Ka hunt the hidden Shadowscales** as an armed order the state does not control.
- **b) The Toh-Reel-Ka want them as their blade**, and offer a charter (the old plan had this as a quest, "The
  State's Hand").
- **c) The Toh-Reel-Ka do not know they survive.**

**Recommendation: b), turning to a) if refused.** It ties a faction line to the main quest's state without making
it depend on it, and it fits the irony: the party that rose against secret power wants a secret blade. The school
stays sealed: the novices train at Ra'tal Den, not in the school. The Brotherhood is "the Brotherhood of
Cyrodiil", not the Empire's. Cost: three lines in the thieves' page, one in the Shadowscales' page, and a short
paragraph in each on the Toh-Reel-Ka.

## 4. The Lamps (detectives) and the Twin Lamps (Chainbreakers): decided, no answer needed

You settled this on the names call (#74): the detectives are the Lampbearers, and the Chainbreakers see themselves
as the more militant successors of the Twin Lamps. The rest of this item is kept only as the record of why.

**What the plots say.** The detectives of Soulrest are "the Lamps", with ranks Wick, Flame, Lantern and Beacon and a
reward called the Lamp (`lamps.md:111-115`, `:152-156`). The Chainbreakers now descend from Morrowind's Twin
Lamps, by your ruling. The Chainbreakers also have two pages: the old world's (`chainbreakers.md`, seated at
Blackrose) and the new world's (`chainbreakers-newworld.md`, under Stormhold); neither says where they came from.

**What the lore says.** The Twin Lamps' sign was two lamps in a safe house's window (UESP Lore:Twin Lamps;
`topics/loading-facts.md:724-725`).

**Options.**
- **a) Rename the detectives** to the faction-names call's recommendation, the Wasseek-Mota ("the Bright
  Hunters"), and keep "lamp" for the Chainbreakers' safe houses.
- **b) Keep both.** Two bodies called after lamps, one for justice and one for freedom, who are often mistaken for
  each other.
- **c) Give the Chainbreakers a lamp in their sign only**, and leave both names as they are.

**Recommendation: a)**, answered in the faction-names call (item 4 there), and one paragraph in the new
Chainbreakers' page on their Twin Lamps origin. The old world's Chainbreakers page is retired and stays as the
record. Cost: a text change across two pages and the explorer, under an hour.

## 5. The Imperial merchants at Gideon, and who gives letters of marque

**What the plots say.** The Nibenese Company, "the Imperial merchants of Gideon", ships Cyrodiilic goods by sea
into Topal Bay (`merchants.md:10`, `:18`, `:124-127`). Its founder, Gaius Cassena, "wrote letters of marque"
thirty years ago, about 4E 171 (`merchants.md:45`). The Lamps' page has "the Company's pay chest" at Naxan
(`lamps.md:235`). The merchants' and pirates' pages make the Archwardens "the province's rulers" who charter
privateers and "bought" the pirates (`merchants.md:12`, `:35`, `:48`; `pirates.md:16`, `:48`, `:73`).

**What the lore says.** The Empire and the East Empire Company left at the secession; Imperial people remain, with
at most a trade factor at Archon (lore §5); Cyrodiil's trade is the Blackwood Road, shut in 4E 171–172 when the
Dominion took Leyawiin (lore §10.1, §10.3). A letter of marque is a licence a *state* gives; the province's only
navy is the privateers it charters at Archon (lore §5).

**Options.**
- **a) The Company is a Gideon house of Imperial descent**, not an arm of the Empire, trading by the road and by
  sea from Leyawiin. The letters of marque were the Xeech-Leel's, issued at Archon; Gaius bought them for his
  captains. The pay chest at Naxan is the Nibenese Company's.
- **b) The Company is a real Imperial charter company come back**, which the lore would have to allow as the one
  Imperial trade presence since the secession.

**Recommendation: a).** It keeps the merchants' story whole and puts the letters where the lore puts them. Thirty
years ago becomes "in 4E 173, when the road opened again". Cost: about six lines across the merchants', pirates'
and Lamps' pages; the "province's rulers" lines are in the rename map.

## 6. The Argonian order of magic, and necromancy under the law

**What the plots say.** The Sisei-Xul, an old, loose order of magic and alchemy, have a mother hall in Helstrom's
tree-minders' hall and branches at Aroxhu and Tsonac (`sisei-xul.md:27-29`). The Rothalens, Dunmer necromancers,
work behind a trading house at Thorn (`owing-house.md:81-83`, `rothalen-house.md`). One plot has a soul "denied its
return to the Hist" (`sisei-xul.md:66-69`).

**What the lore says.** There are no magical institutions in the province at all (`gap-register.md`, G4.10); the
two live foreign magical interests are the Synod's artefact-hunting and the College of Whispers' Umbriel
necromancy, both deniable. The lore says nothing on whether necromancy is a crime.

**Options.**
- **a) The Sisei-Xul are not an institution** but tree-minders' pupils who share a name, with no hall of their own.
  Necromancy is no crime in Argonian custom, but anything that keeps a soul from its Hist is the Toh-Reel-Ka's
  business.
- **b) Change the lore** to allow one native order of magic, since the gap register's rule was written for foreign
  guilds.
- **c) As a), and the Rothalens are the College of Whispers' hidden hand** at Thorn.

**Recommendation: b) for the Sisei-Xul, with a)'s rule on necromancy.** The order is native and loose, and the lore
rule was about foreign guilds; the Toh-Reel-Ka's interest in a soul kept from the Hist gives the necromancers' line
its danger from the state. Cost: one row in the gap register and a line in each of the two pages.

## 7. The pirates' sea, the Stormwidow's letter and the Arorha boatmen

**What the plots say.** Bramman's Brethren are seated at Skeith Isle and Soulrest (`pirates.md`). The merchants' page
says the Stormwidow's letter of marque was kept by One-Eye in Rainbreaker's strong room (`merchants.md:56`,
`:264-265`); the pirates' page says her captain hid it under the Grey Tooth cairn (`pirates.md:74-75`, `:224-226`).
The merchants' page has the Company's boatman at Arorha land the player on Skeith Isle (`merchants.md:186-188`);
the pirates' page says Arorha's boatmen will not row there (`pirates.md:209-211`).

**What the lore says.** Two pirate grounds: the Archon coast, chartered privateers who stand in for a navy, and
Topal Bay's mangrove river, Bramman's outlaw heirs (lore §5). Skeith Isle is on the Archon side (lore §10.2).

**Options.**
- **a) The Brethren are Bramman's heirs from Topal Bay** who took Skeith Isle from the chartered crews, which is
  their grudge with the state. One fact for the letter (the Grey Tooth cairn; One-Eye kept a copy). The Company's
  boatman is the one exception to Arorha's refusal, because the Company pays.
- **b) Move the Brethren's seat** to the mangrove river near Soulrest.

**Recommendation: a).** Nothing built moves. Cost: about five lines across the two pages.

## 8. The Hist, sap and the tree-minders against the Toh-Reel-Ka

**What the plots say.** In the main quest a root-herald, Vosh-Leera, brings the player into the hidden order and
gives them sap to drink (`main-plot.md`, quests 2 and 3); the order's dreamers see what any tree has seen.

**What the lore says.** The great Root Talk at Helstrom and Lilmoth's two-tender rule exist so that no tree speaks
to one faction alone (lore §2.2, §4). The Toh-Reel-Ka's charge is that no tree, and no one allied with a tree,
brings another Umbriel (lore §2.5). Tree-minders are allied with trees by definition.

**Options.**
- **a) The Root Talk and the tree-minders do not know of the dreamers.** Vosh-Leera serves the Toh-Reel-Ka without
  asking, as her row in the plot already says. The reveal at the gate is then also a political scandal.
- **b) Some minders know and consent**, for fear of another Umbriel.

**Recommendation: a), with b) for one person**: a minder at Helstrom who knows, as a face for the ending. Cost: one
line in the main plot's truth and a row in the cast.

## 12. Hanging, cells and who polices the cities

**Options.** a) Gideon alone keeps Cyrodiil's rope, by its old habits; Thorn does not hang. b) No one hangs.

**Recommendation: a).** Cost: one line in the Argonian order's page.
