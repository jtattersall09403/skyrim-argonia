## 1. The short answer

**We hold far more than we use. The first job is to find out exactly what, by looking, and put it to work. New
art comes after that, for the short list that is genuinely missing, and it is made by hand in a 3D scene with
the generators as helpers.**

Five times in one day I told you something did not exist and it did: a tree pack on Nexus, grave-stakes,
cattails and egg nests, fish weirs, native boats. Each time the cause was the same: searching by name, in one
place, without looking. A lane then checked the "we would make this" lists properly and most of them fell
away (section 2). So the plan is now, in order:

1. **Know what we hold.** Draw every model we hold once, label what it is, and keep that as a searchable
   index. No "make" list is trusted until it has been checked against the index.
2. **Use what we hold and what Nexus has** (the "least" option): held pieces wired into our tables, things
   composed from held pieces, mossy and wet variants by record, lore-true sources, and flat emblems.
3. **Build the model workshop and prove each kind of making once** (the "more" option), on things that are
   genuinely missing.
4. **Widen track by track**, each your choice after its proof.

I still recommend relaxing the rule. But the honest size of the art need is smaller than every earlier version
of this page said, and I will not put a number on it until the index exists.

## 2. What we hold, can get, or must make

### The method, which is now the rule

For every item, in this order, and "not held" may only be written with the searches and looks listed:

1. Name it every way: the lore word, the plain word, the real thing, what a modder might have called the file
   (the grave-stakes are files named "roadsign").
2. Search what we hold by record name, display name and model path, across every mod we hold, vanilla and the
   Creation Club content included; and list model files that no record uses.
3. Search what we have built: drafted places' plans, place records and source places, for the thing existing
   as an arrangement of pieces. Record the recipe.
4. **Look at every candidate.** Our tools already draw any single model alone. A name is never enough either way.
5. Check use: in our tables, placed somewhere, or held and idle.
6. Only then Nexus, in both Skyrim games, by name and description.
7. One verdict: have, compose, get, adapt something close, make, or could not look.

**The tool that makes this cheap:** a looked-at index. We hold 19,385 distinct models, 9,087 of them from
mods. Each drawn once to a small picture and labelled by what it is, stored beside its catalogue row. The lane
estimates a few hours of drawing on one machine for the mod models (not yet run).

## 3. How anything new would be made

### By hand, with eyes, in a workshop

The method is the one your place drafting uses: the agent names what to look at, the tool places the camera,
the agent measures for truth and looks for judgement, changes one thing and looks again. A model workshop is
those same actions pointed at a model. It is built once: Blender on the build machine; our own model tool
taught to put a new shape into a donor's file, to write collision, swaying plants and worn things; a texture
converter; and a few studio actions (grid snap, a piece palette, stairs that go up, placing by relation).

### Measure and look, at every stage

No stage passes on numbers alone or on a look alone, and each stage can send the work back.

| Stage | Measured | Looked at | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brief and reference images | views present, plain background, size | reads as the thing, in its builder's style, beside its anchor | new prompt |
| Generation | triangle count, closed shape, proportions | the outline from fixed views against the reference | throw away, regenerate |
| Shaping by hand | dimensions, grip point and axis, length against the donor | from all sides, in the hand or on the body | edit again |
| Clean-up | see below | before and after from the same views | undo, clean more gently |
| Surface | pixels per metre; colour and brightness inside ranges measured from the game | beside vanilla neighbours in one lighting set-up | re-wrap, re-grade |
| Into the game's format | collision fits, tags and markers kept, the file re-read | the written file drawn in the studio | fix the write step |
| Interior planning | floor heights, headroom, stairs proven, walking mesh connected | from each door and in each room at eye height | re-plan |
| Placement and dressing | seated, reachable, nothing floating or blocking | how it reads where it stands, at a player's distances | move it |
| In game | your check | your check | back to the stage at fault |

**Clean-up is its own checklist:** stray vertices, loose faces and hidden inner geometry removed; holes closed;
tangled patches of mesh rebuilt; the triangle count cut as far as the outline and shading allow, compared
before and after from the fixed views so nothing visible is lost.

**Real geometry, never bounding boxes.** Whenever a model is placed, snapped, seated, fitted, stacked or
dressed onto a surface, the tool works from the mesh's actual triangles and true contact surfaces. A box is
only a first filter for speed. This holds for studio actions, kit snap points, stair ends, door plates, worn
fit, and every "rests on something" check.

### Generators for the shape, shared materials for the surface

Fal already hosts Tripo and Hunyuan 3D on the account we use for music. They make solid objects seen from
outside, at any size. The default for anything solid, small or large, is: **generate the shape from reference
images, clean it by hand, and replace its texture with shared repeating materials** wrapped at a constant
number of pixels per metre. That removes the blur that one stretched texture gives on a big object, and it
makes two things of the same wood the same wood by construction. Skyrim's own ships and buildings are made
this way. A generated texture is kept only where the surface carries meaning (a carving, an emblem). Modelling
from nothing by hand is for what must snap together exactly, and for when the generator's shape is wrong.
Generators do not make rooms, leaves or cloth.

### "Acts like an existing thing"

Your rule, and it runs through everything: copy an existing model file and record, replace only the visible
shape, keep the rest. Read from the vanilla files: a sword's file carries the tag for the bone it hangs from
and its drop collision; a bedroll its sleep markers; a chest opens by three named parts; a bow is one shape on
seven bones. By your ruling, a pike, halberd, spear, quarterstaff, whip, rapier or claw of ours copies the
existing record of that type from Animated Armoury or our other sources.

### One game: the hierarchy

| Level | Fixed once | Who approves |
|---|---|---|
| 0. The game | colour, brightness and sharpness ranges measured from the game's textures; pixels per metre; triangle limits; one lighting set-up for review; a size chart; a "never" list | you, on a line-up beside vanilla pieces |
| 1. Material | one library material per ladder and building material: bone, bog iron, jade, reed weave, mud, thatch, plank, stucco, stone | you, once each |
| 2. Builder | each culture, story line and foreign builder: colours, two or three materials, one sentence of shape, its emblem, references, one hero piece | you, on the sheet and the hero |
| 3. Family | one anchor taken into the game; every sibling made from the anchor, never from another sibling | you, on the anchor |
| 4. Object | purpose, size, the record it copies, the one feature that makes its outline its own | automatic checks; you see the family as a line-up |

Anything built also has a **state** (maintained, neglected, abandoned, ruined, drowned, reclaimed) and a
**location**, applied by record and by hand-dressing (section 4, track E).

### Skills that learn

One map skill, read first, routing to a sub-skill for each stage and each kind of thing, each written as a
clean recipe. Your feedback on any model goes into the skill-fixes list that day; the root cause is fixed; once
you verify it, its general shape is folded into the recipe. The first proofs are where the first version of
each skill is written, from what worked by hand. The have-get-make method above is the first of them.

### Prices and policies

A reference image $0.06 to $0.16; a bare shape $0.20 to $1.00; a textured model $0.38 to $1.30; a kept model
with retries $1.00 to $2.70 (Fal's pages, 24 November). Hand work moves the cost from fees to agent time, which
I cannot estimate; the proofs measure it. Nexus allows AI-made content if labelled (existing mods to comply by
2 January 2027), which our music already triggers if we publish there. Bethesda's paid programme bans it and
is not our channel.

### C. Things genuinely missing

Decided item by item from the index, never from a name search. Today's confirmed list is short: small craft

### D. Plants

Use the held variety first (track A). Then, where the ground layer is still thin: reed, sedge and rice clump
sets as hand-built cards with painted sway, judged at 2, 10 and 40 metres and from a boat; a fern that sways;
a tall stilt-root mangrove form; a signature planting per wet region. New trees are capped by the game: 87 of
256 distant-tree slots are free. A sick and dying Hist: the cheap form is a bark texture swap by record.

### E. The Black Marsh treatment and ruins

A second record of the same model with its own projected material is how Skyrim does snow, ash and moss:
2,691 of its statics carry one. King of the Murkmire has 176 mossy records, each with a clean twin, and it and
Black Marsh & Valenwood carry clean, dirty, wet and moss texture sets for Ayleid and Imperial stone.

**The file its moss material names is not missing from our mods; the game itself does not ship it.**
Twenty-four material records name that file, 21 of them Bethesda's own, and 1,017 vanilla statics use them
(every lightly snowed barrel and rock). Those records are flagged to draw from their own colour. King of the
Murkmire's three moss materials carry Dragonborn's exact moss colour but with that flag off, which is the one
thing needing a look in game. Our own mossy, silted and algae variants copy Dragonborn's record, flag on.

Then hand-dressing by state and location with held vines, roots and rubble, and hand-built pieces only where
the held ones run out.

### F. Wearables, for all ten races

Measured on the game's records: body items need one set of models for all ten races; only headgear multiplies,
to three head models (human and elf; Argonian; Khajiit), so every head item is a three-model set. First the
held pieces are looked at on all ten races, both sexes, thin and heavy. Then, where still needed: rigid head
pieces (one shape on one bone); outfits reshaped from vanilla ones, which keep their fit (rags to a wrap, a
robe to a kilt), with real retextures; shell and bone plates on a cloth base; a horn or feather head part and
tint markings per tribe; a bow on a vanilla bow's bones.

### G. Buildings and interiors

**How Skyrim does it (measured):** a house exterior is a closed shell with its door and windows painted on;
the working door is a separate record; the interior is a separate space reached by activating it. So inside
and outside need only correspond: a believable footprint, doors and windows in matching places, the same
materials. Open-sided structures are the exception.

**Doors: an invisible plate over the painted door.** The building is generated and finished as one mesh, door
included. The agent finds the door on the model by looking, takes its outline from the real geometry, and a
script makes a thin invisible plate to that outline with a collision shape, a hair in front of it. The plate
is the door record: the prompt appears only when pointing at the door, and it loads the interior with no
animation. Vanilla has activatable invisible things already (an invisible manhole door, invisible chairs, an
invisible cooking pot), so the mechanism is ordinary; our own plate still needs one look in game. Windows,
hatches and boat cabins work the same way. The fallback is lifting the door's triangles into their own model.

**Two routes:** organic buildings (mud domes, root-grown houses, tents) are generated, cleaned and re-surfaced
in shared materials, with an interior shell modelled to the same footprint and character, one room or
several; carpentry and masonry are a hand-designed snap-together kit of about 24 pieces on the game's grid,
grey-boxed into three buildings before any finish, then re-skinned for Imperial-colonial and Dunmer building.

**Stairs:** measured on 13 vanilla pieces, steps rise 14.5 to 24 units and floors sit 257 to 264 units apart
in small houses. The loft stairs we hold match the Riften one, so one rule fits. The studio gets a stairs
action (two floor heights and a start point) and a proof for each flight: a walk from foot to head, headroom,
both ends meeting their floors on the real geometry.

**How our buildings enter a place draft (your ruling):** the source place is brought in whole, untouched. The
plan at step 2 records, per structure: kept, relabelled, pruned, or replaced by one of ours, with its interior
kept or ours. At the adapting stage the replacements come first: the old body piece is removed, ours is placed
with its door on the source door's position and facing, and it is seated on our ground. The door link, the
room and everything keyed to them stay valid. An interior of ours goes in at the rooms step, before people are
put on furniture. No hand-fitting is spent beforehand on a structure the plan marks for replacement. Tool
faults found for this, to fix first: the carried walking mesh is not reworked when a piece's model changes; a
model with "root" or "mound" in its name is classed as ground; our own-records tool cannot write a new model,
a door or a room; sizes are cached by path.

**Dressing rooms well.** Measured over 112 vanilla and mod house interiors: plates, cups, food, books and coins
are on the floor under 5% of the time; floor-standing things are within about a metre of a wall or furniture
96% of the time; 84% of beds sit in a corner; three quarters of the middle floor stays clear; 79% of small
things sit in groups of three or more. Research on language models placing objects found that placing by
stated relations scores about twice as well as placing by coordinates. So the skill is: a dressing order
(function, anchor furniture, secondary furniture, surface sets, hung things, the few floor things, one or two
small stories from the household, light); studio actions that place by relation (on a surface, against a
wall, beside, hang from, set a table, fill a shelf, a group of big, medium and small); eleven checks with
those numbers, including a wet-floor rule for Black Marsh (stores raised or hung); and fixed views for the eye
(from the door, from the bed, straight down, each surface close). The published interior guidelines of the
big Elder Scrolls mod teams could not be read by machine and are worth fetching by hand.

### The first test: a dugout canoe

Made on 24 November at the owner's ask, as far as a standard 3D model, and shown in the
[asset explorer](https://claude.ai/artifact/QcwA43dLwFabYVNJi8hSpC) (its record: `newworld/sources/art/dugout-canoe.json`;
the tools: `tools/newart/`). Fal fees about $3.80 by list price. What it showed:

- **The loop works and earns its keep.** The first generation was thrown away on both measure and look: a rope in the
  reference image came back as stray lumps (287 separate pieces on one model). A rope-free reference fixed it. Rule for
  briefs: no thin parts in a reference.
- **Two generators from one image differ a lot.** Tripo H3.1 gave one closed piece that reads as carved wood; Tripo P2
  a slimmer boat with a pebbly surface and loose pieces. Running two and choosing by eye is worth the extra dollar.
- **Clean-up has a floor.** 19,978 triangles to 8,000 is invisible from six views; 4,000 turns the rim angular. Going
  lower means rebuilding the rim by hand.
- **The shared-material idea is not yet proven for look.** On this object the generator's own texture has more
  character than the repeating material, which reads as wood but flat. Carrying the generation's shading as vertex
  colour over the shared material sits between the two. The owner's eye decides the direction.
- **Not done:** Skyrim's model format, collision, the game's measured colour ranges, a distant-view model, placement.

- **The rule.** Decision 0006 point 5 and the "Never make art" line in CLAUDE.md would be amended to say what
  is allowed and how: only what the index shows is missing, made by hand in the workshop under the style
