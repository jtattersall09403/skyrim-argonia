## The task (1.15 §a)
Replace the water planner with `water/solve.py`: one water level per cell, the vanilla way.
1. **Classify every hydrology body** to one level per cell in its footprint: the sea at 0 (`sea`); sea-level
   inland marsh at 0 as `brackish` or `blackwater` by salinity (the dossiers and `50-hydrology-climate.md` say
   which; coastal and tidal reaches brackish, peat and deep-marsh blackwater, upland `fresh`); lakes, lagoons,
   tarns and ponds at their `level_m`; the 1,085 inland bodies between 0.5 and 3 m snap to 0 or to their basin's
   level by a rule you write down (the owner accepted "one level per cell" as the rule).
2. **Rivers**: each reach's profile quantised to steps at cell edges only: flat inside a cell, at most one step
   per edge, the drop bounded by the tallest falls piece in `steps.py`. A reach too steep for that is
   `cell_kind = 'dry'` (a dressed dry gully with bed pieces, vanilla's own answer), re-routed only where a reach
   would need several steps inside one cell. Junctions: the downstream level wins; conflicts between a lake and
   a river in one cell move the step to the edge before the lake.
3. **`water_step` rows** by the vanilla rule (§11): banks above the upper water, the channel the only crossing
   (`cross_lo..cross_hi` vertices), the bed through the edge 100–300 units under the upper surface, the chain id
   from `steps.py` sized to the drop (rapids line, ring, falls body and skirt, mist blast; the counts §11 measured).
4. **Retire** the plane cover, the regrade, the oriented tiles and the budget: delete what nothing imports; keep
   `steps.py`'s piece table (re-measured against §11's counts) and whatever the solve reuses. The pipeline's water
   stage runs the solve and emits per-cell XCLW/XCWT and the step pieces as refs for the ESM writer (no placed
   `Water1024` planes anywhere). The `check` command is the L1 gate and goes into `make world` if not already run by
   `db check`.
5. **Unit tests** of the step rule on synthetic reaches (bed, bank, slope, chain choice, the dry-gully cut-off, two
   levels in a cell rejected).
