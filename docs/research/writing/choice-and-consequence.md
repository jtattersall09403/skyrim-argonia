## What the sources establish

**What makes a choice a choice.** A meaningful choice needs two things: the player knows enough to act (not
everything), and the result touches something the player cares about. Where one option is plainly better, the
player is doing a calculation, not choosing. [V gd-illusion] Meier's tests from the other side: a player who
always takes the first of three, or picks at random, is facing a weak decision; good ones carry a trade-off,
depend on the situation, let the player express a style, persist, and pay off at different speeds. The game should
answer clearly once the decision is made, and too much information beats too little. [V gd-meier]

**Options on different planes.** Options may be incomparable (a gain in one currency against a gain in another) or
unpredictable in outcome; both can be meaningful. [V gd-illusion] Real branching multiplies writing and testing
(Mass Effect 3 tracked about 656 story variables); the cheap middle path is a short acknowledgement, a line or a
letter, which makes the choice feel noticed. [V gd-illusion] That acknowledgement is not an outcome in play. [I]

**Why Mass Effect 3's endings were resented.** In order of weight: dozens of earlier decisions were swept into one
final pick; the three options differed mostly in colour; the companions vanished into a montage that was the same
whatever was chosen. [V psych-games] Add plot holes and promises the endings did not keep; the Extended Cut added
epilogue slides varying by earlier choices and a refusal option but left the three endings in substance as they
were. [V wp-me3] The peak-end rule (the end weighs most in memory) is why a flat ending spoils hours of good play.
[V psych-games] Our fault is the mirror image: where ME3's endings differed in colour, ours differ in telling. [I]

**Asymmetric rewards turn a choice into a sum.** In BioShock harvesting a Little Sister gave about 180 ADAM
against 80 for rescuing her, but every third rescue paid 200 ADAM and extra powers; the designers tilted the whole
towards mercy, so the "hard" choice cost nothing in the end, and the author reads the result as story and
mechanics pulling apart. [V harvard-bioshock] Dishonored's chaos was meant to be neutral, but players read the low
chaos ending as reward and high chaos as punishment, and the tracked score was hidden. [S Wccftech, GameRevolution]
Smith says Arkane wants several solutions, styles and moral stances, and accepts that it costs more than unlocks on
a fixed schedule. [V gd-smith]

**The Witcher 3.** Blacha: a good decision makes the player stop and think about the people involved; consequences
may come later; the player may be unsure they chose well; interests compete without a clear hero; some decisions
are meant to cause regret. [V blacha]

**New Vegas.** Per-community reputation with mixed states of its own; four faction endings each with major flaws;
a writer later doubted the Yes Man route because it let the player finish without dirtying their hands. [S
GameBanshee, PC Gamer] The pages did not load; our reading rests on summaries.

**Morrowind and its expansions.** The player may normally join one Great House of three; each has its own line and
a stronghold built in stages, and some house quests require killing members of a rival house. [S UESP, forums] In
Bloodmoon the werewolf choice splits the line: cured, the player helps the Skaal; uncured, the player hunts for
Hircine, gains a strong beast form but cannot use weapons, armour or magic in it, must feed each night, and is
attacked on sight by everyone once seen changing. The Raven Rock colony changes by the player's sides through its
quests, and either ending makes the player its Factor with an estate. [V wp-bloodmoon] Tribunal forces the fight
with Almalexia; no choice changes play there. [V wp-tribunal] Killing an essential person in Morrowind shows a
message that the thread of prophecy is severed, and play goes on. [K] Our who-must-live message is that rule.

**Oblivion.** Shivering Isles ends with the player made Sheogorath; Mania and Dementia differ in look. [V wp-si] The
Duke choice and its powers, and the Daedric artefacts chosen between, were not readable this pass. [K]

**Followers cost the most.** A follower needs pathing, combat tuning, lines for every place and a part in every
scene; Aranea as the price of the Black Star is the one follower-as-consequence read here. [V fx-blackstar] We add
no followers: our people stand where they are placed and nobody walks for a quest ([quest-form](../../design/quest-form.md)). [I]

## The kinds of consequence in play

Each is something the player can use or notice the next day. [I] Examples from the sources above.

1. A unique thing, with a counterpart one cannot also keep (the two stars; the ring or the hide). [V]
2. A power, spell or passive ability (Vampire Lord, the Nightingale powers). [V/S]
3. A skill gain (a trainer opened; a skill book). [K]
4. Gold: the weakest; the player forgets it (Maro's 3,000 against a whole line). [V gp-dbdestroy] [I]
5. A trader, fence, trainer or service opened or closed (the Greybeards' word walls; the Blades' help). [S]
6. A home, bed or base (Castle Volkihar, Fort Dawnguard, the Raven Rock estate). [V]
7. A door or place opened or closed (a stronghold; a sanctuary). [S]
8. Faction rank and what it unlocks. [K]
9. A follower or ally (Aranea): not ours, see above.
10. Prices and regard (per-community reputation; the Masque's prices). [S/V]
11. Who is alive to trade or teach (Sinding, Barbas, Paarthurnax). [V]
12. A group's hostility (seen as a werewolf: everyone; a rival house). [V wp-bloodmoon]
13. A title, and what people call the player (Thane, Sheogorath, Factor). [V/S]
14. A later quest made easier or harder. [K]
15. Another line's door, opened or shut (the Brotherhood line shut by destroying it). [V]

## Principles

1. **Each outcome changes what the player can do, not only what is said.** [V psych-games, V wp-me3] Method: for each
   ending list what the player can do afterwards that they could not before; an ending whose list is empty, or the
   same as another's, is not finished. An epilogue line may follow; it is the least of it.
2. **Different in kind, not better and worse.** [V gd-illusion, V gd-meier, V fx-blackstar] Method: give each outcome
   a different kind from the list above (a power against a place against a thing against standing), so a player who
   optimises still has to decide what kind of play they want.
3. **No strictly dominant pick, no outcome that only punishes.** [V harvard-bioshock, V gp-daedra, V gp-dbdestroy]
   Method: tabulate gains and losses; if one row is better in every column, trade a column. Ask which a player who
   ignores the story takes: the answer must split.
4. **The kind option costs something felt.** [V harvard-bioshock, S Yes Man] Method: name what mercy gives up (a
   power, a trader, a purse the player would spend) and keep it a real loss; never tilt the rewards back towards
   mercy until it costs nothing; no route spares every cost.
5. **The cost falls on someone the player has met.** [V blacha, V gp-moonlight] Method: the loser is a person or
   group the player spoke to; their loss is a shop closed, a teacher gone, a greeting turned cold, where the player
   will walk past it.
6. **Say the trade before; let the texture be found after.** [V gd-illusion, V gd-meier, V blacha] Method: before
   the choice, a speaker says in plain words what each side gives and costs in kind; who is pleased and who grumbles
   is found later. Never hide the trade itself.
7. **Show what is opened and closed as things to use the next day.** [V wp-dawnguard, V wp-bloodmoon] Method: each
   ending names one place, power, person or service, and the player can reach it in the first minutes after.
8. **Access beats a purse.** [V gp-dbdestroy, V wp-dawnguard] Method: reward with a service, a place, a lasting power
   or a standing before gold; gold alone is never an ending's whole gain.
9. **Fixed rewards in proportion.** [K, I; decision 0035] Method: size each thing or power to the work and to the
   strongest of its kind already in the stack; nothing scales with the player's level; a power must not trivialise
   the next quest.
10. **A power may be swappable where testing it is the fun.** [S Nightingales] Method: a once-a-day swap at one
    place, through a line, rather than a blind lock-in, when the choice is between powers alone.
11. **A faction's ending reaches only as far as its own people and places.** [I; quest ceiling] Method: an ending
    may open or shut its own traders, doors and greetings; another line's door is opened or shut only through a
    stored number that line itself reads, never by moving its stages.
12. **Promise only what is built.** [V gd-illusion, S GameDev.net] Method: every sentence that says "this will change
    things" names the thing, and that thing is a result row of the form.
13. **End on a thing the player did.** [V psych-games] Method: the last beat uses the places, people and powers the
    choice touched, not a summary of the world.
14. **Standing is never an outcome by itself.** [owner, 2026-11-13; S MSFD via outcome-kinds] Method: a rank, a regard
    or a stored number is written only with what it opens or shuts that the player can use (a trainer, a seller's
    stock, a bed, a supply chest, a door, a price, the next job, a power), as Morrowind's ranks each opened a service.

What each kind builds as in our game is measured in [outcome-kinds](../skyrim/outcome-kinds.md).

## Spellmaking (SM): a proposal for a later phase

Morrowind let the player buy the making of spells at a spellmaker: choose effects, magnitude, duration and area,
pay, and keep the spell. [K] Skyrim has no such service. Mods add it two ways: Spell Crafting for Skyrim (SE port)
lets the player combine over forty effects into saved spells through a menu, in Papyrus with SKSE and SkyUI, without
concentration or location-targeted spells; Altars of Spellmaking adds an altar where one primary effect takes up to
three secondary effects; SKSE plugins such as SpellCraft Functions add Papyrus calls to change a spell's effects and
costs, but no altar or menu. [S Nexus search; the pages refused the fetch]

What a plugin of ours would need [I]: a pool of blank spell records shipped in our plugin (Skyrim cannot create a
record at run time), each filled when the player makes a spell (its effects, magnitudes, durations, area, cost and
name set through an SKSE call), saved with the game (the SKSE co-save), and a small menu or a dialogue tree to choose
effects from the effects the player already knows. It is our own SKSE plugin, which the build rules allow where no
mod, record or script does the job (owner, #51); a ready mod is reused first if one fits our load order (CLAUDE.md,
reuse first).

The Argonian way in [I; story/lore/topics/magic-practice.md]: Black Marsh has no robed mages, and Hist-magic does not
follow ordinary spellcraft; spellmaking is a service a Nisswo or a tree-minder offers. The player learns it in the
story as a gift of a Hist: a dream-wallow (isolation and potent herbs) at a sap-speaker's tree lets them shape what
they already know into a spell of their own, and the sap-speaker's hollow, opened by the quest that earns it, is
where it is done afterwards. In the quest form today that is an ending that unlocks a door and opens a person's
lines; the making itself waits for the plugin. To add it to the plan: one phase item, "spellmaking at the Hist",
after the main quest's line is built.
