## Principles

1. A heist is a problem with several valid answers, not a path. Immersive sims set a goal and do not enforce the means; a guard can be sneaked past, bypassed, distracted, bribed or fought, and each has consequences. [V S11, S13] Spector's rule set: problems, not puzzles; the player never has to read the designer's mind; every problem has multiple solutions; spaces have many entrances. [V S12]
2. The plan is half the play. A heist differs from a dungeon crawl because the players are expected to know the floor plan and some defences beforehand, learnt through prep effort. [V S9] Hitman lets players eavesdrop on NPCs to learn target routines and find openings. [V S4]
3. Show the goal, not the way. Spector says to always show the goal, since working out what to do is not the fun part. [V S12] For us, the goal is stated in words (the journal or a person says what is wanted); the route is never marked. [I]
4. Failure escalates and never silently ends the job. Heist failures should become complications that create new problems; avoid twists that scrap the whole plan, since these discourage future planning. [V S9] Spector lists "no forced failures". [V S12]
5. The world remembers, through simulation rather than a global flag. In Thief: Deadly Shadows guards notice missing items and open doors. [V S5] Dishonored tracks chaos (violence, deaths) and changes dialogue, NPC loyalty, enemy presence and endings without calling it good or evil. [V S6] Caution: Death of the Outsider removed chaos, partly because carry-over consequence can feel punishing. [V S11]
6. A deduction is fair when the game controls how guesses are confirmed. Obra Dinn gives a logbook, auto-fills basic facts, and validates fates only in sets of three to deter guesswork. [V S2] This corrects the old note that it "needs several independent clues"; the confirmation is batched, and that pattern became common in later detective games. [V S2]
7. The player finds the discrepancy; the game does not flag it. In Papers, Please the player checks documents against a rule list that grows daily, may interrogate, demand missing papers or scan, and a citation arrives by fax after the traveller has gone. [V S1] The feedback is delayed and tied to a rule, not an instant highlight.
8. Accusation has consequences that play out in the world. In Pentiment the player accuses either who they think did it or who deserves punishment, and evidence is deliberately ambiguous ("rumour and innuendo"); in 1525 an accusation sends the suspect fleeing to a mill the townspeople burn down. [V S8] Disco Elysium's murder is layered: the hanging was staged to hide that the man was shot first, and a vigilante group claimed credit; final scenes ask whether the player solved the case. [V S7] (Earlier claim that these "punish confident wrong conclusions" is unverified; what is verified is layered truth and consequence by choice.)
9. Moral weight lives in cargo and cost. Papers, Please puts a spouse without full papers against a possible terrorist, and keeps rent, food and heat on a budget that can end the game. [V S1] The RDR2/New Vegas cargo claim stays [K].
10. A crime needs a victim the player can meet. [I] Hitman writes a small story for every hotel room using set dressing alone. [V S4] Use that for the victim's house.
11. Guards act on bounded senses. Thief's sound, light and surface model governs detection; sword or bow drawn raises visibility. [V S5] Hitman gives each NPC a routine and ties suspicion to behaviour ("social stealth"). [V S4]
12. Every route has a price. [I] Violence in Dishonored is faster but spends resources. [V S6]
13. Rewards should differ by route. [I] Oblivion's guild was praised for a heist finale and Skyrim's criticised for a dungeon crawl with a boss; Skyrim jobs did not punish killing, and many tasks did not suit the faction's theme. [V S10] Oblivion also removed NPCs whose death ends a questline; that was the stated trade for freedom. [V S10] Morrowind-style fragility is our choice to record. [I]

## Methods

### Method A: Heist in five steps (adapted from S9)
1. Score: name the thing and its holder. It arises from a patron, a rumour or the player's own search. [V S9]
2. Gather: at least three buyable, overheard or readable pieces: layout, defences, an event schedule that offers cover. Expect the player to succeed at gathering; only 10% of the fun is surprise. [V S9]
3. Surveillance: decide which facts can only be learnt on site (S9 suggests plans easy, security limited). Worst-case outcomes of a bungled look: partial facts, misleading facts, or heightened security. [V S9]
4. Prep: alter the information: new entrance, bribed guard, tampered lock, forged pass, supplies. [V S9]
5. Operation: favour complications from failed checks over prepared gotchas; chain them so each new problem follows from an earlier choice. [V S9]
Handouts: write three plan texts per target: complete, outdated or partly wrong, and one clerk's partial memory. [V S9]
Hitman additions: split NPCs into roamers (follow and learn patterns) and stationed (find a way in); public, professional (disguise) and private spaces; when the player holds the facts, stop giving instructions. [V S4]

### Method B: Failure ladder
Three rungs: noticed (search), identified (bounty or faction reaction), compromised (item moved, partner turns). Each rung has a recovery route in data. [I] Align rung one with Thief's detection model and rung two with Dishonored-style reaction by accumulated disturbance. [V S5, S6]

### Method C: Inside man and forged paperwork
A partner with a weakness opens one route at the price of a later favour. [I] Forgeries fail at a named check the player was told about, as in Papers, Please where rules are stated and applied. [V S1] Disguises: Hitman lets players take clothing to reach restricted areas, but some NPCs ("enforcers") see through it. [V S4] Use one such watcher per disguise route. [I]

### Method D: Theft solved (detective)
1. Write the true sequence (who, when, how, what moved, where now). Include a staged layer if useful: the visible scene is a cover-up, as in Disco Elysium's hanging. [V S7]
2. Build the ledger first. Pope used spreadsheets linking characters and fates, and split the story into chapters around ten catalyst events after testers lost the thread when deaths appeared out of order. [V S2] Do the same: one table of suspect, clue, source, what it rules out; group clues into stages. [I]
3. 4 to 7 physical or documentary clues; each conclusion rests on two independent clues, one document and one witness where possible. [I]
4. Confirmation by batch: the investigating NPC (a magistrate or guild-detective superior) only judges the player's report when three conclusions are given at once, and replies with how many are right, not which. [I from V S2]
5. A red herring eliminated by a stated fact. [I]
6. Each suspect hides a non-criminal secret. [I] Pentiment gives many townsfolk motives for one murder. [V S8]
7. The wrong accusation is permitted and costs something in the world (flight, fire, a ruined name); the true culprit stays recoverable until a deadline stage. [I from V S8]
8. Evidence is ambiguous in period terms: no forensic oracle, only testimony and objects. [V S8]

### Method E: Ledger audit (fraud)
1. Choose the lie: padded stock, double-sold goods, forged seals, false relics, skimmed tithes, false weights. [I]
2. Write at least two records that must agree (harbour tally, warehouse book, buyer's receipt) and one number that does not. Keep arithmetic eye-sized. [I] Papers, Please runs on exactly this comparison. [V S1]
3. Layer rules: reveal one new record rule per stage, as Papers, Please adds a rule per day. [V S1]
4. Plant the fact where a topic or a book can be read; no highlight. [I]
5. Confrontation: showing the right records changes disposition or opens an ending. [I]
6. Endings: expose, blackmail, join, let lie. Papers, Please attaches different endings to who was admitted, including a faction that plots against the state. [V S1] Each ending shows in prices, faith or standing. [I]

### Method F: Route and checkpoint (smuggling)
1. Define cargo, tell (smell, noise, weight, seal), origin, drop, two routes. [I]
2. Checkpoint inspectors have a named method (search, ask, lift the lid) and ways past: bribe, hide, distract, forge, wait for a shift change. Papers, Please has interrogation, document demands, body scan and bribes, and bribes risk discovery. [V S1] Sea of Thieves cargo runs stay [K].
3. The cargo's nature is known or learnt before the point of no return; the player may drop, deliver or sell out. [I]
4. After delivery, show a result in the world. [I]

## Anti-patterns

- One corridor, one lock, a cutscene; or a dungeon crawl with a boss as the finale of a thieves' line. [V S10]
- Guild tasks that ignore the guild's theme (a bards' college that starts with a dungeon). [V S10]
- Alarm equals failure; scripted failure the player cannot react to. [V S12]
- Twists that automatically scuttle the plan. [V S9]
- A journal that states the conclusion for the player. [I]
- Documents with one obvious wrong line, or needing outside knowledge. [I]
- Fraud without a victim. [I]
- Cargo revealed as a person only in a cutscene. [I]
- A marker standing in for casing. Counterpoint: Dishonored's first levels gave so little direction that testers got lost or obeyed NPC orders to stay out, so the team added visual cues and verbal hints. [V S6] Without markers we must supply spoken or written directions in-world. [I]
- Guards that share knowledge instantly. [I]
- Fixed reward whatever the route. [V S10 as complaint]
- Brute-force accusation with no cost; batch confirmation is the known fix. [V S2]

## Player-perspective notes

- The player wants to feel they thought of it: 90% of heist fun is solving, not surprise. [V S9]
- They want choices to say something about themselves, not their avatar. [V S12]
- They resent being told the answer, and puzzles needing a guess or designer-reading. [V S12]
- Papers, Please was kept deliberately non-judgemental, with plain status screens so as not to steer feeling. [V S1] Do the same with victim and family text. [I]
- Obra Dinn's own critics found the late ledger fiddly and punishing on exact cause of death, so allow several accepted wordings. [V S3; S2 notes some deaths accept more than one solution]
- A failed heist leading to a harder second job beats a reload. [K]

## Without quest markers in a text-led game

- Directions come as sentences: landmark, walking distance, named person, time of day. [I] Dishonored's playtests show why they must exist. [V S6]
- State the goal in words early (Spector), and withhold the route. [V S12]
- Casing is topic work; each answer names another source; the journal records what was said, not what is true. [I]
- Documents are readable items; the journal notes where found, not meaning. [I]
- Keyword topics become links after the player has heard the word. [I]
- Evidence presentation is a topic action. [I]
- Time and schedule are the map: shift change, ship docking, ledger opening, stated as text. [I; routines per V S4]
- Give the player a self-written plan page assembled from what they learnt, with gaps listed, mirroring the logbook of S2 and the blueprint handouts of S9. [I]

## Checklists

### THEFT (commit)
- [ ] Item, true place, owner; owner has a visible loss.
- [ ] Three information sources (named NPC or object); one fact only learnt on site.
- [ ] Three plan texts: full, outdated, partial.
- [ ] Three routes with visible requirements.
- [ ] Guard knowledge bounded (routine, senses); no global alarm.
- [ ] Failure ladder, three rungs, recovery for each; no scrapped-plan twist.
- [ ] Escape defined: pursuers and where they stop.
- [ ] Aftermath at 1, 3, 10 days.
- [ ] Reward varies by route or cleanliness.
- [ ] Hidden truth: what the item is, who gains.
- [ ] No Nordic element; no marker; no voice.

### THEFT (solve)
- [ ] True sequence, with any staged layer.
- [ ] Ledger table built; 4 to 7 clues; two independent clues per conclusion.
- [ ] Report confirmed in batches of three, count only.
- [ ] Red herring with stated elimination; suspect secrets.
- [ ] Wrong accusation costs; culprit recoverable until deadline.
- [ ] Every clue reachable by reading, asking or searching.

### FRAUD
- [ ] Lie, scale, beneficiary, victims named.
- [ ] Two or more records; one mismatch checkable by eye.
- [ ] Records are items or topics in distinct voices.
- [ ] Confrontation uses evidence; four endings with world change.
- [ ] No highlight; player compares.

### SMUGGLING
- [ ] Cargo, tell, origin, drop; nature known or learnt with a choice.
- [ ] Two routes, named checkpoints, methods past.
- [ ] Shift change as text; failure escalates.
- [ ] Affected person met; delivery result visible; betrayal ending.
