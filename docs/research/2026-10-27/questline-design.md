## Principles

1. **A line is a story with a shape, not a job list.** Pagliarulo says he had long wanted to make a fantasy assassin game, came from Thief's moral ambiguity, and asked what if the protagonist were a remorseless killer rather than an anti-hero thief; the Brotherhood line was his greatest personal achievement. [V https://www.gamebanshee.com/k66yd] Correction: the earlier claims that it aimed to use every stealth tool and cited The Bourne Identity are not on this page; treat as [K]. Lesson: one governing story and one governing mechanic per line. [I]
2. **The best lines turn the mechanic into a puzzle, not a kill list.** PC Gamer's staff single out Oblivion's "Whodunnit?" as a spiderweb of possible outcomes, with the manor a miniature immersive sim, and Oblivion's Ultimate Heist as a multipart infiltration; they say Skyrim's Brotherhood quests mostly end with you killing targets as you would a bandit, and Morrowind's last Thieves Guild job is a plain assassination. [V PC Gamer opinions] Lesson: the finale must use the line's verb in its richest form. [I]
3. **Stakes should climb, and the climb should be earned.** Skyrim's Thieves Guild advances only by "procedurally generated filler jobs" in one editor's account. [V PC Gamer opinions] So the problem is a dull gate, not only a missing one (earlier text said gates were absent; corrected). Skyrim lists six joinable factions including the Companions, College, Thieves Guild and Brotherhood. [V Skyrim WP] The "leader in a few quests" complaint and quest counts (Companions six, College nine) stay [K]; mods adding Thieves Guild prerequisites stay [K].
4. **Rank should be a real gate with varied work behind it.** Fix the filler problem by making each rank's jobs differ in verb and place, not by adding more of the same. [I]
5. **Becoming leader is a finale, never a milestone.** [K/I] If the player leads, it ends the line, changes the hub and costs something. L.A. Noire shows a cost-bearing rank path: Phelps is demoted to Arson after a scandal and the final desk reveals the conspiracy. [V L.A. Noire WP]
6. **A recurring cast carries the emotional weight.** L.A. Noire gives each desk a captain and a partner with a different attitude (Galloway cynical, Earle corrupt, Biggs reclusive). [V L.A. Noire WP] Lucien Lachance and the Night Mother, and the Gray Fox arc, remain [K].
7. **Give factions their own internal disagreement.** Sawyer says the NCR is made of virtuous, well-meaning-but-harmful and petty people, and he likes factions complicated enough that the player keeps weighing their place in them. [V PC Gamer Sawyer] He built factions around "remaking the new world in the image of the old" and tied Legion ranks to Roman history; he picked factions to suit regional history. [V GameBanshee kd6c3; RPG Codex 7106] Lesson: derive each guild from one local history and one contested ideal. [I]
8. **Rivalry must have mechanical teeth.** In New Vegas reputation is tracked per faction, helping one can lock you out of quests for another, a faction can send assassins, and companions can leave if a faction dislikes you. [V Fallout NV WP]
9. **Fiction and the main quest can lean on factions.** Morrowind's main quest sends the player to win each Great House and the Ashlanders as Hortator. [V Morrowind WP] Rolston described the central plot as a chance to introduce a "distracting crosscurrent" of factions and characters. [V Morrowind WP]
10. **Urgency is a budget.** Rolston identified a lack of "narrative urgency" in Morrowind's main plot and later said it might have been presented with greater force, as in Oblivion, without losing free-form play. [V Morrowind WP; the "narrative urgency" sentence carries a citation-needed tag there, the later statement is cited] Pagliarulo notes Oblivion and Fallout 3 keep side quests unconnected to the main quest or each other, so the player can jump freely. [V Game Developer Pagliarulo] Urgency is felt through what the world does while you wait. [I]
11. **A late reveal must reframe earlier cases.** In Ace Attorney's first game the last case ties back to Phoenix's childhood theft accusation and to Larry, and to Edgeworth's father's murder. [V Ace Attorney WP] L.A. Noire reveals the Suburban Redevelopment Fund fire conspiracy only at the Arson desk, and newspapers found at scenes drip-feed the overarching plot. [V L.A. Noire WP]
12. **Choice weight comes from a character the player has mixed feelings about.** Sasko built the Bloody Baron as a parallel to Geralt and had him help Ciri first as "a narrative trick" so the player doubts he is bad; the quest started from one line (a baron wants a monster killed and offers information) and one designer built the events while a writer did dialogue. [V PC Gamer Witcher] The player finds the truth by examining the fort, which shows signs of violence and spilled wine. [V PC Gamer Witcher] That is a clue-by-scene model with no marker.
13. **Companion-style loyalty tasks pay off at the end.** In Mass Effect 2 each loyalty mission's result decides whether a squad member's loyalty is gained, unlocks a unique power and helps in the final battle; endings range from everyone surviving to everyone dead. [V ME2 WP] One blogger found the missions culturally rich (Grunt, Tali) but nearly all ended in gunfights. [V Game Developer ME2 blog] Lesson: make early cases change finale options, and vary the resolution verb. [I]
14. **Divide authorship by line.** Rolston wrote outlines of all Morrowind's quest lines because there were three designers; for Oblivion he delegated outlines to many designers to get more voices. [V https://www.gamebanshee.com/k8c6c] For Argonia, the line record (outline) should be fixed before dialogue is written, matching the quest-write to dialogue-write flow. [I]

## L.A. Noire corrections and structure notes

- The desks are Patrol, Traffic, Homicide, Vice and Arson (five), not an unspecified set; the player starts as a patrolman. [V L.A. Noire WP]
- The "26 cases" figure is not on the page; keep [K]. Eleven cases across the Bunko and Burglary desks were cut, reportedly for disc space. [V L.A. Noire WP]
- Cases: investigate scenes for clues, follow leads, interrogate. Success decides how much of the story is revealed and the case rating. [V]
- Interrogation: each answer is believed, doubted or called a lie; calling a lie requires evidence; a notebook holds evidence, locations and people. [V]
- At the station with two suspects the player chooses whom to charge; the wrong choice lowers the rating but the story continues. [V]
- Aids: Intuition points reveal clues; the investigation music stops when all key clues are found. [V] Argonia has no such cue; replace with a journal list of open questions. [I]
- Critics: some found the game repetitive or weaker late; IGN rated the cases that broke the formula, especially Homicide, among the best. [V L.A. Noire WP] Lesson: break the case template at least once per rank.
- A Homicide arc ends with the real killer found but unable to be named publicly because of a powerful relative. [V L.A. Noire WP] That is a model for an ending where truth and justice diverge.

## Ace Attorney notes

- Five cases are played in order, each alternating investigation (gather information and evidence) and trial (cross-examine, present evidence); they can be replayed in any order afterwards. [V Ace Attorney WP]
- The added fifth case is longer and adds a new tool (forensics such as luminol) for scene work. [V] Lesson: a late, longer case is the place to add one more verb.

## Method: planning a faction line (agent template)

Fill every field; hand the result to `quest-write` as the line record. Do not start job text before fields 1-9 are complete. Use the shape of each field as a gate.

1. **Premise card (five sentences).** Governing question; governing verb; cost of joining; what the faction wants from the player; what it lies about. Check: the question can be asked in one sentence a player could say to an NPC.
2. **Hidden spine.** Culprit or truth, motive, timeline by day, list of physical and spoken proofs, who knows what. Write it first. Cases are slices.
3. **Local root.** One local history and one contested ideal that the faction embodies, with a reason it competes with each other guild (as New Vegas factions do). [V Sawyer]
4. **Rank ladder (5-7).** For each rank: title, entry test (reputation plus a skill, task or knowledge test, never time alone), unlock (tool, topic set, shop, room, cast member), job count, and the single new verb. Final rank is the finale reward.
5. **Job table.** Columns: id, rank, type (fetch, find, follow, question, scene, confront), verb taught, places used, clues planted, who appears. Rule: no two consecutive jobs share a type; at least one job per rank breaks the template (L.A. Noire lesson).
6. **Cast sheet.** Mentor, peer, rival inside, external antagonist, informant, villain on screen by rank 2. Each speaks in at least three jobs before the turn. Each has one contested belief (Sawyer lesson), not one trait.
7. **Rival-faction map.** For each other guild: attitude, one shared case, one conflict case, standing effect, lockout rule, assassin or hostility trigger. [pattern from NV V]
8. **Seeding table.** Columns: conclusion, clue A (job, form), clue B (job, form), decoy meaning, fair-play check (can a player who read only the journal reach it). Minimum two independent clues per finale conclusion, one in a minor job, one in another guild's work.
9. **Mid-line turn.** Position (about 50-65 percent, [I]); what it reverses; earlier cases that bear a second reading; what the player can still do.
10. **Mid-ladder branch.** One case with a wrong-accusation branch that costs reputation but does not end the line (L.A. Noire charge model). [V]
11. **Finale.** Set-piece using the line's verb in full; a choice with a cost; ending states per outcome; at least one ending where truth and public justice diverge. [V pattern]
12. **Hub reaction.** For each rank and ending: changed topics, prices, who greets you, who avoids you.
13. **Abandon path.** The state the world is left in if the player stops at each rank.
14. **Authoring order.** Spine, ladder, cast, seeding table, job table, then dialogue briefs. Review with `quest-review` before any dialogue.

## Anti-patterns

- Leader in a handful of quests, or a leader post requiring no skill. [K; criticism pattern in PC Gamer opinions V]
- Dull gate: promotion by repeated filler jobs. [V PC Gamer opinions]
- Finale resolved by the same verb as every prior job (kill like a bandit). [V PC Gamer opinions]
- All missions of one type resolved by one kind of fight. [V ME2 blog]
- A hub that does not react to rank or ending. [I]
- A mid-line twist needing no earlier clues. [I]
- Villain appears only at the end. [I]
- Rival factions as set dressing. [I; contrast V NV WP]
- Countdown timers in an open world. [I]
- Verb taught as a dump instead of a job that forces it. [I]
- Finale solvable by guessing or a marker (Argonia has none). [I]
- Line unrecoverable if abandoned. [I]

## Notes for the detectives' faction line

- **Position.** A civic, evidence-first order between assassins and thieves; cases involve the other two guilds as victims, suspects or sources. [I]
- **Desk model.** Use named desks, one per rank, each with its own captain, partner type and tone, as L.A. Noire does with Patrol, Traffic, Homicide, Vice, Arson. [V] Suggested Argonian desks: Quarrels (missing item, false accusation), Fences (theft ring), Missing (vanished person), Poisons (poisoning or arson touching an assassin contract), Graves (Hist or temple grave robbery), Crown (the grand murder). [I]
- **Break the template per desk.** One case per desk changes the verb or perspective (a scene-only case, a pure interview case, a case you cannot solve fully), echoing IGN's praise for the formula-breaking Homicide cases. [V]
- **Teach investigation in stages.** Examine a scene (clue by place, as in Family Matters, where signs of violence and spilled wine reveal the truth [V]); interview by topic keyword; challenge a statement with a named piece of evidence (L.A. Noire's lie-with-proof rule, expressed as a topic the player must have in the journal [V/I]); compare two statements; follow a lead; accuse in the journal. No markers. The journal states what was learnt and lists open questions. [I]
- **Accusation with consequence.** At the finale and once mid-line, the player names the culprit from the journal. A wrong choice lowers standing and changes the ending but the story continues. [V L.A. Noire]
- **Seeding.** Choose culprit and three proofs before case 1. Hide one in a petty case's detail, one in a thieves' job, one in an assassins' contract. Add two decoys guilty of something else. [I]
- **Sympathy trick.** Give the culprit or a suspect an early act of kindness so the player doubts the verdict, as with the Baron. [V]
- **Recurring cast.** Rival detective who becomes a suspect; assassin informant; fence seen in three cases; the victim's relative at the finale; a partner per desk with a different attitude. [V pattern L.A. Noire; I]
- **Conspiracy truth and justice.** Finale may reveal a guilty person too powerful to name publicly; the player chooses to leak, bargain or bury it, each with hub consequences. [V pattern L.A. Noire Homicide arc]
- **Sandbox finale.** Set the final reveal in one place with many possible actions and outcomes, as Whodunnit does for guests in a manor. [V PC Gamer opinions] Apply to a gathering in a household or temple, with suspects moving on a timetable. [I]
- **Fair play.** A player can name the culprit before the finale from gathered text only. Correct deduction gives a different ending branch, not a lockout. [I]
- **Rank and restriction.** Closes no other guild; costs thieves' standing above a given rank. [I]

## Checklist for a questline review

1. Premise, governing verb and hidden spine written down.
2. 5-7 ranks, each with a non-time entry test and unlock.
3. Leader post only at the finale, with a cost.
4. Jobs per rank mixed in type; at least one template break per rank; counts stated.
5. One new verb per rank; no case demands an untaught verb.
6. Cast present before the turn: mentor, peer, rival, antagonist, informant, villain.
7. Mid-line turn placed, with named earlier cases it re-reads.
8. Seeding table complete; every conclusion has two early clues.
9. Rival-faction map with one cooperative and one conflicting case, and a hostility trigger.
10. Hub text, prices and topics change per rank and per ending.
11. Abandoning the line leaves the world consistent.
12. No marker dependence; the journal alone carries the thread.
13. Urgency shown through consequences, not timers.
14. Vanilla Skyrim assets or words routed through quarantine review (decision 0012).
15. Ending states exist for each outcome and are testable at L4.
16. Content written through `quest-write`, `quest-review`, then `dialogue-write` (CLAUDE.md, decision 0017).
