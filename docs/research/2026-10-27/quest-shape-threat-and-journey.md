## Corrections to the earlier draft
- The "triangle rule" is from the Nintendo **CEDEC 2017** talk "Field Level Design in The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild" by Hidemaro Fujibayashi (director) and Makoto Yonezu (senior lead artist), translated by Matt Walker; it is not the GDC 2017 talk, which was Takuhiro Dohta's "Breaking Conventions". [V S4, S5, S6]
- The triangle rule is about choice and concealment (go over or around; what is behind it?), not "shapes that draw the eye on" alone. It has three scales with different jobs. [V S5, S6]
- The Outer Wilds ship log is a computer record, shown by location or as a web of connections; it is the only thing that survives a loop. [V S1]
- Pathologic is not "no timer": it has a visible twelve-day clock, daily quests that vanish at midnight, and a world that carries on whether or not you act. [V S2]

## Principles

1. **A threat is a puzzle about knowledge before it is a fight.** Witcher 3 offers a bestiary for learning enemies and preparing, and contracts are elaborate monster-hunting side missions; some monsters change with the day-night cycle (a werewolf is stronger on a full-moon night). [V S7] The "rumour, trace, identify, prepare, fight, twist" sequence and Monster Hunter's research habit are [K]; S10 confirms hunters work alongside a research team studying monster behaviour. [V S10] For us: the fight is the last stage, and the clues decide how well the player enters it. [I]
2. **The reward of investigation is a usable advantage.** The bestiary and witcher senses exist so players can prepare; one reviewer preferred the senses to objective markers. [V S7] Monster Hunter's loop is loot to craft to face stronger monsters. [V S10] Every PREDATOR clue should map to a choice (bait, weapon, time of day, route, ally). [I]
3. **Warn, then let the player choose.** Dark Souls passes warnings between players through ground messages and bloodstains that replay a death. [V S8] Bonfire rest respawns most enemies, so danger is learnt, not erased. [V S8] Fog gates, a lone guard and boss-arena legibility are [K]. Elden Ring's Caelid is a wasteland of undead monsters beside the gentle Limgrave, which is fixed regional difference. [V S9] For Argonia's D0-D5 bands, the warning is part of the quest, never a number. [I]
4. **Bounty-board quests fail when the creature is only a marker.** Witcher 3 hangs contracts on town noticeboards, but a reviewer's praise for senses over markers shows what players value: reading the scene. [V S7] Criticism of "kill X at Y" Morrowind and Skyrim quests is [K]. We have no markers, so cause must come from the community's own account. [I]
5. **A contamination quest is an investigation of source, spread and cure.** Pathologic: three healers try to find the source of a lethal "sand plague"; each has a set of people they must keep alive; endings differ in what the town pays. [V S2] Morrowind's blight and corprus tie to place and named cures, and Fallout water quests are [K]. The structure: who fell ill first, where they drank, what they shared, what stops it. [I]
6. **Pressure should be felt, with a visible clock or a fixed ladder, never a hidden one.** Pathologic's pressure is open: twelve days, quests erased at midnight, a game that continues regardless. [V S2] Reviewers of Outer Wilds also criticised unfair loop deaths and dead-ends without direction. [V S1] Prefer a fixed ladder of consequences that fire on the player's acts or visible in-world delay (another house sickens a day after the second clue); record the ladder in the quest record. [I]
7. **Curiosity is the engine of expeditions.** Outer Wilds is self-directed; its computer shows what you have found and the links between findings; its writing is made of many small mysteries so that learning is the feedback. [V S1] Each Nomai conversation hints where earlier ones can be found and where the writers meant to go next, because players read out of order. [V S1] Beachum drew on Wind Waker NPCs who tell tales of distant lands to tempt the player there. [V S1] Subnautica gates depth through survival stats (oxygen among them); radio and wreck cues are [K]. [V S11] Our journal plays the log role: it records claims and leads in the player's terms. [I]
8. **Landmarks beat markers, and concealment beats a map.** Triangle rule: big triangles are visual landmarks, medium ones block the view, small ones set tempo; rectangles hide something completely; the chain was hill, bridge structure, tower, revealed in turn. [V S5, S6] The towers were first used to space out events and felt too guided and too linear, so they were moved to pull players off the beaten path ("gravity"). [V S5, S6] Climbing nearly anything lets players reach places without a set path. [V S3] Sea of Thieves treasure quests give a map or a riddle. [V S12] Compass bearings in them are [K]. An Argonia target needs one landmark visible in advance and two on the way. [I]
9. **Morrowind's direction text is the baseline, and its journal is the warning.** Morrowind lets the player roam and follow quests in any order. [V S13] The journal grew into a long, unorganised "muddled mess" that reviewers almost universally disliked. [V S13] That is a rule for us: the journal must group by quest and show state. [I] The structure of direction text (start, bearing, landmark, stop cue) is [K]. Boats, swimming and climbing widen routes, so directions describe the destination and its signs. [I]
10. **An expedition has preparation, a point of no return and a changed return.** Preparation = learn what is needed (air, antivenom, boat, guide). Point of no return = a place where retreat costs something. Return = the world, player or NPCs differ. [I]
11. **Danger bands are a promise.** Two independent signs precede any D4 place (NPC fear, remains, wildlife behaviour, terrain, sound). [I] Souls-like practice of fair warning before commitment is [K]. Design art with less detail where there is nothing to find, as Outer Wilds does, so detail itself signals "look here". [V S1]

## Methods and patterns (agent-followable)

**PREDATOR**
- Pattern "Traces, Account, Lair": three clue tiers. Traces are found in the world; the Account is what the community says, with one wrong belief; the Lair confirms and sets the fight. [I]
- Write the creature's habit first (time, tide, bait, territory), as the Witcher werewolf's moon dependence shows habit can matter. [V S7] Then exploit it without a number. [I]
- Method: for each clue write "Clue / where / what choice it changes / who else knows" in the quest record; reject any clue with an empty third field. [I]
- Declare a twist category: wrong culprit, creature provoked, community feeding it, solution is not killing, remedy is a trade. [I, from K]
- The request needs a cost and a witness: why it is unsolved. [I]
- Provide a non-combat resolution (move the herd, seal the nest, bribe the guardian, redirect the flood) whenever the creature's band exceeds the likely player band. [I]
- Place one weak, visible sample of the threat (a D1 scout of a D4 pack) where the player will meet it unprepared and survive; it teaches the habit. [I]

**CONTAMINATION**
- Pattern "Source, Spread, Sign, Cure": source (well, sap, fish, ruin, Hist tree), spread path (people, water, trade), a visible sign (colour, smell, fungal growth, dead insects), and a cure that needs gathering. [I]
- Make diagnosis available by examination in dialogue and by environment: contaminated places look wrong first. [I]
- Misdiagnosis beat: two plausible sources, one confirmed by a test the player can run (upstream and downstream water, an animal). [I]
- Method: write a "first ill, last well" order of five households in the hidden truth; each must be consistent with exactly one spread path. [I]
- Use Pathologic's lesson that factions disagree about cause: give each faction a wrong cause that suits it. [V S2 for the disagreement over what to do with the town; cause rumours K]
- Cure requires a journey or a trade, so CONTAMINATION often nests an EXPEDITION. [I]
- The end state persists: wells closed, a healer remembered, survivors altered. [I]

**EXPEDITION**
- Pattern "Rumour, Route, Gate, Prize, Return". [I]
- Three kinds of preparation hint: an NPC who went before, the land at the threshold, a store (what a merchant refuses to sell without warning). [I]
- Place the first reveal as a Outer Wilds-style lead: a conversation or find that names the next place, so leads chain without a marker. [V S1 for lead-chaining]
- Plan the approach with the triangle: a large landmark at the far end, a medium obstruction midway that hides the goal, small obstacles for tempo. [V S5, S6]
- Failure that is not death: lost cargo, drowned boat, companion left behind, closed route. [I]
- Flag the point of no return in dialogue before and again in the place. [I]
- Use the Dark Souls message-and-bloodstain idea as flavour: remains of an earlier party (a wreck, a cairn) carry the warning. [V S8 mechanic; Argonia use I]

## Anti-patterns
- A creature whose only clue is its location. [I]
- Contamination cured by fetching one named item with no cause shown. [I]
- Directions that name only a route a boat or climb would trivialise. [I]
- A warning that is only the word "dangerous". [I]
- Levelled or rescaled enemies to rescue the player (breaks D0-D5). [I]
- Surprise lethal danger with no earlier sign. [I]
- A twist made by reversing an NPC's earlier, truthful statement. [I]
- A hidden timer. [I]
- Dialogue that restates the journal verbatim. [I]
- A journal that appends entries in time order with no quest grouping; Morrowind's did. [V S13]
- Placing the goal on a straight line from the start, which makes the trip feel guided; BotW found this. [V S5]

## Player-perspective notes
- A player who reads everything wants the clue chain to pay off; one who skips should still be warned by the place. [I]
- Players test the safe option first and will walk up to the monster without clues; the band warning must prevent death through sign, not invisible walls. [I]
- Players arriving by boat or climb may come from the wrong side; the destination must still read as the destination. [I]
- Players read out of order (Outer Wilds' writers planned for it). [V S1] Each clue should say where its siblings are.
- After a return the player wants acknowledgement: at least one NPC line changes. [I]

## Writing directions without markers
1. Start from a place the player has certainly seen (a named landing, shrine, bridge). [I]
2. Give a bearing in the world's terms (upriver, toward the dead trees, sun behind you on the morning tide). [I]
3. Give a count or distance in effort (two bends, half a morning's poling). [I]
4. Name one hard-to-miss landmark and one that confirms arrival; make the first the "large triangle" of the route. [I, from V S5]
5. Include a failure sign ("if the water turns black you have gone too far"). [I]
6. Offer a second way if swimming, climbing or a boat changes the route. [I]
7. Let different NPCs give overlapping directions; the player triangulates. [I]
8. The journal restates only what the player was told, in the speaker's words, grouped under the quest. [I, from V S13]
9. Test: could a player who lost the journal reach the place from the text alone? If not, add a landmark. [I]
10. Test: from the destination, can a player see one of the landmarks named? If not, add one that is visible on arrival. [I]

## Checklists

**PREDATOR**
- [ ] Habit, cause and reason it is unsolved written down.
- [ ] Three clue tiers (trace, account, lair); at least one clue is in the world, not in dialogue.
- [ ] Each clue changes a choice (gear, time, route, ally).
- [ ] Band signalled by two independent signs before reach.
- [ ] A non-combat resolution exists, or the record says why not.
- [ ] A twist is declared and fair.
- [ ] After-state recorded: community, route, journal.

**CONTAMINATION**
- [ ] Source, spread, sign and cure all in the hidden truth.
- [ ] The place looks wrong before anyone says so.
- [ ] One misleading explanation is plausible and testable.
- [ ] No hidden timer; consequences are a fixed ladder or a visible clock.
- [ ] The cure needs gathering, travel or a trade.
- [ ] The changed world persists.

**EXPEDITION**
- [ ] Rumour, route, gate, prize and return are each a stage.
- [ ] Directions pass the "lost journal" test and survive boat, swim and climb routes.
- [ ] A landmark is visible in advance and two more on the way.
- [ ] Preparation hints come from an NPC, the threshold and a vendor.
- [ ] The point of no return is flagged twice.
- [ ] A non-lethal failure state exists.
- [ ] The return changes something in the world or a person.
