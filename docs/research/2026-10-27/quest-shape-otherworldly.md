## Principles

1. **A haunting is a biography with a locked door.** The dead person's story is the quest. [V] The Bloody Baron quest (Family Matters) hangs on a human story: the Baron's drunken assault on his wife caused a miscarriage, the hastily buried child became a botchling, and Geralt finds this by reading the abandoned fort (signs of violence, spilled wine) before anyone says it (pcgamer Baron piece). The monster is a consequence of a person's act. [K] "Laid to Rest" in Skyrim is the weaker form, with clues present but the answer largely handed over.
2. **The ghost's wish and the ghost's need differ.** The best hauntings let the dead ask for something that does not settle them; the player's understanding of the gap is the climax. [I]
3. **Resolution is a verb from a small set: lay to rest, bind, release, avenge.** [V] In the botchling case Geralt can make the Baron give it a proper burial, freeing it, or kill it, which turns it into a boss fight; the piece calls burial the more interesting route because it shows the "haunted soul behind his jokes" (pcgamer Baron piece). So the kind resolution is a consequence for a living person, not only for the spirit; the violent option exists but is the blunter one. Give two or more with different costs, and let the unkind one be reachable. [I]
4. **Make the player's judgement of a living person the real stake.** [V] Sasko built the Baron as a mirror of Geralt (two fathers with blood on their hands) and deliberately made players hesitate, "to show both sides", so the choice carries weight (pcgamer Baron piece). A haunting's accountable party should be someone the player has come to half like. [I]
5. **Dread comes from small wrongness, not spectacle.** Repetition with variation, objects out of place, sounds without source. [K] Silent Hill and Fatal Frame lean on this. In an unvoiced journal game, description and journal text carry the equivalent. [I]
6. **Folk horror wants a community that knows and will not say.** [V] Darkwood's setting is a forest that has trapped survivors and a plague that turns victims into monsters, and its storyline changes with whose requests the player honours (Wikipedia Darkwood). [K] The settlement-as-accomplice reading is stronger in Pathologic; not read. The earlier claim that Darkwood makes a village complicit is corrected: it is a trapped-survivor, branching-by-favour structure, not a conspiracy of silence. Evasive topic answers from a haunted village remain the Morrowind-compatible form. [I]
7. **A rite is a three-beat skeleton.** [V] Van Gennep: separation (detachment from earlier status, often symbolic "cutting away", e.g. shorn hair), liminal phase (ambiguous status, "threshold people"), incorporation (re-entry with a new status, marked by outward symbols such as the cord, knot, ring, belt or crown). Wikipedia Rite of passage. [V] The liminal rite follows a prescribed sequence under the authority of a master of ceremonies (Wikipedia Liminality). The quest mapping is [I].
8. **A rite with its third beat missing is the template for a haunting.** [V] Wikipedia notes that modern imitations of rites of passage lose force when the society's recognition and reincorporation phase is missing (Rite of passage; Outward Bound example). Argonia use: a person left in the liminal phase, never publicly recognised, becomes the restless one. [I]
9. **Liminality must end.** [V] Turner: liminal states are intense and cannot last without structure; either the person returns to the social order or the liminal group builds its own ("normative communitas"). The liminal phase also suspends hierarchy and invites scrutiny of the culture's values (Wikipedia Liminality). So a lingering liminal site, such as a threshold swamp-camp where roles are reversed, should be on a clock or visibly decaying. [I]
10. **A rite has breakable elements.** Place, hour, offering, officiant, words; quests branch on which is missing, wrong or forced. Stopping a rite needs the same knowledge as performing it. [I]
11. **Wonder is a short, complete moment, not a quest in miniature.** [K] Morrowind's falling Tarhiel and the Mudcrab Merchant (not re-checked this session; UESP blocked). [I] Under two minutes, one image, one rule.
12. **Unexplained must be consistent, and the answer must exist.** [V] Outer Wilds' designer states the world runs on its own clock whether or not you are present, that knowledge is the only thing carried across loops, and that the only reason to explore is curiosity, with collectibles and upgrades removed (Game Developer). [V] The ship log records discoveries and links clues (Wikipedia Outer Wilds). [V] Shadow of the Colossus is minimal in narrative but its premise has a hidden mechanism the player eventually learns: the colossi hold fragments of Dormin, and Wander physically deteriorates as he kills them (Wikipedia SotC). The silence works because the data under it is complete. Correction to the earlier draft: Outer Wilds does not withhold the why; it makes it recoverable from in-world text.
13. **Curiosity needs familiarity first.** [V] An early Outer Wilds build dropped an object from space into the village; it failed because players did not yet know the village, so nothing registered as new. "You can't expect someone to be curious about something specific unless they're already familiar with everything in their immediate vicinity" (Game Developer). Wonder encounters therefore belong in places the player has already walked past. [I]
14. **Text is read when its writers share the player's predicament.** [V] Outer Wilds' designer found players care about found text when its authors are going through the same thing as the player, which turns "I found a book in Skyrim" into wanting to know what happened to them (Game Developer). Use for haunting notes and rite remnants: the writer is mid-crisis, not an encyclopaedia. [I]
15. **Restraint scales with frequency.** Rare wonders stay wonders; do not repeat a trick in one region. [I]
16. **Daedric quests are tests of a patron, and tone is the craft.** [V] The Shivering Isles splits into bright Mania and gloomy Dementia, and Sheogorath's personality mirrors the realm: goofy ramblings that usually end with a grim threat; the PC Gamer piece credits the "gurning" NPCs and the portal waiting room (an officious clerk, a ticking metronome) as tonal deadpan before the strangeness (pcgamer Shivering Isles). [V] Wikipedia: the main quest can be ignored for as long as the player likes. [K] Other Princes (Clavicus Vile's bargains, Hircine's hunt, Molag Bal's cruelty, Boethiah's betrayal-for-glory) not re-checked.
17. **Mundane delivery of the uncanny amplifies it.** [V] The Isles' entry works because the waiting room is "profoundly ordinary" until the walls dissolve (pcgamer Shivering Isles). [I] The Daedric ask should arrive in a flat, bureaucratic or domestic voice.
18. **Criticism of the expansion's later quests is repetition.** [V] GameSpot and GamePro reviews quoted in Wikipedia rate some later Shivering Isles quests repetitive or uninspired, while praising the creative early ones. [K] The Skyrim Daedric quests are often fetch-kill with a late twist; not re-checked.
19. **Text-led direction is a deliberate tradeoff, not a flaw to patch.** [V] Morrowind has no compass; quest givers give verbal directions, and ESO's director says few modern players would accept "the third tree on the right and 50 paces west" (pcgamer Firor piece). [V] The journal was widely criticised as an unorganised "muddled mess" (Wikipedia Morrowind). So Argonia must keep the landmark-based directions but fix the journal: organise by quest and record what is known. [I]

## Methods and patterns

**M1. Haunting spine (agent-followable).**
1. Write the hidden truth first: who died, how, who knows, what wrong remains, and who is accountable. If nobody is, add someone.
2. Write the dead person's last scene as physical evidence (a site, a placed object, a trace), because the Baron quest teaches the truth through traces in a room before dialogue states it. [V pattern, pcgamer Baron piece]
3. Choose the manifestation (sound, object, shape, a double). Make it identical each time with one variation as the clue.
4. Place three clues in three holders: a place, a living person, a thing. Each is solvable alone and sharper combined.
5. Give the living a reason to hide it, and make the main living party sympathetic enough that the player hesitates (principle 4).
6. Define two resolutions with different costs, one of which is blunt violence and one which requires the living to face the truth. Add a failure that leaves things worse but not dead-ended.
7. Write the aftermath: the manifestation changes or ends, and a topic or journal line reflects it.

**M2. Something wearing their shape.** The shape is wrong in one way a careful player can learn (a habit, a name, a detail only a relative knows). Telling it from the real spirit is the investigation. [I; Argonia candidates: Hist-sap, ancestral spirits, Daedric mimicry, Kothringi remnants]

**M3. Rite skeleton.** Write the rite as data: required elements, officiant, place, hour, taboo, and for each of separation, liminal, incorporation: what is given up, what is endured, what symbol marks the new status and who witnesses it. Then state the quest as "what changed":
- Gone wrong: one element failed, giving a haunting or blight.
- To be stopped: the player learns the elements to break one.
- To be performed: the player gathers elements and may substitute at a cost.
- Stalled in the liminal phase: someone is stuck between statuses, with a clock (principle 9).
Incorporation must be spoken in the world by a witness. [I, using V rite structure]

**M4. Wonder card.** One image, one rule, one reward or none, no journal entry unless the player acts, under about two minutes, off the critical route. Place it near ground the player has already crossed (principle 13). [I]

**M5. Daedric/oddity card.** Patron or odd source, errand tone (flat and ordinary, or gleeful, per patron), the squeeze (what the player gives up or does to someone), the reward and its quirk, the refusal path. Open with a mundane framing and let the strange arrive second (principle 17). The errand site is dangerous at one stated level. [I]

**M6. Clue economy for a text-led game.** Each clue is a topic keyword in a journal entry, note, spoken line or book. New keywords unlock new answers from named NPCs. Journal entries record knowledge, grouped by quest, to avoid Morrowind's "muddled mess" (principle 19). Notes are written by someone mid-predicament (principle 14). [I, extending V Morrowind practice]

**M7. Familiarity gate.** Before a mystery is seeded in an area, make sure the player has been given the ordinary version of the thing (the village, the shrine type, the burial custom). Seed the strange variant afterwards. [I, from V Outer Wilds lesson]

## Anti-patterns

- Ghost who explains everything in the first conversation. [K]
- Arbitrary cause: nobody accountable. [I]
- One right answer with a "wrong" branch that only fails. [I]
- Mystery sprung before the player knows the ordinary. [V, Outer Wilds village failure]
- Jump-scare in place of dread; the text-led format cannot support timing-based scares. [I]
- Rite as a shopping list with no stakes about whose it is. [I]
- Wonder that doubles as a hidden quest chain with rewards. [I]
- Mystery with no answer in the data. [I]
- Found text written by a narrator who is not in the same situation as the reader. [V, Outer Wilds playtests]
- Daedric fetch-kill with a speech at the end. [K; V that later Isles quests drew "repetitive" criticism]
- A journal that is one growing list. [V, Morrowind]
- Importing Nordic burial, draugr or any vanilla ghost model unreviewed (CLAUDE.md, 0006, 0012). [project rules]
- Marker-dependent staging ("go to the glowing spot"). [I]

## Player-perspective notes

- The player wants to feel clever for noticing, not for following. [I]
- Use the dead person's name early and consistently. [I]
- A rite performed correctly should feel like a privilege. [I]
- Wonder is judged by the one sentence a player tells another. [I]
- Refusing a Daedric offer should be possible and remembered, not punished. [I]
- Unvoiced means short, concrete sentences. [I]
- Expect some players to dislike pure text directions: a mainstream MMO director says so on the record [V], which is why Argonia's directions must be accurate and the journal good, since this is a deliberate niche choice.

## Without quest markers in a text-led game

- Direction is in the words: named places, landmarks, bearings by sight, time of day, who is where. [V that Morrowind works this way; I for the rest]
- Every location clue names something visible on approach (a scarred tree, a split stone, a smell). [I]
- A haunted place shows wrongness before arrival: birds absent, water still, NPCs avoiding the route. [I]
- Rite times and places are world facts in topics, so the player can wait or travel. [I]
- The journal records what is learned, may end on an open question, and lists clues by quest. [I]
- Wonder is found by wandering; never signpost it. [I]
- A haunting site is dangerous at one stated level, and NPCs warn those who ask. [I]
- Outer Wilds' ship log shows the unexplored edges of what is known rather than orders [V Game Developer / Wikipedia]; a journal "open questions" line does the same job. [I]

## Checklists

**HAUNTING**
- [ ] Hidden truth: name, death, wrong, who knows, who is accountable.
- [ ] Death scene present as physical trace.
- [ ] Manifestation identical each time, one variation as clue.
- [ ] Three clues, three holders, each solvable alone.
- [ ] Living reason for silence; living party sympathetic enough to cause hesitation.
- [ ] Two or more resolutions with different costs, one blunt.
- [ ] Aftermath changes the place, a topic and the journal.
- [ ] No Nordic or vanilla ghost imagery.
- [ ] Reachable from spoken or written directions alone.

**RITE**
- [ ] Rite as data: elements, officiant, place, hour, taboo.
- [ ] Separation, liminal trial, incorporation each present, with a witness for the third.
- [ ] Missing or wrong element changes the outcome.
- [ ] Player can perform, stop, or corrupt it.
- [ ] Any liminal site has a clock or visible decay.
- [ ] Rite is Argonian (Hist, ancestors, Sithis, Kothringi), not a borrowed Nordic or Imperial form.

**WONDER**
- [ ] One image, one rule, under about two minutes.
- [ ] No faction stakes; no explanation, but an answer in the data.
- [ ] Off the critical route, not signposted, near already-familiar ground.
- [ ] Not repeated in the region.
- [ ] Reward none or small and odd.

**DAEDRIC / ODDITY**
- [ ] Patron's character is in the errand's tone, opened in a mundane voice.
- [ ] A real moral squeeze, shown not announced.
- [ ] Refusal path exists and is remembered.
- [ ] Reward single, odd, costly, expressive of the patron.
- [ ] Danger fixed and stated; no marker.
- [ ] Not fetch-kill with a speech at the end.
