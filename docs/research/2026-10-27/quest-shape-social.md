## Principles

1. **Factions are made of people who disagree.** Sawyer said the aim was for faction members to feel like their own people. A monolithic faction is untrue to life and gives the player too simple a decision. The NCR holds virtuous people, well-meaning people who do harm, and petty malicious ones; the Legion pairs an ideology and a clever leader with repulsive rank and file. [V 1] Apply: each party in a DISPUTE has at least two members who would argue the case, and one privately doubts. [I]

2. **Complexity is a design goal, and the player stays a participant.** Sawyer wants players to keep weighing their own place in the situation, feeling central without controlling everything. [V 1] Apply: every ending leaves the player owning a consequence they can name. [I]

3. **Reputation is plural.** In New Vegas some factions dislike each other, so helping one can bar quests from another; companions can leave over a faction's dislike. [V 15] Apply: a ruling moves at least two ledgers in opposite directions. [I]

4. **Reveal order makes a person grey.** The Baron's abuse of his wife is revealed after the player learns he sheltered Ciri; Sasko calls this a narrative trick to make you doubt he is really bad. He was also given charisma and humour so the player feels conflicted. [V 3, 4] Apply: show the claimant's good case before the damning fact. [I]

5. **Give the dilemma a parallel.** Sasko built the Baron as a mirror of Geralt: two fathers who lost family, with blood on their hands. [V 4] Apply: write the hardest party in a DISPUTE as a foil of the player's stated values. [I]

6. **Consequences must be visible, and delay strengthens them.** Sasko: subtle consequences go unseen; delayed ones, returning at the least expected time, leave the biggest mark; do not invest in what is not telegraphed. [V 2] Apply: every ending leaves a place, an NPC line and a topic changed, findable without a marker. [I]

7. **Choices need stakes, dilemmas and expected options.** Sasko advises ambiguous situations, stakes set up before the choice, and options that match the player's natural urge. [V 2] Apply: write the obvious response as an option, then make it cost. [I]

8. **Failure should redirect.** Disco Elysium's failed rolls led the reviewer onto more interesting paths rather than dead ends, and mistakes were expected rather than punished by a karma meter. [V 6] Human Revolution's conversations likewise close one route on a wrong choice and force another. [V 14] Pentiment cannot be won or lost, only failed at individual checks. [V 8] Apply: no negotiation hinges on one stat or one fact; a failed attempt closes a route and opens another. [I]

9. **Time pressure without a right answer.** Pentiment limits conversation time on purpose, so players often accuse whoever they like least; its designers never name the culprit, because a right answer would spoil a narrative game where choices matter. [V 8] The player accuses on either belief or desert. [V 7] Apply: give a DISPUTE a deadline and no omniscient ending screen; the journal never says who was right. [I]

10. **People are not archetypes.** Pentiment's suspects were deliberately not made paragons of a class or creed; each has personal reasons. [V 8] Apply: no NPC stands for a group in a way a reader could point at. [I]

11. **A lopsided war plot disappoints.** GameCritics grants Skyrim's two sides roughly equal appeal, each with racism and complicity, but finds the quest lines mirror images, the forts largely unoccupied until late, and the conquered forts reverting to their old occupants. [V 18] The "only the Thalmor win" and "reversible choice" arguments are [K]. Apply: a faction choice changes the map afterwards and the two sides play differently. [I]

12. **Houses and a vote template.** Morrowind's Nerevarine must be declared Hortator by every Great House and Nerevarine by the Ashlander tribes, while the Temple normally persecutes such claimants. [V 17] House attitudes to slavery are [K], fan-sourced. Apply: a SUCCESSION holds a vote ledger, each backer won by one thing only. [I]

13. **A bound person in a quest.** In "Family Matters" Anna is turned into the Crones' slave by branded hands, hidden under another identity; the protagonist deduces it. She had used the talisman as protection and was taken when it was lost. [V 3] The endings differ in cost: one frees her at the cost of her life, one leaves her mad. [V 3] Apply: show the bound person living a life inside the bond before the player knows. [I]

14. **Slavery done seriously: powerlessness over triumph.** Freedom Cry's team refused an artificial triumph, stating no single person could end slavery, and took control from the player at the end to convey powerlessness. Strong beats positive. [V 9] Detection by the AI made plantation owners attack the slaves as well, so noise has a victim. [V 9] Apply: let consequences of the player's rashness land on the bonded. [I]

15. **Do not turn the freed into currency.** In Freedom Cry each freed slave is a resource spent on upgrades. [V 10] Kotaku calls this uncomfortably close to the treatment the game opposes, and says the people being fought for belong nearer the centre of the story. [V 11] Apply: no counter, no points, no reward scaled by number freed. [I]

16. **Gamified resistance is contested.** Lauro asks whether turning enslaved people into digital commodities, and resistance into mechanics, deepens the harm or commemorates resistance; the book covers small acts such as slow work or breaking tools, flight, and revolt. [V 13] Mukherjee asks what freedom and agency mean when a player prevents an NPC from acting freely, as in Fallout 3's slavers, Bioshock Infinite and Freedom Cry. [V 12] Apply: put small refusals and a hidden, slow resistance alongside the big act. [I]

17. **Pace by tension graph.** Sasko mapped the Baron quest on a tension-over-time graph, marked story-only, combat and mixed scenes, and placed key moments at the lowest and highest tension; too many story scenes together feels slow, too much play loses purpose. [V 5] Apply: for each quest draw the graph once, with talking, travel and a risk as the three kinds. [I]

18. **Not re-checked.** Planescape: Torment conversation and Dragon Age companion dissent are good models; Oblivion's linear plots are not. [K]

## Methods an agent can follow

- **Claim ledger (DISPUTE).** For each party: basis (law, custom, deed, kinship), strength, weakness, price. Each claim holds one true and one false or partial element. [I]
- **Three verdicts.** Side A, side B, or a settlement that costs the player (time, coin, standing), reachable through clues alone. [I]
- **Foil pass.** After drafting the hardest party, give them one trait they share with the player's expected values (Baron and Geralt). [V 4; method I]
- **Reveal-order table.** List each damning fact beside each sympathetic fact and number the order the clues surface. Sympathy first, damnation second, route-independent. [V 3; method I]
- **Leverage as world objects (NEGOTIATION).** Leverage is a debt note, a witness, a harvest, a boat, a secret held by a named person; gathering it is the quest's middle. [I]
- **Information as currency.** Each cast member knows one useful fact, traded once for a favour or act; spent facts stay spent. [I]
- **Disposition without dice.** Fixed disposition changes; a person yields when a stated objection has been answered in the text. [I]
- **Objection-and-answer.** Each side voices two or three objections, each with an answer in the world (document, promise, witness, sacrifice); the journal lists the open ones. [I]
- **Route-closing failure.** For every attempt write the line that closes it and the topic it opens. [V 6, 14; method I]
- **Vote ledger (SUCCESSION).** Voters, wants, fears, what each knows of each candidate. Candidates are built so none is both legitimate and fit. [I]
- **World deadline.** Funeral, market day, flood season advance without the player and are announced by NPC talk. [V 8 for time pressure; method I]
- **Aftermath pass.** For each ending write: three places within a day's travel, one changed greeting, one new topic, one reputation delta, one thing now impossible. [V 2; method I]
- **Bondage sheet.** Name the debt, holder, terms, exit in law, exit in practice, and what the bonded person wants. The bonded person can refuse help, and refusal has reasons. [I]
- **Cost-to-the-bound test.** For each player act, write what happens to the bonded person afterwards, as in Freedom Cry's retaliation. [V 9; method I]

## Anti-patterns

- One side is simply right and the dispute tests whether the player finds out. [I]
- A negotiation solved by the highest stat or a single fact. [I; V 6 for the opposing virtue]
- A hidden meter deciding a conversation. [K]
- Endings that differ in reward only, in a world that does not react. [V 18]
- Mirror-image campaigns for opposing sides. [V 18]
- A culprit screen that names the right answer. [V 8, as the thing Pentiment avoids]
- Succession where the heir is kind and capable and the rival a cruel schemer. [I]
- Bonded people as scenery, a score, a mass to be rescued, or spectacle cruelty. [V 11 for score and resource]
- A rescue where the freed person has no preferences, debt or aftermath. [I]
- A free-hero player deciding the oppressed's fate unasked. [I]

## Player-perspective notes

- Players remember being wrong, and being told so, more than a clean win. [K]
- Hard choices are tolerated when reasons are legible in text, resented when a hidden check decided. [I]
- Bondage: players may want to help at once. Let them, and show that help has terms. [I]

## Without quest markers in a text-led game

- **The journal is the map.** Each stage names a person, a place by landmark and a question; entries restate open objections and the leverage list. [I]
- **Topics carry the thread.** Each clue is a keyword another NPC can answer; unanswered keywords stay highlighted. [I]
- **No hidden meters.** State shows in greetings and topics. [I]
- **Deadlines announced in talk, enforced by the world.** [I]
- **Bondage needs a findable paper:** a described ledger, deed or witness. [I]
- **Keep the pacing graph in the brief.** Even without markers, remind the player of the purpose when play outnumbers story. [V 5; method I]

## Checklists

### DISPUTE
- [ ] Two parties, each with a true and a flawed element.
- [ ] Each party has a dissenter the player can talk to.
- [ ] Neither verdict is free; the third settlement is findable.
- [ ] Hidden truth explains both claims, reachable by two or more routes.
- [ ] Sympathetic fact surfaces before the damning one.
- [ ] Reputation moves in opposite directions.
- [ ] Aftermath in three places; one NPC reproaches or thanks later.
- [ ] No culprit screen; no single fact or skill gates the verdict.

### NEGOTIATION
- [ ] Wants, gives and never-gives per side.
- [ ] Leverage is named things or facts held by named people.
- [ ] Each objection has an in-world answer shown in text.
- [ ] Thresholds are fixed and explained by dialogue.
- [ ] A failed attempt closes one route and opens another.
- [ ] Terms are written, in the journal, and have a later consequence.
- [ ] Spent information stays spent.

### SUCCESSION
- [ ] At least three kinds of legitimacy in conflict.
- [ ] Each candidate has one real strength and one real fault.
- [ ] Vote ledger with wants and fears.
- [ ] The inheritance carries a cost.
- [ ] A world deadline that advances alone.
- [ ] The dead or the spirit speaks through documents, memory or custom.
- [ ] Ending changes who holds power and what is possible.

### BONDAGE
- [ ] Debt, holder, terms, legal exit and practical exit named.
- [ ] The bonded person has goals, knowledge and the right to refuse.
- [ ] No counters, scores or resource value for the bonded.
- [ ] Holders have reasons, some sympathetic, some not; the system is the wrong, not one man.
- [ ] Freedom has cost and aftermath for the freed person.
- [ ] Player rashness lands on the bonded, not only on the player.
- [ ] Violence is neither the default nor a spectacle.
- [ ] Bonded people and their families act on their own.
- [ ] A person in this position would recognise themself.
- [ ] Not mapped one-to-one onto a real-world group.
- [ ] If the player cannot fully succeed, that limit is deliberate and explained. [V 9]
