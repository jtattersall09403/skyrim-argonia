# Items: Black Marsh's material ladder

Every tier of Skyrim's gear has a Black Marsh equivalent for weapons, heavy armour and light armour. A few Skyrim
materials are shared where they still feel like Black Marsh. Imperial gear is rare and appears only where a place gives
a reason for it. What a place holds varies by province, region, locality, place, household and person. Nothing is
random and nothing is levelled (decision 0035). Frequency is set by three things only: the tier ceiling, the
conditions that let a material appear, and the fixed counts of authored stock rows. This page explains the model. The
tables hold the facts, and each fact has one home (below).

## The ladder

The ranks are unchanged: T1 Common to T6 Exceptional. An item's tier is its **power**: a weapon's damage, or an armour
piece's rating, compared with Skyrim's own base gear of the same weapon type, or the same armour class and slot. The tier
is the lowest one whose strongest base gear reaches the item's power, so it reflects what a player feels in a fight, not
the item's keyword or price. A tier with no base gear for a kind sits midway between its neighbours. Explicit lists
(soul gems, gems, jewellery) come first. Items with no power (clothing, wares) take the tier their material keyword
gives, or else their value. An enchantment can raise the tier. An `item_material` row can set a tier only where the
record's own numbers are a mod's mistake, and it must give its reason. Two rows do this: the Naga Priest Armor, a body
piece wrongly filed as a helmet, and BM&V's Argonian Sword, whose damage of 17 does not match its price.

| Tier | Weapons | Heavy armour | Light armour |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 (iron, hide, leather) | flint tecpatl and fishing knives, wooden clubs and bows, bone daggers, Naga macana, bonemold and Kothringi blades | Shellback turtle shell; bog-iron (Masu iron) | hide and fibre, marsh leather, Naga scale, wooden masks |
| T2 (steel, imperial) | bone and wooden spears, harpoons | Shellback, Kothringi shields | marsh leather, scale |
| T3 (dwarven, elven, scaled, chitin) | Saxhleel sabres, maces and axes (copper-edged steel), the obsidian ceremonial tusik, Shadowscale daggers | Reel-ka and Masu plate (bog iron over masu skin), chitin | Masu leather, BM&V scale boots and gloves, chitin |
| T4 (glass, gilded elven) | Xanmeer obsidian blades, the Saxhleel bone sword | Ancient Argonian (bronze), Zuuk (Kothringi relic) | BM&V's Argonian scale guard armour (35), Saxhleel leggings |
| T5 (ebony, glass armour) | great obsidian weapons, the Shadowscale blade | Xanmeer relic plate | Saxhleel leather (38), Cuauhtli obsidian |
| T6 (daedric, dragon) | named relics only | Xanmeer heavy (49), as a relic | Shadowscale Reborn |

Several items move away from their keyword tier because power decides. BM&V's guard set is Argonian scale at T4: its
cuirass rating of 35 matches gilded elven. So `guard_issue` gives it only where the ceiling reaches T4, and other places
issue plain gear at T2. Saxhleel leather has a rating of 38 and is therefore T5, a rare piece. The Saxhleel bone sword
does glass-level damage and is therefore T4, as rare bone, not glass. Skyrim's scimitar does 11 damage and is therefore T3.

## Bands, groups and reach

- **Bands**: common (wood, bone, flint, hide, leather), uncommon (bog iron, scale, shell, chitin), rare (obsidian,
  bronze), situational (Imperial, bonemold, Shadowscale), ruin (Xanmeer and Kothringi relics, Ayleid elven, glass,
  ebony), unique (Daedric, dragon: by story in the ledger), never (Dwarven, Orcish and decision 0006's Nordic list).
- **Groups** (the target mix): organic, plate (the marsh's fine make), metal (bog iron), foreign.
- **Reach**: a material may appear at a place when any one of its `material_reach` rows holds. The kinds are:
  anywhere; the place's kind or family (a fort, a ruin); its culture (stone-grey, Gideon's pale-green); a pattern in
  the place's record (name, summary, holder, aliases, notes, but not the source note), read one sentence at a time and
  ignored when a negation comes before it; or a ledger row. `never_material` is judged on our material, so a Black Marsh
  piece whose record carries a Nordic or Forsworn keyword is filed under what it is really made of and is not blocked.

## Regions, localities and places

Each region has a profile (`regions.json`), built from its notes:

- the peoples present, each with a share (major, minor or few);
- its materials, each marked local, traded, scarce or absent;
- its signature goods;
- what a player should notice there;
- what is notably missing.

Some examples: Murkwood has no Imperial or Dunmer gear at all. The Valley of Storms has bonemold and chitin as local
goods. Skeith Isle holds a pirates' mixed hoard. Aunike Ridge works tin and nothing else.

A place's **expected mix** starts from its class target in `scale.json` `mix`:

| Class | Organic | Metal | Plate | Foreign |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Village (default) | 70 (organic and plate together) | 25 | (counted with organic) | 5 |
| City | 45 | 35 | 10 | 10 |
| Tribal camp | 100 (organic and plate together) | 0 | (counted with organic) | 0 |

The `bends` then adjust it. Each bend names its rule:

- the region's peoples: Dunmer, Imperial, outlaws, Khajiit, Naga;
- the place's culture: Warden Marches, Gideon, Soulrest, Shadowfen border;
- its trade: a bog-iron smith works there;
- its region's metal: local or scarce.

The shares are then brought back to 100. A group within 15 points of its expected share counts as expected. A
group whose expected share is 0 (metal or foreign gear in a tribal camp) fails as `place class`. Carried-in goods are
thinned by road distance through the existing provenance rule (`scale.json` `provenance`), and goods are judged
against `good_material` and `good_kind`.

## Households and people

A person takes the first trade kit (`kits.json`) whose pattern fits their trade or whose roles include their role. They
also take any overlay that fits their race: a Dunmer household keeps chitin and bonemold, and an Imperial household
keeps Imperial issue. A household's store follows its head's kit. A kit lists:

- what is carried (slot, type, preferred materials);
- what is worn;
- what is kept at home and the food in the house (goods);
- what that person would never own.

For example, a fisher never owns heavy armour, a metal two-handed weapon, or anything rare. The derive draws a
person's weapon from their kit, and the reach check reports each contradiction under the rule of the kit that flags
it. Any stock row may carry an `exception` giving its reason in words, and is then never flagged.

## Checks and staging

`python -m tools.places stock reach <place> [--source P] [--md P]` lists every flagged row with its rule: `material
reach`, `region`, `place class` or `kit <id>`. It exits 1 while any row is flagged. `stock mix <place>` prints the
place's gear by material, group and band against the expected mix, with the rule behind each share. Both read the
stock source and the database, not the built plugin. The hand-over check (`python -m tools.places handover`, the last
step of `place-check`) requires `stock reach` to pass. It is not a consistency rule, so the gate does not fail for the
six Act I places, whose agents restock them against their worklists (`out/places/stock-audit/<place>.md`). The check
becomes enforced when those six pass: a rule is then added to `tools/newworld/consistency.py` that runs
`reach.flagged` for every stocked place, together with its row in [consistency-rules](consistency-rules.md).

## Smithing

Every weapon and armour piece on the ladder gets a forge recipe and a tempering recipe: a grindstone for weapons and a
workbench for armour, as in Skyrim. The rules are authored in `recipe_rules.json`, which sets each material's
ingredients, the binding, the stations, the materials that can only be tempered, and the new items. The tool
`python3 -m tools.newworld.stockdb recipes` turns those rules into `recipes.json`, and from there into the tables
`recipe` and `recipe_ingredient`. The tool works as follows:

- **Shape.** It measures the shape of Skyrim's own forge recipes for each weapon type and armour slot: how many of the
  main material and how many leather strips. For example, a sword takes 2 and 1, and a heavy cuirass takes 5 and 3.
- **Perk.** It measures which perk each Skyrim material keyword asks for. Each of our materials then takes the perk
  that matches its power class. Bone, for example, takes Steel Smithing, obsidian takes Glass Smithing, and chitin
  takes Elven Smithing.
- **Mod recipes.** It keeps a mod's own recipe when every ingredient in it is one of ours. Otherwise it replaces the
  recipe: no horker tusk or steel ingots for a bone weapon, and no ebony for a Shadowscale blade.
- **Relics.** Relic and ruin materials, and every T6 piece, can be tempered but never forged. Each such piece has its
  forge row written with the reason.
- **Ingredients.** Every ingredient is a ware with an `item_good` row, and its good is made by some region or place, so
  the ladder can be crafted in play.
- **Flint.** No owned mod has a flint item, so flint is a MISC record of ours (`ANW_Flint`), reusing the model of
  HTBM's Obsidian.

The test (`tools/tests/test_stock_ladder.py`) proves that every piece has both recipes, or a written reason, and that
every ingredient can be obtained. The recipes and `ANW_Flint` are not yet built into the plugin. That would be a stage of
the world build (`ArgoniaNewWorld.esm`, beside its ForeignCopy records) that writes a COBJ for each `recipe` row and
disables each overridden mod recipe.

## Mods

The ladder uses only content we own: HTBM Cipactli, King of the Murkmire, BM&V and Dragonborn. Five more mods were
added for breadth and fetched by `infra/gpu-box/stack.py fetch --record`: New Armory Series Argonian Weapons,
Beyond Skyrim Bonemold Weapon Pack, Stormhold Warrior, Ancient Argonian Armor and Shadowscale Reborn. Their plugins
are read from `out/places/catalogue/plugins/<mod>/`, which the catalogue unpacks. No model exists anywhere for jade,
turtle-shell shields, Hist amber or crocodile-hide gear, so these stay as lore and trade goods.
