# Items: Black Marsh's material ladder

Every tier of Skyrim's gear has a Black Marsh equivalent for weapons, heavy armour and light armour. A few Skyrim
materials are shared where they still feel like Black Marsh. Imperial gear is rare and appears only where a place gives
a reason for it. What a place holds varies by province, region, locality, place, household and person. Nothing is
random and nothing is levelled (decision 0035). Frequency is set by three things only: the tier ceiling, the
conditions that let a material appear, and the fixed counts of authored stock rows. This page explains the model. The
tables hold the facts, and each fact has one home (below).

**Gear is decided for our place.** What a place's people wear and carry follows from what the place is in our world: its
region's profile, its culture, its kind and settlement tier, its wealth band and ceiling, and each holder's trade kit and
faction. What the place's source held or its source's mods supplied is never a reason, for or against: most sources have
no Argonian gear of their own, and every item of the ladder is ours (copied into our plugin by the build), so any ladder
item is available to any place the rules allow, whatever plugin it came from.

## The ladder

Each of our materials has one tier, as in Skyrim, where every iron sword does iron damage (decision 0034). The tier
is `ladder_tier` in `materials.json`. Every item of the material takes Skyrim's stats for that tier and for its
weapon type or armour slot: damage or armour rating, and value. Weight, speed and reach stay the item's own. The
stats come from one Skyrim base per tier and kind (`scale.json` `power.stat_bases`, measured into `power.json`
`stats`):

| Tier | Weapons | Light armour | Heavy armour and shields |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | iron | hide | iron |
| T2 | steel | leather | steel |
| T3 | elven | scaled | dwarven |
| T4 | glass | gilded elven | orcish |
| T5 | ebony | glass | ebony |
| T6 | daedric | dragonscale | daedric |

The exceptions to these bases:

- Bows take the hunting bow at T1.
- Light helmets, gauntlets and boots take elven at T4.
- An item whose own numbers already match its tier is left untouched. MN01's Naga Cuauololli, Macana, Bamboo Bow,
  Yaomitl and Tecpatl are iron-level, so they keep their records.

The world build writes the new stats as overrides on our copies of the mods' items (section Smithing). The tiers by
material:

- **T1:** wood, bone, flint, hide and fibre.
- **T2:** marsh leather (Naga-Kur among it), shell, bog iron, Saxhleel bone-and-leather work, Kothringi relics,
  Imperial issue (East Empire Company among it), Dunmer netch leather and Ashlander make, and corsair's steel.
- **T3:** chitin, bone plate (Xit-Xaht; Bone Cultist relabelled), Argonian scale (BM&V's guard set, issued to the watch of an Argonian town or city of ceiling T3), copper-edged steel and bonemold.
- **T4:** obsidian, bronze (Ancient Argonian) and Shadowscale.
- **T5:** Masu plate (Reel-ka, Masu plate and Masu ebony) and Hist amber (the Creation Club amber set relabelled: a
  shrine's or a rich city's treasury).
- **T6:** Xanmeer relics.

A finer make of a material stays in its material's tier: a Saxhleel bone mace and a Saxhleel dagger are both T2.
An item is written at another tier only by an `item_material` row's `tier`, which must give its reason. Great
obsidian (a two-handed Xanmeer weapon) and Xanmeer light armour are written at T5 this way. Items off the ladder
(vanilla gear) keep their tier from their Skyrim material keyword. An item with neither a ladder row nor a keyword
takes the tier its measured power reaches (`split.power_tier`). After a build, power is also measured to check that
the overrides took.

## Weapon types and how they swing

Six weapon types have no Skyrim base: pike, halberd, quarterstaff, rapier, whip (a chain flail) and claw. Each
takes its stats per tier from Animated Armoury's iron, steel, elven, glass, ebony and daedric piece of that type
(`scale.json` `power.bases`, measured by `stockdb power`). The records keep Animated Armoury's own type keywords,
and `split.power_kind` reads those before the vanilla type each one also carries.

Under the collection's combat set-up, attacks are keyed on the engine's animation type:

| Type | Swings as | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pike | greatsword | |
| Spear, trident, harpoon | greatsword | Heavy Armory's, HTBM's and New Armory's pieces are typed greatsword or sword |
| Shortspear | sword | |
| Halberd and glaive | battleaxe | |
| Quarterstaff | battleaxe | Animated Armoury and Heavy Armory |
| Whip | mace | Animated Armoury's own animations where its pack covers them |
| Rapier | sword | the collection's SkySA rapier pack (keyed on Animated Armoury's keyword) |
| Claw | dagger | the SkySA claw pack |
| Crossbow | crossbow | Dawnguard's own crossbow animations (part of Special Edition); told apart from a bow by its animation type (9), since its keyword is the bow's |
| Blowgun | crossbow | the blowgun takes the crossbow's action, so it is the crossbow's lowest rung (T2) |

The crossbow is a seventh new type: Dawnguard's crossbow (T2, bog iron and wood), its enhanced make (T3) and steel
bolts, with stats from Dawnguard's crossbows, plain to enhanced Dwarven. It is situational: Imperial posts, Dunmer
holdouts, pirates and Company marines, and a rich trader's guards. The Dwarven crossbows stay never, and so does
the Dawnguard order's armour: only the crossbow and its bolts are filed.

The kit rules judge each new type as the vanilla type it swings as. For example, a fisher's metal two-handed weapon
is barred, except a harpoon or a trident (the kit's `except`).

## The six Act I places

The wider ladder is for places from here on (owner, 2026-11-22). The six Act I places are not restocked for it. A
test pins their `stock reach` flags and `stock mix` as they stood (`tools/tests/data/stock_six_places_pin.json`).
New trade-kit options are added only as alternatives, after each kit's first carry row, which is the one the derive
draws.

Records of asset-only mods are read for their own new records only. Heavy Armory, for instance, re-keywords
Skyrim's bows, but its plugin is never active, so its overrides of Skyrim's or another mod's records never reach the
game and are never read.

## Bands, groups and reach

- **Bands**: common (wood, bone, flint, hide, leather), uncommon (bog iron, scale, shell, chitin), rare (obsidian,
  bronze), situational (Imperial, bonemold, Shadowscale), ruin (Xanmeer and Kothringi relics, Ayleid elven, glass,
  ebony), unique (Daedric, dragon: by story in the ledger), never (Dwarven, Orcish and decision 0006's Nordic list).
- **Groups** (the target mix): organic, plate (the marsh's fine make), metal (bog iron), foreign.
- **Reach**: a material may appear at a place when any one of its `material_reach` rows holds. The kinds are:
  anywhere; the place's kind or family (a fort, a ruin); its culture (stone-grey, Gideon's pale-green); a pattern in
  the place's record (name, summary, holder, aliases, notes, but not the source note), read one sentence at a time and
  ignored when a negation comes before it; or a ledger row. `never_material` is judged on our material, so a Black Marsh
  piece whose record carries a Nordic or Forsworn keyword is filed under what it is really made of and is not blocked.

## Regions, localities and places

Each region has a profile (`regions.json`), built from its notes:

- the peoples present, each with a share (major, minor or few);
- its materials, each marked local, traded, scarce or absent;
- its signature goods;
- what a player should notice there;
- what is notably missing.

Some examples: Murkwood has no Imperial or Dunmer gear at all. The Valley of Storms has bonemold and chitin as local
goods. Skeith Isle holds a pirates' mixed hoard. Aunike Ridge works tin and nothing else.

A place's **expected mix** starts from its class target in `scale.json` `mix`:

| Class | Organic | Metal | Plate | Foreign |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Village (default) | 70 (organic and plate together) | 25 | (counted with organic) | 5 |
| City | 45 | 35 | 10 | 10 |
| Tribal camp | 100 (organic and plate together) | 0 | (counted with organic) | 0 |

The `bends` then adjust it. Each bend names its rule:

- the region's peoples: Dunmer, Imperial, outlaws, Khajiit, Naga;
- the place's culture: Warden Marches, Gideon, Soulrest, Shadowfen border;
- its trade: a bog-iron smith works there;
- its region's metal: local or scarce.

The shares are then brought back to 100. A group within 15 points of its expected share counts as expected. A
group whose expected share is 0 (metal or foreign gear in a tribal camp) fails as `place class`. Carried-in goods are
thinned by road distance through the existing provenance rule (`scale.json` `provenance`), and goods are judged
against `good_material` and `good_kind`.

## Households and people

A person takes the first trade kit (`kits.json`) whose pattern fits their trade or whose roles include their role. They
also take any overlay that fits their race: a Dunmer household keeps chitin and bonemold, and an Imperial household
keeps Imperial issue. A household's store follows its head's kit. A kit lists:

- what is carried (slot, type, preferred materials);
- what is worn;
- what is kept at home and the food in the house (goods);
- what that person would never own.

For example, a fisher never owns heavy armour, a metal two-handed weapon, or anything rare. The derive draws a
person's weapon from their kit, and the reach check reports each contradiction under the rule of the kit that flags
it. Any stock row may carry an `exception` giving its reason in words, and is then never flagged.

## Guard issue

A place's guards are issued by a table of ours (`guard_issue.table`), read top to bottom; the first row whose
conditions all hold is the place's issue. The conditions are the place's kind, its culture, its region, words of its
holder, and a ceiling that reaches the row's `min_ceiling`. A row names either a set filed whole (`items`) or materials
in order of preference, filled slot by slot from the ladder at or below the row's tier and the place's ceiling, then
its fallback materials; the sidearm is the row's weapon or weapon materials, else the plain issue's sword. Every guard
also carries the torch.

| Row | Where | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Imperial post | a place whose holder is the Legion or an Imperial garrison (none in 4E 201) | Imperial issue, T2 |
| Dunmer holdout | a place whose holder is Dunmer, ceiling T2 and up | Dunmer bonemold, leather where the ceiling stops short |
| Argonian scale | a city, town, fort, keep or prison of an Argonian culture, ceiling T3 and up | BM&V's Argonian scale guard armour, T3 |
| Saxhleel issue | any other settled place of an Argonian culture (village, hamlet, outpost, farm, ferry, mine and the like), ceiling T2 and up | Saxhleel bone-and-leather, T2 |
| Hide and flint | a tribal or outlaw camp, or a cave | hide, with flint, bone or wood arms |
| Plain issue | everything else | light armour at the faction's issue tier |

`stock reach` flags a guard's armour that is not of the place's row (rule `guard issue`); a place that keeps another
issue by choice gives the row an `exception` that says so.

## Checks and staging

`python -m tools.places stock reach <place> [--source P] [--md P]` lists every flagged row with its rule: `material
reach`, `region`, `place class` or `kit <id>`. It exits 1 while any row is flagged. `stock mix <place>` prints the
place's gear by material, group and band against the expected mix, with the rule behind each share. Both read the
stock source and the database, not the built plugin. The hand-over check (`python -m tools.places handover`, the last
step of `place-check`) requires `stock reach` to pass. It is not a consistency rule, so the gate does not fail for the
six Act I places, whose agents restock them against their worklists (`out/places/stock-audit/<place>.md`). The check
becomes enforced when those six pass: a rule is then added to `tools/newworld/consistency.py` that runs
`reach.flagged` for every stocked place, together with its row in [consistency-rules](consistency-rules.md).

## Smithing

Every weapon and armour piece on the ladder gets a forge recipe and a tempering recipe: a grindstone for weapons and a
workbench for armour, as in Skyrim. The rules are authored in `recipe_rules.json`, which sets each material's
ingredients, the binding, the stations, the materials that can only be tempered, and the new items. The tool
`python3 -m tools.newworld.stockdb recipes` turns those rules into `recipes.json`, and from there into the tables
`recipe` and `recipe_ingredient`. The tool works as follows:

- **Shape.** It measures the shape of Skyrim's own forge recipes for each weapon type and armour slot: how many of the
  main material and how many leather strips. For example, a sword takes 2 and 1, and a heavy cuirass takes 5 and 3.
- **Perk.** It measures which perk each Skyrim material keyword asks for. Each of our materials then takes the perk
  that matches its power class. Bone, for example, takes Steel Smithing, obsidian takes Glass Smithing, and chitin
  takes Elven Smithing.
- **Mod recipes.** It keeps a mod's own recipe when every ingredient in it is one of ours. Otherwise it replaces the
  recipe: no horker tusk or steel ingots for a bone weapon, and no ebony for a Shadowscale blade.
- **Relics.** Relic and ruin materials, and every T6 piece, can be tempered but never forged. Each such piece has its
  forge row written with the reason.
- **Ingredients.** Every ingredient is a ware with an `item_good` row, and its good is made by some region or place, so
  the ladder can be crafted in play.
- **Flint.** No owned mod has a flint item, so flint is a MISC record of ours (`ANW_Flint`), reusing the model of
  HTBM's Obsidian.

The test (`tools/tests/test_stock_ladder.py`) proves that every piece has both recipes, or a written reason, and that
every ingredient can be obtained.

**Into the game.** The ladder's records are built from `smithing.records()` (`tools/newworld/world/smithing.py`) by
`ItemLadder.cs`, a writer shared by two builds:

- every ladder item copied as ours (ForeignCopy);
- the stat overrides set on those copies;
- `ANW_Flint` added;
- one COBJ per forge or tempering recipe, with its bench keyword and perk taken from Skyrim.esm.

**The places plugin carries them for now.** Every places build (`tools.places build`, daily from main) writes
`items.json` beside `ArgoniaNWPlaces.esp`. Its writer then does one of two things:

- When the world file does not carry the ladder, which is the case today, the writer puts the ladder into the
  places plugin and says so: "items ladder: the world file does not carry it yet; written into this plugin".
- Once `ArgoniaNewWorld.esm` carries the ladder, the writer links to it instead. That happens when its `smithing`
  stage and `esm` writer have run and `smithing/copies.json` beside it names records the file holds. The writer
  then says "items ladder: linked to the world file's copies".

So the ladder reaches the game with the places deploy alone: no ground stage needs to run. Its home moves to the
world file by itself at the next main-tree world rebuild (when the owner asks for one). The province's ground is
a fixed base that rebuild never touches: `rebuild.sh run --to esm` runs only the stale stages before `esm`.

The mods are asset-only, so their own plugins and recipes never load. Every recipe, kept or overridden, is written
as ours, and none of theirs needs switching off. The stock check reads either copy back to its source:
`<esp>.copies.json`, or the world's `smithing/copies.json` inverted.

A stock row whose tier the ladder raised is listed as owed to the place's restock and does not fail the check, so
long as its tier under the keyword-and-value rule it was stocked by fits. A row that was always wrong still fails.

`smithing check` reads a world file that carries the ladder and proves three things:

- every item is copied;
- every override's stats are the ladder's, and every item has its tier's power;
- every recipe is one COBJ at the right bench, with the right perk and ingredients.

The small test world proves it, and a places build in a scratch `--out` proves the places path.
