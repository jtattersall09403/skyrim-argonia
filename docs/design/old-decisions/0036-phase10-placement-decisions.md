# 0036 — Vegetation placement: graded density, near-native trees with landmark giants, marsh by saturation

**Status** accepted · **Extended by** [0048](0048-vegetation-density-ladder.md)
(the between-region ladder and its gates)

Evidence: [mined placement rules](../research/placement-settlements/shipped-world-placement-rules.md),
[vegetation density design](../research/vegetation/vegetation-density-design.md),
`world/sources/placement/`, `world/sources/flora/`. The run-book, the
question packet and the round logs are archived in
[phase10-rounds](../research/archive/phase10-rounds/0036-runbook-and-questions.md).

## Density: match the reference, graded by geography and varied within it

Dense jungle is much denser than open grass, mountain forest denser than
bare ground, and no area is samey inside.

- **Graded from our own climate fields**: a region's density is
  `850 × humidity² × (0.25 + 0.75 × canopy)` from `regions.CLIMATE`, so the
  ladder falls out of climate (rootland deep marsh about 818 per ha, jungle
  767, interior swamp 627, fringe marsh 327, firm lowland 214, upland hills
  134, bare mountain 61).
- **Varied within a region**: the reference's density varies 2.3–3.1× its
  mean between neighbouring cells, decorrelating by about 60 m. A shared
  openness field (90 m glades) and per-species patchiness (190 m stands)
  reproduce it, calibrated on the same statistics both sides (open-space
  radius p75 21.3 m against 21.1 mined).
- Palettes are generated by `worldgen/build_palettes.py` (archetypes encode
  the evidence once); look feedback is routed to the archetype or field that
  produces the look, never to per-spot tweaks.

## Trees: mostly near-native, a few landmark giants

The ordinary canopy sits at 0.5–1.25× native (19–30 m cypress). Each forested
region carries a `role: landmark-giant` layer at ×1.8–2.6, about one per
8–16 ha, with a 14 m clearance stamp. A world without quest markers navigates
on distant distinctive silhouettes; these are those landmarks.

## The region map: marsh follows saturation

A marsh is low, flat, slow-draining ground that saturates, not only ground
with standing water: below 30 m, under 6°, and within 6 m of the water table
or above the wetness index's pooling threshold. Uplands need drainage;
mountains start at 150 m (`regions.py`). Wetland classes take about 36 % of
land; the hill and mountain third stays, because the terrain is genuinely
high there.

## Groundcover: bare ground stays bare

Bethesda binds grass to painted ground, at most three species per ground
type, and many ground textures carry none (mined rule R10).
`world/sources/flora/groundcover.json` follows it: mud, silt, rock, roads and
seabed are bare. A reed bed reads as a reed bed only if the mud beside it is
mud.

## Traps the pipeline guards against

1. Kit asset ids travel in glTF `extras`, never node names, which three.js
   sanitises.
2. Instances are bucketed by species and LOD across chunks, never one
   instanced mesh per chunk.
3. A raster's texel scale is derived from the image's own size against the
   province extent, never from another raster's `meta.json`.
4. A killed raster job leaves half-written outputs; restore from HEAD and
   re-run.
