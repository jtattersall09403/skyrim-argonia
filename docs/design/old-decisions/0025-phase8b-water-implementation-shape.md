## Decisions

1. **One continuous water surface, not per-body meshes.** The compiler bakes a
   province-wide surface-height field `W(x,z)`: the real surface over water
   (sea 0, lakes at their level, rivers monotone downstream) and buried under
   the ground where dry, so one camera-centred clipmap grid displaced by `W`
   and waves draws sea, lakes and rivers in one pass with no seams.
2. **Per-pixel water character from a class table.** Flow (direction, speed,
   shore distance) and class (coast, estuary, river, lake, marsh; turbidity,
   salinity, season response) rasters drive per-class profiles (module 60
   §40) from one material.
3. **Material = `MeshStandardMaterial` + `onBeforeCompile`**
   ([0020](0020-phase8a-light-implementation-shape.md) precedent),
   CSM-patched, aerial perspective chained after, cache key `es-water`. That
   gives exposure-correct sun and moon glints with shadows, PMREM sky
   reflections including moons and stars, ACES with eye adaptation and haze.
   Injected: vertex `W` and Gerstner displacement, wave and flow-advected
   normals, foam albedo, Beer–Lambert transmission, tiered SSR.
4. **Reflections are PMREM plus tiered SSR; planar reflections nowhere.**
5. **One scene render per frame.** Opaques and sky render once into a
   half-float target with depth; a tone-mapped blit puts it on screen; water
   renders on top sampling that target for refraction, thickness, SSR and
   depth occlusion. Underwater, the water underside (with Snell's window)
   renders into the scene target and a post pass applies per-channel
   extinction fog.
6. **CPU and GPU in lockstep.** One TS module owns the wave table and
   generates both the shader uniforms and the CPU Gerstner sampler; the same
   module implements `WorldWaterQuery.sample` from the baked rasters with tide
   and season offsets. Vitest envelope tests guard parity.
7. **Tide and season are world state**: spring and neap tide from the two
   moons' phase (module 55 §95) on coast and estuary classes; the wet season
   raises season-responsive water. Renderer and CPU query share the
   functions.
8. **Vendored code**: Gerstner, foam and underwater GLSL and the CPU sampler
   adapted from WaterThreeJS (MIT); flow-map advection after three.js
   `Water2` and Valve; the flow-map data contract after SeedOcean (MIT),
   generated by our own compiler. Credits in the root README and
   THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.
9. **Quality tiers** in one declarative table: T0 mobile (environment-only
   reflections, no SSR, reduced-resolution scene target, fewer wave bands),
   T1 desktop (SSR, full foam); automatic by device, `?wq=` overrides.

## Hard-won rules

- No data in PNG alpha channels, ever: canvas premultiply destroys it.
- Never scale an oscillating velocity by absolute time in a shader.
- GPU resources from `useMemo` are never disposed by effects with unstable
  dependencies.
- One physics for every water level; no per-feature level heuristics.
- Texture identity is verified by contact sheet, not by slot name;
  `build_ground_materials` checks `BMV_OVERRIDES`, which overrides the base
  table for the default set.
- Siting reads the natural snapshots (ungraded heights, natural routes and
  waterways), never the graded or repaired surfaces it caused, so committed
  records do not move.
- `refine_province` is given the `hydrology-pass1.npz` beside
  `heightfield-f32.npy`, never the stale copy in `province-refined/`.
