# 2.3 — Items, containers, loot, inventory

**Goal.** Every thing the player can hold has one record in one home table
at Morrowind scale; every placed item and container in the slice places is
a real object: visible, collectible, placeable, ownable and usable in the
Morrowind way; inventory, equipment, the belt and carrying weight run in
the studio and the game on the 2.2 screens, and agents prove all of it
before the owner walks.

## Outcome

Done. `world/sources/registries/items.json` is the one item table (376
records: the whole weapon and armour set promoted from the sandbox, plus
lore-grounded exemplars of every other kind), with locks, the blanket
loot rules and documents beside it. Pickup, placing, the item session,
the belt, the reader and the burden bands of world/76 §122 run in the
studio and the game on the 2.2 screens, and every slice `container` and
`item` socket is filled. Exploration rewards (§ c) ship with their
`reach` and `effort` gate and two slice caches. Item icons come from one generator, the three.js
renderer under `tooling/ui/item-icons/`. Deployed in Pages run
38990902286 and walked by the owner. Reports: `lanes/2.3/`.

## Decisions taken with the owner

The owner answered these; a lane does not reopen them.

1. **Scale now.** All weapons and armour promote into the shared table
   now. Every other kind (consumable, book, note, key, tool, light, misc,
   quest item) gets a few real, lore-grounded exemplars; the province-wide
   load is Part 4's.
2. **Names.** Sets that exist anywhere in Tamriel (iron, steel, leather,
   hide, bone, Imperial, Dwarven, Daedric, glass, ebony, elven, orcish and
   the like) keep their names. Sets tied to a Skyrim faction or place
   (Blades, Stormcloak, Nord hero, Dawnguard, Falmer, and so on) are
   re-flavoured from a dossier or left out of the slice. Every included
   set answers "why here?".
3. **Pickup is Morrowind's.** Every item lies in the world as its own
   object with its own model; look at it, the 2.2 prompt names it, one
   input takes it. Dropped and placed items are real objects that persist
   in the save. No glowing loot points.
4. **Quick belt**: four slots, Souls-style: two item slots (potion, torch,
   food) and two hand swaps. D-pad or number keys; on touch a tap on a
   slot uses it (the host's `onBelt` is required). 2.9 adds spells to it.
5. **Carrying weight is already ruled** (world/76 §122): limit from
   Strength, four bands (fast, mid, fat, overloaded) that change roll,
   i-frames, stamina and speed; overloaded means no roll, no run, no jump,
   no swimming up, a slow walk. This chunk implements it and deletes the
   sandbox's hard-coded cap.
6. **Refills, Morrowind style.** Chests and loot never refill. Anything
   the player sets down anywhere stays there forever; no safe-storage
   class. Merchant stock and gold return every few days (2.6 builds it).
   Harvestable plants come back: chosen already-placed plant and
   ground-cover types act as one-slot containers holding a linked
   ingredient that regrows after a set number of in-game days. No new
   plant models, no new placement.
7. **Consumables** are carried, traded, placed and dropped here; using
   one does nothing until 2.9 owns effects. No hunger or "fills you up".
   Scrolls likewise.
8. **Books and notes** get a reader here, on the journal's book look (two
   leaves, page turns by swipe, stick and wheel); a note is one sheet.
   Two or three short books and a handful of notes, written from the
   dossiers, placed in the slice. Skill books (a skill rises on first
   read) are a hook 2.8 fills.
9. **Keys open locks.** A locked container or door shows "unlock" when
   the player holds its key and opens; without it the prompt reads
   "locked". Lockpicks and probes exist as items; picking, traps and the
   lockpick window are 2.10.
10. **Lights.** A torch equips in either hand, lights the surroundings,
    burns down on its condition bar and is gone. A lantern does not burn
    out. A torch attacks from either hand as a club at the floor of the
    weapon band; burn damage is a hook 2.9 fills. The swing clips and
    hit numbers are a named requirement on 2.7, which already lists
    "the torch's look".
11. **Loot authoring**: blanket rules for the many, hand-authored lists for
    the few. The blanket rule list is written here, wide and varied
    enough that no two households feel the same, and each rule states
    where it belongs (a fisher's basket, a hunter's cache, a shrine
    bowl, a smuggler's crate). Notable sockets per place (the chief's
    chest, a hidden cache) carry an authored list with a `storyNote`.
    Every socket passes the test "does finding this here make sense and
    feel earned?". The one name is `lootTable`; the retired reward-profile
    name goes in the same change. `valueTier` is filled on every socket.
    Rules run at authoring time and the result is fixed on the record
    (rule 9); nothing rolls at play time.
12. **Condition** wears with use (a weapon on hit, armour when hit); a
    worn armour guards less; a weapon does full damage until it is
    broken, and a broken one cannot attack (world/76 §121.6). Repair hammers are
    items; repairing is 2.6's.
13. **Quest and unique items** are ordinary items the player may drop or
    sell; stranding a quest is their prerogative, and the game says so
    plainly (a hold-to-confirm naming the quest). They live in a key
    items pocket and weigh nothing. Unique weapons and armour stay in the
    main bag with a "one only" mark.
14. **Placing**, Tears of the Kingdom's Ultrahand model: choose "place"
    (quick "drop" remains) and the item appears as a ghost within a
    maximum reach equal to the prompt's interaction reach; the ghost sits
    on whatever is under it; confirm, then it settles by physics for a
    moment and freezes; the resting pose is what the save records.
    Controller, exactly as that game: right stick moves the ghost (the
    camera holds behind the player; the left stick walks), d-pad up and
    down push it farther and nearer, holding R turns the d-pad into
    rotation (left and right spin it about the screen's vertical axis,
    up and down tip it about the screen's horizontal axis, in steps; a
    hold turns smoothly), ZL lays it flat, A places, B cancels. Mouse and
    keyboard: mouse moves the ghost, wheel sets distance, holding the
    right button turns the arrow keys into rotation, one key lays flat,
    click places, Escape cancels. Touch: a temporary right stick moves
    the ghost, a held "rotate" button makes the stick spin and tip
    instead, nearer and farther buttons, lay flat, tick and cross; the
    camera is a drag on any free part of the screen. Two axes reach
    every orientation (spin, tip, spin again), so no third control.
    Placing onto a moving boat is named in 2.15, not solved here.
15. **Exploration pays** (owner directive; the one home is
    [world/20 §12.3b](../../world/20-province-design.md)): a hard-to-reach
    spot pays roughly in proportion to its effort, risk and remoteness,
    and every reward has a causal story. § c holds its rows.

## a. Records

- The `Item` record: one home table keyed by stable id; kinds weapon,
  armour, clothing, consumable (potion, food, ingredient, scroll), book,
  note, key, tool (lockpick, probe, repair), light, misc, quest item;
  `condition`; value; weight; `pocket` (bag or key items); `unique`;
  the model and icon reference; the verb id the prompt shows; the
  semantic ladder reference compiled to numbers (world/76 §128);
  `schemaVersion`. The sandbox's weapon, armour, arrow and light code
  records become derived views of this table, regenerated, never a
  second home (standard 18; world/98 updated in the same change).
- Lock and key: a lock record (level, key id) on containers and doors;
  a key item references the lock.
- Containers: kind, lock, owner, `lootTable` or blanket rule id,
  regrow days (plants only), `valueTier`. The blanket rule list as its
  own table. The harvestable plant class joins the socket vocabulary.
- Burden per world/76 §122 in `game-core/src/inventory`: capacity from
  Strength, the four bands, the multipliers to roll, i-frames, stamina
  and speed read by combat and movement; the sandbox override deleted.
- The belt, the key items pocket, condition wear and torch burn as
  inventory and equipment state in the save contract.
- The semantic compiler extended to loot.
- Plugin-sourced gear takes its material set from the plugin (keyword,
  else temper recipe), recorded as `materialFrom` and checked by
  `worldgen.items_material`; the five Saxhleel blades are bronze (King of
  the Murkmire tempers them with corundum).
- No item model is a stand-in; the clutter config has no `placeholder`
  class. Models come from plugin records (the seed doll, the leviathan
  bone, the mnemic egg and the vakka stone from King of the Murkmire; the
  turtle-shell bowl and the grave stake from Here There Be Monsters); an
  item with no model names its redesign in `assetAvailability.notes`. The
  six shape-wrong items are re-sourced from Nexus mods 36509 and 63100
  (permission held).

## b. World

- Items as world objects: every `item` socket spawns its record's model
  as an interactable the 2.2 prompt can pick; containers open the 2.2
  container window; books and notes open the reader; keys unlock;
  plants harvest and regrow; torches light and burn.
- Ownership on pickup: taking an owned item is theft only if witnessed
  (2.10 adds the witness); unowned is the wilderness norm.
- Inventory, equipment, belt, key items pocket, burden bands on the 2.2
  screens in the studio and the game; the equipped-hands corner and the
  "too heavy" notice fed from the records.
- Placing and dropping as decided above, as one component with three
  input mappings and a ui-lab screen.
- The slice fill: every `container` and `item` socket in every slice
  place filled per its rule or list, `valueTier` set, the notable
  sockets hand-authored with a note; every book, note, item name and
  description in the catalogue, reviewed by `text-review`.

## c. Exploration rewards

A find pays for the effort of reaching it (decision 15). The rule, the
gate and two slice caches are built.

- **Rule as data**: the container and item socket gains `reach` (one of
  `walk`, `climb`, `swim`, `dive`, `magic`) and `effort` (1 to 5) beside
  `valueTier`; `worldgen.loot_fill` refuses a socket whose `valueTier`
  sits below the band its `effort` names, and an `effort` of 3 or more
  needs an authored `lootTable` with a `storyNote`, never a blanket rule.
- **Container kind**: a `hidden-cache` blanket rule family in
  `loot-rules.json` for the low-effort finds, each with its `belongs`
  line (a diver's stash, a climber's ledge bundle, a wreck's sealed box).
  A hidden cache never gets a map marker.
- **Item tag**: `exploration` on items that may only be placed behind
  effort 3 or more (uniques with the "one only" mark among them).
- **Gate**: an integrity-gate check that every socket with `effort` 3 or
  more has an authored list, a `storyNote` and a `valueTier` at its band,
  and that each `reach` other than `walk` holds on geometry (the place
  tools' reachability check run in that mode). It fails on purpose on a
  fixture before it is trusted.
- **Two slice examples**, from `lanes/2.5/x1-crawl.md` § Example spots.

## Loop

Copied from 2.2: one batch per pass, never one finding at a time; the
parent agent judges, diagnoses by record, component or scene, fixes in
one batch, re-runs. No GPU, no studio boot, minutes per pass.

- **Records and rules**: unit tests beside the code for the table, the
  derived views, burden bands, keys, belt, pocket, wear, regrow timers,
  and a save-and-load round trip of every item kind.
- **Screens**: the container window, belt, key items pocket, reader and
  placement controls as ui-lab screens in the existing grid (every
  viewport and input mode); on touch the container and barter take two
  steps (the first tap selects and shows the item's details and its
  Take, Steal, Put back, Buy or Sell button; a second tap acts), the
  automatic bars, one reader pass over the
  contact sheets against the Morrowind and Souls references.
- **World behaviour**: the combat sandbox gains one items scene on the
  real shared packages (a chest, a table of items, a plant, a locked door
  with its key, a torch, a dark corner). Headless Playwright runs the
  prompt pick, take, place with each input, read, equip, swap on the
  belt, overload, harvest and the day skip; `visual:check` for the
  movement and hand changes. What works here works in the studio and
  the game through the 2.1 seam, so neither is booted for the loop.
- **The slice fill**: the integrity gate plus a geometry check from the
  place tools that every socket is reachable and its model does not
  intersect its surface.
- RunPod only for a check that genuinely needs the whole world running;
  then one boot and every such check in that session
  ([0119](../../old-decisions/0119-runpod-is-the-gpu-lane.md)).

## Polish routing

Missing pickups, dead containers, a wrong prompt, an item that cannot be
placed or that floats or sinks are blockers. Item icons and inspection
renders are `P3`; a record field every later item would need is `P1`.

## Gotchas

- Never read a plugin's random-fill tables for loot: contents are
  authored and fixed per record (00-core rule 9).
- Ownership is placement data (standard 3); do not add a second flag.
- The sandbox's weapon tables are the source to promote, not a second
  home to keep: after this chunk `game-core` holds derived views only.
- The camera holds while placing on controller, as in the source game;
  do not put rotation on the right stick.
