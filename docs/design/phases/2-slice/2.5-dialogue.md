# 2.5 — Dialogue

**Goal.** The dialogue system in `packages/`, Morrowind-shaped in its
mechanics and layered in its voice from culture down to the person
([0139](../../old-decisions/0139-cultures-down-to-the-person-facts-once-opinions-each.md)):
every fact once, every person with a personality the player can hear,
no two people a sentence alike, and the **complete** dialogue of every
person placed in the slice.

`packages/dialogue` holds the topic-web loader, the condition evaluator
for quests/85, `KnowledgeState` (a save family), line selection,

## The shape

Four layers, each a home table in [world/98](../../world/98-data-model.md):

1. **Culture sheet**: one typed record per node of the hierarchy
   (province, region, people as race plus upbringing, faction and office,
   settlement, life group, household), each with a parent. Holds norms
   and taboos, values and contempts, stock opinions per topic class,
   speech habits (address terms, formality, oaths, what is never said
   aloud), the current talk (grievances and rumours, feeding
   `rumoursByRegion`), and its view of each neighbouring node. Every
   trait cites a lore dossier or is marked inferred. The four register
   layers are the province, region, people and faction sheets' speech
   rows; the sheets do not duplicate them, they reference them.
2. **Stance**: per person, per topic class, composed down the chain, with
   a deviation field carrying its reason from the person's record and a
   planned deviation rate per group. The voice sheet (one quirk, a
   sentence-length band, a contraction rate, an address term, a favourite
   subject, a never-mentions list) is part of the same row and is
   checkable.
3. **Pool line**: the canonical answer per topic × condition set (race,
   upbringing, faction and rank, region, settlement, disposition,
   standing, crime, time, quest state). The only home of a fact. Every
   person answers from it; nobody is silent.
4. **Override**: a person's own line for a topic, rendered from their
   stance, their sheet and the pool fact. Differs in opinion and voice,
   never in fact. Derived from its pool line: when the pool line changes,
   its overrides are flagged for regeneration.

Facts flow down, never sideways: a line never carries a fact its person's
record or the pool does not hold.

## Loops

Three loops, all CPU-only, all on the working tree, all closed by the
planner on the readers' `Recommendations`, never by the lane that wrote
the thing ([loops](../loops.md)). **Judgement is the measure, readers
are cheap.** Quality, style, voice, personality, variety and
Morrowind-likeness are judged by `voice-reader` agents (one lens,
one batch, many in parallel as one `Workflow`) and the `corpus-reader`
(the whole corpus in one sitting); the `reader` keeps the
screenshots. The linter catches only exact string patterns (a banned
word, a duplicate sentence) and is never the bar; a fault a reader can
see and a regex cannot is still a fault. The planner reads reports,
never lines in bulk. Writers follow the
[dialogue-write](../../../.claude/skills/dialogue-write/SKILL.md) skill, so
a batch reaches its readers gate-green on the first draft; its sequence
is the order of the loop below. The loop continues in
[2.6 § Dialogue](2.6-trade-and-services.md#dialogue),
with every ruling in the [dialogue rulings index](../../research/text-and-voice/dialogue-rulings-index.md).

### The Morrowind corpus (2.5a, before any line)

A research dossier `docs/research/text-and-voice/morrowind-dialogue-corpus.md`
of about two hundred real Morrowind lines pulled from UESP, tagged by
slot (greeting, topic, rumour, service, persuasion response, quest
step), condition (race, class, faction, disposition) and personality,
with a dozen multi-line sequences showing how a conversation moves.
Comparison only, never copied, and never a name ([quests/86](../../quests/86-never-reuse-names.md)).
It is the yardstick every voice review below measures against.

### Loop 1: voice (every writing batch, through the whole phase)

- **Measure.** For each batch (one place's lines, or the pool for one
  condition set): the linter for exact patterns; then a **reader panel**
  run as one `Workflow`, one reader per lens, in parallel:
  - *Tells*: the tells table ([quests/60](../../quests/60-writing-and-lore.md)
    §45e.1) and [style-core](../../standards/text/style-core.md), line by line.
  - *Voice*: each speaker against their registers and voice sheet; does
    the quirk show, does the address term hold, does faction speak on
    duty and race slip underneath.
  - *Personality*: given only the lines, write each speaker's stance
    back; a reader who cannot recover the stance row from the lines has
    found a person with no personality.
  - *Variety*: the batch as a body: sameness of shape, every line a
    hook, stacked triads, closers, flourish count, bland politeness,
    grandeur without a particular, a town that sounds like one writer.
  - *Culture*: does the place sound like one place; is the norm audible
    and the dissenter distinct; does the sheet's current talk surface.
  - *Blind mix*: the batch's lines shuffled with corpus lines of the
    same slot and condition, names masked; the reader marks which are
    ours and says why. Above chance means a tell, and every "why" is a
    candidate row.
  - *Morrowind feel*: the batch beside corpus sequences of the same
    slot; plainer, shorter, more concrete, or not.
  Each reader returns hits (`line | lens | reason | rewrite`) and
  `Recommendations`: rows for the tells table, rules a register or the
  style guide is missing, lines or people to rewrite.
- **Diagnose.** The planner merges the panel: a fault two lenses saw is
  one fault. Every new fault becomes a worded row in the skill's tells
  list (`references/style.md` § I) or a register, with an example of the fault and of the fix, so the next
  panel's readers carry it; only an exact string pattern also becomes a
  linter rule. Faults of overuse are rows with a ceiling in words ("at
  most one closer in ten lines"), judged by the variety reader.
- **Fix.** The style guide, the registers and the tells table are
  rewritten in place in the same batch as the lines; lines are rewritten
  per person, never patched per word.
- **Re-measure** the batch; then the next batch starts from the grown
  rules, so later places are written better than earlier ones and the
  earlier places are re-run through the grown gates in the last loop.
- **The running corpus read.** After every third batch, one long-context
  reader takes everything written so far in one sitting (the Loop 3
  corpus read, on the partial corpus) so drift across places is caught
  while it is three places old, not thirteen.
- **Exit (the planner).** Two consecutive batches add no new row, the
  blind mix sits at chance for two batches, every lens returns an empty
  `Recommendations`, and an owner sample read (one village, twenty
  lines, "does this feel like Morrowind?") passes.

### Loop 2: function, on every device and input

- **Measure.** The headless walker over every slice person from five
  world states (newcomer, mid-quest, a crime witnessed, a faction rank
  held, night); the knowledge save round trip (save, load, compare); the
  ui-lab Playwright run with real slice content loaded (the longest
  line, the person with the most topics, every service kind, the
  persuasion ladder at each rung, a bark) at the three viewports
  (desktop, phone landscape, the unfolded fold) in each input mode
  (mouse and keyboard, touch, the scripted gamepad focus walk), light
  and dark; the reader sweeps the sheets on the 2.2 checklist (nothing
  cut off, tap sizes, focus order reaches every topic and service,
  state kept across fold and unfold mid-conversation).
- **Diagnose and fix** as one batch; a screen fault goes to the UI
  package with its ui-lab fixture, a reachability fault to the data with
  the walker case that found it.
- **Exit.** Walker, save and sheets green; the reader finds nothing on
  two runs.

### Loop 3: the catalogue (once, over everything, last)

Over every line in the slice at once. First the **corpus read**: one
reader with a long context window takes the whole slice's dialogue in
one sitting (every pool line and every override, grouped by place, with
the culture sheets and the Morrowind corpus beside it; about 1,500
lines fits with room to spare) and reports on the body as a whole, what
no per-place panel can see: a phrase or shape that recurs across
places, the province's favourite words, a cadence every village shares,
how often the same opinion appears in the same clothes, whether regions
and peoples sound different from each other, whether the whole reads as
Morrowind or as one writer, and which places are the best and worst
written and why. Its `Recommendations` name corpus-wide rows and the
places to rewrite. Then the exact-duplicate check, then reader panels
per place with the Loop 1 lenses plus three catalogue-wide ones: *drift* (each override beside its pool line, any
fact that differs), *particular* (does each line hold something from
its speaker's own life), and the *blind grouping test* (a place's lines
shuffled, grouped by speaker; below chance means clones). Every earlier
place is re-read through the rows Loop 1 grew. One fix batch across all
places, never person by person; a place the grouping or culture lens
fails is rewritten as a place. Exit: every lens empty and the owner
check below.

### Cost rules

Batches are one place or one condition set, never the whole slice, so a
row learnt early pays across the rest. Many cheap readers beat one
expensive one: a panel of seven `voice-reader`s on one place costs less
than one planner turn. Readers see lines; the planner sees reports. No
publish inside a loop. A loop runs twice at most without a written
reason.

Panel rules:

- Per-place panels stop after two fix rounds; hit counts fall but never
  reach zero, so what remains goes to the Loop 3 fix batch.
- The planner runs each panel as a `Workflow`, because the role guard
  keeps readers off delivery lanes; it resumes the lane with the hits.
- The blind mix masks setting nouns and address terms in both sets, so
  it judges cadence, not vocabulary.
- Reader briefs name the per-topic pool files (`local`, `role`,
  `newcomer`, `faction`, `rumour`); readers cannot find them otherwise.

## Owner check

A village URL; "talk to everyone: do they sound like one place, and like
different people in it? Who sounds wrong, who knows too little?"

## Polish routing

A silent or placeholder person is a blocker; a place the grouping or
culture lens fails is a blocker. Voice refinements the reader rates minor are `P2`.

## Gotchas

- Dialogue is a layer on the people records ([0140](../../old-decisions/0140-layers-on-a-built-place-never-rebuild-it.md)):
  the generator, the gates and the readers run per changed record id; a
  line edit never regenerates, republishes or re-reads the whole corpus,
  and a place's people are never republished to add their lines.
- Never reuse a name from another mod ([quests/86](../../quests/86-never-reuse-names.md)).
- A line never carries a fact of its own: facts live in the record and
  the pool.
- Write a person whole, never a topic across people.
- Register never by spelling or broken grammar; by address, vocabulary
  and formality (the research §7).
- The sheets and gates must hold at three thousand people; a rule that
  needs a hand check per person is a defect.
