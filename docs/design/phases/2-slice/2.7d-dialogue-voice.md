# 2.7d Dialogue voice: a loop that hears Morrowind

A sub-phase of Part 2 that runs beside 2.7 to 2.11. It replaces the
2.7 dialogue training loop (loops 1 to 9, `lanes/2.7/dialogue/loop-log.md`).
The owner's words, all 71 quotes in time order by theme:
`lanes/2.7d/owner-words/README.md` (the Q-numbers below cite it).

Status: d10 landed (11ae8be94): the whole Morrowind pull stored, 7,952
lines; the selector; the old loop's gates stripped from `check` and the
skill. d11 merged the owner's words and set the critic questions. Round 1
is Chasepoint (a salvage station on the Mercantile Coast).

## Goal, in the owner's words

Terms: the Hist are the Argonians' sacred trees; disposition is how much
a person likes the player (0 to 100, as in Morrowind); the skill is the
written recipe the writing agent follows.

1. **The player learns what they asked**, "provided helpfulness of the
   NPC's personality and disposition towards the player allow" (Q72).
   The player never sees our design files, so what they should know
   about a place comes from the people in it (Q1). Asked "what is the
   hist", a person "didn't tell me what the hist is" (Q3). A hatching
   detail alone is "of limited actual usefulness ... It needs to say what
   'a Hist' actually is! Based on lore. Writer needs to be able to look
   things up in the lore (using Find crawl agents)" (Q72): a helpful
   person says, plainly enough for a stranger, that a Hist is a great
   sapient tree of the deep swamps, older than any people, that
   Argonians hatch at its roots and drink its sap (the dossier
   `world/sources/lore/topics/hist-and-sap.md`); a cold one says less.
2. **People talk like people.** "More useful from player perspective, a
   bit less curt, and less AI" (Q2). "People don't talk like that, they
   spell things out, they chat, they describe stuff, they meander, and the
   specific ways they do all of these things are subtly different
   depending on their personality and context. It's ok for some
   personalities to be short and curt but not everyone!" Never "weirdly
   trying to pack too much information into each pithy phrase" (Q7, Q8).
3. **No AI voice**, meaning the whole rulebook: "there is far more to
   this than the few examples you've listed in the plan. We have a whole
   style guide and list of banned phrases and constructions and overuses
   somewhere. find it and build it into the skill" (Q73). The writer
   reads `.claude/skills/dialogue-write/references/style.md` before
   drafting: every ban, construction and overuse from the
   [style guide](../../standards/text/style-guide.md), the
   banned-constructions table (`docs/quests/60-writing-and-lore.md`
   §45e.1), the prose linter's rules and the tells list, one line each
   with its linter rule. The owner's own named cases are among them
   (Q9-Q15). Overuse across the whole body counts, and much of it is "a
   vibe check that must be done by qualitative reviewer agents" (Q10,
   Q32).
4. **British English commas** after a fronted phrase: "should have one
   after "naming day"" (Q16).
5. **Who they are is heard.** "Every person must have a personality, and
   that must be reflected in their dialogue"; factions, villages, towns,
   areas and social classes have "lore-grounded cultures", and the player
   gets "a sense of all of that from interacting with people" (Q20).
   Personality, upbringing, faction, the speaker's race and the player's
   race all shape speech; dialogue so far is "poor in terms of personality
   and variation between people" (Q22). Regions and kinds of place differ
   in their mix of races (Q17, Q19); not everyone has a faction (Q18).
   Anyone may explain a thing, in "slightly different words" with
   "different perspectives"; helpfulness follows "personality, faction,
   race", never a cap (Q21).
6. **Everyone unique.** Facts are set; views are each person's own, and
   "nobody should have word for word identical sentences to each other,
   otherwise they're just clones"; no "converging on one AI voice" (Q24).
7. **True in the world.** A price in salt means the player pays in salt;
   a rumour of bandits means something is out there; people may lie, but
   never "an AI hallucination" (Q25, Q26). Judged in the real place, since
   fake places hide "whether what they're saying is nonsensical in the
   in-game location and context" (Q27).
8. **Disposition changes the talk**; "quest-essential topics probably
   shouldn't ever be gated on disposition unless there's some guaranteed
   way to get it back up again" (Q28; [0145](../../old-decisions/0145-disposition-changes-the-talk-essential-topics-never-gated.md)).
9. **Morrowind is the yardstick.** Branching topics "just like morrowind"
   (Q29). The writer holds "real and relevant Morrowind lines ... relevant
   to the person and their personality and register ... verbatim ones from
   morrowind, that's like the single most useful thing" (Q30, Q31).
10. **Made right first time, judged by a critic.** A skill that makes the
    writing "right first time", "not just gates and tests" (Q35); the
    writer has "basically no other context" (Q36); "massively strip back
    those gating checks and ... lean much more on the qualitative critic
    judgment" (Q42); a broad read of the whole body (Q33); every round's
    feedback updates the skill (Q39, Q40); before-and-after examples for
    the owner to read when they choose (Q37, Q51); "I don't have time to
    read every round. I want you to train this skill for me using the
    loop" (Q74); Sonnet for writing and review (Q47); "Do the Opus
    experiment, but don't make it the main writer unless both (a) it is
    demonstrably better and (b) I read some examples and approve it"
    (Q53, Q75).
11. **Greetings in passing**, by friendliness, race and mode of address,
    "outside of proper dialogue" (Q44).

Acceptance stays 2.5's: the owner talks to everyone in a place and they
sound like one place and different people in it, none wrong, none
knowing too little.

## Why the old loop failed (lessons, binding here)

1. **Invented people in an invented place.** Six fake sheets in a scratch
   place could never be judged for sense in the world, so the judge
   measured only "can you tell them apart", and length bands did that.
2. **Gates in place of judgement.** Every loop added a check; the writers
   learned the checks. Critics never passed 3 of 5 and fell as the gates
   grew. The gates also ate the writer's revisions (mechanical reds first).
3. **The writers never heard the target.** The verbatim-line bank the
   owner asked for was built once (259 lines of about 7,700 pulled), cut
   to six lines printed last in the packet, and the skill never told the
   writer to use it. The selector ignored chatter, traits, faction and the
   player's race, and a "no two speakers share a line" rule overrode race.
4. **Judges saw no context.** Critics saw masked lines only: no sheet, no
   place, no clock, no quests, no Morrowind lines to compare against.
5. **Noise read as signal.** Six writers, six critics, scores swinging by
   four points between loops with no lever changed.

## The loop

Each round is one real slice place, its real people, packets from the
tree, results applied by the layer path ([0140](../../old-decisions/0140-layers-on-a-built-place-never-rebuild-it.md)),
never a scratch place.

### a. The corpus the writer hears (first)

Status: done (2.7d10): 7,952 lines stored with the dossier
`world/sources/lore/morrowind-dialogue-corpus-full.md`; `worldgen.exemplar_select`; the packet opens with
"Hear this voice" (`lanes/2.7d/loop-log.md` § Setup).

- Store the whole Morrowind pull (about 7,700 lines) as
  `world/sources/dialogue/exemplars/morrowind-full.json` with a dossier
  under [0002](../../old-decisions/0002-era-and-source-confidence.md): each
  line tagged speaker race, sex, faction or class, register (curt, plain,
  chatty, rambling by length and shape), kind (greeting, topic, rumour,
  directions, refusal, service), disposition band where Morrowind banded
  it, and the listener's race where the line is race-conditioned.
- A selector gives every person about twenty verbatim lines, chosen in
  this order: their race; their faction or office; their register from the
  sheet (a chatty person hears chatty lines, never curt ones); the kinds of
  their topics; low and high disposition greetings; two lines spoken to
  the player's race. Two neighbours may share a line when race demands it.
- The packet opens with these lines under one heading, "Hear this voice",
  and the skill's first instruction is to read them before anything else
  and write in that cadence, never copying a run of six words.

### b. The writer

Status: tools and skill done (2.7d10): `check` holds the four hard rules only; round 1 is Chasepoint.

Sonnet, as the owner ruled. Two passes: the voice sample and draft from
the heard lines, the sheet and the person's day; then the facts fitted.
Hard rules only remain in `check`: the prose linter, every pool fact
present, no six-word run lifted from an exemplar or the pool, the sheet's
length band. Before drafting the writer reads `references/style.md`
(every ban and overuse, Q73). Where a topic names a lore term, the
packet's section 5 carries "Lore facts of your topics": one or two
sentences from the dossier saying what the thing is
(`worldgen/dialogue_packet.py` `LORE_SOURCES`). When a topic needs a
fact the packet lacks, the writer looks it up with a `find` agent crawl
over `world/sources/lore/` and `docs/world/`, never a keyword grep of
Skyrim or mod files, by `references/lore-lookup.md`; where nothing
exists it says less and never invents (Q72). Every other gate from loops 1 to 9 (marks, devices, cue
shapes, pool-noun counts, stub and aphorism tests, region oaths) is
deleted from the tool and the skill in the first round.

### c. The judge: a critic panel with the whole context

One qualitative panel per place, Sonnet, each critic with the person's
Morrowind lines, sheet, day, place record, roads and quests in hand. The
questions, one per owner ask (the round's copy:
`lanes/2.7d/round1/critic-questions.md`):

1. **Asked and answered.** Would the player learn what they asked, as
   far as this person's helpfulness and disposition allow? Asked what the
   Hist is, does a helpful speaker say what a Hist actually is, true to
   the lore? (Q1, Q3-Q6, Q72)
2. **Length by personality.** Does a chatty person chat, describe and
   wander, and a curt one stay curt, as the sheet says? (Q2, Q7, Q8)
3. **No AI voice.** Read against the whole of `references/style.md`:
   which bans, constructions or overuses appear, by line number? Does a
   speaker name their own doings in an accuser's words? (Q9-Q15, Q73)
4. **British comma.** Is there a comma after every fronted phrase? (Q16)
5. **Who is heard.** Can you hear their race, region, upbringing and
   faction, and the player's race where it matters? (Q17-Q22)
6. **Disposition.** Do low and high read as this person cold and warm,
   and does an essential topic give its fact at zero? (Q28)
7. **Here and now.** Does every answer fit this place, this hour, this
   work spot, these roads and quests, and is every claim true in the
   world? (Q25-Q27)
8. **Morrowind ear.** Blind pairs, a Morrowind line against ours of the
   same kind and register: which is Morrowind? At chance, we pass. (Q10,
   Q29-Q31)
9. **This person.** Is it this sheet and no neighbour's, with views of
   their own, the personality in how they say it, not a stamped quirk?
   (Q20, Q24)
10. **No clones.** Does any sentence repeat word for word in another
    person's lines? (Q24)

Each critic answers in words first and a verdict (a person of this place:
yes or not yet) second. Fixes go back to the writer as the critic's words.
After every round the lane rewrites the skill from the critics' words as
prose, never as a check, and logs the round in `lanes/2.7d/loop-log.md`.

### d. The loop trains the skill; the owner reads when they choose

"I don't have time to read every round. I want you to train this skill
for me using the loop" (Q74). The owner does not read every round: the
critic panel's words train the skill, round after round, with no wait
for an owner's read. Each round still writes a one-page sheet in the
shape of `lanes/2.7/dialogue/before-after.md` (who the person is in
plain words, the lines they heard, before and after for four topics, low
and high) and keeps it in `lanes/2.7d/` for the owner to read when they
choose. Any reply the owner gives goes into the skill as prose and into
the critic's questions.

### e. The Opus experiment, once

"Do the Opus experiment, but don't make it the main writer unless both
(a) it is demonstrably better and (b) I read some examples and approve
it" (Q75). The experiment runs: one round, same place, the voice pass on
Opus against the Sonnet voice pass, facts fitted the same way, the same
panel judging both blind; cost logged (about six short drafts). Opus
becomes the main writer only if both hold: the blind panel shows it
demonstrably better, and the owner reads examples and approves. Until
then Sonnet writes and nothing loops on Opus.

## Exit, then roll-out

Exit for the loop: two rounds running where every speaker is "a person of
this place" on every critic and the Morrowind ear at chance. Then: every slice place by the layer path with low and
high variants on topics (today only Xuth-Ra's greeting has them), the
greeting barks rewritten in the same loop, player-race pairs where the
corpus has them, boat crews addressed by their boat's name (the rule
sits unused in the Riverwalk sheet,
`world/sources/placement/promises/place.dunmer-north.riverwalk.json`),
`dialogue_gates --all` on the hard rules, one corpus read.

## Cost and lanes

A round is small and fast, about ten minutes: one place, three people of
different registers, one write (two passes inside the writer), one panel,
the skill note, the owner sheet. Six people, a revision pass and a
re-read made round 1 take over half an hour. Writers and critics on
Sonnet. The corpus store and selector are one `deliver` lane first.
Rounds run as `dialogue-lane` beside the combat, progression and magic
chunks and never block them; a phase deploy carries open dialogue work
into the next phase's session (`tooling/repo-standards/phase_chain.json`).
The planner reads every round's owner sheet and its tallies against the
two rounds before: two rounds with no improvement is a plateau; no more
rounds launch, the samples and the question go to the owner's inbox, and
the pause rides the hand-off until the owner replies.

A change to the skill, the packet or the critic questions lands between
rounds, as its own lane, never into a live round: a message to a live
round rebuilds its packets and rewrites its people, and round 1 ran three
write passes for that reason. The next round picks the change up.
