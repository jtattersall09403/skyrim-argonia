1. **What I'm meant to do next:** Go back to Never-Sold for the next job. Every quest so far has ended with her saying to come back for more work, and my last check was to see whether she had another. I also want to know what the Broker asks next. If nothing is active in my journal, I think I'm just meant to keep taking her work.

2. **Who I work for:** Never-Sold, as one of the Chainbreakers. She named me one after the first job, and she pays me in gold for each one. I'm not sure I believe in the cause. She says plainly that we'll be called the people who loose killers, and that it will be true. I work for her because she's honest about the cost and nobody else is offering me anything. I did kill Brings-Them-Back and Tsaxi for her, and the game didn't let me forget it.

3. **Most confusing moment:** The Broker's door. I had been steered toward talking my way through, but the door was locked at a high difficulty, and I only got in because I had lockpicks from earlier. The persuasion also failed because I lacked the 1000 gold, and the game never explained why I'd needed that money. The tokens were never resolved either. I didn't take Never-Sold's book, yet the quest finished anyway. I couldn't tell whether I'd skipped something or whether it just didn't matter.

4. **What I'd tell the writer:** The moral weight is the best part. Nobody is a villain, every exit costs somebody, and Never-Sold says so to your face. The Broker quest is the best of the three. Finding the list of eleven names and handing it to Heavy-Rope felt like a real solution. But I stole the list in front of Ussa-Rekh and nothing happened, and that broke the spell. Make the consequences exist, or let me choose to do it quietly. Make the locked-door and gold requirements clear in the dialogue so I know what I'm missing. Don't leave loose objects like the book and tokens hanging. And please let the earlier killings echo in later conversations the way they did a little here, because that was the most interesting thing the game did.
