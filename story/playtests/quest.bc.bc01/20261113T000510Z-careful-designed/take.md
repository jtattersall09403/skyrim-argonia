Short, clear quest. I understood who everyone was and what was at stake. Never-Sold framed the problem well. Hulm-Tei was a nicely grey character: his book was technically true, but the sum was unjust, and he conceded to custom when pressed. I liked that I could keep the 100 gold by talking, and that the ending said it freed one woman without changing how the estate treats the next. Rough spots: persuasion options were listed twice (a Persuade and a plain version), and the world was thin, with no doors or things to explore. I would want to see what Rows-For-Another does next and what else the Chainbreakers do.
