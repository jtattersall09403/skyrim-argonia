Short, clear quest. I understood it throughout and the moral texture was good: Hulm-Tei insists every line in the book is true, and Never-Sold says true lines can still add up to a wrong sum. Persuading him worked on the first try, so the check felt easy and I kept the 100 gold. One thing felt odd: Hulm-Tei struck the page on a promise of payment that I never delivered, and the journal says I kept the gold. That is a small logic gap. Rows-For-Another notes that the estate will still treat the next person held the same way, which I liked. I would want more of the Chainbreakers and Blackrose, but nothing further started, so I stopped.
