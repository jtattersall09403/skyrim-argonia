1. Nothing is left for me to do. My journal showed no active objective after I told Ei-Tuja what I saw at Low-Reed, so I stopped. If the game continued, I'd expect Nesh-Deeka to send me after the cutters: the sap, the ship's hold with roots through its deck, the door that was never built, and the woman with the knotted cord. The Mnemic Egg that Ei-Tuja mentioned might be the next lead.

2. I work for the Veiled Reed, the state's quiet service, through Nesh-Deeka. I took the work because I'm on no roll, so the Reed can use me where it can't use its own people. She paid me, and I want to find out why the knotted-cord woman spared me. I'm not sure how far I trust the Reed. It admits it keeps hands that can be disowned.

3. The most confusing moment was the ending. Once I'd told Ei-Tuja, the quest closed with no pointer to what came next. I had also expected wasps on the way to the grove and never met any. I went to Kaska-Meen hoping for a new thread and got nothing, so I couldn't tell whether the game was finished or I had missed a trigger. The sap's purpose was also never explained. The dry voice and the door that was never built stayed unexplained, though that felt deliberate.

4. I'd tell the writer the setting, the tone and the Pays-Late chapter worked well. His hidden cut and my condition that he confess to Hires-By-The-Season gave me a real moral choice. The mystery is intriguing. But the end of the quest line needs a hook, such as a journal line or a lead from Nesh-Deeka, so players know whether to keep going. Dead-end NPCs like Kaska-Meen should either say so or offer something. And a threat I'm warned about, like the wasps, should actually appear.
