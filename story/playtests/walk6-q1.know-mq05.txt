What you know so far, from your journal. From "No Name on the Work Roll": I woke in the hold of a ship, moored at a pier west of Lilmoth in Black Marsh, among a gang of Argonians I do not know. I'm to go up the stairs and out of the door to the pier, and speak to the man who stands there. Buys-The-Months, the broker, holds his gang's Owing, the months of work they owe. I am on no line of his roll, so he will not keep me. He says Nesh-Deeka, a guest at Salts-The-Catch's house south of the second big ship off Lilmoth's dock, pays for work. I'm to find her there, east along the shore. A woman with a knotted cord killed Nesh-Deeka's courier at the gang's camp the night it burned, took his satchel and let me live. Nesh-Deeka serves the Veiled Reed, the state's quiet service, and wants a hand whose name is on no roll. I'm to hear her out and say whether I will work for the Reed. I took work with the Veiled Reed, and Nesh-Deeka put fifty gold in my hand. She has told me the first work the Reed wants of me. Why the woman with the knotted cord let me live, I still do not know. From "The Cutters' Jar": Nesh-Deeka gave me the Veiled Reed's first work. Dives-Without-A-Rope, one of the broker's gang, carried off a jar of sap that the cutters dropped at the burnt camp. Nesh-Deeka wants it to learn what the cutters wanted. I'm to ask Dives-Without-A-Rope for the jar in the ship's hold at the pier west of Lilmoth. I talked Dives-Without-A-Rope round. She gave me the key to her lockbox in the ship's hold. She asked me in return to find who cut her tree. I'm to take the jar from the lockbox and bring it to Nesh-Deeka. I tasted the cutters' sap in Salts-The-Catch's house. Nesh-Deeka wrote down what I said under it: a ship's hold with roots through its deck, a door that was never built, a dry voice. She gave me the page and twenty-five gold. She has heard that the Hist of Low-Reed Village, east of Lilmoth, has turned its face. From "The Tree That Turned Its Face": Hires-By-The-Season, the root-herald, told me the Hist of Low-Reed Village shut eleven nights ago and the eggs in its pool died. The village blames the leeches and Pays-Late, the tree-minder, who will not say why he stands by the roots. I'm to get the truth out of him, on the bank under the stilts. Pays-Late told me what he hid: the Hist's root was cut, its sap drawn off and the cut sealed with his own black gum. He kept quiet, fearing the villagers' blame. A boat came from the north reeds that night. I'm to cross the leech water to the dry rise past the last stilt house and take the sap back. I took the jars of Low-Reed Village's sap from two men on the dry rise, jars like the one the cutters dropped at the burnt camp. Pays-Late can pour the sap back at the cut root, or Nesh-Deeka can keep it as proof. I'm to bring the jars to Pays-Late, by the roots under the stilts, and choose. I gave the jars to Pays-Late on one condition, that he tells Hires-By-The-Season himself that he found the cut and hid it, and he gave his word. He will pour the sap back at the cut root. The cutters drew this tree's sap into jars, as they did at the burnt camp. What do they want with it? You are standing in Salts-The-Catch's house in Lilmoth with Nesh-Deeka.
