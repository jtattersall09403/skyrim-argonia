1. **What I'm meant to do next.** I'm not sure. My last journal check was meant to find a new objective, and I don't have a clear one to point to. I expect the next step is to go back to Never-Sold for more Chainbreaker work, since she has offered it every time. I'm also curious about the "dead levels", so I'd ask around about them.

2. **Who I work for.** The Chainbreakers, through Never-Sold. She named me one of them after the Rows-For-Another job. I took the work because it was a way in, and I kept it because the jobs are moral puzzles rather than errands. She is not pleased with me. I picked Mends-Nets over the prisoner she wanted freed, and she took that coldly. I think I'm a Chainbreaker on probation, not a trusted one.

3. **Most confusing moment.** Giving Tsaxi the singed leaves to win his word. The leaves were the evidence that mattered, and handing them over seemed to cost almost nothing. I didn't understand why the game let me trade away the proof, or whether it was meant to be a sacrifice. The Rose and the heirs were also hard to follow. They came up only in dialogue, and I never saw why they mattered. Separately, one lockpick command failed because of how I quoted it, which was my mistake and not the game's.

4. **What I'd tell the writer.** The moral knots are the best part: two prisoners with the same words, a witness who won't name either, and a choice with no clean answer. The Seven-Floods and Rows-For-Another connection hit hard because I had killed Brings-Them-Back earlier, and that kind of callback is what you should do more of. Make choices cost something visible. Giving up the leaves should have a price, and the game should say so. Show the Rose and the heirs in the world, not only in speech. Instant travel makes the marsh feel thin, so give me one or two places where I have to look around. Keep letting NPCs stay unhappy with me.
