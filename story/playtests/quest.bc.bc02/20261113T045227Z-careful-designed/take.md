I liked this more than I expected. The writing is dense and a bit mannered, but it is consistent, and the moral weight is real. The second quest gave me a proper puzzle: two prisoners both claim the same words, a man was drowned in a net, and one suspect mends nets. I could tell something was off before I read the proof. Tracking down the leaves felt like detective work. The old woman refusing to name "her daughter" was a good touch. Choosing Walks-Far was easy to justify, but it still left Mends-Nets and Seven-Floods behind, and Never-Sold being "pleased and uneasy" matched how I felt. The first quest, where I killed the fetcher, was bleaker. She did not thank me for how I did it.

What bored or confused me: the dialogue is exposition-heavy. Tsaxi gave me huge walls of lore (reckoners, leaves, the dead levels, being put under) in one go, and I had to hold a lot of terms in my head. My Restoration and Alchemy options with Seven-Floods were dead ends that just said I was too weak. That was a bit irritating, since I got no hint of what would have worked. The lamia fight and the lockpicking were over in one command, so they had no tension. Travel is instant, which is fine. The ending left me with no new objective, and Never-Sold had nothing more to offer right now. I would like to know what happens with the dead levels, the Rose, and the Broker, and whether Mends-Nets gets found out.
