# What the owner has ruled about quests and story

One place for every ruling of the owner's on what the quests, the story and the writing should be like and how they
should feel. Each line gives its source: `OR` is [owner-rulings](../../docs/owner-rulings.md) with the entry's date,
`#n` a GitHub issue, a decision its number. Anyone who writes a story line, a quest record or a line of dialogue
reads this first; a new ruling is added here the day it is made, as well as to owner-rulings.

## The bar

- What Morrowind achieved inside minimal scripting: "beautiful storytelling, deep world building, compelling
  narrative lines, moral ambiguity, genuinely interesting conflict (of various kinds)" (OR 2026-11-12).
- It should "flow like a really nicely written exciting story throughout that any elder scrolls fan would fall in
  love with and have a lot of fun playing through" (OR 2026-11-12).
- The story is not "patched or shoehorned" into its world but "really naturally and deeply integrated with it",
  keeping the factions and the spirit of the original; there is creative licence to rewrite and re-author
  (OR 2026-11-12).
- The old main quest, factions and quest designs are the foundation, improved not replaced (OR 2026-10-28). Keep
  the treasure hunt, the spies and the main quest's spirit; make it "more Black Marsh, more exciting, less
  bureaucratic" (OR 2026-11-06, the quest review loop).
- The main quest's treasure hunt is the main theme of its plot, "like the film National Treasure! But with a better
  reason for doing the hunt": many quests, some leading one to the next, across most regions and every kind of place
  (wrecks, mines, caves, forts, outposts, ruins), rising in danger and in the worth of their rewards; "make it fun!"
  (OR 2026-11-13, story explorer notes).
- The player is a blank slate drawn into the world's intrigue, never a chosen one, and the plot does not lean on
  learning about the player's own past (OR 2026-11-13, story explorer notes).
- A new player is shown early why the story's central thing matters: "I'm an elder scrolls fan and I didn't know
  about them. I was a bit like... They're just trees?" (OR 2026-11-13, story explorer notes).
- Every faction is "fun and intriguing and exciting" with "drama and tension, all of their own". A faction's
  premise may stay as background, but each quest is something daring the player does, never chores: "do you have to
  pickpocket somebody to get a key to break into their house to change something on a record about owed months,
  for example. Use your imagination, make them fun" (OR 2026-11-13, of the Chainbreakers and "all the factions").
- A new player with no context must be able to tell what is going on as it happens: the walked quests were
  "incredibly confusing" and "super impenetrable" (walk 6, OR 2026-11-12).
- A mystery keeps its question open. The player finds how a thing is done by gathering clues in quests, and is
  left asking why it is done that way; the twist answers. The player works it out, never their employer (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- The owner likes a side that does "something fundamentally bad but for a good reason", an enemy it "legitimately
  wronged", and someone on the player's side tied to that enemy by what they did to her, who lies about it (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).


## Action and the player's skills

- Quests have "action" (the player's skills used, varied); no talk-to-X-then-Y quests (OR 2026-10-28).
- Every quest uses named Skyrim skills and names which: One-Handed, Two-Handed, Archery, Block, Destruction,
  Alteration, Illusion, Conjuration, Restoration, Alchemy, Sneak, Lockpicking, Pickpocket, Speech, Mysticism
  (OR 2026-11-06, #50). Swimming and climbing are not skills and do not count (OR 2026-11-06, #53).
- Every main quest offers at least two ways through, each with different named skills, so different builds can
  finish it (#50). "Different builds being able to complete main" (OR 2026-11-12).
- A build is a mix of melee (of several kinds), ranged, stealth and magic, or one of them alone; stealth is always
  paired with another. "Talker" is not a build: "no one is playing Skyrim purely for speechcraft". A few quests may
  have a Speech way, and no quest assumes a player's main build is Speech (OR 2026-11-13, story explorer notes).
- Faction quests are tied to the faction's own skills, "for consistency and immersion" (#50); "factions have
  their own build leanings" (OR 2026-11-12).
- A quest that does not use the player's in-game skills is not fun, and that is a blocking review finding
  (OR 2026-11-06). Using in-game skills in every quest "is really key to the fun" (OR 2026-11-12).
- Quests lean on action, danger and discovery, not on law and custom (OR 2026-11-06). "Lean towards activity and
  action (of various kinds, I don't just mean combat, use your imagination and anchor on the variety of in game
  skills) over processes and procedures and records" (OR 2026-11-12).
- Combat is always paired with a reward in proportion to its difficulty (OR 2026-11-09, on what places give).

- A choice or an ending matters in play, "beyond just description and text": "what are the actual gameplay
  impacts? In terms of powers, rewards, abilities, unlocking fun stuff" (OR 2026-11-13). Each outcome says what it
  gives and what it costs the player.
- Side quests met on the main road are plainly side quests. They give a riddle in place of directions, with no
  marker until the place is found; the journal then says the player guessed right, and the work there has markers
  and uses the player's skills. Their rewards are made for the main quest's final fight, so a player who does them
  all finds it easy (Majora's Mask's Fierce Deity mask) (OR 2026-11-13, story explorer notes; the riddle place
  with no marker approved in #71).
- A faction's ranks bring rewards in proportion, and things unlocked, as Skyrim's guilds do (OR 2026-11-13).
- A power or rule of the story is explained to the player once, at the point it matters; nobody then needs to say
  why again (OR 2026-11-13, story explorer notes).
- A choice of ways is real only when no way is free: "if the Reed gives you the ring freely/automatically then
  this is a pointless 'choice', you are just always going to use the ring" (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- A skill way in an early quest can be done by a level-one character who chose that skill: the lock is easy, the
  pocket is easy, and the player starts with a lockpick or two (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- A sneak way is truly possible: guards few enough and far enough from what the player must reach; the journal says
  the player may fight or sneak; markers show the key and the cage (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- Every faction's line climbs "an objective hardness ladder in terms of what they *require*, regardless of the
  player's level": skill levels asked, danger bands of the places visited. Nothing is levelled to the player.
  Rewards get "progressively better in proportion (and be hand authored, not random)" (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- A quest's place has the danger band its point in the line calls for, the way to it included. A first quest does
  not send a new character to a D4 ruin (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- A faction quest that teaches a spell, a recipe or a thing has the player use it in that quest, as one step of
  several that lead into each other (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).


## The factions

His words on each faction (skills, how it is joined, its rival, its choice and its ending) are in
[the factions overhaul](../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05c-factions-overhaul.md) and are read whole before
a faction is written. Every earlier ruling of his on factions, from this repository and the first one, is gathered
with its source in [owner-rulings-on-factions](../../docs/research/writing/owner-rulings-on-factions.md).

- The joinable factions are eight: the Chainbreakers, the detectives (the Lamps), a thieves' guild (not called the
  Night-Reed), the Shadowscales (back in secret; the death of the "last" one was cover), the pirates, an Argonian
  order of magic and alchemy with a name in Jel, Dunmer necromancers behind a respectable trading house at Thorn,
  and Imperial merchants at Gideon. The main quest has the Veiled Reed and the Unbound. A Dunmer house that profits
  from the Owing cannot be joined. Every other line is cut, the League among them (OR 2026-11-16).
- Rivals: the Lamps, the thieves and the Shadowscales, three ways; the Argonian order and the necromancers; the
  pirates and the Imperials; the Chainbreakers (the pirates' allies) and the Owing house. "It's fun if they're in
  conflict with each other in various combinations" (OR 2026-11-16).
- Each line has about as many quests as a guild of Morrowind's or Skyrim's. Between them the factions have a seat
  or a branch in every major city (OR 2026-11-16).
- In the three-way rivalry the player is shut out of the other two at some point, and late in the line chooses
  which rival to side with against the third. The Lamps' last case has a very hard Speech check that avoids the
  choice (OR 2026-11-16).
- A detective's case: the player names the suspect, then motive, means and opportunity, each line open only with
  its evidence found. Every suspect has a motive and a means; all but the culprit have an alibi to find. A
  magistrate confirms the case or shows what was missed. A wrong answer costs a penalty and another case at that
  rank; three wrong at one rank and the player is put out (OR 2026-11-16).
- The thieves' line builds to one great heist (first repo, decision 0026: a first heist, preparations chosen from
  several, then the great heist with more than one way in). The assassins' contracts reward doing them a named
  clever way, and give a unique enchanted thing nearly every quest (the same decision, from Oblivion).
- People the Chainbreakers free turn up at fixed posts with cheap training and good prices, and help in other
  lines without ever being needed (OR 2026-11-16).
- To Argonians, raising and binding the dead is their own enslavement over again: the order of magic and the
  necromancers are enemies for that reason (OR 2026-11-16).
- Rewards by rank "stack low, lock high": the first ranks' rewards can be held together across factions, the top
  ones shut each other out, and the player is told before they commit (first repo, decision 0028, 2026-08-26).
- A new player is assumed to know no Elder Scrolls lore "beyond having maybe played Morrowind" (first repo,
  decision 0026).

## The vibe of Argonia: not a bureaucratic place

- "Too many ledgers and rolls and tallies. For some reason AI agents are obsessed with these. It's all a bit too
  bureaucratic. I'm not saying never have them I'm saying we should just have a small number" (new-world feedback,
  2026-11-09). Cut to a few, in vocabulary Black Marsh's own lore uses (OR 2026-11-09).
- It is the theme as well as the word: "Black Marsh is not a bureaucratic place, and the player's play should not
  revolve round ledgers and documents." Concepts built on records, registers, counts and papers are reworked into
  Argonian life (the Hist, eggs, trade by water, raiding, ruins, beasts, kin, memory carried in speech and carving),
  keeping a few where a document is natural (an outsider's papers), faithful to canon and the one extrapolation
  spine (OR 2026-11-09).
- Law and custom only as background (OR 2026-11-06).
- Not absolutely none: "at most a very small amount across the board. Might be some particular people or factions
  or contexts where it's appropriate. Use your judgment" (OR 2026-11-13).
- The province's mood is written up, cited to the lore, in [province-vibe](province-vibe.md).

## Shape of a quest and a line of quests

- Nothing more complicated than Morrowind-level scripting, and much less world editing per quest (walk 6,
  OR 2026-11-12; the ceiling: [quest-form](../../docs/design/quest-form.md)). Before that ruling the bar was
  "nothing harder to script than an average Skyrim quest" (OR 2026-11-06); the lower ceiling replaces it.
- Six narrow additions to the ceiling, each only for the use named: one staged ending scene (fade, music, the trees
  shown changing); a final choice on a message box; a named ally called by a power as an ending's reward; a stage
  that kills one named person in an ending; riddle side quests with no marker until found; sick Hist bark from the
  recolour tool. A person at a finale is already there when the player arrives, never summoned, and nobody follows
  the player (#71, OR 2026-11-13).
- A branching budget keeps quests simple, with some branching for interest. Within a questline, earlier choices
  and endings do not change later quests' stages (no multiplying branches); only planned finale choices carry
  forward (OR 2026-11-06).
- Every stage makes plain what to do (#50). The opening needs a quest stage and a journal entry for each thing to
  do next (walk 4, OR 2026-11-08). Quest markers on the map and in the game for objectives and for things to pick
  up or use; directions in dialogue are not relied on alone (walk 6).
- The opening must not be slow (#50), and happens where the player already is, so a new player cannot wander off
  before it completes (OR 2026-11-10). It is now small and Morrowind-like: the man outside the barge's door speaks
  first, the player is not on his list, and is sent to a named person who inducts them into the main quest's
  faction (walk 6).
- No pointless or buggy activity as a step (MQ05's tree climb was cut: #50).
- Anyone can die. A person the main quest or a faction's line cannot go on without gets our version of
  Morrowind's "thread of prophecy" message; a minor side quest just fails with a journal entry (OR 2026-11-12).
- A place or a quest promises only what Skyrim's own systems can deliver (OR 2026-11-09).
- "Everything described in all parts of the story need to be known to be deliverable": while writing, the writer
  refers to the known deliverable set (the world's places as the data gives them, the meshes, creatures and items
  our mods hold, and the quest ceiling) and chooses only from it, so no burnt huts without a burnt hut to show, no
  knotted cords without a cord to hold, no diving sphere without a sphere (OR 2026-11-13, story explorer notes).
- Nothing is levelled against the player; rewards are fixed (decision 0035).
- A detectives' faction, rival to the assassins and the thieves (decision 0025). Its cases are solved by evidence the
  player must have found (who, means, motive, opportunity), judged right or wrong; later cases break the pattern
  (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- A story is written from the source place: the writer first looks at what the place's source holds (rough layout,
  each building, interiors, caves and mines, rooms, containers, where new things could go) and writes from that,
  "using what is already available where possible and only requiring minimal changes/additions". A cellar, a
  flooded vault or a great door is written only where the source has one (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- Each quest says how it leads to the next. A report back to a handler is written out: what is said and where the
  player is sent. Markers back up every "how the player knows" (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- Branches are thought through: for each way in, what state it leaves and how the next step plays in that state;
  what the line needs next can never be left behind (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- Nobody walks ahead for the player to follow ("scripting the divers to dive down and you follow them will be a
  pain"): they say where to go, and markers show it (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- A line's plot is read by the owner as a short page in the main plot's form (what the faction is at the top, acts,
  one numbered paragraph per quest) before any quest record is written (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- Endings' visions are cutscenes made later; messages are the fallback and carry what a cutscene cannot show (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes).
- The factions the owner wants, their skills, how each is joined, their rivalries and their endings are in
  [the factions overhaul](../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05c-factions-overhaul.md), in his words
  (OR 2026-11-16, story explorer notes). Each faction is heard of through rumours, which start a small quest to find it; a late point in a rival's
  line shuts its door.


## Truth and lore

- Every world, people and quest call cites the lore dossiers or the agreed extrapolation (decision 0032).
  Everything about every quest is consistent with the lore, canon and extrapolated, including people's roles and
  the structure of society (Phase 2 brief). Agents write only what the lore and the data give, so no unsourced
  claim is made in the first place (OR 2026-11-06).
- Nothing invented from nothing: what the lore does not give is derived from it by logical extrapolation, as one
  consistent story and timeline (OR 2026-11-09).
- Every fact a person tells is true of the world the player is standing in (OR 2026-11-07, 2026-11-08).
- "The actual game world and built places and dialogue and quests all need to be completely consistent with each
  other. This is super important" (OR 2026-11-13). In practice:
  - nothing is said that the world's systems do not do (no tides in a province that has none);
  - a thing's place is told relative to things that do not move, never "next to us", since a speaker moves with
    their routine;
  - nobody speaks of an event that is not there to see (a house on fire with no fire);
  - a place is named for what it is only if something built clearly reads as that (no "hatching sheds" without a
    building that reads as one);
  - a person's stated work is work they are seen to do (no "lifting crates" from someone who never does).
- That consistency is "not just ... a check but somehow built into how quests are created in the first place"
  (OR 2026-11-13): a story lists what it needs of each place; the place is built to those needs; a quest's record
  and words are written only on the built place, from what is measured there.
- What a place or thing is, is judged from the player's side: "to the player there's no way to know it's supposed
  to be a hatching shed, it just looks like a house" (OR 2026-11-13). The player knows only its look, the names the
  game shows, what stands there and what they have been told; every promised place and thing says which of those
  tells them.
- No Nordic element reaches the player through what we add (decision 0006).
- A native Argonian place has no jail; the Owing is the sentence; resisting arrest is a fight to the death
  (OR 2026-11-10).
- Quests, places, dialogue, characters and facts are consistent first time, by how they are written.

## People and talk

- Dialogue is unvoiced text, Morrowind-like in structure and style; lines read as if they were Morrowind's,
  adapted to Black Marsh (OR, "The game" and "Writing").
- Everyone the player can walk up to talks, with a light backstory grounded in their place, trade and spot, their
  own personality and their own knowledge, and their own true topics; people who all say the same things are
  "pretty boring" (walks 2 and 3, OR 2026-11-07, 2026-11-08).
- People in one place do not repeat each other; the writer is aware of who else speaks there (walk 5,
  OR 2026-11-10). A lore fact said by two or three people is fine (OR 2026-11-08).
- Directions are Morrowind's: a route by real landmarks from the world, in words, never a count of paces
  (walk 3, OR 2026-11-08, 2026-11-12).
- A speaker never describes their own doings in an accuser's words (a robber does not say "robbery"). No
  closing question that does not follow from the line. The player does not know a speaker's trade until told. No
  two people share a sentence word for word. Talk of trade or of danger is true of what the player will then meet
  (first repo, the owner's notes of 2026-10-17 to 2026-10-19).
- No personification, no verbless fragments, no dangling modifiers, nothing opaque to the player; British
  punctuation (OR "Writing"; walk 4).
- Every story, quest and line passes through our text rules: the owner finds "a lot of annoying AI writing style"
  in the stories. A sentence that says nothing a reader can picture ("given back their no, the first thing the trees
  refused was the sick") is cut. Paragraphs are a few sentences each at most (OR 2026-11-13, story explorer notes).
- The rules on writing live in: this page; [owner-rulings](../../docs/owner-rulings.md) ("Writing" and the dated
  entries); [style-core](text/style-core.md), the checklist, and the [style guide](text/style-guide.md) behind it,
  both brought from the first repo; [culture-registers](text/culture-registers.md); dialogue-write's `bans.yaml`
  and its linter; the story-line skill's bar and `buildable.md`; quest-write's principles; the ceiling in
  [quest-form](../../docs/design/quest-form.md); and decision 0032 on grounding in the lore.
- "My friend" at most once or twice a person; friendly address only where standing warrants it (walk 3).
- Speakers and players may be any of the ten races; address and manner vary by player race, faction and rank.

## How the story is made

- The overall story of the main quest and of each major faction is written by Fable, knowing the quest rules and
  the ceiling so that nothing undeliverable is written (OR 2026-11-12).
- The story is read on paper by fresh readers, quest by quest, before quests are written; quests are played on
  paper by fresh players as they are written and once built. Every round gathers all readers' findings and answers
  them in one coherent set of changes; readers' personas are honest and a little hard to please, not harsh
  (OR 2026-11-12).
- A paper player's finding that a fix clears is folded into the skill that produced the fault (OR 2026-11-12).
- Review findings are weighed by what the player would notice (OR 2026-11-11). Reviews of look are the owner's
  (OR, #43).
