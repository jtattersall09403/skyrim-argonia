## The peoples

**Lilmoth and the Murkmire Coast.** Argonians of rebuilt Lilmoth, its delta villages and estates, with a foreign dock population. A smaller, lower, wholly Argonian town on stilts over the mass grave of 4E 48, rebuilt but not restored, with its Old Imperial quarter left drowned. A merchant council runs it, as it has twice before, and the old rule that unlicensed foreigners stay on the docks is a lapsed law some officials still enforce. Its third Hist is young and feared: a rotating tree-minder's post and a two-tender rule ensure that communion is never held by a single person again. The farms, rice estates, ferries and villages of the delta and the Althii islands feed it and trade through it; Truowa, a city up the Nushara waters, belongs to its trade. Lilmoth was the An-Xileel's seat and their weapon killed it, so it is commercially strong and openly hostile to the successor state. They hold 16 places (Althii Archipelago 11, Tulmek Washes 3, Nushara Bayou 1 and Lake Ulaish 1), about 297 people in all. <!-- culture:rose; place:culture=rose -->

**Soulrest and the Topal Coast.** Argonians, Khajiit, Imperial-descended administrators and Bosmer seafarers of Soulrest, Cape Percar and the Topal shore. Soulrest was the Third Era capital and is now an administrative city whose Imperial government quarter is half empty. Its people are the most mixed in the province. A merchant council, an ex-Imperial civil apparatus with no sovereign and the river interests contest it, and the mine-burning tribes of the interior hold the hatching grounds its Argonians need. Its villages, logging camps and quarries along Cape Percar work timber and stone for the merchant council, paid for in food and protection, and Mir-Xal and Chasepoint carry the coastal trade. Whoever holds Bramman's river, the hidden channel from Topal Bay to the Blackrose lakes, holds the province's smuggling. Outsiders are welcome and are watched. They hold 16 places (Cape Percar 13, Tulmek Washes 2 and Banujha Basin 1), about 248 people in all. <!-- culture:umber; place:culture=umber -->

**The Agaceph.** Agaceph Argonians of Middle Argonia. Needle-faced Argonians whose scales come in a rainbow of ruby, lemon, green, purple, blue and orange. They travel on razor-thin one-person rafts driven by the tail, live in small hamlets on the eastern Okuwun-Rahei waters, and are educated after their fashion: they read, keep books taken from dead missions, and are accomplished illusionists, as most day labourers in Black Marsh are. Yulca is their one town. They are wary of strangers and will freeze an intruder with a spell before deciding what to do with him. They have no use for the Archwardens and little for the cities. They hold 5 places (Okuwun Rahei 4 and Chixathe Mesa 1), about 60 people in all. <!-- culture:lime; place:culture=lime -->

**The Archon Harbour Folk.** Amphibious Argonians of Archon and its pilots, wharfingers and ferrymen. A port people who work the water in three dimensions: children play underwater games, guar are kept that breathe underwater, and cargo is moved as often under the surface as over it. Archon is the province's principal legal port, run by a harbour authority and merchant council in the East Empire Company's old buildings, with the state's customs officers alongside. A charter separates licensed trade from piracy. The town charters the pirates that it cannot defeat. Its ferrymen run the inland crossings that feed the old Archon, Lilmoth, Soulrest and Tear trade line, Lujei Ferry among them. Archon was barely touched by 4E 48 and is the most at ease of the cities with the successor state, but it pays the state as little as it can. They hold 1 place (Nushara Bayou 1), about 322 people in all. <!-- culture:gold; place:culture=gold -->

**The Chime-Keepers (Kothringi-named towns).** Argonians who resettled the Kothringi towns of the interior after 2E 560, with a few mixed families. When the Knahaten Flu killed the Kothringi in 2E 560, Argonians, immune, moved into their empty towns between Helstrom and Blackrose and kept the old names, Korydon, Toklea, Tydeos and the rest, because a name belonged to the place. Over the centuries they took on Kothringi ways the Hist did not forbid: stone footings under stilt houses, chimes of tin and copper hung from every eave, metal-woven rope, shallow-draft sail-and-paddle boats and a quiet regard for Z'en and Dibella beside Sithis. They are craftsmen and traders, better off than their neighbours, and their towns are the most orderly in the interior. The nativists of 4E 48 called them half-foreign, and they do not forget it; they deal with the Archwardens coldly and with Gideon warmly. They hold 9 places (Tulmek Washes 3, Ultherus Swamp 2, Aunike Ridge 2, Murkwood 1 and Banujha Basin 1), about 169 people in all. <!-- culture:oxblood; place:culture=oxblood -->

**The Clay-Mound People of Aroxhu (Oxhu-Teeleel).** Argonians of the eastern Okuwun-Rahei mound country. The most populous tribal country of the east interior, built on raised mounds of red clay above the flood, with Aroxhu as its great mound-town. They farm the mound slopes, fire pottery and trade clay and dye down the rivers to Archon and Thorn. A council of village elders meets at Aroxhu under its Hist and speaks for the whole confederacy, so the Archwardens must bargain with it. They avoid the Agaceph and the Paatru and fight with them often, without lasting grudges, as Argonian tribes do. They hold 11 places (Okuwun Rahei 7, Hatandros Bight 1, Nushara Bayou 1, Lake Ulaish 1 and Chixathe Mesa 1), about 180 people in all. <!-- culture:rust; place:culture=rust -->

**The Gideon Frontier.** Lukiul Argonians and Nibenese-descended families of Gideon and the Blackwood border. The most Cyrodiilic country in Argonia: majority lukiul Argonians, assimilated to Imperial habits, beside Nibenese families two centuries settled. Gideon keeps written records, a working courthouse and a civic council in the old Governor's Mansion; its villages trade along the Blackwood Road to Leyawiin when the monsoon allows, hunt, deal in herbs and red-clay pottery. Dibella is worshipped here, a devotion the region took from the Kothringi. The An-Xileel's weapon killed Gideon's lukiul majority in 4E 48. The town openly denies the successor state's legitimacy. The ruin of Castle Giovesse and the old customs post at Slough Point belong to this frontier. They hold 8 places (Kotherra Mangroves 4, Nha Enasa 2, Barsaebica 1 and Blackwood 1), about 321 people in all. <!-- culture:pale-green; place:culture=pale-green -->

**The Hutan-Tzel.** The Hutan-Tzel tribe of the Panther River, north-west of Helstrom. A tribe of the upper Panther River and the Murkwood edge, governed by an elder styled naheesh. They mark their ground with totems carved in runes only they can read; meddling with one draws dangerous beasts. They keep no towns, only fortified outposts along the river, and are cautiously willing to bargain with outsiders who come openly. They sit between Helstrom's Root-Folk and the Gideon country and are often go-betweens for both. They hold 4 places (Blackwood 2 and Murkwood 2), about 75 people in all. <!-- culture:magenta; place:culture=magenta -->

**The Island of Blackrose.** Argonians and the mixed descendants of prison staff, ex-prisoners, deserters and refugees of Blackrose and its lake villages. A lake-island city and its ring of stilt villages at the meeting of three rivers, the hinge of the south through which everything moving between Murkwood, Oliis Bay and Bramman's river passes. The city is governed by whoever holds the island core. The government is narrow, defensive and self-perpetuating and it claims legitimacy by possession. The villages around the lake pay it for protection and toll their own channels. The city's Hist is drowned in the lake and reached only by boat and dive, so the island core controls hatching and naming. Its people are the oddest mix in the province and trust no institution; the Rose, a day's march off and not theirs, is their standing problem. They hold 10 places (Tulmek Washes 10), about 96 people in all. <!-- culture:olive; place:culture=olive -->

**The Mine-Burners of the Banujha (Banu-Jaleel).** Interior Argonians of the Banujha Basin and Cape Percar hinterland of Soulrest. The tribes of the basin behind Soulrest who burned the Imperial mines and timber concessions in 3E 427 and have never been reconciled to the coast. They live in fortified towns, Jarecai and Kharaxos (a name left from older maps that they never bothered to change), and watch posts along the basin's edges. Their Hist is the nearest to Soulrest, so the port's Argonians must come into their country to hatch, name and bury, which gives them a hold over the city that they use. They are one side of Soulrest's struggle for regional hegemony and despise the ex-Imperial administrators on the other side. They hold 7 places (Banujha Basin 6 and Cape Percar 1), about 122 people in all. <!-- culture:steel-blue; place:culture=steel-blue -->

**The Naka-Desh (Riverbacks).** Naka-Desh Argonians of the Blackwood and Kotherra rivers. They fish the black rivers south and east of Gideon with osheeja gars and keep small river stations and no towns. They are taken for secretive but are simply incurious, with one exception: they love riddles, and a stranger who trades a good one is let pass. They were close friends of the Kothringi and still hang chimes in their memory. They ignore the Archwardens, deal with Gideon only for salt and iron, and are rumoured to be the Blackwood tribe that says Gideon's Hist cutting was taken from them. They hold 5 places (Nha Enasa 2, Cape Percar 1, Kotherra Mangroves 1 and Banujha Basin 1), about 50 people in all. <!-- culture:indigo; place:culture=indigo -->

**The Root-Folk of Helstrom.** Saxhleel of the Middle Argonian Hist forest, under Helstrom's trees. The heartland people of the great Hist forest, the most traditional Argonians in the province. They build nothing in stone, live on stilts and in root-hollows, and move by water; their small outposts are hunting, sap-gathering and egg-tending stations spread through Murkwood and the Lake Ulaish country. Helstrom, never taken by any invader, is their centre and the great Root Talk convocation, the occasional gathering at which the Hist speak to all the tribes, is held there. They accept the Archwardens' seat at Helstrom as a fact and do not obey it. They say the trees decide what matters. Outsiders who come uninvited into the interior do not come back. **The Sedge-Wardens of Jal-Veesa (Veesa-Teeleel).** Northern Argonians of the Valley of Storms and Barsaebica. They keep the sedge beds and fish weirs that feed Stormhold, and they guard the Ayleid ruins in their country, which they hold to be sick ground, against looters. Jal-Veesa, their one town, is a market of reed boats, mats and dried fish beside a Hist pool. They trade daily with Stormhold's Dunmer and Argonians and send men to the frontier screen for pay, but keep their own elders and refuse the Vicecanons' courts. **The Shadowfen Border (Stormhold).** Argonians and Dunmer of Stormhold and the stone-causeway farmsteads. Stormhold's culture is the province's oldest mixed one: Argonians beside the largest and oldest Dunmer minority in Argonia, descended from a colonial population. Its people build stone causeways and farmsteads on raised ground, are governed by Vicecanons, the civil magistrate office the city invented, and hold the river customs and the state's working offices on the Morrowind border. The farms, outposts and hamlets in this style, Surkina, Tenmal and those further south such as Julo, Luntoc and Yalugo, are smallholdings in the Stormhold manner, and a few distant farmsteads and the Seafalls lighthouse follow the same habit of raised stone. They hold 11 places (Valley of Storms 6, Lake Ulaish 3 and Barsaebica 2), about 220 people in all. <!-- culture:stone-grey; place:culture=stone-grey -->

**The Spray-Folk of Seafalls.** Coastal Argonians of Seafalls, beyond Hist reach. A single cliff-and-surf village on the eastern coast north of Archon, under the falls where a river drops to the sea. Its people live too far from any tree to hear the Hist clearly, and like the Tide-Born of Solstice they hold that the sea carries the memories the Hist let fall: they nest on the shoals, leave votives in the spray and keep death-dancers for their burials. **The Stone-Sleepers of Tsonac (Tsona-Kaleel).** Argonians of the Arnesian jungle uplands north and west of Thorn. A jungle people of the uplands between Thorn and Middle Argonia who, unusually for Argonians, still build in stone, over old Velothi and Ayleid foundations in the Arnesian country. Tsonac, their city, is terraced stone and timber around a Hist they say is asleep, and they live by its dreams, as read by a tree-minder. They hunt and quarry, sell stone and timber to Thorn, and fear and despise the old slavers of the forts. They treat the Archwardens as one more power to be outlasted. **The Three Waters Tribe.** Argonians of the Nushara Bayou between Archon and Lilmoth. They hold 6 places (Nushara Bayou 5 and Althii Archipelago 1), about 52 people in all. <!-- culture:plum; place:culture=plum -->

**The Warden Marches.** Argonian garrisons of the Archwardens' frontier screen and the ex-Archein families who keep the old Imperial forts; the Blackguards' heirs at the Rose. The stone forts, keeps and posts the Empire built are the only dry, walled ground in much of the marsh, and in 4E 201 they are held by Argonians, not Imperials. ## Where the data is silent

What a story writer will want and the database does not hold. These are for the lead to research or rule; the digest never fills them.

- Which people a place belongs to: 93 places carry no culture (Benthic Yobesh 9, Althii Archipelago 8, Chixathe Mesa 8, Barsaebica 7, Nha Enasa 6, Okuwun Rahei 5, Hatandros Bight 5, Skeith Isle 5, Blackwood 4, Tulmek Washes 4, Valley of Storms 4, Murkwood 4, Banujha Basin 4, Aphon Isle 3, Kotherra Mangroves 3, Nushara Bayou 3, Padomaic Ocean 3, Aunike Ridge 2, Arnesian Jungle 2, Topal Bay 1, Ultherus Swamp 1, Lake Ulaish 1, Sea of Mists 1). Most are ruins, wrecks and camps, but some are lived in (Chalkmoth Pit, Dry-Fang Camp, Fort Swiftmoth, Hanaxi Roost, Hiss-Bank Camp, Hollow Tide Camp, Ixha-Neeth Camp, Kalaxo Cut, Karsh-Naal Camp, Kelp-Mouth Cave, Marque Landing and Mud-Croak Camp and others).
- Which culture holds a region: the data does not say for Chixathe Mesa, Hatandros Bight, Lake Ulaish and Skeith Isle.
- Who rules a region: there is no ruler column for a region or a culture; a place's holder is a line of free text (held_by), and nothing joins the holders of several places into one authority.
- The names of rivers and lakes: of 1770 bodies of water in the regions, 0 carry a name; a river is named only where a region's or a place's notes name it.
- What a culture believes: its row says nothing of belief for Soulrest and the Topal Coast, The Agaceph, The Archon Harbour Folk, The Hutan-Tzel, The Shadowfen Border (Stormhold) and The Three Waters Tribe (the places' faith notes say what each place keeps).
- Named people: the person table holds 58 people, all of Helstrom; every other place's people are numbers only. Faction memberships (0 rows) and ties between people (0 rows) are empty.
- Goods: what places make, trade and need is a table of 7 rows (one place's market); the rest lives only in the economy notes.
- Uniques: the world's unique things are a table of 0 rows; two places promise a unique item in their gives.
- The make-up of a source place: the mining counts buildings, interiors, residents and hostiles, and names its piece families, but how many of each kind of building (houses, halls, piers, shrines) it holds: the data does not say.
- Rows marked for deletion: 1 give read '(delete entry)' and are left out here (Tzel-Haar, shrine blessing).
- How many live in a place: for 135 places the population was reset from the research's estimate to the residents of the place's source, so the number in a place row can differ from the number its people note gives.
- How many live in Helstrom: the place row says 17 (its source's residents), the person table names 58.
- Built places: only Helstrom is drafted; every other place is a record and a source, not yet ground a quest can be checked against.
