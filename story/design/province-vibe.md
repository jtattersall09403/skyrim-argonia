# The province vibe: Black Marsh in 4E 201

Every quest, person and line refers back to this page, adds to it, and must not jar against it. Skyrim's vibe
was Nordic, martial and split by a civil war. Ours is below. Each line cites its source in the lore
(`story/lore/`). Tags follow the lore's own: **C** is canon (`CANON_EXPLICIT` or `CANON_DERIVED`), **X** is
extrapolated (`EXTRAPOLATED`, or an owner decision recorded there). Short names used below: *state* is
`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`, *register* is `extrapolation/settlement-register.md`, *labour* is
`topics/labour-and-bondage.md`, *culture* is `topics/material-culture.md`, *guilds* is
`topics/guilds-and-orders.md`, *faith* is `topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md`, *frame* is
`../design/quests/10-political-frame.md`. A claim that is not on this page and not in the lore is a gap. It goes
to `extrapolation/gap-register.md`; it is never assumed.

## 1. In one paragraph

Black Marsh in 4E 201 is quiet, inward-turned, poor and very dangerous. For a century and a half it has been
recovering from a disaster it brought on itself (X, state §0). It is a patchwork of tribes, river-ports and city
polities that share a language, a religion of change, and the memory of being purged by their own government.
A thin state holds them loosely, and its reach "ends where the water gets deep" (X, state §0, §1). The people do
not think of it as one nation, and have no word for it (C, `black-marsh-province.md`). The mood is **melancholy
and dignified, with horror kept local** (X, owner Q5, state §9). Skyrim marches and shouts. Black Marsh waits,
remembers, lets go, and survives.

## 2. The tensions of the time

- **A state nobody quite believes in.** The An-Xileel successor administration (Archwardens, the Organism) has
  its predecessor's offices but not its mandate. Gideon and Lilmoth deny it openly, because its predecessor's
  weapon killed them (X, state §1; frame §5). Who says "An-Xileel" aloud, and in what tone, places a speaker
  politically (X, state §1).
- **The wound of 4E 48, at the distance of a century and a half.** No one alive saw Umbriel. People hold family
  stories, carved grave-stakes and memorials they argue over. History here can bore the young (X, state §9).
- **Holding on against letting go.** The Nisswo teach *shunatei*, the refusal to cling to what has passed.
  That teaching argues with the province's memorials, its grudges and its debts, and both sides are argued
  honestly (C/X, faith; state §9; labour §4).
- **The Owing.** There are no courts above the tribe except Gideon's working courthouse and the
  Vicecanons' magistracy in the northern cities, and the only prisons are ruins, the Rose among them, so wrongs
  are paid in owed labour (X, labour §1, §3; state §2.2, §4).
  The Owing is humane where it works. Elsewhere it has drifted into bondage that can be sold, extended and inherited, mostly under
  the ex-Archein families. It is not universal (X, labour §2–3; frame §5).
- **Lukiul against Wild Ones.** Assimilated, mixed and urban Argonians argue with the un-assimilated interior
  over what being Argonian means (C/X, `an-xileel.md`; guilds §5; frame §5).
- **A judiciary dying out.** The last known Shadowscale dies in 4E 201. The Nisswo partly fill the gap but by
  doctrine cannot standardise anything (C/X, state §2.4).

## 3. Who holds power, and how

- **The tribes govern most of the province** (X, state §2.1, the strongest the tribal layer has been since before
  the Second Empire). A village has its offices: the tree-minder (tends the Hist, usually the default
  arbitrator), sap-speaker, root-herald, kaal or raj-kaal, naheesh (elders, respected but **not in power**),
  deelith, grave-singer, egg-tender, chime-maker (C, culture "Society").
- **The Hist** has an authority the state cannot replace: eggs, names, souls, form and burial all run through it
  (C/X, state §2.2). There is no Hist government. There is an occasion, the great Root Talk at Helstrom in
  Hist-Tsoko, which issues no orders (X, owner Q2).
- **Clutches** are the egg and the uxith, tended by egg-tenders. The Keepers of the Shell at the Hatching Pools
  are the north's most defended institution (C, `topics/hist-and-sap.md`; guilds §6).
- **Cities** keep old offices with new occupants: Vicecanons in the north, merchant councils at Lilmoth and
  Archon, a captain of the port at Alten Corimont, a civic council in Gideon's old mansion (C/X, state §2.2, §4).
- **Force** is small companies of shellback levies, a frontier screen of tribal irregulars and no navy. The
  deterrent is guerrilla skill, and no one believes an invasion would work (C/X, state §2.3).
- **Rural power is the crossing.** Where a route crosses water and cannot be bypassed, somebody owns it and
  takes a toll (C/X, state §3, §6).
- **Alliances are bonding ceremonies, not treaties.** Feuds between tribes are frequent, violent and soon
  forgiven. A grudge against collaborators and outsiders is cold and long (C, `tribes.md` "Tribal politics").

## 4. How people live and what they do

- **Subsistence, not surplus.** Cash-cropping fails in this soil (C, state §6). People fish, hunt, gather herbs
  and reeds, dig clay, cut building timber, take sap and salt, and make tools (X, state §6). They move small
  loads by raft and rootworm (C/X, state §6).
- **Real jobs**: tree-minder, egg-tender, grave-singer, root-herald, kaal, deelith, hunter, fisher (the Naka-Desh's
  osheeja gars in Blackwood, `regions/middle-argonia.md`), raft-poler, potter (Onkobra red clay), bone-worker, woodcarver, weaver, chime-maker, healer and
  alchemist, boardwalk-cutter and channel-dredger (the work an Owing pays in), crossing-keeper (C/X, culture;
  `tribes.md`; labour §2; state §6). In the cities and ports add pilot, wharfinger, dock hand, sailor, merchant,
  Vicecanon, and customs officer at Archon and Stormhold (X, state §1, §4).
- **Magic is folk literacy, not a profession.** A mage here is a neighbour with a knack, a Nisswo, a sap-speaker
  or a tree-minder, never a robed academic. Foreign mages exist only where foreign institutions do (C/X,
  `topics/magic-practice.md` §2).
- **Building is made to be replaced.** Villages move, which is why a map is never quite right. Shadowfen builds
  in mud, Murkmire on stilts, the interior in trained root. Stone is a moral error, except for xanmeers, which
  tribes keep as warnings (C/X, culture "Build implications" and "three kits"; state §3).
- **A settlement**: a Hist, an uxith, the offices above, grave-stakes in the mud, totems of wood, mud and plumage,
  chimes at the thresholds in the interior, all of it built to be replaced (X, state §3; culture).
- **The year** runs by the Jel months of the Hist's life. Nushmeeko is the whole tribe's hard work; Hist-Tsoko
  brings the Root Talk; Xulomaht mourns the year (C, culture calendar). Cyrodilic month names belong to
  foreigners and written records (C, `topics/sky-moons-calendar.md`).

## 5. How information, law, trade and debt move

- **News and dealings are spoken and carried by people.** The root-herald gathers news and rumour, keeps the
  relationships and negotiates trade, "closer to ambassador than spy" (C, culture "Society"). The travelling
  Nisswo are welcome "in almost every village" (C/X, state §2.4). Entertainers travel in Thtithil-Gah (C, culture
  calendar). Raft traffic carries the rest (X, state §6).
- **Memory is recited and carved.** The naheesh carry the Root Talk and the naming secrets, and recite Saxhleel
  history in Hist-Tsoko (C, culture). A grave-stake is carved with the dead one's whole story. The grave-singer
  reads the stakes, and family accounts of 4E 48 can be read off them (C, faith; X, state §9). Hutan-Tzel's totems
  carry runes only that tribe knows (C, `tribes.md`).
- **Literacy is uneven, and where you find writing tells you whose ground you are on.** Canon shows Argonians who
  read (the Agaceph looted a monastery's books, `tribes.md`). But Jel is a spoken language "inseparable from body
  language" (C, culture), and the province's records are where its foreign and civic habits are: Gideon, the
  "records city" with a working courthouse (X, state §4; guilds §4); Archon, "where the paperwork is" (X, state §4);
  the state's customs regime at the recognised ports, chiefly Archon and Stormhold (X, state §1, §4). Tribal ground is spoken-first: there a written document is a foreign object, and the Agaceph's books and
  Hutan-Tzel's runes are the exceptions.
- **Law is arbitration, witnessed and then forgotten.** Disputes go to the tree-minder or to an arbitrator both
  sides choose (C, `regions/murkmire.md`; culture). The award is an apology and months of work, counted in
  nushmeekos. When the work is done, by custom nobody may mention it again (X, labour §2). The old ku-vastei
  arbiters are almost gone (C/X, state §2.4).
- **Trade has three tiers.** Septims in the cities, ports, roads and border; barter in tools, salt, sap, hide,
  craft and owed labour in the villages, where a root-herald negotiates reciprocity, not price; nothing in the
  deep interior, where value is food, tools, safe passage and information (X, register §7; that no Argonian
  mint exists is project-original).
- **Debt is owed work.** Where the Owing has drifted, it is assessed by those who hold the crossing, the estate or
  the hiring office. The ledgers are the ex-Archein families', the ones who "changed only their letterhead". In Gideon an
  Owing has a filing number (X, labour §2–3). **The ledger is the collaborator's tool**, and that is its meaning
  when it appears.

## 6. Faith and death

Argonians revere the Hist ("their servitude is voluntary") and Sithis as change, serene, plural and public, not
as evil (C/X, faith). The Nisswo travel, have no central governance and no single doctrine, make art and destroy
it, and teach letting go (C, faith). Souls return to the Hist. A Hist drained or a body far from its tree can leave
a soul with nowhere to go (C, faith; labour §2). The dead are staked into the mud so the floods cannot lift them.
A pulled stake makes a bog blight (C, faith). The washed-out look of someone kept long from their Hist is visible
(C/X, labour §2).

## 7. Violence and danger

Danger is stratified by species, not level. The marsh kills non-Argonians as terrain: insects, water, rot,
disease. It does not kill Argonians that way (C, state §7). The layers come in order: attrition, then terrain and
season (monsoon closures, tides, peat-gas), then people (tolls, tribal ground, tribeless Naga brigands, Sul-Xan
raiders, Blackguards, the Naga-Kur, the guards of Helstrom and Thorn, who are themselves the hazard), then set-piece creatures (X,
state §7). Horror lives in named places: Veeskhleel-Tzel, Mazzatun, Deepmire, the Rose, Stillrise, Lilmoth's
drowned quarter (X, state §9).

## 8. The outsiders' fringe

- **Imperials**: no official presence, but Imperial people everywhere. Nibenese families have lived in Gideon two
  centuries, and there may be at most a trade factor at Archon (C/X, state §4, §5). Gideon keeps Cyrodiilic legal
  habits, and the Cyrodilic Collections revive there (X, guilds §4).
- **Dunmer**: the largest minority at Stormhold, and large at Thorn and Archon. The relationship across the Tear
  road is unsentimental trade and an old grievance nobody acts on (C/X, state §4, §5).
- **Khajiit** at Soulrest and on the Leyawiin road (X, state §4–5).
- **Pirates**: on the Archon coast, ex-privateers "with paperwork", chartered by a shipless state. Bramman's heirs
  on the mangrove river are genuinely outlaw. Alten Corimont takes anyone, with a price list (C/X, state §4–5).
- Every foreign institution is extinct, absent or working through intermediaries (X, guilds §7).

## 9. What jars (only where the lore and the vibe put them)

These motifs are not banned. Each belongs where the lore puts it and jars anywhere else. A quest that uses one
names its place and its source.

Also jarring: a Nordic element (decision 0006); a professional standing army or navy; the Thalmor or the Empire
in plain sight, beyond the Cyrodilic Collections and at most a trade factor at Archon (state §5; guilds §4); a quest with more than a third of its stages hollow, or whose central action is delivering, filing, counting or
reading a record (quest-write §6b.3).

## 10. What sings

- A grave-stake read aloud. A pulled stake and its bog blight.
- A crossing-keeper's toll, and the question of who owns the crossing.
- An Owing assessed, worked off or bought out, with its washed-out debtor and both sides argued honestly.
- An arbitration that both parties chose, and that ends with nobody mentioning it again.
- A root-herald's bargain in reciprocity, a bonding ceremony, or a cancelled one, which is a rupture.
- A Nisswo telling a grieving village to let go, and being right and wrong at once.
- The Hist: a dead clutch, a tree-minder who feels something is wrong, a Root Talk, sap smuggled out.
- The marsh as the enemy of outsiders and the home of Argonians; travel by water, tide and season.
- Feuds between tribes that are fierce and soon over. Cold, long contempt for collaborators.
- A state whose name people avoid saying. A memorial that no two people read the same way.
- Coin that stops working the further in the player goes.
