# Many-Root: the line's story

**Status: to be rewritten.** This story rests on facts of the main quest's earlier plot that [the plot](main-plot.md) no longer holds; it is to be rewritten to the plot and is not yet. What in it is untrue, by section:

- Section 1 (The faction in a page): the walkers never connect Helstrom's sealed spring or any sick tree to a tree the main quest tends, but the plot has the Unbound cutting roots and the trees joined at the root, so one cut tree sickens the rest.
- Section 4 (What each quest needs of its places): it keeps the Sealed Spring urn at the foot of Helstrom's sacred tree, but the plot's Helstrom has no spring and the urn was made for a quest the plot no longer holds.
- Section 5 (Read on paper): its rounds leave the sealed spring unexplained as the main quest's alone, but the plot has no spring at Helstrom.
- Section 6 (For the other lines): it says the hall never explains the sour spring, never names Vel-Sapha and never says one tree's sickness reaches another, but the plot has no sour spring, uses Vel-Sapha Stand for its third quest and holds that a cut tree sickens its neighbours.

The story of Many-Root's six quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the [province plan](province-plan.md) gives the line (its sections 3.4, 4 and 7) and under the rulings that
make fourteen stories one province ([consistency](consistency.md), section 8, "Many-Root"). A quest writer turns
each outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world (`story/design/world/`): "D ultherus-swamp, Korydon"
is that place's record or hooks in that region's file, "D people" the table of named people, "D province, Peoples" a
section of the province file. Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation
from the cited data and lore (decision 0032). Helstrom is drafted and is not redrafted: this story is fitted to the
town as it stands ([helstrom-needs](../review/helstrom-needs.md); consistency H6 to H8). The other places are not
built yet: an outline names a place by what its record says it is, and anything else it relies on is a row of
section 4. The hooks in the places' notes are raw material; where this story changes one, section 7 says so.

**Words used below.** *Hist*: the sentient trees every Argonian is hatched under and returns to at death. *Sap*: a
Hist's blood. A sip a minder gives is safe to anyone; raw sap drawn from the root is madness or death to anyone not
hatched under that tree (consistency T9). *Tree-minder*: the tender of one village's tree. *The hall*: the
Tree-Minders' Hall at Helstrom, which is what everyone calls Many-Root. *Walker*: a minder of the hall who goes out
to other people's trees. *The Listener*: the eldest walker, who sends the others. *Naheesh*: a tribe's elders.
*Root Talk*: the gathering at Helstrom at which the tribes hear the trees; it is late this year. *The Owing*: the
native sentence, months of owed work. *Danger bands*: Safe, Settled, Wild, Dangerous, Deadly, Lethal, fixed in the
world (D province, Danger). *Tumnatu*: a small Argonian people of swamp hunters who keep no tree of their own in
this country (D ultherus-swamp, Tumnatu Cattail Camp). *Mine-Burners*: the tribes of the Banujha Basin in the
south-west, whose one great tree stands at Jarecai (D province, Peoples: Mine-Burners).

## 1. The faction in a page

**The thing they tend.** A village's tree is its eggs, its names and its dead. A Hist's sap is its soul and becomes
the blood of everyone hatched under it (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:29`); an egg whose tree fails it "sinks into the
roots and fades away" (`:78`); a person held far from their tree fades, and comes back to colour only at home
(`:63`). So a sick tree is a whole people in danger, and in 4E 201 the data holds sick trees from one coast to the
other: Tsonac's dreams have turned bad, the sap-jar at Yuva-Goli has gone cloudy, Meris-Keth's tree died and emptied
its village, and sap is slashed and stolen within a morning's boat of Helstrom (D arnesian-jungle, Tsonac; D
hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli; D banujha-basin, Meris-Keth; D murkwood, Tseli-Oon).

**Who they are.** A village tree-minder tends one tree for life, has never seen another sicken, and loves her own
too well to taste it honestly (X). The walkers are the few minders of Helstrom's hall who go out: "the minders who
stay between convocations and carry what the trees said out to the tribes" ([province-plan](province-plan.md)
§3.4). Many-Root is their name for their one teaching: a tree has many roots and no two drink the same water, so a
sickness is always in one root before it is in the tree, and a walker's craft is to find which. Their hall is the
cave by the waterfalls at Helstrom's north-west edge, whose door shows "Tree-Minders' Hall" (helstrom-needs; D
people, Taleeka-Ma). They are six or seven, and old; the walking kills the young ones (X).

**How they work, in the line's own skills.** A walker is a healer and a tracker of a living thing.

- **Alchemy** first. The sap is tasted where it beads on each root, and what is in it is named: a poison, salt,
  another tree's sap. The cure is a draught or a salve worked from what that country gives and carried to the
  wound.
- **Restoration** second: the people a sick tree has harmed are eased before they are asked anything, and the
  unquiet dead of a dead tree are turned.
- **Archery** third, with Sneak beside it, for the country between. A root surfaces where it likes: on a hummock of
  giant spiders, under a city, in a camp of raiders. Sap draws predators (D murkwood, Tseli-Oon, dangers), so
  something is usually sitting on the wound. The beasts and bands stand at the world's fixed levels, and a walker
  goes past them more often than through them.
- **Speech** for the hand at the end of the trail, who always has a reason; and **Alteration** where the Stone-
  Sleepers' tree lies under stone (D arnesian-jungle, Tsonac).

The faction suits an alchemist and healer first, a hunter or a sneak second and a talker third; every quest can be
finished by any of the three. Nothing is levelled (decision 0035).

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **The hands.** Every sick tree in this line was made sick by somebody, and none of them is a villain: a hunting
  people tapping a root they tapped before the town was built, a keeper drinking from the jar that keeps him far
  from home, a mason paid to cut where his elders had cut, a gatherer selling sap because the hall releases too
  little to live on. This is true of the trees the walkers are sent to and is never said of Helstrom's own or of
  any tree the main quest tends (consistency T14).
- **The sap crews.** Sap is "dear in Helstrom, because the tree-minders allow little to be taken" (D people,
  Wujeel), and smuggling it is the most natural crime in the province (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:167` to `:170`).
  Raw, in anyone not hatched under the tree it was drawn from, it brings a killing rage (`:37`; consistency T9).
  Outlaws buy it on the east coast, tribeless Naga steal it on the river north of Helstrom, and Khajiit smugglers
  cook it with moon sugar in a dead village in the south-west (D hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli; D murkwood, Dry-Fang
  Camp; D banujha-basin, Meris-Keth; X for the road that joins them). Their case is the Naga leader's: no tree
  claims her band, the hall would let nine of them starve sooner than release a skin, and she wants only the price
  of a boat.
- **The village minders**, who have reason to doubt the hall. A walker arrives as a stranger, tastes a tree that is
  not hers and tells its people what to think. The Listener "stood one day under Meris-Keth's tree and went home"
  (X), and the interior knows it.
- **The League of Open Water** is not an enemy and is refused: the hall holds that no tree is cut for another
  ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §4, "A tree for a city"). Why the hall holds it is the line's turn. Taleeka-Ma
  greets a player who holds a rank in the League with one cold line: "You carry the League's water on you. We heal
  here. We do not sell cuttings, and you may tell Soulrest so" (consistency C31; a greeting gated on League rank).
- **The Wardens' boat people.** The minders say the tally-keeper's lodgers in the house by the city landing ask
  what the walkers hear, and are told the price of fish ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §4; consistency H7).
  The hall knows nothing more of them.

**What it pays, what each rank opens and what joining costs.** The hall is poor in coin and rich in what a healer
wants. Its gifts are fixed and differ with how each job was ended (the outcome tables in sections 2 and 3). The
ranks open things in the hall:

| Rank | Earned | What it opens that the player can go and use |
|---|---|---|
| Root-Hand | taking the first job (MR01) | the **Walkers' Chest** in the hall is the player's to take from: salves, a few draughts and gathered herbs, fixed |
| Walker | MR03 ended | Okeeh-Ja gives two measures of released sap (not where the stone was sold), and the next road |
| Walking Minder | MR05 ended | a skill book of Restoration from Taleeka-Ma; the hall's stilling draught can be had from Okeeh-Ja for the asking, once |
| Named Walker | one ending of MR06 only | the **Listener's Chest** in the hall and his teaching (section 2, MR06) |

The cost: a walker is a stranger in every grove and is blamed for every tree that dies after she has tasted it; the
sap crews learn her face; and the Listener will ask her to tell grieving people something other than the truth.
The player never becomes the Listener: the line ends with the player as the one who knows (consistency S15).

**What it lies about.** The hall says a walker tells every village the truth of its tree. Inside the hall the
teaching is Okeeh-Ja's: "Tell them what lets them sleep." Under that lies what he has never told anyone, and one
thing he does not know himself (section 2).

**What the hall will not say.** Helstrom's own spring is sealed: a lidded urn at the foot of the sacred tree, which
the game names "Sealed Spring" ([main](main.md) §6; consistency H7). The walkers, who track every other tree's
sickness, do not speak of their own tree's. Asked, Okeeh-Ja says only, "That is not asked in this hall." Nobody in
this line explains it, guesses at it or connects it to any tree the player tends: the cause is the main quest's
alone ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §3.4, "A boundary"). What the player sees is the silence, and a hall that
has begun sending walkers to every sick tree but its own. At the line's end Okeeh-Ja says the one thing the hall
can honestly say: "I have read every sick tree in the marsh but my own, and I cannot read it. Whoever can is not in
this hall."

**Why a player wants in.** Each job is a trail: taste, follow, find the hand. The trail always leaves the village
for somewhere dangerous, something is always sitting on the wound, the hand always has a face, and the cure is the
player's to give, withhold or put a price on. The patron is worth unpicking, and what the player carries home is
never only a report.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X throughout, on the cited lore), in the order it happened.**

1. A cutting of a Hist can be planted and will grow (`topics/hist-placement.md:93`, `:139`). It costs the tree it
   is taken from: the cut is made at the heart-root, and for some years after, fewer of the mother tree's eggs
   turn. A tree asked for a cutting gives it; a tree does not count its own eggs. It is the people under it who
   bury the shells (consistency T10).
2. When Okeeh-Ja was a boy of the hall, Gideon had no tree. In a hungry year the elders of the river folk, the
   Naka-Desh, sold a cutting of their tree to Gideon's council for salt and iron, the two things they still take
   from the city (D province, Peoples: Naka-Desh). A walker of Helstrom made the cut, because nobody else knew how,
   and his apprentice carried the basket. The apprentice was Okeeh-Ja. For twelve wet seasons after, the river
   folk's clutches failed. The elders who sold never told their people what they had done. They left a riddle
   instead, and the tribe has remembered a theft ever since; Gideon's families remember a gift and quarrel over
   whose (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon).
3. The hall made a rule of its shame, "no tree is cut for another", and stopped saying where the rule came from.
4. In Okeeh-Ja's middle years, thirty years ago, the tree of Meris-Keth, a Mine-Burner fishing village, sickened
   with a rot of the heart-root (D banujha-basin, Meris-Keth: it "sickened within living memory"). He was sent
   for. He tasted it and knew the one cure: a graft from the heart-root of a sound tree of the same water, and the
   only such tree is Jarecai's, under which the whole Mine-Burner people and the port of Soulrest hatch (D
   province, Peoples: Mine-Burners). He carried a grafting knife and a jar of binding-sap into the village. He
   thought of the river folk's twelve seasons and weighed a village against a tribe and a city. He told the village
   there was no cure. To its minder, Aojee-Dar, alone, he told the truth, laid the knife in front of him and said,
   "Then you cut Jarecai's tree, and tell its people why." He went home without the knife, sure that the minder
   would not go either, and he has believed it ever since.
5. Aojee-Dar went. Jarecai's tree-minder of that day heard him out and said, "Cut. A tree says yes, and so will I.
   We will bury the shells." She asked nobody else, and she died within a few seasons without telling her people
   what she had promised. He came home with her word and the Listener's knife, and could not use either: a graft
   cut by a hand that does not know the cut kills both trees, and only a walker knows it. He gave his word for a walker's
   own ear, and for no other, to the last family to leave, since the hall had lied to the village once already.
   The family got as far as Jal-Resh Post, with a clutch to carry, and twice sent north by the salt boats that a
   walker was wanted there. Okeeh-Ja, who led the hall by then, took it for a dead village's reproach, answered
   that no walker goes to the Banujha, and forbade the basin to his walkers, so that none of them would see what
   he had left.
6. The village's eggs stopped hatching. Its families carried their last clutches to Jarecai and did not come back,
   and the dead of that last year were never staked (D banujha-basin, Meris-Keth). Aojee-Dar stayed with his tree,
   carved his own grave-stake with all of it, and died against the root with the knife in his lap, waiting for the
   one man who could have made the cut.
7. Khajiit smugglers took the dry halls, because nobody comes near a dead tree. They cook stolen sap with moon
   sugar there for the coast. The sap comes down from the north: from the Naga of Dry-Fang Camp, who take it from
   the gatherers of Tseli-Oon (D murkwood, Dry-Fang Camp, Tseli-Oon).
8. This year the Root Talk is late. Okeeh-Ja expects that when it is held the naheesh will ask the hall to answer
   for every sick tree in the interior; one of them, Hixee-Ra, has begun asking already. He is sending a stranger
   to read them all first. He forbids one place.

**The arc.** Six quests, each a different kind of job ending in a different kind of act, so that the line is never
the same trail walked six times. Danger climbs by the world's bands and nothing is levelled: the first four can be
finished by a character of modest level who uses the quiet or the knowing way, and the last two are on the
hardest ground in the province and say so.

- **MR01, The Bitter Root** (Korydon; the Tumnatu's hummock). *A poison traced to its root; ends on an act at the
  root.* A town's tree tastes bitter and each half of the town blames the other. The player tastes the sap root by
  root, follows the bitterness out of the town to a tap driven into a far root with a spider sitting on it, heals
  the wound with a salve, and then pulls the tap or leaves it in. Skills: Alchemy, Sneak, Archery or a weapon,
  Speech. Carried on: the rank of Root-Hand; the tap pulled, left or sold, and what each opened and shut; the
  walkers' hitch seen.
- **MR02, The Cloudy Jar** (Yuva-Goli). *A small household with one liar, and a cove; ends on the truth, or what
  lets an old woman sleep.* A sea station far from any tree reads a jar of Helstrom's sap as its counsel, and the
  jar has gone cloudy. The tree is well. One of five keepers has been drinking the jar and thinning it, outlaws in
  the cove have been buying from him, and the elder has been reading rainwater as the voice of the Hist. Skills:
  Alchemy, Restoration, Speech, Lockpicking, Archery or a weapon. Carried on: what the elder was told; the captain
  dead or alive.
- **MR03, What the Sleeper Dreams** (Tsonac). *A descent under a city; ends on a bottle poured or kept.* The
  Stone-Sleepers' tree has been asleep since it was planted and must never be woken; its dreams have turned bad
  since a mason cut too deep under the mound. The player brews the hall's stilling draught, gets below, and at the
  root stills the tree, takes away the light that is waking it, or lets it wake. Skills: Alchemy, Alteration,
  Lockpicking or Pickpocket, Sneak or a weapon. Carried on: the rank of Walker; the tree stilled, settled or
  waking; the knowledge that the hall keeps a draught that can put a tree down.
- **MR04, The Borrowed Tree** (Gideon; Teth-Kasa Station). *The turn: a break-in under a garden, and the walkers'
  own hitch on what is found.* The river folk say Gideon's tree was stolen from them and two city families each
  say it was given to theirs. Okeeh-Ja asks, for the first time, that what is found be brought to him before
  anyone. The tree was sold, the hall cut it, and Okeeh-Ja carried the basket. The player answers the river folk's
  riddle or does not, tells the city or does not, and then faces him. Skills: Sneak, Lockpicking, Alchemy, Speech.
  Carried on: who knows; the player now knows what the hall's rule is made of.
- **MR05, The Sap Road** (Tseli-Oon; Dry-Fang Camp). *A captive in a raiders' camp on Lethal ground; ends on a
  bargain struck or refused.* A gatherer of Tseli-Oon is held for ransom by the Naga of Dry-Fang with the
  station's stolen sap. The player gets her out by stealth, by the leader's own price, which is a boat, or by a
  draught in the ransom, and learns two things: the gatherer was selling to her captors, and the sap goes south
  "to the cats under the dead tree". Okeeh-Ja forbids the trail. Skills: Sneak, Lockpicking, Alchemy, Speech,
  Archery. Carried on: the rank of Walking Minder; the band gone, asleep or still on the river; the gatherer named
  or not; the forbidden lead.
- **MR06, The Tree That Was Left** (Jal-Resh Post; Meris-Keth; then the hall). *The hard place, and then the
  Listener given his own medicine.* The dead village: smugglers in the halls, the unstaked dead on the walkways, a
  leafless tree in its root-hut, and against the root a dead minder with a walker's knife in his lap and his own
  grave-stake across his knees. The stake says one thing the Listener has never known. The player carries it home
  and tells him the truth, or what lets him sleep.

**The turn** is MR04, two-thirds through. Before it the hall is a house of healers with a strict rule; after it
the rule is a scar, the Listener's "tell them what lets them sleep" is a habit of deciding what peoples may know
about their own trees, and what the player told the elder of Yuva-Goli reads differently. He says so himself, from
the stored number: to a player who told her the comfort, "At Yuva-Goli you told her what let her sleep. Now you
know why I do"; to one who told her the truth, "At Yuva-Goli you told her the truth. Tell me this one is no
different." **The misjudgement** is in MR04 as well: before the cellar is opened the player can back either
family's story, each of which is false, and is believed; it takes that family's gift, loses the other's for
good, and does not end the line.

**What carries.** Rank, the journal, stored numbers, who is alive, the things held and what the player knows.
Earlier choices change greetings, single lines and what is given, and never a later quest's stages; only the
finale's choice carries into the world after the line.

### The later quests, at arc level

In these tables a trader gained is a second placement of that person with their stock, switched on from far away
by the ending's stage; nobody's barter is shut by a quest, since the game cannot shut it, and no price changes. A
loss is a thing not given, a person gone, a door locked or a people's leave withdrawn.

**MR03, What the Sleeper Dreams.** *Place*: Tsonac, the Stone-Sleepers' one city, "terraces of squared stone" round
"a central mound with the Hist and a ring of carved stone steps" (D arnesian-jungle, Tsonac). *The truth (X)*: the
grave-singers say the tree is "a sapling of that line", the line of Tsono-Xuhil, the mad Hist "that its neighbours
put to sleep" (D arnesian-jungle, Tsonac, history). A buyer from Thorn, the Dunmer **Golis Andrethi**, who lodges
by the quay, wants an Ayleid welkynd stone "cut from the foundations" and paid a mason, **Utadeek**, to cut for it
under the mound. The cut broke into an Ayleid chamber where a root of the tree hangs through the roof. The elder
masons walled the cut with a door and said nothing; the buyer's three hired diggers went back in from a quarry cut
on the mound's far side and are still there, at the Dangerous band. The welkynd's light on the bare root is half
waking the tree, and the town's hatchlings dream what it dreams. *What the player does*: the tree-minder,
**Druja-Seel**, lets the player take sap at the stone ring, and her line shows the dream as a message: white
light, and something turning over. She adds, "Your Listener offered us the stilling once before, the season after
Meris-Keth emptied. We said our tree slept well enough, and we have let no walker taste it since." Okeeh-Ja has
given the recipe and three words: "Still it. Remember Lilmoth." The draught is a thing given by a stage: the
player works it at Tsonac's sap-seller's bench (Alchemy 40, the gathered parts held) or the sap-seller makes it
from the parts for 100 gold. The way down is the masons' door (Utadeek's key by Pickpocket or Speech; the lock; or
Druja-Seel's own way, a line gated on Alteration whose stage unlocks it for one who knows the stone magic) or the
quarry cut (Sneak past the diggers, or a fight). In the chamber two things can be done, each a stage of its own,
in either order: the welkynd stone, whose glow is its own, is taken from its sconce; the root is used, and the
draught is poured. *The choice*, argued before the player goes down by those it concerns, each saying what they
will give: Druja-Seel wants it stilled and the stone brought up to her to be walled back into the mound ("my whole
people's peace is its sleep; still it, bring me the stone, and I will teach you how we speak to stone; the stone
is not yours to sell"); her pupil **Pojeel**, who keeps the town's tomes, says the tree spoke one word to him
through the light and that a sleeping elder is a jailed one ("leave the stone and pour nothing, and what is on my
shelf is yours; put it back to sleep and I have nothing for you"); Andrethi will pay 500 gold for the stone and
does not care what the tree does. The ending is the line the player then says, tried in this order: to
Druja-Seel, the draught poured and the stone held, which she takes (stilled); to Andrethi, the stone held, which
he buys (sold: the tree settles if nothing was poured, and is stilled if it was, and either way the minder has
nothing for a walker who sold the mound's stone); to Pojeel, at the stage where nothing has been taken or poured
(waking).

## 3. The first two quests

Common to both.

- **Okeeh-Ja** stands inside the Tree-Minders' Hall at Helstrom for the whole line. He must live: his death fails
  any running quest of the line before MR06 with the faction's message. Nobody is essential. People stand where
  they are placed.
- **Working stages.** After its briefings each quest sits at working stages at which the ways through can be
  taken in any order. What the player learns or does on a way is a plain fact (a thing held, a person dead) or a
  stage of its own where the way gives or takes something; the lines that carry the result to the person waiting
  for it set the stages.
- **A thing taken moves a stage only where the table says so.** Every other thing only opens lines for whoever
  holds it, however and whenever it was got.
- **A death moves no stage.** Lines check who is alive. Each job is offered only while its people live.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense
  and say only what the player has just done; what a thing means is carried by its name and by people's lines.
  Thresholds are fixed. Each failed check has its own written refusal and leaves another way open.
- **Things.** Everything uses existing forms. Each sap-bead, jar and cord is a plain thing that cannot be
  eaten or drunk, and carries what the player must know in its name, since nothing else of a plain thing is shown
  in game. A salve or draught the story needs is given by a stage, never brewed at the player's own bench, so the
  form held is always the form checked; MR01's root-salve is no thing at all, only a stage.
- No step depends on the hour.
- **How the hall is found.** Wujeel at his stall has a topic, "sick trees", for anyone, and so has Taleeka-Ma
  inside the hall. Each names Okeeh-Ja and says where he stands: "inside the Tree-Minders' Hall, the cave by the
  waterfalls at the north-west edge of the town" (consistency H6). The door shows the hall's name.
- **How each place is reached.** Cheeval-Xu, at the sacred tree, calls the rootworm (D murkwood, Helstrom); the
  raft-master Poles-Upriver stands at the city landing. Okeeh-Ja names the way at each briefing, and the first
  objective's marker is on the person the player is sent to.

### MR01. The Bitter Root

**What a new player is told, and when.** On first speaking to Okeeh-Ja, before any job, five optional lines, each
two or three sentences. *The trees*: "A Hist is the tree a village is hatched under. Its sap is in their blood and
their dead go back to it. When it sickens, the eggs under it go dull first." *The walkers*: "A village minder
tends one tree all her life and cannot taste it honestly. We go out and taste other people's. A tree has many
roots and a sickness is in one of them first; that is all our craft, and it is why they call this hall Many-Root."
*The Root Talk*: "When every elder of the forest comes here to hear the trees. It is late this year. When it is
held they will ask this hall about every sick tree in the marsh, and I mean to have answers." *Why me*: "A walker
must be a stranger to the tree. You are the strangest thing that has come into this hall. The tree does not need
you to hear it; it needs you to taste, look and follow. Taste with the tip of your tongue and spit. Raw from the
root and swallowed, it maddens anyone who was not hatched under that very tree" (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:37`;
consistency T9). *What it pays and why I might not*: "The walkers' chest by that wall is yours while you walk for
us, salves and draughts and all. What else you are given depends on how you leave a root. And you will be the
stranger who touched their tree, in every town where one dies." "Not yet" stays open and free. The job is five
sentences: Korydon's minder has sent word that his town's tree tastes bitter and its clutch lies dull; the
rootworm that Cheeval-Xu calls at our sacred tree comes up in Korydon's root pool, at the foot of the village
tree, and the minder stands there; find which root the bitterness comes in by, and if a hand put it there, stop
the hand; if there is a wound, dress it with the hall's salve, which a walker carries; the herb-wife of the
hunters' camp north of the town is Meer-Zah, and she knows every root in that country. "When a village asks what
ails its tree, a walker tells it what lets it sleep. You will learn." He gives passage money and the walker's
cord, and says of it: "Three turns and a tuck: the walkers' hitch. Every minder in the marsh knows it." And one
warning, which is the ending's trade said first: "If you find a thing in a root that should not be there, do not
pull it out until you know whose hand put it there and who lives by it. A pulled tap is a closed root, and a
closed root feeds nobody. This hall pays its walkers for closed roots, and for nothing else. Others will pay you
for open ones."

**The hidden truth (X).** Korydon is a town of chime-smiths and boat-builders on a ring of dry ground, and its
village tree's roots run out under the channel (D ultherus-swamp, Korydon). One root surfaces on a hummock north
of the ring, in the dragon's-tongue beds of the Tumnatu's camp, who have hunted that ground "since before
Argonians resettled the Kothringi towns round it" (D ultherus-swamp, Tumnatu Cattail Camp). The Tumnatu have
always drawn a little resin from that root through a reed tap. Hist-sap resin is what seals a hull
(`topics/material-culture.md:297`), and Korydon sells boats to Helstrom, Truowa and Blackrose. Two seasons ago
orders from Blackrose doubled, and **Jaseen**, the caulker at the boatwright's slip, asked the Tumnatu herb-wife,
**Meer-Zah**, for three times the resin. A tapped root closes itself in days. To keep it running she packed the
wound with a paste of dragon's-tongue, which is "beautiful but deadly" (`topics/fauna-hazards.md:97`) and keeps a
wound from closing (X). The paste has been creeping down the root into the tree ever since, and the running sap
has drawn a spider that has webbed the root. The town's minder, **Demeepa-Ru**, who is ashamed of how much metal
his people work, blames the chime-smiths' smelter, whose quench-water has run into the channel beside the tree for
as long as there have been smiths. The smiths blame the Tumnatu, whom the elders already accuse of stealing a
chime (D ultherus-swamp, Tumnatu Cattail Camp). Nobody has walked out to look, because the beds are full of
spiders and are another people's ground.

The things at the root, and the camp's goods on its poles, are the camp's, and the camp keeps Skyrim's own law of
theft. To one who has the camp's leave they are free to take; to anyone else, taking them in the hunters' sight is
theft. The hall's salve is no thing in the pack: a walker is taken to carry it, and dressing the wound is a stage,

**The ways through.**

- *Where it comes in* (any one moves the quest; all are open together).
  1. *The three beads.* Each bead carries a marker. Hold all three and ask Demeepa-Ru to taste them in turn (no
     skill; he keeps none): the forge root is the sweetest and the north root the worst. "Then it is not the
     forge. It comes in from outside the ring, from the north. One root of this tree comes up on the Tumnatu's
     hummock, across the channel north of the town; I have never walked out to it."
  2. *The taste (Alchemy 25, any bead held: one line for each bead).* "There is dragon's-tongue in this." He says
     where it grows and which root lies among it. Refused: "You do not know what you are tasting. Bring me all
     three and I will tell you which is worst."
  3. *The herb-wife (Speech 30, or 60 gold).* Meer-Zah, asked about the town's tree: "Their root comes up in our
     beds. We drew from it before their town had a name. Go and look, since you ask openly; my hunters will know
     you. Something has webbed it, and that is yours to deal with." Her answer gives the camp's leave. Refused:
     "Ask your town what it accuses us of this season. Or put sixty on my mat, as a trader would." The same two
     lines stay open after the way is learned from Demeepa-Ru, until the pot is taken.
- *Reaching the far root.* Demeepa-Ru and Ruleem both say what is in the beds before the player goes: the great
  spiders, and a lesser one that has webbed the root. Three ways: with the camp's leave, by the camp's own side of
  the beds, past the two hunters and short of the big spiders; unseen past the hunters (Sneak); or round the far
  side, through the three spiders and out of the hunters' sight (Archery or any weapon, Destruction). On every way
  one spider sits on the root itself, at the Wild band, beyond the hunters' notice: it is shot or fought, by the
  player alone, or the things are lifted from under it unseen (Sneak). A talker with the camp's leave still meets
  it; it is the smallest fight in that country, and the Walkers' Chest holds three draughts for it.
- *The find, and the hand.* Taking the pot of paste is the find. The gourd's tin names the town's side of it:
  shown to Jaseen (the gourd held; no skill), he owns it. "I asked for three times the resin. I did not ask how
  she would get it. Then I did not ask again." Shown to Meer-Zah: "The slip paid me. Jaseen. I packed it because
  the town wanted more than a root gives."
- *The wound.* Using the far root, the pot found, cuts the paste out and dresses the wound with the hall's salve,
  there and then; nobody is gone back to.
- *The tap.* It is still in the root. What is done with it is the ending.

### MR02. The Cloudy Jar

Offered once MR01 has ended completed.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Okeeh-Ja's briefing, in answers the player picks. *Yuva-Goli*: "Five of
our own people on the sea coast, far east, days from any tree. They go home in turns." *The jar*: "A stoppered jar
of this tree's sap stands on their shrine. Their elder reads it as her counsel: how it moves, how it clouds. It is
all the tree they have." *The job*: the jar has gone cloudy and the elder fears the tree is ill. "Sap in a sealed
jar does not sicken by itself. Something was done to it. Find what, or the new one goes the same way." He gives a
fresh sealed jar and passage money, and says of the jar, "Drawn before the spring was sealed. I will not draw
another this month, and that is not asked in this hall. Lose it and there is no other." And last, the ending's
trade said first: "Onuja-Mei has read that jar for thirty years. Whatever you find, tell her what will let her go
on reading, and the hall will pay you for the work. Tell her otherwise and you will have what a sea station can
give you, and nothing from me but your fare." The way is told as far as the hall knows it: the rootworm from our
sacred tree to Aroxhu, east to the clay town of Arorha on the coast, and north-east along the shore from there;
the first marker is on the elder, who stands at the station's shrine (D murkwood, Helstrom; D hatandros-bight,
Yuva-Goli).

**The hidden truth (X).** The station's keepers "go home in turns" (D hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli), because the
Root-Folk fade fast away from their tree (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:63`; `topics/labour-and-bondage.md:112`). Two
years ago the elder, **Onuja-Mei**, read the jar on whether the keeper **Ushu-Lan** should take his turn, and read
"stay". He stayed, and greyed. He began to drink from the jar by night: a mouthful eases the ache for an evening
and gives back no colour. To hide the level he topped it up with rainwater and a pinch of kelp-dye from the
station's own racks, which is salt. Outlaws "beach a boat in the cove" (D hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli). Their
captain, called **Salt-Throat**, a sea-raider whose boat belongs to neither of the camps on Skeith Isle
(consistency C58), caught Ushu-Lan at the shrine one night, and since then takes a gourd of sap each time he
beaches, for a little coin and his silence. He doses his deckhands with it before a raid, and one of them, who was
hatched under no tree of Helstrom's, has not come out of it (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:37`; consistency T9). Each
thinned reading has been murkier than the last, and the elder reads murk as "stay": she has refused Ushu-Lan his
turn home four times, on the word of his own watering. Now the jar is cloudy outright. Another keeper,
**Bun-Zeer**, a woman who trades the station's kelp, salt and dye, knows that Ushu-Lan has not gone home and
thinks the elder is hard on him. Nothing is wrong with any tree.

**The ways through** (all open at the working stage, in any order).

- *What is in the jar.* The elder's line "Let me taste it" tells what the drop tastes of: thin, faintly salt,
  nothing foul. With Alchemy 25 a second line of hers tells more: this is rainwater and kelp-dye; it has been
  thinned; nothing is wrong with the sap that is left. Neither moves a stage. Refused: "You have a tongue. You
  have not a minder's tongue." Without the skill, Bun-Zeer says the same thing sideways: "It used to pour like
  honey."
- *Who.* Three leads, from three kinds of source. Bun-Zeer: Ushu-Lan has given up his turn home four times, two
  years now, "and the jar told her so each time; ask her". His box, in the keepers' house, which Bun-Zeer points
  out as the stilt house nearest the net line: the wet dye and the foreign coin, by Lockpicking, or by Pickpocket
  for his key; it is his, so taking from it is theft, in a house of people who "send word ... to Helstrom" of an
  unwelcome visitor (D hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli). The cove chest: the gourd on the shrine's cord.
- *His confession* sets the stage. Ushu-Lan tells it all to a player who eases the grey in him first (Restoration
  25: "Sit. Let me see your hands"), who presses him (Speech 35: "Why have you not gone home?"), who offers to pay
  (80 gold: "You are not the first to pay me to talk"), or who shows him the dye pot or the gourd (a thing held;
  no skill). Refused: "I have nets to see to." If he is dead, Bun-Zeer, shown both the pot and the gourd, says
  whose they are; if both are dead, the elder herself, shown both. That way has a stage and an entry of its own,
  which says only what the two things show.
- *The cove.* Bun-Zeer gives the way, south-east along the shore past the kelp racks, and she and the elder both
  warn of it; Bun-Zeer says what made the deckhand mad. Salt-Throat and his deckhand are one hard fight for a new
  walker and an easy one for a seasoned one, at levels that never move (Archery, a weapon skill, Destruction), and
  his chest is the fight's reward. The chest can instead be opened behind his back (Sneak, Lockpicking Apprentice)
  for the gourd, which proves the sale and stops nothing.

## 5. Read on paper

### The first round

One round, on the first draft, all reading one version: two players given the story only as a player meets it
(MR01, then MR02, then the later four in summary), asked at each step what they thought was going on, whom they
trusted, what they expected and what they would choose; and one reader given the ceiling, the quest form and the
outlines in full.

**What they said.**

- *The player who loves the lore* would join, could retell it ("the tree-doctors who lie to keep trees alive, and
  the one their leader let die") and put the line at about eight in ten, rising from the turn. The knot on the
  cord under Gideon's garden was "the best beat of the line" and the grave-stake "superb"; the Listener ended as
  someone understood, not a villain. Hard to please on: every job ending on "who gets the truth or the object";
  the first job having no danger that could not be walked round and no surprise; the marker on the caulker solving
  the first mystery before it was investigated; the comfort with a Speech check being plainly the best ending at
  Yuva-Goli; no consequence being seen of what was chosen; Hixee-Ra, the Root Talk and the Mine-Burners arriving
  unexplained at the climax; Tsonac barely touching the spine; why the knife was left in a dead man's lap; why a
  tree could not simply be asked; small untruths (a jar that tastes of salt and was thinned with rain, a man who
  drinks sap and still greys, an "oldest riddle" about something in living memory, a plug whose cost was never
  clear); a non-Argonian tasting sap unremarked; and the shut spring never answered.
- *The impatient player* enjoyed doing things with a sneak's and an alchemist's skills, guessed the first
  mystery from the caulker's marker, called the first job "clever and quiet" and wanted "one tense moment, such as
  being caught at the root"; found the salve's herbs a fetch, the bow idle, and the pirate's death changing
  nothing to be seen. The same finding on endings: "whom do you tell, six times". Would join; the second half was
  the stronger.
- *The deliverability reader* found the stages climbing, every order of ways sound, the deaths covered and the
  danger fair, and found these blocking: hand-in lines for the tap opened later than the form allows; a take
  trigger open where its thing was no target; "quest items" that the build does not protect, so a salve drunk or
  a bead sold broke the table; the Listener asking what the town was told when no step let the player tell it
  anything; knots, stamps and marks carried in descriptions that plain things do not show; a journal entry that
  Bun-Zeer's telling could not support. Lesser: journals claiming what the player might not know, the camp's leave
  closing with its line, one failed stage for two different deaths, a look and a pose used as consequences, and,
  in the arc, a band switched off beside the player, a freed captive leaving in sight, a light going out, and a
  knife with no taker if three people were dead.
