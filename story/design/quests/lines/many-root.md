# Many-Root: the line's story

The story of Many-Root's six quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the [province plan](province-plan.md) gives the line (its sections 3.4, 4 and 7). A quest writer turns each
outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world (`story/design/world/`): "D ultherus-swamp, Korydon"
is that place's record or hooks in that region's file, "D people" the table of named people, "D province, Peoples" a
section of the province file. Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation
from the cited data and lore (decision 0032). The places are not built yet: an outline names a place by what its
record says it is, and anything else it relies on is a row of section 4. The hooks in the places' notes are raw
material; where this story changes one, section 7 says so.

**Words used below.** *Hist*: the sentient trees every Argonian is hatched under and returns to at death. *Sap*: a
Hist's blood, drunk in small measure for counsel and healing. *Tree-minder*: the tender of one village's tree.
*Walker*: a minder of the Helstrom hall who goes out to other people's trees. *Naheesh*: a tribe's elders. *Root
Talk*: the gathering at Helstrom at which the tribes hear the trees; it is late this year. *The Owing*: the native
sentence, months of owed work. *Danger bands*: Safe, Settled, Wild, Dangerous, Deadly, Lethal, fixed in the world
(D province, Danger). *Tumnatu*: a small Argonian people of swamp hunters who keep no tree of their own in this
country (D ultherus-swamp, Tumnatu Cattail Camp). *Mine-Burners*: the tribes of the Banujha Basin in the
south-west, whose one great tree stands at Jarecai (D province, Peoples: Mine-Burners).

## 1. The faction in a page

**The thing they tend.** A village's tree is its eggs, its names and its dead. A Hist's sap is its soul and becomes
the blood of everyone hatched under it (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:29`); an egg whose tree fails it "sinks into the
roots and fades away" (`:78`); a person held far from their tree fades, and comes back to colour only at home
(`:63`). So a sick tree is a whole people in danger, and in 4E 201 the data holds sick trees from one coast to the
other: Tsonac's dreams have turned bad, the sap-jar at Yuva-Goli has gone cloudy, Meris-Keth's tree died and emptied
its village, and sap is slashed and stolen within a morning's boat of Helstrom (D arnesian-jungle, Tsonac; D
hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli; D banujha-basin, Meris-Keth; D murkwood, Tseli-Oon).

**Who they are.** A village tree-minder tends one tree for life, has never seen another sicken, and loves her own
too well to taste it honestly (X). Many-Root are the few minders of Helstrom's hall who go out: "the minders who
stay between convocations and carry what the trees said out to the tribes" ([province-plan](province-plan.md)
§3.4). The hall calls them walkers. Their name is their one teaching: a tree has many roots and no two drink the
same water, so a sickness is always in one root before it is in the tree, and a walker's craft is to find which.
They sit in the tree-minders' hall at Helstrom (D province, What the world offers; D people, Taleeka-Ma). They are
six or seven, and old; the walking kills the young ones (X).

**How they work, in the line's own skills.** A walker is a healer and a tracker of a living thing.

- **Alchemy** first. The sap is tasted where it beads on each root, and what is in it is named: a poison, salt,
  another tree's sap. The cure is a draught or a salve worked from what that country gives and carried to the
  wound.
- **Restoration** second: the people a sick tree has harmed are eased before they are asked anything, and the
  unquiet dead of a dead tree are turned.
- **Archery and Sneak** for the country between. A root surfaces where it likes: on a hummock of giant spiders,
  under a city, in a camp of raiders. Sap draws predators (D murkwood, Tseli-Oon, dangers), so something is
  usually sitting on the wound. The beasts and bands stand at the world's fixed levels, and a walker goes past
  them more often than through them.
- **Speech** for the hand at the end of the trail, who always has a reason; and **Alteration** where the Stone-
  Sleepers' tree lies under stone (D arnesian-jungle, Tsonac).

The faction suits an alchemist and healer first, a hunter or a sneak second and a talker third; every quest can be
finished by any of the three. Nothing is levelled (decision 0035).

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **The hands.** Every sick tree in this line was made sick by somebody, and none of them is a villain: a hunting
  people tapping a root they tapped before the town was built, a keeper drinking from the jar that keeps him far
  from home, a mason paid to cut where his elders had cut, a gatherer selling sap because the hall releases too
  little to live on.
- **The sap crews.** Sap is "dear in Helstrom, because the tree-minders allow little to be taken" (D people,
  Wujeel), and smuggling it is the most natural crime in the province (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:167` to `:170`). In
  an outsider it is a drug that brings a killing rage (`:37`). Skeith outlaws buy it on the east coast, tribeless
  Naga steal it on the river north of Helstrom, and Khajiit smugglers cook it with moon sugar in a dead village in
  the south-west (D hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli; D murkwood, Dry-Fang Camp; D banujha-basin, Meris-Keth; X for the
  road that joins them). Their case is the Naga leader's: no tree claims her band, the hall would let nine of them
  starve sooner than release a skin, and she wants only the price of a boat.
- **The village minders**, who have reason to doubt the hall. A walker arrives as a stranger, tastes a tree that is
  not hers and tells its people what to think. The Listener "stood one day under Meris-Keth's tree and went home"
  (X), and the interior knows it.
- **The League of Open Water** is not an enemy and is refused: the hall holds that no tree is cut for another
  ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §4, "A tree for a city"). Why the hall holds it is the line's turn.
- **The Wardens' boat people.** The minders say the boatmen's houses by the landing ask what the walkers hear, and
  are told the price of fish ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §4). The hall knows nothing more of them.

**What it pays and what joining costs.** The hall is poor in coin and rich in what a healer wants: Taleeka-Ma's
teaching, the hall's own bench, a measure of released sap, and fixed gifts that are an alchemist's and a healer's
(vanilla rings and necklaces enchanted for Alchemy and Restoration; the record writer names the forms). The cost: a
walker is a stranger in every grove and is blamed for every tree that dies after she has tasted it; the sap crews
learn her face; and the Listener will ask her to tell grieving people something other than the truth. Ranks:
Root-Hand on taking the first job, Walker after MR03, Walking Minder after MR05; the Listener's place is offered in
one ending only.

**What it lies about.** The hall says a walker tells every village the truth of its tree. Inside the hall the
teaching is Okeeh-Ja's: "Tell them what lets them sleep." Under that lies what she has never told anyone (section
2).

**What the hall will not say.** The spring under Helstrom's own tree is shut behind a wicker door with a spear of
the levy at it ([main](main.md) §6). The walkers, who track every other tree's sickness, do not speak of their own
tree's. Asked, Okeeh-Ja says only, "That is not asked in this hall." Nobody in this line explains it, guesses at it
or connects it to any tree the player tends: the cause is the main quest's alone
([province-plan](province-plan.md) §3.4, "A boundary"). What the player sees is the silence, and a hall that has
begun sending walkers to every sick tree but its own. At the line's end Okeeh-Ja says the one thing the hall can
honestly say: "I have read every sick tree in the marsh but my own, and I cannot read it. Whoever can is not in
this hall."

**Why a player wants in.** Each job is a trail: taste, follow, find the hand. The trail always leaves the village
for somewhere dangerous, something is always sitting on the wound, the hand always has a face, and the cure is the
player's to give, withhold or put a price on. The patron is worth unpicking, and what the player carries home is
never only a report.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X throughout, on the cited lore), in the order it happened.**

1. A cutting of a Hist can be planted and will grow (`topics/hist-placement.md:93`, `:139`). It costs the tree it
   is taken from: the cut is made at the heart-root, and for some years after, fewer of the mother tree's eggs
   turn. A tree asked for a cutting gives it; a tree does not count its own eggs. It is the people under it who
   bury the shells.
2. When Okeeh-Ja was a girl of the hall, Gideon had no tree. In a hungry year the elders of the river folk, the
   Naka-Desh, sold a cutting of their tree to Gideon's council for salt and iron, the two things they still take
   from the city (D province, Peoples: Naka-Desh). A walker of Helstrom made the cut, because nobody else knew how,
   and his apprentice carried the basket. The apprentice was Okeeh-Ja. For twelve wet seasons after, the river
   folk's clutches failed. The elders who sold never told their people what they had done. They left a riddle
   instead, and the tribe has remembered a theft ever since; Gideon's families remember a gift and quarrel over
   whose (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon).
3. The hall made a rule of its shame, "no tree is cut for another", and stopped saying where the rule came from.
4. In Okeeh-Ja's middle years the tree of Meris-Keth, a Mine-Burner fishing village, sickened with a rot of the
   heart-root (D banujha-basin, Meris-Keth: it "sickened within living memory"). She was sent for. She tasted it
   and knew the one cure: a graft from the heart-root of a sound tree of the same water, and the only such tree is
   Jarecai's, under which the whole Mine-Burner people and the port of Soulrest hatch (D province, Peoples:
   Mine-Burners). She carried a grafting knife and a jar of binding-sap into the village. She thought of the river
   folk's twelve seasons and weighed a village against a tribe and a city. She told the village there was no cure.
   To its minder, Aojee-Dar, alone, she told the truth, laid the knife in front of him and said, "Then you cut
   Jarecai's tree, and tell its people why." He did not go either. She went home without the knife.
5. The village's eggs stopped hatching. Its families carried their last clutches to Jarecai and did not come back,
   and the dead of that last year were never staked (D banujha-basin, Meris-Keth). Aojee-Dar stayed with his tree,
   carved his own grave-stake with what she had said and what he had not done, and died against the root with the
   knife in his lap.
6. Khajiit smugglers took the dry halls, because nobody comes near a dead tree. They cook stolen sap with moon
   sugar there for the coast. The sap comes down from the north: from the Naga of Dry-Fang Camp, who take it from
   the gatherers of Tseli-Oon (D murkwood, Dry-Fang Camp, Tseli-Oon).
7. This year the Root Talk is late. Okeeh-Ja expects that when it is held the naheesh will ask the hall to answer
   for every sick tree in the interior; one of them, Hixee-Ra, has begun asking already. She is sending a stranger
   to read them all first. She forbids one place.

**The arc.** Six quests, each a different kind of job ending in a different kind of act, so that the line is never
the same trail walked six times. Danger climbs by the world's bands and nothing is levelled: the first four can be
finished by a character of modest level who uses the quiet or the knowing way, and the last two are on the
hardest ground in the province and say so.

- **MR01, The Bitter Root** (Korydon; the Tumnatu's hummock). *A poison traced to its root; ends on an act at the
  root.* A town's tree tastes bitter and each half of the town blames the other. The player tastes the sap root by
  root, follows the bitterness out of the town to a tap driven into a far root with a spider sitting on it, heals
  the wound with a salve, and then pulls the tap or leaves it in. Skills: Alchemy, Sneak, Archery or a weapon,
  Speech. Carried on: the rank of Root-Hand; the tap pulled, left or sold; what the town was told; the hall's knot
  seen.
- **MR02, The Cloudy Jar** (Yuva-Goli). *A small household with one liar, and a cove; ends on what an old woman is
  told.* A sea station far from any tree reads a jar of Helstrom's sap as its counsel, and the jar has gone cloudy.
  The tree is well. One of five keepers has been drinking the jar and thinning it, outlaws in the cove have been
  buying from him, and the elder has been reading rainwater as the voice of the Hist. Skills: Alchemy,
  Restoration, Speech, Lockpicking, Archery or a weapon. Carried on: what the elder was told; the captain dead or
  alive.
- **MR03, What the Sleeper Dreams** (Tsonac). *A descent under a city; ends on a bottle poured or kept.* The
  Stone-Sleepers' tree has been asleep since it was planted and must never be woken; its dreams have turned bad
  since a mason cut too deep under the mound. The player brews the hall's stilling draught, gets below, and at the
  root stills the tree, takes away the light that is waking it, or lets it wake. Skills: Alchemy, Alteration,
  Lockpicking or Pickpocket, Sneak or a weapon. Carried on: the rank of Walker; the tree stilled, settled or
  waking; the knowledge that the hall keeps a draught that can put a tree down.
- **MR04, The Borrowed Tree** (Gideon; Teth-Kasa Station). *The turn: a break-in under a garden, and the hall's
  own knot on what is found.* The river folk say Gideon's tree was stolen from them and two city families each say
  it was given to theirs. Okeeh-Ja asks, for the first time, that what is found be brought to her before anyone.
  The tree was sold, the hall cut it, and Okeeh-Ja carried the basket. The player answers the river folk's riddle
  or does not, tells the city or does not, and then faces her. Skills: Sneak, Lockpicking, Alchemy, Speech.
  Carried on: who knows; the player now knows what the hall's rule is made of.
- **MR05, The Sap Road** (Tseli-Oon; Dry-Fang Camp). *A captive in a raiders' camp on Lethal ground; ends on a
  bargain struck or refused.* A gatherer of Tseli-Oon is held for ransom by the Naga of Dry-Fang with the
  station's stolen sap. The player gets her out by stealth, by the leader's own price, which is a boat, or by a
  draught in the ransom, and learns two things: the gatherer was selling to her captors, and the sap goes south
  "to the cats under the dead tree". Okeeh-Ja forbids the trail. Skills: Sneak, Lockpicking, Alchemy, Speech,
  Archery. Carried on: the rank of Walking Minder; the band gone, asleep or still on the river; the gatherer named
  or not; the forbidden lead.
- **MR06, The Tree That Was Left** (Jal-Resh Post; Meris-Keth; then one of three people). *The hard place, and
  then the patron's secret exposed or kept: the line's one hand-over.* The dead village: smugglers in the halls,
  the unstaked dead on the walkways, a leafless tree in its root-hut, and against the root a dead minder with a
  walker's knife in his lap and his own grave-stake across his knees.

**The turn** is MR04, two-thirds through. Before it the hall is a house of healers with a strict rule; after it
the rule is a scar, the Listener's "tell them what lets them sleep" is a habit of deciding what peoples may know
about their own trees, and what the player told Korydon and Yuva-Goli reads differently. She says so herself, from
the two stored numbers: to a player who told a comfort, "Twice now you have told them what let them sleep. Now
you know why I do"; to one who told the truth both times, "You have told them the truth every time. Tell me this
one is no different." **The misjudgement** is in MR04 as well: before the cellar is opened the player can back
either family's story or the river folk's, each of which is false, and is believed; it costs standing with the
others and does not end the line.

**What carries.** Rank, the journal, stored numbers, who is alive, the things held and what the player knows.
Earlier choices change greetings and single lines and never a later quest's stages; only the finale's choice
carries into the world after the line.

### The later quests, at arc level

**MR03, What the Sleeper Dreams.** *Place*: Tsonac, the Stone-Sleepers' one city, "terraces of squared stone" round
"a central mound with the Hist and a ring of carved stone steps" (D arnesian-jungle, Tsonac). *The truth (X)*: the
grave-singers say the tree is "a sapling of that line", the line of Tsono-Xuhil, the mad Hist "that its neighbours
put to sleep" (D arnesian-jungle, Tsonac, history). A buyer from Thorn, the Dunmer **Golis Andrethi**, who lodges
by the quay, wants an Ayleid welkynd stone "cut from the foundations" and paid a mason, **Mazeeh**, to cut for it
under the mound. The cut broke into an Ayleid chamber where a root of the tree hangs through the roof. The elder
masons walled the cut with a door and said nothing; the buyer's three hired diggers went back in from a quarry cut
on the mound's far side and are still there, at the Dangerous band. The welkynd's light on the bare root is half
waking the tree, and the town's hatchlings dream what it dreams. *What the player does*: the tree-minder,
**Druja-Seel**, lets the player take sap at the stone ring, and her line shows the dream as a message: white
light, and something turning over. She adds, "Your Listener offered us the stilling once before, the season after
Meris-Keth emptied. We said our tree slept well enough." Okeeh-Ja has given the recipe and three words: "Still
it. Remember Lilmoth." The draught is a thing given by a line: the player works it at Tsonac's sap-seller's bench
(Alchemy 40, the gathered parts held) or the sap-seller makes it from the parts for 100 gold. The way down is the
masons' door (Mazeeh's key by Pickpocket or Speech; the lock; or Druja-Seel's own way, a line gated on Alteration
that unlocks it for one who knows the stone magic) or the quarry cut (Sneak past the diggers, or a fight). In the
chamber the welkynd stone, whose glow is its own, can be taken from its sconce like any thing, and the root can
be used once: the draught is poured, and that sets the quest's stage. The ending is then read from what is true
when the player next speaks to Druja-Seel. *The choice*, argued by those it concerns: Druja-Seel wants it stilled
("my whole people's peace is its sleep"); her pupil **Ah-Mota**, who sells the town's tomes, says the tree spoke
one word to him through the light and that a sleeping elder is a jailed one; Andrethi will pay 500 gold for the
stone and does not care what the tree does. *Endings*: stilled, the root used (the dreams stop; Ah-Mota will not
speak to the player); settled, the stone held and nothing poured (the dreams fade slowly; Okeeh-Ja: "A half
measure. I have let one tree die sooner than wake a harm. You will not understand that yet"); waking, neither
(nobody knows; the ring's blessing is switched for another while the player is in Helstrom, and Druja-Seel greets
with fear). *Carried on*: a stored number for the tree; the stone held, sold or given; Xilsa-Jee's remark that
the hall keeps "the stilling, and older things"; Hixee-Ra, now waiting by the hall, asks the player what Tsonac
was told. *Moment*: standing under a city with a bottle that will put its god back to sleep, and a young man
upstairs who says the god asked not to be.

**MR04, The Borrowed Tree.** *Places*: Gideon, whose "Hist garden holds a planted cutting whose ownership is
disputed", "in the Ayleid gardens that two families both claim", with "a cellar door in the closed Ayleid quarter
that has been forced" (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon); Teth-Kasa Station, the Naka-Desh post in the mangroves south
of the city (D kotherra-mangroves, Teth-Kasa Station). *What a new player is told*: Okeeh-Ja says a riddle-stick
has come to the hall from the river folk, who "love riddles" and are "rumoured to be the Blackwood tribe that says
Gideon's Hist cutting was taken from them" (D province, Peoples: Naka-Desh); that a claim on a tree is the hall's
to answer before the Root Talk; and, for the first time, "Bring what you find to me, before you say a word of it
to anyone." *What the player does*: hears three stories, each believed by its teller. The **Caprenius** family,
Nibenese-descended, say Helstrom gave the cutting to their forebear. The **Deep-Wader** family, Lukiul, say their
grandmother brought it as a bride-gift from the river. The pilot of Teth-Kasa, **Hapu-Jeer**, asks the riddle his
people teach every hatchling and none of them can answer: "What went out in a basket and came home in sacks, and
whom do we not thank?" A taste of the garden tree's sap (Alchemy 30, for a player who tasted Helstrom's at
Yuva-Goli: MR02 stores it) refutes the gift from Helstrom. The proof is below: past the garden's watch and the
forced door (Sneak and Lockpicking, with the city's own law for a break-in; or the key the garden's tender
carries, by Speech or Pickpocket), through cellars whose Ayleid guardians are fixed at the Dangerous band and can
be fought or passed unseen, to the planting-place under the tree: a basket of river weave, salt-sacks with the
council's seals, and the basket's carrying-cord, a thing named for the hall's knot, the same name as on the cord
the player has carried since MR01. Someone forced the door first: a body named as the Caprenius steward lies
among the guardians with lamp-oil and a flint, sent to burn the proof. *The turn*: taking the cord is the find.
Then three things can be done in any order, each a line: the riddle answered to Hapu-Jeer ("a tree's child, for
salt"), who says that is the answer nobody gives and that his grandmother counted twelve wet seasons in which no
egg turned; the cord shown to Gideon's council merchant, which ends the families' quarrel and makes the tree a
thing the city bought; and the cord brought to Okeeh-Ja, who says who carried the basket ("I was a girl. I counted
the river folk's dead eggs for twelve seasons. Do not tell me what a cutting costs") and learns from the player's
own mouth who else now knows. Each telling is a stored number; the quest ends at her. *Her case for burying it*:
"If the groves learn a walker's knife took a tree's child for salt, no village opens its tree to us again, and
trees will die of that." *The crossing*: this is the truth of Gideon's tree that the League of Open Water's line
also tells ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §4); after MR04, Taleeka-Ma's line on cuttings changes to "We cut
one once, for a city. Ask the river folk what it cost." *Carried on*: who knows. *Moment*: the knot.

**MR05, The Sap Road.** *Places*: Tseli-Oon, the Root-Folk sap station where "a sap-skin has been slashed and
swapped for a skin of plain water" (D murkwood, Tseli-Oon); Dry-Fang Camp, "about nine tribeless Naga ... led by a
scarred old female", hostile on sight and Lethal, where "a Root-Folk sap-gatherer is held ... for ransom; the camp
hides the sap-skins stolen from Tseli-Oon; the old leader will bargain for a boat" (D murkwood, Dry-Fang Camp).
Both are reached from Helstrom by water. *The truth (X)*: the gatherer, **Dusha-Wan**, sold sap to the Naga for
two seasons, because Tseli-Oon barters what the hall releases "for dried fish and cloth" and wanted iron for the
spears it keeps "at each door". When the hall cut the release she could not deliver, and the band took the skins
and her. Their leader, **Thekka**, sells south and is saving for a boat to take her band off a river where
Helstrom's levy and every other band want them dead. *What the player does*: learns at Tseli-Oon what was taken,
and from Wujeel in Helstrom what a boat costs. Then the camp, with three ways, and two people who say a fight
there will kill. The quiet way: in past the lean-tos to the pen (Sneak; Lockpicking, or Pickpocket for the pen's
key), where Dusha-Wan says she will go by the water once the player has gone. The spoken way: one of the band,
**Jurr**, stands outside the camp and speaks first (the lead's ruling for places hostile on sight); Thekka's
price, carried by him, is a boat, which the raft-builder of Helstrom sells by a line as a carved mooring-peg (D
people, Xuhal-Teeus; 800 gold, or 500 with Speech 50), and Jurr's line, the peg held, unlocks the pen and makes
the player one the band does not attack. The knowing way: a skin of sap laced with a sleeping draught, a thing
given by a line (Alchemy 50 at Wujeel's bench, with a jar of the honey of Wattle-Pool Farm that "tastes of sap
and sends those who eat it to sleep", D althii-archipelago, Wattle-Pool Farm), handed to Jurr as the ransom; a
line of Wujeel's back in Helstrom ("Give it until you have eaten") switches most of the band off, the player
being in the city, and the camp is found thinly watched. However she was reached, the player's report to

## 3. The first two quests

Common to both.

- **Okeeh-Ja** stands in the tree-minders' hall at Helstrom for the whole line. She must live: her death fails any
  running quest of the line before MR06 with the faction's message. Nobody is essential. People stand where they
  are placed.
- **Working stages.** After its briefings each quest sits at working stages at which the ways through can be
  taken in any order. What the player learns or does on a way is a stored number or a plain fact (a thing held, a
  person dead); the lines that carry the result to the person waiting for it set the stages.
- **A thing taken moves a stage only where the table says so** (one find in MR01). Every other thing only opens
  lines for whoever holds it, however and whenever it was got.
- **A death moves no stage.** Lines check who is alive. Each job is offered only while its people live.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense
  and say only what the player has just done; what a thing means is carried by its name and by people's lines.
  Thresholds are fixed. Each failed check has its own written refusal and leaves another way open.
- **Things.** Everything uses existing forms. Each sap-bead, salve, jar and cord is a plain thing that cannot be
  eaten or drunk, and carries what the player must know in its name, since nothing else of a plain thing is shown
  in game. A salve or draught the story needs is given by a line, never brewed at the player's own bench, so the
  form held is always the form checked.
- No step depends on the hour.
- **How the hall is found.** Taleeka-Ma, who heals and sells tomes at the hall and trains Restoration (D
  murkwood, Helstrom), has a topic "sick trees" for anyone; so has Wujeel at his stall. Each names Okeeh-Ja and
  says she stands inside the hall.

### MR01. The Bitter Root

**What a new player is told, and when.** On first speaking to Okeeh-Ja, before any job, five optional lines, each
two or three sentences. *The trees*: "A Hist is the tree a village is hatched under. Its sap is in their blood and
their dead go back to it. When it sickens, the eggs under it go dull first." *The walkers*: "A village minder
tends one tree all her life and cannot taste it honestly. We go out and taste other people's. A tree has many
roots and a sickness is in one of them first; that is all our craft." *The Root Talk*: "When every elder of the
forest comes here to hear the trees. It is late this year. When it is held they will ask this hall about every
sick tree in the marsh, and I mean to have answers." *Why me*: "A walker must be a stranger to the tree. You are
the strangest thing that has come into this hall. The tree does not need you to hear it; it needs you to taste,
look and follow." To a player who is not Argonian she adds: "Taste with the tip of your tongue and spit. Swallowed,
it does to your kind what it did to the Blackwood Company" (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:37`). *What it pays and why I
might not*: "Healer's goods, my teaching and a measure of sap when there is any. And you will be the stranger who
touched their tree, in every town where one dies." "Not yet" stays open and free. The job is four sentences:
Korydon's minder has sent word that his town's tree tastes bitter and its clutch lies dull; go by the rootworm or
the raft; find which root the bitterness comes in by, and if a hand put it there, stop the hand; "and tell the
town what will let it sleep." She gives passage money and the walker's cord, and says of it: "Three turns and a
tuck: the hall's knot. Every minder in the marsh knows it. Show it and they will let you near the tree." And one
warning: "If you find a thing in a root that should not be there, do not pull it out until you know whose hand
put it there. A pulled tap is a closed root, and a closed root feeds nobody."

**The hidden truth (X).** Korydon is a town of chime-smiths and boat-builders on a ring of dry ground, and its
village tree's roots run out under the channel (D ultherus-swamp, Korydon). One root surfaces on a hummock north
of the ring, in the dragon's-tongue beds of the Tumnatu's camp, who have hunted that ground "since before
Argonians resettled the Kothringi towns round it" (D ultherus-swamp, Tumnatu Cattail Camp). The Tumnatu have
always drawn a little resin from that root through a reed tap. Hist-sap resin is what seals a hull
(`topics/material-culture.md:297`), and Korydon sells boats to Helstrom, Truowa and Blackrose. Two seasons ago
orders from Blackrose doubled, and **Jaseen**, the caulker at the boatwright's slip, asked the Tumnatu herb-wife,
**Meer-Zah**, for three times the resin. A tapped root closes itself in days. To keep it running she packed the
wound with a paste of dragon's-tongue, which is "beautiful but deadly" (`topics/fauna-hazards.md:97`) and keeps a
wound from closing (X). The paste has been creeping down the root into the tree ever since, and the running sap
has drawn a spider that has webbed the root. The town's minder, **Demeepa-Ru**, who is ashamed of how much metal
his people work, blames the chime-smiths' smelter, whose quench-water has run into the channel beside the tree for
as long as there have been smiths. The smiths blame the Tumnatu, whom the elders already accuse of stealing a
chime (D ultherus-swamp, Tumnatu Cattail Camp). Nobody has walked out to look, because the beds are full of
spiders and are another people's ground.

### MR02. The Cloudy Jar

Offered once MR01 has ended completed.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Okeeh-Ja's briefing, in answers the player picks. *Yuva-Goli*: "Five of
our own people on the sea coast, far east, days from any tree. They go home in turns." *The jar*: "A stoppered jar
of this tree's sap stands on their shrine. Their elder reads it as her counsel: how it moves, how it clouds. It is
all the tree they have." *The job*: the jar has gone cloudy and the elder fears the tree is ill. "Sap in a sealed
jar does not sicken by itself. Something was done to it. Find what, or the new one goes the same way." She gives a
fresh sealed jar and passage money, and says of the jar, "Drawn before the spring was shut. I will not draw
another this month, and that is not asked in this hall." And last: "Haxal-Mei has read that jar for thirty years.
Whatever you find, tell her what will let her go on reading." The way is told as far as the hall knows it: the
rootworm to Aroxhu, east to the clay town of Arorha on the coast, and north-east along the shore from there (D
murkwood, Helstrom; D hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli).

**The hidden truth (X).** The station's keepers "go home in turns" (D hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli), because the
Root-Folk fade fast away from their tree (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:63`; `topics/labour-and-bondage.md:112`). Two
years ago the elder, **Haxal-Mei**, read the jar on whether the keeper **Ushu-Lan** should take his turn, and read
"stay". He stayed, and greyed. He began to drink from the jar by night: a mouthful eases the ache for an evening
and gives back no colour. To hide the level he topped it up with rainwater and a pinch of kelp-dye from the
station's own racks, which is salt. Skeith outlaws, sea-raiders from the island off this coast, "beach a boat in
the cove" (D hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli). Their captain, called **Brine-Eye**, caught Ushu-Lan at the shrine one
night, and since then takes a gourd of sap each time he beaches, for a little coin and his silence. He doses his
deckhands with it before a raid, and one of them has not come out of it (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:37`). Each
thinned reading has been murkier than the last, and the elder reads murk as "stay": she has refused Ushu-Lan his
turn home four times, on the word of his own watering. Now the jar is cloudy outright. The keeper **Bun-Zeer**
knows he has not gone home and thinks the elder is hard on him. Nothing is wrong with any tree.

## 5. Read on paper

One round, on the first draft, all reading one version: two players given the story only as a player meets it
(MR01, then MR02, then the later four in summary), asked at each step what they thought was going on, whom they
trusted, what they expected and what they would choose; and one reader given the ceiling, the quest form and the
outlines in full.

**What they said.**

- *The player who loves the lore* would join, could retell it ("the tree-doctors who lie to keep trees alive, and
  the one their leader let die") and put the line at about eight in ten, rising from the turn. The knot on the
  cord under Gideon's garden was "the best beat of the line" and the grave-stake "superb"; Okeeh-Ja ended as
  someone understood, not a villain. Hard to please on: every job ending on "who gets the truth or the object";
  the first job having no danger that could not be walked round and no surprise; the marker on the caulker solving
  the first mystery before it was investigated; the comfort with a Speech check being plainly the best ending at
  Yuva-Goli; no consequence being seen of what was chosen; Hixee-Ra, the Root Talk and the Mine-Burners arriving
  unexplained at the climax; Tsonac barely touching the spine; why the knife was left in a dead man's lap; why a
  tree could not simply be asked; small untruths (a jar that tastes of salt and was thinned with rain, a man who
  drinks sap and still greys, an "oldest riddle" about something in living memory, a plug whose cost was never
  clear); a non-Argonian tasting sap unremarked; and the shut spring never answered.
- *The impatient player* enjoyed doing things with a sneak's and an alchemist's skills, guessed the first
  mystery from the caulker's marker, called the first job "clever and quiet" and wanted "one tense moment, such as
  being caught at the root"; found the salve's herbs a fetch, the bow idle, and the pirate's death changing
  nothing to be seen. The same finding on endings: "whom do you tell, six times". Would join; the second half was
  the stronger.
- *The deliverability reader* found the stages climbing, every order of ways sound, the deaths covered and the
  danger fair, and found these blocking: hand-in lines for the tap opened later than the form allows; a take
  trigger open where its thing was no target; "quest items" that the build does not protect, so a salve drunk or
  a bead sold broke the table; Okeeh-Ja asking what the town was told when no step let the player tell it
  anything; knots, stamps and marks carried in descriptions that plain things do not show; a journal entry that
  Bun-Zeer's telling could not support. Lesser: journals claiming what the player might not know, the camp's leave
  closing with its line, one failed stage for two different deaths, a look and a pose used as consequences, and,
  in the arc, a band switched off beside the player, a freed captive leaving in sight, a light going out, and a
  knife with no taker if three people were dead.

**What changed, as one set.**

1. *The line no longer ends six jobs the same way.* MR01 now ends on an act at the root (the tap pulled, or left
   in and granted by a line); MR03 on a bottle poured or kept; MR04 on three tellings that may each be made or
   not, with the Listener last; MR05 on a bargain. Only MR06 is a hand-over. MR02 keeps its telling, which is its
   whole matter.
2. *The first job has teeth and a surprise.* A spider sits on the tapped root on every way, so the bow or the
   knife is used or the things are lifted from under it; hunters watch the root, so an uninvited taker is a thief
   by Skyrim's own law. The caulker is no longer a lead with a marker: the gourd's tin names him, and he is faced
   with it. The salve needs no gathering. The walker's plug became the walker's cord, a badge whose knot the
   Listener names, and her warning about pulling a tap now stakes the ending.
3. *Nobody gets the best ending for a skill check.* The Speech line that sent the keeper home under the comfort
   is gone: the truth frees him and breaks her, the comfort keeps her and leaves him waiting on a jar.
4. *Consequences are found.* Korydon's nest holds some bright eggs afterwards; what the town was told is asked by
   its minder and shows in three people's greetings; the captain alive is a boat that came again and asked the
   next keeper. The Listener reads the player's two tellings back to them at the turn.
5. *The climax's people are met early.* Hixee-Ra waits by the hall from MR03 and asks after each job what the
   village was told; the Root Talk is one of the first optional lines; the Mine-Burners and the Banujha are placed
   when Meris-Keth is first named. Tsonac's minder ties the stilling to Meris-Keth.
6. *The secret is sharper.* The knife is in the dead minder's lap because she laid it before him and he could not
   use it either; the tree was asked and said yes, and it is the people under it who were not. The line's one
   honest word on the shut spring is the Listener's at the end.
7. *Small truths.* The dye is salt; a mouthful of sap eases and does not restore; the riddle is taught to
