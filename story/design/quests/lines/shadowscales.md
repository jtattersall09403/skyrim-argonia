# The Shadowscales: the line's story

The story of the assassins' line, eight quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the province plan gives the line ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §3.10, §4, §7). A quest writer turns each
full outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything. The old design, The Empty Cradle
([40-factions](../40-factions.md) §31; [cast roster](../36-cast-roster.md), "The Empty Cradle"), is its foundation:
its three bidders, its dead last Shadowscale, its boat-builder who was born to the knife and its question are kept,
and its hearings, councils and records offices are gone.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world (`story/design/world/`; "D nushara-bayou, Archon" is
that place's record or hooks in that region's file). Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is
`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`; *scales* is `topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md`. **X** marks this
document's own extrapolation from them (decision 0032). The places are not built yet, so a place is named by what
the digest says it is, and everything else a quest needs of it is a row of section 4.

**Words used below.** *The Shadow*: a birth sign. By old custom a hatchling born under it was given away at birth
to be raised a Shadowscale (*scales*:61). *Shadowscale*: one so raised, trained by the Dark Brotherhood, a guild of
paid killers from outside the marsh. *Ku-vastei*: "one who brings needed change"; a Shadowscale who came of age and
chose to serve Black Marsh as a judge who carries out his own judgements (*scales*:64 to :66). *The brass*: the
brass medallion a finished Shadowscale wears (*scales*:68). *A stake*: here, a short black tally a ku-vastei cuts
for each judgement, naming the wrong and the one who did it (X: section 1). *The Owing*: the native sentence,
months of owed work instead of a cell. *The half-made*: the pupils turned out untrained when the school shut (X).
*The cradle*: the nest-room of the school, where given eggs were kept. *Black-Tongues*: a tribe of alchemists of
the southern marsh who gave more Shadow-born than any other (`tribes.md:29`).

## 1. The faction in a page

**What it was, and what is left.** For as long as anyone tells it, every tribe gave its Shadow-born to the school
at Archon, and those who lived to come of age either joined the Dark Brotherhood or came home as ku-vastei: judges
who "act with impunity" and bring "swamp law" down on native and foreigner alike (*scales*:61 to :67). They were
the only court most of the province ever had (*state*:205 to :208). The school shut in 4E 187 (*scales*:84;
`archon.md:44`). Archon's harbour authority sealed it and pays a townswoman to watch the door (D nushara-bayou,
Archon; *state*:409 to :411). One very old ku-vastei, **Ux-Teeba**, went on living in a hut beside it and went on
judging: fourteen years of grievances left for him on the water stairs under the school, among the offerings
Archon's sailors leave there for the drowned (D nushara-bayou, Archon, faith; X that the same stairs take both).
This year he dies, and the province says the last of them is gone (*scales*:85; province-plan §4, "One timeline").

**How a ku-vastei works (X, from *scales*:64 to :72).** Nobody issues his writ. He hears a grievance, judges it
alone, and cuts a **stake**: a short black tally with the wrong and the name on it, "killing written small". He
kills the one it names, quickly, and no one else; a slow or cruel death is a disgrace (*scales*:72). Afterwards the
stake is driven at the water stairs where the grievance was left, and the matter is closed like an Owing: nobody
speaks of it again. The stake is to a judgement what a grave-stake is to a life (*scales*:123 to :126). That is the
whole of the old order's procedure, and the line keeps to it: a stake, a named person who stands where they stand,
and the question of whether the stake is right.

**What the player becomes.** Not a pupil. The school has no teachers. The player is the stranger who goes into the
dead man's hut, finds his last stakes uncut and undriven, and takes up his brass. From then on three hands bid for
whoever carries it, because each needs a blade the province will accept as the real thing, and the province accepts
a brass and a stake.

**The three bidders, and why each is arguable.**

- **The state.** The Archwardens live on "tolls at crossings and customs at ports, nothing else" and have no navy:
  on the water their arm is the chartered privateer (*state*:704 to :707; D province, Peoples: Archon Harbour Folk,
  "the town charters the pirates that it cannot defeat"). **Collector Lartius Xeen**, the Wardens' customs chief
  on Archon's quay, of an old Archein family, wants the same for the knife: a chartered ku-vastei, paid and
  answerable. His case: a judge nobody appoints is a murderer with good manners; the old one was right for forty
  years by luck; a charter can be read, challenged and taken away. What is wrong with it: the state would choose
  the names, and the state lives on tolls.
- **The Brotherhood.** In 4E 187 the Brotherhood's Listener and her associate Rasha planned to reopen the school
  (*scales*:84; `topics/guilds-and-orders.md:213` to `:219`). **Tirele Sadryn**, a Dunmer of Cheydinhal, was sent
  ahead that year to arrange it and arrived to find the door sealed. No letter from the sanctuary that sent her has
  come since (X). She has sold Imperial and Dunmer books on Archon's beach road for fourteen years (D
  nushara-bayou, Archon, "Bookseller"), kept the Five Tenets alone, and never stopped being what she was sent as.
  Her case: the Brotherhood made every Shadowscale there ever was; the marsh only borrowed them; and a contract is
  the one honest writ, because the one who wants the death pays for it and says so. What is wrong with it: she asks
  no reason, and she may be a family of one.
- **The Black-Tongue keepers.** The Black-Tongues "dedicate almost all their resources" to producing Shadowscales
  (`tribes.md:29` to `:31`), and have gone on marking their Shadow-born with nowhere to send them (*state*:214 to
  :217). **Ohl-Katta**, an egg-keeper of theirs, holds that the Shadow chooses and nobody else does: not a mother,
  not a state, not a guild from Cyrodiil, and not the hatchling. Her case: it worked for a thousand years, and the
  judges it made belonged to nobody who could pay them. What is wrong with it: nobody is asked, least of all the
  one in the egg.

**The fourth voice.** **Waits-at-Stairs**, the townswoman who has watched the sealed door for fourteen years and
carried the old man his food, wants no master for the brass at all. She is the line's steady voice and its patron
in all but name, and she has something to hide (section 2).

**Who else stands in it.**

- **Iiran-Vekh**, a boat-builder of Archon, whom people call the Second Knife and who did not ask them to. He was
  given as an egg, trained for seventeen years and turned out when the door shut, a season short of his brass. He
  is the best-trained living hand in the province and wants to build boats. All three bidders want him more than
  they want the player, and he has refused all three; that is why they bid for a stranger.
- **Three-Ropes**, Archon's harbourmaster, who sealed the school in 4E 187 and has never said why.
- **The marks**: each a named person on their own ground, each with a case of their own.

**What it pays and what joining costs.** A bed and a chest in the dead man's hut; his knife; after each stake a
fixed gift of his own things, which are an assassin's (vanilla pieces enchanted for Sneak, One-Handed, Archery and
Illusion, and poisons; the record writer names the forms); fixed coin from whoever wanted the death. It costs the
old impunity, which died with him: Archon is under foreign law and has cells (D nushara-bayou, Archon), its guards
do not know a dead man's brass, and a killing seen is a murder with a bounty. Native villages have no cells at
all, and a killer who will not pay there fights everyone (owner-bar, "Truth and lore";
`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:196` to `:201`). The getting away is always the player's own affair. It costs two
doors: the Lamp-Bearers will not raise a senior Shadowscale to their senior rank, nor this line a senior lamp
(province-plan §4).

**What it lies about.** That the school is empty. That the last Shadowscale was clean. That a brass makes a judge.

**The build leaning.** Sneak first, then One-Handed and Archery for the kill itself, Illusion for getting close and
getting out, Alchemy for the blade (the profile `shadowscales`, kept). Every mark can be reached by a quiet blade,
by a bow, by poison put in their pocket or on a blade, or by a fair fight they accept when the stake is named to
their face. Lockpicking, Pickpocket and Speech serve the learning of the ground: who the mark is, where they stand,
whether the stake is true. Nothing is levelled (decision 0035). A talker can finish every quest of the line, since
every stake can be laid down or answered some other way, but the line is written for the knife, and says so. The
line never checks whether a kill was seen or how it was done: Skyrim's own crime system is the consequence, and
what the line reads is only who is alive.

**Why a player wants in.** Each quest is one person the player must decide about and then reach. The player is
courted by three powers who each offer something real, in a port city that is easy to love. The patron is worth
unpicking. And the brass is the player's own from the first quest: nobody ranks above them, which is the trouble.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** In 4E 187 the Brotherhood was falling apart outside the marsh. The school's last
mistress, **Sheer-Tulan**, a Shadowscale, resolved to take the pupils then in the school west to Cheydinhal and
swear them all to the Brotherhood at once, before there was no Brotherhood to swear them to. Ux-Teeba, the school's
old blade-teacher, held that the choice at coming of age, the guild or the marsh (*scales*:62 to :65), was the only
thing that made the giving bearable, and that children carried to Cyrodiil would never be offered it. He killed
her in the teaching hall. The order's first law is that a Shadowscale may not kill a Shadowscale, and the penalty
is death by "an unaffiliated assassin sent by the Argonian Royal Court" (*scales*:69 to :72). There is no court:
the Scalded Throne has been empty for centuries, if it ever stood (*scales*:98 to :101).

He walked to the harbourmaster and told her. Three-Ropes had no law for a ku-vastei and wanted none. She sealed the
school over the body, which no grave-singer would enter to stake; turned the pupils out to trades; set a watcher on
the door; and said the school had shut for want of pupils. Ux-Teeba cut a stake with his own name on it, laid it in
the hall, built a hut by the door and waited for the stranger the court would send. He told the watcher to send
him every stranger who would go in. None was the one. He served alone meanwhile, and judged well.

Two years ago, failing, he decided that he had been wrong in one thing: the order must not end with his guilt. He
sent to the Black-Tongues, and Ohl-Katta came in by the water stairs with her tribe's held Shadow clutches, which
her craft keeps asleep in the egg (X, from the tribe's mastery of its own laying: `loading-facts.json`,
`fact.black-tongue-gloom-nectar`). Through an Archon pilot, **Olik-Tei**, he made a bargain with a Vicecanon of
the north who wanted judges again: the north's Shadow clutches, carried south, against judges in twenty years
([lamp-bearers](lamp-bearers.md) §2). He asked nobody how the north's clutches were got. Ohl-Katta knows: the
baskets come packed in haste, with no mother's knot on them. She holds that the Shadow's claim is older than a
mother's. So the sealed school is not empty. Its cradle is full of sleeping eggs, with no one to raise them and no
order to raise them into, kept by a woman who is waiting to learn whose they will be.

This year Ux-Teeba asked Ohl-Katta, who is no Shadowscale, for the quiet cup, and drank it in his chair with his
last unfinished stake on his knees. Three days later the player arrives.

**What the patron hides.** Waits-at-Stairs knows all of it. She has let the baskets round to the water for two
years. She sends the player into the hut as she has sent every stranger for fourteen years: to be, at last, the
one who kills him. She is three days too late and does not say what the errand was.

**The arc.** Each quest is one mark, and no two are the same kind of person on the same kind of ground. In the
first three the player finishes the dead man's work and the bidders court them with gifts. The fourth is the turn.
In the next three each bidder cuts a writ of their own, and each is a worse thing than the dead man ever asked. The
last is a master chosen, or none.

SS05, SS06 and SS07 are offered together once SS04 is done and can be taken in any order, refused or left: that is
the bidding made into play. SS08 opens when each of the three is closed, however it closed.

**The ranks.** *Brass-Bearer* (SS01 ended: the hut, the bed, the chest); *Knife-Bearer* (SS03 ended); *Writ-Keeper*
(SS04 ended; the senior rank, refused with its own line to a Long Lamp of the Lamp-Bearers, for whom the line ends
there). No rank is refused for laying a stake down. The finale's endings each give a last title and only one of
them leaves the player answerable to nobody.

**The turn** is SS04. Afterwards the three stakes the player drove read as the judgements of a man under his own
sentence, the watcher's first words read as a request for a killing, and the "empty" school is the fullest house
in Archon.

**The planned misjudgement** is SS06: a hasty player kills a fellow Shadowscale for coin, is told so afterwards by
three people, and goes on. SS01 lets the same player kill a man the stake wrongly names.

**What carries.** The rank; a stored number for how each quest ended and, from SS05 on, a standing with each
bidder; who is alive; the brass, the knife and the water key. Earlier endings change greetings and single lines and
never a later quest's stages. A person a later quest cannot start without, if dead, closes that quest with a line
from its giver ("answered before it was given") and the line goes on. The line can be left at any rank: after
SS03 the player is Archon's ku-vastei in the town's eyes and nothing more is owed.

**The hub reacts.** After each quest Waits-at-Stairs, Iiran-Vekh and each bidder the player has met greet with
their own view of what was done. The stairs change: each stake driven is a stake standing there (switched on
while the player is indoors elsewhere), and the petitioner of each stands by it with a line. Sounds-Twice, if
alive, tells who berths where. A player with a bounty in Archon is told by Waits-at-Stairs, once, that the old one
never had to run.

### The later quests

**SS04, The Water Door.** With the third stake answered, Waits-at-Stairs gives the player an iron key: "He said:
when a stranger has answered three, give them the key to the stairs. I never asked what for." The water stairs
under the school end at a grille, which the key opens. Behind it is the cradle: rows of moss baskets with eggs in
them, and Ohl-Katta, who is glad to be found and has been waiting to see what the old one's stranger would be. She
tells the player what the cradle is, who brings the baskets (the pilot Olik-Tei, when he is shown a purple stone)
and what she is waiting for: someone with the right to say whose eggs these are. She will not go further in: "The
hall beyond that door is hers." The inner door is locked (Expert; its key is the second key on the dead man's body
in the hut, which a player may have held since SS01). In the teaching hall the school's last mistress walks,
unstaked for fourteen years, one fixed and hard opponent of the dangerous band, on ground the player has been
warned of twice (Ohl-Katta's words, and the bones of the school's guar at the foot of the stair). She can be
fought, passed by Sneak, or held by Illusion or Restoration. At the hall's far end two stakes lie on a
bench: hers, a full grave-stake cut by her killer, which tells her life and her death and what she meant to do
with the children; and a short black one, the fourth stake, which names Ux-Teeba, for her death, "to be driven by
no hand of the order". Taking the fourth stake moves the quest. What the player does with it is theirs: Waits-at-
Stairs, shown it, says what she sent the player in for, and that she was glad to be late; each bidder, shown it,
says what it proves to them (to Tirele, that the school was stolen from the Brotherhood by a murder; to the
Collector, that the order's own law ended the order; to Ohl-Katta, nothing she did not pour into his cup). The
mistress, once down, can be staked by a line to Iiran-Vekh, who drives her stake as the only pupil who will. The
player comes out a Writ-Keeper. While they are below, a woman of Tapauo, **Reemha**, is switched on at the head of
the stairs with an empty basket; she is asking where a clutch went that her village's tender said had sunk.

**SS05, The Collector's Letter.** The Collector's first writ of his own is a sealed letter of marque for one
death: **Nuleem-Ja**, raft-master of Xi-Inou, the hamlet the Three Waters built so as to sell "without entering
the harbour and paying its customs" (D nushara-bayou, Xi-Inou). She is the woman whose drowned raft-hands the
player's first stake answered. His case, in his own words: every sack that lands at her planks is a spear a fort
does not have, and the forts are all that stand between the south and the Skeith crews. No old law calls it a
wrong. The player can do it, on a landing where all ten people are her kin and under native law; can warn her (she
says she will take the landing's trade up the channel, and is found at Inoio afterwards, and Xi-Inou's planks are
poorer for it); can bring the Collector what he truly wants instead, a quarter-toll she agrees to at Speech 60 or
when the player pays the first year (500 gold), which costs her the thing her hamlet was built to avoid; or can
give the letter to Three-Ropes, who has waited years for the Collector to sign something like it.

**SS06, A Contract Is a Contract.** Tirele's writ is a page with a black handprint and a purse: **Geh-Meema**, an
old woman of Lilmoth to whom the walkways bring their quarrels (D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth, "Elders, including
retired Shadowscales, settle disputes"). No reason is given, by rule. The ground is a city under native law, a
house every neighbour watches, and a mark who is not what she seems: her brass hangs by her door for anyone to see
who looks, and she will say what she is to anyone who speaks to her before striking. The order's first law forbids
the killing to a Shadowscale, and the player carries a brass. Tirele knows it: the contract is her own (the purse
is her savings), and a brass-bearer who has broken the first law has no order left but hers. Before the kill it
can be learnt three ways (the brass on the wall, Geh-Meema's own word, Iiran-Vekh, who knows her name). The
contract can be done; refused to Tirele's face; or voided in the one way the Brotherhood itself honours: canon
has it that the Brotherhood refuses a writ whose target a Nisswo protects (*scales*:25 to :27), and the Nisswo
who stands at Lilmoth will extend that protection if asked rightly (Speech 50, or a thing of the player's own
given up to the water at his word: the purse). Done, three people tell the player what they have done, the Lilmoth
walkways greet a known killer of elders coldly for the rest of the game, and nothing else is taken away.

**SS07, The Low Scale.** Ohl-Katta's writ is a black flask with a name scratched in the glass: **Chiss-Ra**, called
the Low Scale, one of the half-made, nine years a pupil, who sells the school's craft to the smugglers of Tailfall
Mound and, through them, to the garrison of Fort Reedmoth, which "pretends not to know" the den is there (D
althii-archipelago, Tailfall Mound). He leaves stakes of his own on people the fort finds inconvenient. To her he
is rot in the name. His case, made in the lower hall of the mound to a player who speaks before striking: "You
carry a dead man's brass. I carry nine years of his teaching. Which of us is the costume?" If he is a Shadowscale
the first law forbids his death; if nine years and no brass make him none, then a brass and no years make the
player none either. He is the hardest single fight in the line, on dangerous ground among four smugglers and the
Lilmothiit dead (a way round through the tomb-rooms for a sneak; a bow from the broken dome). He can be killed;
told of the cradle, at which he laughs, stops, and says he will go and look, and is found there afterwards,
teaching nobody yet; or left, with Ohl-Katta's contempt.

**SS08, Whose Blade.** With the three writs closed, each living bidder asks for the two things the player holds,
the brass and the water key, and names the rival they cannot work beside: the Collector names Tirele (a foreign
murder-guild in his port, and swamp law on unruly foreigners was always the ku-vastei's first work: *scales*:65);
Tirele names the Collector; Ohl-Katta names nobody and asks the player to lay the brass down and go, since the
Shadow never chose them. Each stands where they have always stood, prepared: the Collector in the customs post
among his officers, Tirele in her shop with her own poisons to hand, Ohl-Katta in the cradle. The finale is the
line's work in its hardest form: the marks are the people the player knows best, nobody orders it but the player,
and no death is required. It ends when the brass and the key are put in one pair of hands, by a line to that
person, who must be alive:

- **The Charter** (the Collector). The school opens under the Wardens' seal, a customs guard stands at its door,
  and the eggs will be raised to a state that pays them. The province has judges again and they have a master.
- **The Family** (Tirele). A black hand is on the door. The eggs will be raised to the Brotherhood, with the
  choice at coming of age promised by a woman who cannot promise there will be a Brotherhood.
- **The Giving** (Ohl-Katta). The old way, whole: the Shadow chooses, nobody is asked, the judges belong to no
  one. The player is no longer of it, and is paid like a friend of the tribe.
- **The End** (Waits-at-Stairs). The fourth stake is driven at the stairs, the brass goes into the water, the
  eggs go to the keeper of Archon's landward tree to hatch as harbour folk, and Reemha has her clutch. The marsh
  has no judges, and the watcher has nothing left to watch.
- **Alone**: the player tells Waits-at-Stairs they will keep both, and is Archon's ku-vastei as he was, with the
  cradle shut and unraised. Nobody ranks above them and every living bidder is cold to them. She asks one thing:
  "Who drives yours?"

The four people each say, when asked, what the other hands would cost. Whoever of the bidders is dead cannot be
given anything; their rivals say so. The journal records what was done and does not rule on it. The Night-Reed
read the stored number of the first ending (province-plan §3.10: no chartered Shadowscale leads thieves).

## 3. The first three quests

Common to all three.

- **Waits-at-Stairs** stands outside the school's shut door for the whole line and gives each stake. The line
  cannot go on without her through SS04: her death fails a running quest of those four with the faction's message.
  Nobody is essential. People stand where they are placed.
- **One working stage.** After the stake is given each quest waits at one working stage. What the player learns
  there opens lines and moves nothing, so it can be learnt in any order or not at all. The quest moves when the
  mark dies or when a line settles the matter another way; the report to Waits-at-Stairs reads who is alive and
  which stages are done, and the outline gives the order in which her lines are tried.
- **A mark's death is the player's choice** (the form's `choice` on its death trigger). The line never asks how or
  by whose hand; journal entries on a death say only that the person is dead.
- **Every stake can be laid down.** A line to Waits-at-Stairs returns it undriven and ends the quest completed,
  with her judgement of that and no gift.
- **Naming the stake to the mark's face** is always possible and always dangerous: the mark makes their case, and
  the player then chooses among lines. No mark's guilt is settled by a check.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. A place is found by its map marker and by a named person's
  directions from something fixed; a thing by the name the game shows on it. Skill thresholds are fixed. Each failed
  check has its own refusal and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms; nothing is modelled. A stake is
  a readable thing; the record writer picks an existing form that reads as a short dark tally.
- "Night" in what people say is their word only; no step depends on the hour.

### SS01. The Stranger He Asked For

**What a new player is told, and when.** Archon's people say, as rumour, that the old ku-vastei by the shut school
has not been seen. Waits-at-Stairs, by the school's door, has five optional lines before any job, two or three
sentences each: *the shut school* (where the marsh's judges were raised from eggs every tribe gave; shut fourteen
years; the harbour pays her to see nobody opens it); *ku-vastei* ("one who brings needed change. He hears a wrong,
judges it alone and ends the one who did it. There was never another court out here"); *the old one* (Ux-Teeba, the
last who still serves, in the hut beside the school, whose door has not opened in three days); *why me* ("He has
asked me for a stranger for fourteen years. No townsman will open a ku-vastei's door, and he will not have me do
it"); *why I might not* ("I do not know what he wants a stranger for. None who went in told me, and most came out
quickly"). "Not today" stays open and free. The job is one sentence: go in and see.

**The hidden truth.** Ux-Teeba is dead in his chair, three days, of the cup he asked for. On his knees is his last
stake, cut but lacking its name. It answers a true grievance: a rice raft of Xi-Inou, the *Mud-Hen*, went onto the
bar at the estuary mouth and three hands drowned (D nushara-bayou, Xi-Inou, "a raft that left for Archon and did
not arrive"). The harbour says, as it has for thirty years, that one hand puts ships on that bar: whoever piloted
the Senchal trader *Tidewarden* onto it in the war years (D nushara-bayou, Wreck of the Tidewarden). The old man,
too frail to wade to the wreck, cut the stake for "the hand that put her there, as it put the Tidewarden there".
The *Tidewarden*'s pilot was **Sounds-Twice**, who was paid forty drakes by men who wanted her moon sugar, drowned
nobody, and has piloted honestly for thirty years since. He never touched the raft: a raft takes no pilot and
steers by the channel lantern. The lantern was shifted by the men of the night quay below Xi-Inou's landing, whose
headman is called **False-Lantern**, and the *Mud-Hen*'s rice is in their cache (D nushara-bayou, Xi-Inou, "a
smuggler's cache under the landing"). So the stake is half right about the wrong man.

### SS02. Letters of Marque

Offered once SS01 has ended completed. If Olan-Gei is already dead, Waits-at-Stairs says the second stake was
answered before it was given, and SS03 is offered.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Waits-at-Stairs gives the second stake and three sentences: a privateer
is a pirate with the harbour's paper, called letters of marque, which makes his takings lawful prizes; this one
killed a man who had yielded; the dead man's sister left the grievance on the stairs in the spring and still comes.
The three gifts on the hut's table say who else is interested. The Collector, called on, explains himself in lines
the player picks: what the Wardens are (the province's thin government, which lives on tolls), what he is (their
customs chief here), and what he offers the bearer of the brass, which he calls "a letter of marque for a knife":
pay, standing and his protection, and to begin with, 200 gold for this stake driven and the key to the impounded
prize. One true reason to hesitate is said by Three-Ropes, if asked: "He has not signed a lawful prize in a year.
Ask him what he has against the one captain I have left."

**The hidden truth.** In the spring **Olan-Gei**, called Fair-Prize, master of the chartered sloop *Charter's
Edge*, took the Thorn coaster *Reed Wren* off the bar. She fought. Her mate killed Fair-Prize's steersman, then
laid his blade on the deck. Fair-Prize cut him down where he stood. He put the *Wren*'s two living hands ashore
at Archon unharmed. By the old law it is a plain wrong: a man who had yielded. The Collector has refused to call
the prize lawful (D nushara-bayou, Archon, "a privateer crew whose prize the customs officers refuse to call
lawful") and gives that killing as his reason. His real reason is in the *Wren*'s hold, which his men have kept
locked since: under her saltrice is moon sugar, his own private venture, on which no duty was ever paid.
Fair-Prize looked in the hold before the customs sealed it, and has been making the Collector pay for his silence
since. The stake is true. The state wants it driven for another reason entirely.

### SS03. Nine Drakes

Offered once SS02 has ended completed. If Marcan Jeel is already dead, Waits-at-Stairs says so and gives the rank.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Waits-at-Stairs gives the third stake and says what is on it; that the
man ran from Fort Reedmoth before the old one could reach him; that the dead man's brother, Juleem, hauls on
Archon's wharf and their mother keeps her house at Sal-Axan, the village beside the fort; and where both lie (by
the harbour boat to Uluo and the road east, or the cart road south-west across the strait: D nushara-bayou,
Archon; D althii-archipelago, Sal-Axan). Tirele Sadryn, called on at her shop because of her purse, says in lines
the player picks what the Brotherhood is ("a family that kills for whoever asks properly and pays; your order was
our school"), what she is to it, what she offers (coin for each death and no questions, ever), and one thing more:
Juleem has made the Brotherhood's prayer against the sergeant, so this death is owed twice, and she will pay for
it as well. The Collector, if asked, says a deserter is the Wardens' to punish and that Reedmoth's quartermaster
pays for word of one.

**The hidden truth.** A year ago **Marcan Jeel**, toll sergeant at Fort Reedmoth's chain, held Okan-Ru, a
raft-hand of Sal-Axan, under the water over a toll of nine drakes, and held him too long. The fort called it a
drowning. Okan-Ru's brother Juleem left the grievance on Archon's stairs. Before the stake was cut, Marcan Jeel
walked out of the fort, went to the dead man's mother, **Bimeesh**, and asked her for his sentence. She gave him
the native one for a killing: an Owing (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:66` to `:77`). He draws her nets and mends
them, and will until her hands stop. The village knows, and hides him from the fort, which jails deserters, and
from Juleem, who was not asked. Juleem, when the old one grew too slow, bought the Brotherhood's prayer from the
bookseller. So three judgements lie on one man: the stake, the contract and the Owing; and only the Owing was
passed by the person the wrong was done to.

## 6. For the other lines

For the consistency pass (nw-05 step 5). None of these changes another line's stages.

- **The Lamp-Bearers.** The egg-thieves' pilot is **Olik-Tei**, an Archon pilot. This line never meets him: he is
  named by Sounds-Twice and by Ohl-Katta, and her lines read whether he is alive ("the boats have stopped coming").
  *Who keeps the door:* Ux-Teeba made the bargain with the Vicecanon two years ago, through Olik-Tei, and meant
  it; he never asked how the north's clutches were got. Since his death the door is kept by Ohl-Katta, a
  Black-Tongue egg-keeper, who knows the eggs are taken and holds that the Shadow's claim is the older. The
  townswoman who watches the door is **Waits-at-Stairs**; she lets the baskets round to the water and will take
  LB08's clutch. The water stairs are the same stairs on which Archon leaves offerings for the drowned, and their
  grille opens only to the key this line gives in SS04, so LB08's "who is behind the door, the line never learns"
  holds. The keeper of Archon's landward tree is named here: **Deesh-Meeus**. After this line's last quest the
  school may be the Wardens', the Brotherhood's, the Black-Tongues' or shut for good; LB08 reads the same in each,
  since it never goes in. The senior rank on this side is Writ-Keeper.
- **The Night-Reed.** The Wardens' customs chief at Archon is named here, Collector Lartius Xeen; he may be dead
  after SS08 and richer or poorer after SS02, and the customs strongroom is untouched by this line. The ending
  that should shut that line's leadership is The Charter (`ss08.master` = charter). The bookseller is this line's;
  the fence is not.
- **Reed-Sail.** Xi-Inou's raft-master is named here, Nuleem-Ja, and may be dead or gone to Inoio after SS05.
  Olan-Gei is a captain chartered by Archon's harbour and is not the half-chartered captain of Skeith. The
  drowning at Fort Reedmoth's chain is a year old and its sergeant is gone; the quarrel over the chain is that
  line's.
- **The Nisswo.** SS06 asks one thing of the Nisswo who stands at Lilmoth (province-plan §4, "Lilmoth's grave"):
