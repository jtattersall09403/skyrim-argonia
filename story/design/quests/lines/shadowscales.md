# The Shadowscales: the plot

This page is the plot of the Shadowscales, the Argonian assassins, written on 2026-11-16 to the owner's notes in
[the factions overhaul](../../../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05c-factions-overhaul.md) and revised on
2026-11-17 to his story explorer notes. The Shadowscales have two rivals, the Lampbearers ([lamps](lamps.md)) and the
Eelskins ([thieves](thieves.md)). Each of the three lines tells its own late story; the rivals appear in it with the
same leaders and the same seats. Every quest is set in a place whose source holds what the quest needs, and a bracket says which source place gives it and how we know: what that source's buildings, interiors and placed pieces are. Everything is inside
the quest ceiling ([quest-system](../../../../.claude/skills/quest-system/SKILL.md)), with the blocks listed in
section 8.

**Words.** *The Shadowscales*: Argonian assassins. Argonians born under the sign of the Shadow are given to the order
as hatchlings. *Ku-vastei*: "those who bring needed change", the Argonian name for the Shadowscales when they served
as judges and enforcers. *The brass*: the brass medallion that every Shadowscale wears. *A contract*: one killing the
order takes on. *The Eldest*: the order's head. *Shadow-born*: an Argonian born under the Shadow. *The Black-Tongues*:
the Murkmire tribe that gives the order most of its hatchlings. *The Dark Brotherhood*: the guild of assassins of
Cyrodiil, a foreign order, which once trained the Shadowscales. *The Lampbearers*: the investigators of Soulrest. *The Eelskins*: the thieves' guild of the south.

## 1. What the faction is

The Shadowscales are a secret order of Argonian assassins who kill for the Argonian people. A Shadowscale takes a
contract on someone who has wronged Argonians (a seller of hatchlings, a man who sells other men's Owings, a killer
the law cannot reach) and kills them cleanly and unseen, in the way the order sets for that contract.

The Archon school, where the order trained, has stood sealed since 4E 187 (`archon.md:48-52`), and the order leaves
it sealed. Its novices train in secret at Ra'tal Den, a Lilmothiit ruin of the Banujha Basin, where the Eldest holds
its trials. Its door to the world is the house of Beem-Xal, the old ku-vastei who lives beside the sealed school in
Archon, where the handler Hulee-Tah meets the order's people. Its branch in Lilmoth is Black-Tongue Brews, a
brew-house whose keepers send the order its Shadow-born. Its skills are Sneak, Lockpicking, Archery, One-Handed and
Light Armour, with Illusion: Muffle, Invisibility, Night-Eye and Throw Noise ([spell-groups](../spell-groups.md);
Chameleon exists only as an enchantment). Its rivals are the Lampbearers, who hunt killers. The Eelskins, who
steal, are rivals too. Joining gives the player contracts with a bonus for the clever way, a unique enchanted thing nearly every
contract, and the order's trainers.

**Why they are back.** In canon the order served Black Marsh as ku-vastei, judges and enforcers who "act with
impunity" (`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:61-67`). Each wears a brass medallion (`:68`), and one Shadowscale
may not kill another (`:69-70`). The Archon school closed in 4E 187 and the Shadowscale Veezara died in Skyrim in
4E 201, called the last of them (`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:85`). The Black-Tongues have had nowhere to
send their Shadow-born since (`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md:214-216`). **X:** an old Shadowscale who stayed in
Black Marsh, Ukka-Teel, took them in. Veezara was real, and his death was real. The word that he was the last was
spread by the order as cover, and the old arbiter Teesh-Vaal, once a Shadowscale, carried it.

**Their history and their name.** After joining, the player can ask Beem-Xal about both. He answers: "We judged the
marsh before the Empire came, and the Empire let us go on judging, because the marsh obeyed us. We are named for the
Shadow we are hatched under, and for the brass scale at our necks. The school shut fourteen years ago, and the world
was told we died with it."

## 2. The truth under the story

**What the player is shown.** The order takes the player in by a test. It sends them on contracts against the
sellers of hatchlings and the men who trade in other men's Owings, each with a clever way that pays a bonus. Then
the Eldest learns who buys the hatchlings: Lucan Vetrix, the Dark Brotherhood's agent. When the player kills
Vetrix, they find that the Lampbearers had already caught him, and that he told them where the order trains. Two
rivals then offer the order a way out, and the Eldest leaves the choice to the player.

**What is true.** The Dark Brotherhood's Listener at Cheydinhal wants the Archon school reopened and the
Shadow-born for her own (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:203-223`; `argonia-4e201-state.md` §2.4). Her agent, Vetrix, has
bought Shadow-born hatchlings for a year from the men of the washes, to train them for the Brotherhood. No one
outside the order knows it lives, and Ukka-Teel means to keep it so. Since the school was sealed the order has had
no one's leave to judge, so in the marsh's eyes each of its killings is a murder (**X**, from `archon.md:48-52`). A
case before an arbiter that proves the order lives would tell the whole south, and the Listener, where its
hatchlings train. Ukka-Teel tells the player he kills for the hatchlings' sake, and that is half the truth.

## 4. Joining

**The rumours.** People in Lilmoth, Archon, Uluo, Blackrose and Soulrest speak of the Shadowscales when asked about
death, the Black-Tongues or the old school at Archon. "The last of the Shadowscales died in Skyrim, they say." "The
old folk say a brass medallion still turns up on a drowned man now and then." "A drowned Argonian lies under the
landing at Xi-Inou with brass at his neck, and the raft-master's family will not touch him." The first line starts
the small quest *The Shadowscales*, with no marker. Each rumour the player hears goes into its journal. The third
names the landing at Xi-Inou, and a marker then shows it; Archon's harbour boatmen sail nearest.

**Being let in.** Under the plank landing at Xi-Inou lie a drowned man's bones and his satchel (the record's landing
and the cache under it). In the satchel are a brass medallion and a sealed writ. The writ is a contract in the
order's old form. It names Golven Rethan, a Dunmer smuggler who puts in at Xi-Inou's quay by night, as the man who
drowned a raft-boy who saw his cargo. "Needed change. The brass goes back to the old one by the closed school in
Archon when the work is done." The player may do the work (quest 1) and carry the brass to Archon (quest 2). The dead
man is a test: Hulee-Tah left a smuggler's bones there with his own satchel, and the order watches who finishes the
work. This is how the order has always found its outsiders, and it is why the rumour of brass on drowned men exists.
The test is easy: Rethan is one man with two crewmen, at a D2 hamlet.

A player who takes the writ to Golven Rethan to warn him, or to the Lampbearers, ends the test. The small quest fails,
and the order never sends another writ.

**What shuts the door.** The Shadowscales' door shuts when the player takes the rank of Flame at the end of the
Lampbearers' 4, The Walled Cellar, or is given the key to the Eelskins' hall at the end of the Eelskins' 4, The
Lampbearer's Man. *The
Shadowscales* then fails, and the satchel under the landing is gone. In this line, the Lampbearers' and the
Eelskins' doors shut at the end of quest 4, when the player takes the brass. Hulee-Tah warns them first: a player who
is a Lampbearer or an Eelskin of the lowest rank is put out of it, and neither will take them again.

## 5. The acts

The Shadowscales' line is thirteen quests in three acts. Act I is the test and the first contracts. Act II is
contracts on the order's enemies, harder each time, each with a clever way. Act III is the Brotherhood's agent, the
Lampbearers' discovery, the choice of ally and the trial.

**Contracts.** Hulee-Tah names each contract's target and its bonus way: a clever method the order prefers. The bonus
is paid if the method's thing was used (a rope cut, a chain pulled, a lamp shot down, a horn taken) or its condition
met (a captive alive, nobody else dead) and the target is dead. A contract done any other way is paid without the
bonus. Nearly every contract also earns a unique enchanted thing, as the Dark Brotherhood's did in Oblivion. Killing
anyone but the target costs part of the pay, and the journal says so before the player goes. The journal names the
target's place and the area where he is found, and the marker leads there; the trap's rope, chain or lamp is for the
player to find.

Danger climbs with the line: a hamlet and a farm (D2), Archon (D0), the ruins and burnt forts (D3), an abandoned
town, a drowned village and a bandits' tower (D4) and, last, Ra'tal Den (D5). Locks climb with it: Novice in Act I,
Adept and Expert in Act II, Master in Act III. Where the player may fight or sneak, the journal says so. Every
objective has a marker. Archon, Lilmoth, Soulrest and Blackrose have boat stops, and the journal of each contract
names the nearest.

**Ranks and rewards.** Each is fixed and hand-made. The order pays fixed gold for each contract: 100 in
quest 1, rising to 1,000 in quest 8. The bonus doubles it.

### Act I: the brass

**1. The Drowned Man's Writ** (Xi-Inou, a raft hamlet on Archon's estuary). The player takes the satchel from under
the landing and reads the writ. Golven Rethan drinks at the quay by night with two of his crew (checked: Xi-Inou's source is Keeba Hollow from King of the Murkmire, an Argonian village of huts with dock pieces, with stilt houses from its Ree-An-Wo, and the plank landing is an addition, with the smugglers placed on the quay by night). The journal says that the player may fight or go quietly, and that the order
prefers the stack. A stack of logs is roped above the landing (Skyrim's rolling-log trap, placed), and its rope can
be cut when he stands below it. The player can also shoot him from the bank (Archery), stab him on his way to his
boat (Sneak and One-Handed) or fight all three. His crew are few and stand at the quay's end, so a quiet player can
reach him. A new character can do it. *Leads on:* the writ says to carry the brass to the old one by the closed
school in Archon. The journal marks Beem-Xal's house, a short walk up the estuary.

**2. The Old One by the School** (Archon, the eastern harbour city). Beem-Xal takes the brass and says nothing for a
long moment. Then he lets the player into his back room, where Hulee-Tah waits, alive: the drowned man was his test.
He tells the player that the order lives and what it does. He sets a first trial. A brass token lies in the
strongbox of the Archwardens' customs officer on the quay (checked: Archon's source is Journey to Baan Malur's waterside city of 162 interiors, whose dockhouses and houses serve for Beem-Xal's house beside the sealed school, with a back room and an alchemy table of Skyrim's set in it, while the customs post on the quay and the sealed school's locked door are additions the place's record lists), and the officer sits beside it
by day. The journal says: "Take the brass token from the customs post on the quay unseen." The marker leads to the
post. The player waits for the officer to turn to the boats and slips in (Sneak), picks the strongbox (a Novice
lock), or lifts its key from his belt (Pickpocket). Seen, they fail the trial and must pay the officer's fine before
trying again. *Leads on:* Hulee-Tah pays 100 gold, makes the player a Hatchling and gives the first true contract: a
toll sergeant who eats each night at Tal-Neesh Farm.

**3. The Supper at Tal-Neesh** (Tal-Neesh Farm, a rice farm on the island road near Fort Reedmoth). The contract is
the toll sergeant of Fort Reedmoth. He drowned a Shadow-born child of the farm's tenants rather than let the order
have her, and he eats each night at the farm (the record says the family sells rice to the fort's cooks and soldiers trade there on credit) (checked: Tal-Neesh's source is Hlaalu Farm from Skyrim, a plain farm with one interior that serves as the low house, and the paddy and the marsh edge are added). Hulee-Tah teaches the player a poison: he shows them the recipe, and the player learns the
effects of deathbell and nightshade. The player gathers the deathbell at the marsh edge past the snakes and buys
nightshade at Black-Tongue Brews in Lilmoth. They make the poison at the alchemy table in Beem-Xal's house. Then they
enter the farmhouse while the family works outside and poison the sergeant's own bowl on the table. The farm's dog is
the only watcher at the door; a player who freed Ulasi in the Chainbreakers' 13, The Vicecanon's Price, has a
sleeping draught from her for it. The sergeant comes in from the road to eat, away from where the player stands, and dies
at his meal. A player who would rather can kill him on the road to the fort (One-Handed, Archery), without the bonus.
*Leads on:* Hulee-Tah sends the player after a man who sells hatchlings. He is at Tailfall Mound.

**4. The Hatchling Seller** (Tailfall Mound, a Lilmothiit ruin near Fort Reedmoth, where smugglers hide). The
smugglers' headman buys Shadow-born hatchlings from desperate families and sells them on. Two hatchlings sit caged
in the lower hall (checked: Tailfall Mound's source is Huluxinoag from King of the Murkmire, an Argonian ruin with one interior of cave pieces that serves as the smugglers' lower hall, and a second interior, Wajeem-Krona's cave rooms, serves as the tomb-rooms beyond it, while the cracked domes are an addition). The smugglers stand at the hall's mouth and on its stair. The journal says: "Kill the
headman in the lower hall." The player sneaks past the smugglers or fights them. The headman sits at his table under
a cracked roof (the record's cracked domes; Skyrim's rockfall trap, placed above his table, with its pull-chain on
the wall). The chain brings the roof down on him. The player can also shoot him (Archery) or knife him. The cage's
lock is Apprentice. Freed, the hatchlings go to Black-Tongue Brews, and the screen fades as they leave. *Leads on:*
the headman's sale-notes name his buyer only as "the rice buyer of Lilmoth". Hulee-Tah gives the player the brass,
the rank of Brass. Taking it shuts the other doors (section 4).

### Act II: the order's enemies

**5. The Spell-Fire** (Mazurath, a ruined Velothi holding on the headland south of Khiar). The smugglers' boss of
Mazurath ships moon sugar into the bayou villages, and two children of Khiar have died of it. His crew keep their
cache in the sea-cave, which a swimmer reaches from the water. Ancestor ghosts walk the halls (checked: Mazurath's halls are built from Fort Velothi Pass in Caves of Morrowind, a Redoran bulwark. Its main hall, foyer and barracks, with metal cages and anchor chains, serve as the upper tier and the halls. The sea-cave is a second interior from Broken Oar Grotto in Skyrim, a sea grotto of waist-deep water, reached through a door at the waterline.). The boss keeps a Khiar fisherman captive on the upper tier (the record's hook).
Hulee-Tah teaches Muffle before the player goes. The journal says: "Kill the boss on the upper tier of Mazurath. The
order would have the fisherman live." The bonus way is to free the fisherman (an Adept lock on his cell) before the
boss dies. With Muffle the player passes the ghosts in the halls unheard. They can also fight the ghosts, or shoot the
boss from the halls below the tier. *Leads on:* Hulee-Tah pays 400 gold. The next contract is a bandit leader at Fort
Lowmoth.

**6. The Burnt Fort** (Fort Lowmoth, a burnt Imperial fort on the island road near Fort Reedmoth). The bandit leader
of Fort Lowmoth robs the island road and sells young men of Sal-Axan into Owings to whoever pays him, though they owe
nothing. The Reedmoth garrison has a price on his head and will not go into the ruin (the record's hooks). He watches
the road from the burnt shell, where his bandits hang a lamp over the oil they burn for light (Skyrim's hanging oil
lamp and oil pool, placed). A shot that brings the lamp down sets the oil alight under him. The journal says: "Kill
the bandit leader in the burnt shell of Fort Lowmoth." The player gets close enough to shoot unseen past the lookout,
fights the bandits in the shell, or knifes him at the strongroom's iron door (checked: Fort Lowmoth's source is a ruined fort from Black Marsh North with 551 fort pieces and one interior, a jail block, which serves as the strongroom behind the iron door, and the charred timbers and lookout post are added). His chest holds the names of the men he sold. *Leads on:* Hulee-Tah pays 500
gold and sends the player into the washes, to Khariton.

**7. The Watch Captain** (Khariton, an abandoned Kothringi town in the washes). The Blackguards have a watch post of tents at Khariton (checked: Khariton's source is a hamlet near Way to Ruins in Black Marsh & Valenwood with Argonian stilt-hall pieces from Keeba Hollow, and the Blackguards' post of tents is an addition made from camp pieces), and their watch captain killed a ku-vastei of Lilmoth last winter.
Hulee-Tah teaches Night-Eye, since the dead town is dark under its rotted stilt houses. The journal says: "Kill the
watch captain at the Blackguards' post in Khariton." The bonus way is to take the post's horn from its peg first, so
that no one can raise the watch, and then kill the captain. Blackguards walk the town in pairs, with gaps a quiet
player can use. The player can also fight them, or shoot the captain from the footings. *Leads on:* Hulee-Tah pays
700 gold. The next contract is on the Gideon road.

**8. The Signal Tower** (Nine Reeds Waystation, a ruined Imperial tower on the road north of Gideon). The bandit
leader of Nine Reeds takes Argonian carters off the road and holds them in the tower until their kin pay. Those whose
kin cannot pay, he sells as Owings to the first buyer. Archers watch the road from the tower top, and his men camp in
the granary (checked: Nine Reeds' source is Driftshade Sanctuary from Skyrim, an Imperial ruin with two interiors that serve as the tower's rooms and the smugglers' cellar, and the granary block and the hearth platform on the tower top are additions). Hulee-Tah teaches Invisibility. At dusk the leader comes
down to the tower door to see the road, under the old portcullis (Skyrim's portcullis and lever, placed at the door),
whose lever is on the stair. The journal says: "Kill the bandit leader at the tower of Nine Reeds." The bonus way is
to drop the portcullis on him. The player can slip past the granary and the stair with Invisibility and Muffle,
fight up to him, or shoot him from the reeds. The carters' shackles open with an Expert lock or the leader's key, and
when the player frees them the screen fades and they are gone. *Leads on:* Hulee-Tah pays 1,000 gold. The Eldest
comes to Beem-Xal's house, makes the player a Ku-Vastei and speaks to them for the first time. He has found who
buys the hatchlings.

### Act III: the Brotherhood's agent and the Lampbearers

**9. The Caged Hatchlings** (Sheh-Ara, a half-sunken stilt village east of Lujei; Lilmoth). Ukka-Teel tells the
player that the "rice buyer of Lilmoth" is Lucan Vetrix, who buys for the Brotherhood. He keeps hatchlings at
Sheh-Ara before they are shipped. The journal says: "Free the hatchlings at Sheh-Ara." The marker leads to the drowned
village; the raft of Lujei Ferry crosses from the Blackrose side, and Sheh-Ara is a short walk east of Lujei. Four of Vetrix's hired men guard a cage on
the minder's platform, and crocodiles lie in the flooded yard below (checked: Sheh-Ara's source is Keeba Hollow from King of the Murkmire, which gives the Argonian stilt halls and posts, and the minder's platform is an addition, with the crocodiles, the men and the cage placed). The player frees the hatchlings (the cage's Expert lock, or its key on the men's leader), and the
screen fades as they go. Then the journal says: "Search Vetrix's room at the stilt-hall tavern in Lilmoth." The room
has an Expert lock (checked: the stilt-hall tavern is the taverna of King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth, and the place's record lists rooms to rent in it). In it lie the Listener's letter, which tells Vetrix to buy the Shadow-born and find where the
order trains, and a note from Marcella Dovi: the Lampbearers have caught him at the hatchling trade, and she offers him
his freedom if he tells her where the order trains its novices. *Leads on:* the Eldest says Vetrix's name goes into a
contract, and that it must be done before he speaks.

**10. The Rice Buyer** (Lilmoth, the rebuilt stilt city). Vetrix has left the tavern for a room at the harbour's
lodging house (checked: the lodging house is a named interior of King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth), with two hired men, to wait for
a ship out. The journal says: "Kill Lucan Vetrix at the lodging house on Lilmoth's harbour." The bonus way is that
nobody dies but him. The player can slip past the hired men (Sneak) and knife him, shoot him from the walkway outside
the door, or fight all three. On his body is his answer to Marcella, sent the day before: he told her that the
order's novices train at Ra'tal Den, in return for being let go. The journal: "Vetrix sold the Lampbearers the place
where the order trains, for his freedom." *Leads on:* the Eldest calls the player to Beem-Xal's house.

**11. Whose Offer** (Archon; Lilmoth, the Eelskins' hall; Soulrest, the Lamp-Hall). This quest holds the line's
choice, written as the player meets it.

1. *At Beem-Xal's house*, Ukka-Teel speaks first. The Lampbearers know where the order trains. If Marcella brings a
   case before Teesh-Vaal, the whole south will know the order lives and that it kills without leave, and the
   Listener will know where its hatchlings are (section 2). Two offers have come to Beem-Xal's door. The
   journal says: "Hear Swims-Without-Ripples at the Eelskins' hall and Marcella Dovi at the Lamp-Hall, then bring the
   Eldest your answer." Markers lead to the hatch on Pusbottom's walkways and to the Lamp-Hall. Hulee-Tah gives the
   player a jar of eel-slime from Black-Tongue Brews for the cistern behind the hatch, where the Eelskins' ripper
   eels swim (checked: Lilmoth's source is King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth, whose sewers have a hideout room that serves as the Eelskins' hall under Pusbottom, entered through a hatch door that we set on the walkways, and whose first sewer room, the Surface Cisterns, serves as the cistern behind it).
2. *In the Eelskins' hall*, the Eelskins let the player through, since Swims has sent word; a player who attacks them
   ends that leave. Swims-Without-Ripples speaks first. The Lampbearers hunt both the order and her thieves. If the
   order kills Marcella Dovi, the Eelskins will take the Lampbearers' house at Truowa, and the two will share the
   south as one underworld.
3. *At the Lamp-Hall*, Marcella Dovi speaks first. Soulrest's merchant council, which pays a third of her Lampbearers,
   wants the Eelskins broken. If the order kills their leader, she will raid the hall, burn the case on the order and
   keep its name from the arbiter.
4. *The answer.* The player may hear both in either order. Back at Beem-Xal's house, Ukka-Teel asks whose offer the
   order takes, and leaves the choice to the player, who will do the work. The player's answer sets the stage for
   that ally, and the other's line closes.

*Leads on:* the Eldest gives the contract the answer calls for.

**12. The Contract** (Soulrest, the Lamp-Hall; Lilmoth, the Eelskins' hall).

- *With the Eelskins.* The contract is Marcella Dovi. The journal says: "Kill Marcella Dovi in the Lamp-Hall at
  Soulrest" (checked: Soulrest's source is Skyrim's Solitude, whose Castle Dour palace interiors are the halls from which the government quarter is to be built, so the Lamp-Hall with its hearing room is made from them as an addition the place's record lists). She sits each evening in the hearing room under its hanging lamp (Skyrim's hanging oil lamp, placed, with
  the lamp-oil spilt under her chair); the bonus way is to shoot it down. The Lamp-Hall's two guards stand at the
  front door, and its back door has an Expert lock. The player can also shoot her from the gallery above the hearing
  room, or knife her. The same night the Eelskins kill J'zahri and her Lampbearers at Truowa, and the house on the
  quay stands empty.
- *With the Lampbearers.* The contract is Swims-Without-Ripples. The journal says: "Kill Swims-Without-Ripples in the
  Eelskins' hall under Pusbottom." The truce is over, so the Eelskins in the cistern and the hall attack. The player
  passes them unseen with the eel-slime and Muffle, or fights them. The bonus way is to empty her strongbox (a Master
  lock, or her key by Pickpocket) before she dies. When she is dead the screen fades, and the Lampbearers raid the
  hall; the hall stands empty after.

*Leads on:* the Eldest calls the player to the trial at Ra'tal Den.

**13. The Trial at Ra'tal Den** (Ra'tal Den, a Lilmothiit ruin of the Banujha Basin). The order's last trial for the
player. The journal says: "Reach the Eldest in the hearth chamber of Ra'tal Den." The marker leads to the den, a walk
west from Blackrose's landing (checked: Ra'tal Den's source is Haj-Uxith from King of the Murkmire, a small Argonian ruin of 236 Argonian pieces whose one interior serves as the dome's dark passages and hearth chamber). Three novices wait in the dark passages, set against the player for the trial alone. The player
must pass them unseen, or fight past them without killing one (a Shadowscale may not kill a Shadowscale,
`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:69-70`); a dead novice fails the trial, and Hulee-Tah sets it again after three
days. The giant mudcrab of the hearth chamber (the record's) the player kills with any weapon. Ukka-Teel waits beside
the hearth. He makes the player an Elder, gives them his own dagger and tells them that when he dies the order is
theirs. *Leads on:* the line ends. Contracts go on, small and repeating, from Hulee-Tah.

## 6. The choice and its endings

**With the Eelskins: the joint underworld.**

- *It gives:* the Elder's rank and the Hook-and-Eye. The Lamp-Breaker from the contract. A share of the Eelskins'
  takings, 1,500 gold, which Swims sends to Beem-Xal's house. The Eelskins' fences buy from the player at the best
  price. Swims-Without-Ripples trains Pickpocket to Master. No investigator ever comes after the player again.
- *It costs:* the player killed the Lampbearers' chief. Soulrest's guards attack on sight until a bounty of 1,000
  gold is paid. Teesh-Vaal will never speak to the player again, and the Lampbearers' house at Truowa stands empty.

**With the Lampbearers: the law's blind eye.**

- *It gives:* the Elder's rank and the Hook-and-Eye. The Eel-Skin Gloves from the contract. The law's blind eye:
  once, Marcella clears the player's bounty in Soulrest, Truowa and Lilmoth. The Lampbearers never take a case against
  a Shadowscale, and they keep the order's name out of every hearing. Teesh-Vaal, glad the order lives, trains
  Illusion to Master.
- *It costs:* every Eelskin left alive attacks on sight, and their fences close. The player killed a thief who never
  killed anyone, for a merchant council's purse, and the journal says so.

## 7. Rivals

- **The Lampbearers** ([lamps](lamps.md)), the investigators of Soulrest. Their chief is Marcella Dovi, in the
  Lamp-Hall in Soulrest's government quarter; J'zahri heads their branch in the house on Truowa's quay; their arbiter
  is Teesh-Vaal. In this line they appear as Vetrix's captors and as one of the two offers.
- **The Eelskins** ([thieves](thieves.md)), the thieves of the south. Their leader is Swims-Without-Ripples, in their
  hall in the drowned cellars under Pusbottom in Lilmoth; their outpost is Corso Delling's hulk at the Blackrose
  boardwalks. In this line they appear as the other offer.
- Each of the three lines tells its own late story. Only the leaders, the seats and the rivalries are shared.

### What it asks of the ceiling

One block for this line and one shared with the other two, each proven once before its first use:

- **The poisoned meal.** Using the sergeant's bowl while holding the poison takes the poison and sets a stage. When
  the player next goes outside, the sergeant, who eats inside, dies. The simplest build: the existing block "a stage
  that kills one named person", used here for this one contract, with the kill made away from the player.
- **The fade after which a person is gone** (the freed hatchlings and captives, and the raid on the Eelskins' hall).
  The same block as the Lampbearers', the Eelskins' and the Chainbreakers'.

The rest is inside the ceiling. A bonus way is a named thing used (a rope, a chain, a lamp, a lever, a horn, a bowl,
a strongbox) or a named person alive, and the target dead, all of which the game can know. The truce at the Eelskins'
hall is a stage during which its people are not hostile. The answer given in quest 11 is a line that sets one of two
stages.
