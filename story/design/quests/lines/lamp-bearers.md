# The Lamp-Bearers: the line's story

The story of the detectives' faction of decision 0025
([0025](../../../../docs/decisions/0025-a-detectives-faction.md)): nine cases, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the province plan gives the line ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §3.2, §4, §7). A quest writer turns each
full outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world (`story/design/world/`; "D valley-of-storms,
Surkina" is that place's record or hooks in that region's file). Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is
`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation from them (decision 0032).
The places are not built yet, so a place is named by what the digest says it is, and everything else a quest needs
of it is a row of section 4. The faction had no quest rows in the old design: its foundation is decision 0025, the
`detectives` profile and the detectives' notes of the questline principles.

**Words used below.** *Vicecanon*: a magistrate of Stormhold; the city invented the office, and two sit at once,
one for each people. *The west quarter*: Stormhold's old Argonian quarter. *Egg-tender*: the keeper of a nest of
eggs. *Clutch*: one nest's eggs. *The Shadow*: the birth sign under which, by old custom, a hatchling was given
away to be raised a Shadowscale, one of the province's judges and killers; their school at Archon has been shut for
fourteen years. *The Owing*: the native sentence, months of owed work. *A lamp*: what people of the Stormhold
country call a Lamp-Bearer.

## 1. The faction in a page

**The thing they want.** To be able to say what happened. Black Marsh has no courts above the tribe except Gideon's
courthouse and the Vicecanons of the north ([province-vibe](../../province-vibe.md) §2), and in 4E 201 the last of
its old judges, the Shadowscales, are dead or dying (*state*:201 to :213). When a body is found, a nest is empty or
a stake is pulled, a village settles it by who is disliked. The Lamp-Bearers settle it by what can be held in the
hand.

**Where they come from (X).** Stormhold was the Dunmer slavers' foothold, and its western quarter was the quarter
of the Argonians who worked for them (`stormhold.md:6`, `:52` to `:54`). When Vicecanon Heita-Meen tried the Dres
slavers and hung them in cages on the Ayleid arches (`stormhold.md:67` to `:70`), her court had to know who had
been in the holds and who had put them there. The order's story is that its first members carried a lamp down into
the holds for her and said who was in them. The order has kept the lamp, the work and the name.

**Who they are.** A dozen people of both peoples in one room of Stormhold's old Ayleid compound, beside the town
mortuary (`stormhold.md:40` to `:41`), because the dead are brought there first.

- **Asks-Twice** (Argonian woman of the west quarter, old), the Keeper of the house. Her teaching is the order's:
  *a lamp shows; it does not strike*. She holds that a lamp-bearer names and then stands back, and that the day
  the order chooses sentences it is one more hand reaching for the empty chair. She took the lamp forty years ago
  after naming her own clutch-sister for a theft, rightly, and has not been asked to a west-quarter hatching
  since. Her fault is the other side of her rule: she has not looked at what standing back is paid with.
- **Varon Sedrys** (Dunmer man, third generation in the city, better in Jel than in Dunmeris), the player's peer
  and the rival inside the house. He holds that a name given quietly to a magistrate is a name the magistrate may
  drown, and that a finding belongs to the square. He is right more often than he is liked, and he acts on it:
  from the second case he is on the boat's trail by himself, and the player keeps finding him ahead of them, the
  worse for it.
- **Old Menas** (Dunmer man), keeper of the mortuary next door, who is not of the order, lets its people read
  the dead on his slab and sells what an alchemist needs.
- **The two Vicecanons**, who pay for every finding: **Keema-Tul** (Argonian woman, the west quarter's) and
  **Arvys Hleran** (Dunmer man, the Dunmer families'). "Several Vicecanons sit at once, one for each people" (D
  valley-of-storms, Stormhold); these are the two (X). Keema-Tul pays the rent of the Lamp House out of her own
  share of the crystal dues, and has for years. Hleran is dry, slow to cage anyone and careful of his own: he
  will not take a Dunmer on a boy's word, and he pays for the grave-stake of a digger's boy nobody asked him to
  bury. He also keeps a little of the Vicecanons' crystal back for himself.

**How they work (X).** A finding stands on **the thing, the tongue and the name**.

- *The thing*: something held. What was taken, what was left behind, what the dead carry, what a liar keeps in a
  locked room. A lamp-bearer goes in after it: a lock picked, a key lifted from a belt, a cellar entered under its
  owner's eyes, a body examined where it lies.
- *The tongue*: a person who says it, because they were shown the thing. A lie is not argued with; it is put
  beside the thing that refutes it.
- *The name*: said aloud by the lamp-bearer, once, to whoever asked for the lamp, with the thing put into their
  hand. There is no hearing and nothing is written. In the city the name goes to the Vicecanon of the named
  person's own people, so that no people is judged by the other (X, from "one for each people"); in a village to
  its reeve or tree-minder; among kin, to the kin. What is done then is theirs: an Owing, exile or, for the worst,
  the cage on the arch, which is the city's own old sentence and not a cell
  (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:222` to `:225`).

Because a lamp's name is believed, a wrong name is a real ruin, and it stands. And a suspicion is nothing: every
quarter has those. That is the weight of every case in the line: the truth of each is fixed, the player's
conclusion is their own, the world acts on the conclusion, and the one name the player most wants to give cannot
be given until there is a thing to put in a hand.

**Where a lamp counts.** In Stormhold and the farms and hamlets of its manner (Surkina and Tenmal, and far to the
south Julo, Luntoc and Yalugo: D province, Peoples: Shadowfen Border) a lamp is sent for and answered. Nowhere else.
The reed people of Jal-Veesa "refuse the Vicecanons' courts" (D valley-of-storms, Jal-Veesa), and a lamp who asks
about their pulled ward-stakes is told it is the reed people's business (province-plan §4). Helstrom's Root-Folk
owe a lamp nothing, the forts answer to the Wardens, and Archon has law of its own. The further the line goes from
Stormhold the less the lamp is worth and the more the work is done by nerve and skill alone.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **The Shadowscales' memory.** The old judges acted "with impunity" (*state*:205 to :207) and needed no proof;
  half the province thinks that was better, because it was quick and it ended feuds. A lamp is slow and ends
  nothing by itself.
- **The Night-Reed**, the thieves of Lilmoth, whose jobs are what the lamps are sent after. A thief says a lamp is
  a magistrate's dog. He is half right: see what the order hides.
- **The quarters themselves**, who would sooner settle a wrong by who is disliked, and who each know that the lamp
  is paid by the other quarter's Vicecanon too.
- **The Vicecanons**, the patrons, who want a name quickly more than they want the right one, and who decide what
  the square is told.

**What it pays and what it costs.** The Vicecanons' silver for each finding, fixed. The Keeper's gifts, which are
tools of the trade (vanilla pieces enchanted for Speech, Sneak, Lockpicking, Pickpocket and Alchemy; the record
writer names the forms). From the first rank a bed and a chest in the Lamp House and Old Menas's shelf at a
lamp's price; and a brass lamp to carry, which is worth coin in the Stormhold country: people there who charge a
stranger for what they saw tell a lamp of rank for nothing (lines gated on rank). It costs the player's ease: a
name given wrongly is lived with, and is seen (a man in a cage, a farm emptied). It also costs a door or two: the
order will not raise to senior rank a senior of the Shadowscales or of the Night-Reed, and those two will not
raise a senior lamp (province-plan §4: a gate on rank, which changes no stage).

**What it lies about.** Two things, and the line finds both.

1. *Whose lamp it was.* The first lamp-bearers knew the holds because they had carried the slavers' lamps into
   them. They were west-quarter collaborators who turned witness and were spared for it (X, from
   `stormhold.md:52` to `:54`). The order tells the second half of that.
2. *That it only shows.* Its house is paid for by one of the two people it names to, and a name given to her is
   hers to bury.

**Why a player wants in.** Each case is a break-in, a body, a liar and a decision, and the decision is theirs and
can be wrong. The cases join: the petty ones turn out to be the wake of one boat, the boat leads up to the person
who hired the lamp, and the player knows it long before they can prove it.

**The build leaning.** Speech first, and always as an act: a thing shown, a lie refuted, a person leaned on, a
name given. Sneak, Lockpicking and Pickpocket second, because what people hide is behind a lock, on a belt or
under a roof that is not the player's. Alchemy third, to read what was done: what a body drank, what moss a basket
is lined with. Fighting is never the whole of a case and is in every one: something or someone fixed and hard,
standing where the truth is (a crocodile in a drowned causeway, a quarter's bully on a doorstep, the dead of a
sunken hall, a culprit who would sooner fight than be named), with a fixed reward, and nearly always with a way
round. Nothing is levelled (decision 0035). The faction suits a talker first, a thief second and a reader of
bodies third; a fighter can finish every case. The profile also names Mysticism, which the quest form cannot gate
a line on; it has no step here (section 6).

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** By custom older than any state, a hatchling born under the Shadow was given away at birth
to be raised a Shadowscale (`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:61` to `:67`), and egg-tenders know each
clutch's hatching month and sign as they know its parents (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:74` to `:76`). The school at
Archon closed in 4E 187, and for fourteen years the tribes have had nowhere to send such clutches (*state*:214).
Two years ago Vicecanon Keema-Tul, whose clutch-brother was given and came home a judge the whole valley obeyed,
made a bargain with whoever now keeps a door at Archon: the north's Shadow clutches, carried south, against the
promise that in twenty years the north has judges again. Nobody gives to a shut door, and the mothers would not
have let them go, so she arranged for the clutches to be taken. An Archon pilot's boats carry them by the back
waters, past no toll post: they lift a causeway's stones, pull a weir's stakes and cut a ferry's rope to get by,
and are paid in uncut Stormhold crystal dug for her under the city. At each nest an old tender who remembers the
giving hands the clutch out and says it "sank into the roots", as a failed clutch does
(`topics/hist-and-sap.md:77` to `:78`).

At Stormhold's own pond the lie failed: a mother saw the empty nest before the tender could tell it, and cried
thief in the square. The quarters turned on each other. Keema-Tul then did the one thing that would calm them: she
sent for the Lamp-Bearers to find the hand that carried the eggs, meaning the finding to stop at the hand. The
line is the player following the boat instead.

**What the player knows, and when.** The line does not pretend its patron is above suspicion. A careful player
suspects her by the end of the second case, and the line lets them say so at once: asked "Who told Shuxal?",
Keema-Tul answers, "Find me the thing that says so, lamp, and I will hear the name, whoever's it is"; Asks-Twice
says a suspicion is what the quarters have. From then on the tension is the order's own rule turned on the
player: they are doing a guilty woman's tidying, case by case, and each thing they find she takes or thanks them
for. The turn (LB06) is not her name. It is the first thing that cannot be taken away, the eggs found alive, and
the Keeper of the house telling the player to put the proof out of sight.

The plan's range offers a case each at Gideon and Thorn. The story did not earn them: the first reading found the
line too long at ten, and its one Gideon case was the plot standing still. Gideon is spoken of in LB04 and not
visited.

**The ranks.** *Unlit* (on trial, LB01); *Wick* (LB01 done: the bed, the chest, the lamp); *Lamp-Hand* (LB03);
*Lamp-Bearer* (LB04); *Long Lamp* (LB06; the senior rank, refused to a senior Shadowscale or Night-Reed with its
own line); *Keeper* (the finale only, and only one ending gives it).

**The planned misjudgement** is LB04, where every thing in the room points at the wayhouse-keeper; and LB02 lets a
hasty player cage an innocent man with a true clue.

**Earlier choices** never change a later case's stages. They change greetings and single lines, read from stored
numbers; only the finale's choice carries into the world after the line. The line can be left at any rank: each
case closes its own matter, and a player who stops before LB06 leaves a city that believes its clutch sank.

**The house reacts.** After each case Asks-Twice, Varon and Old Menas greet with their own view of what the player
named and what it cost; a wrong name is said to the player's face by Varon and not at all by Asks-Twice, which is
worse. People of earlier cases come back: Shuxal stands at Sedgeweir in LB05; Ilver, if he is alive and above
ground, is one of the tongues of LB09; Waleesh is the one the west quarter's ending is given to. If a person the
next case cannot start without has been killed in between, Asks-Twice has a line that says so and the line of
cases ends there, with the faction's message.

### The later cases

**LB04, A Room at Tenmal.** A traveller came to Tenmal's wayhouse by river with a sack of Dres slave-irons and
"has not left his room since" (D valley-of-storms, Tenmal). He is dead in it, behind a door barred from inside.
The truth (X): he was Lucan Teegla, a Lukiul clerk of Gideon's courthouse, where the old northern court's seized
things were carried when the Pact's court ended. He was selling each iron back to the family whose mark is on it.
The big stamps are the Dunmer slaving houses. The small second stamp, a lamp, marks the west-quarter crew that
brought the captive in; and he carried the proof of what the lamp meant, the roll of Heita-Meen's court: the
lamp-carriers who bore witness and were spared, by name and house-mark. Tenmal's ferryman is of such a line, with
kin among the reed people. He put a Morrowind poison in the clerk's supper; the clerk felt it, barred his door
and died; the ferryman went in by the shutter on the landing side and took the roll, meaning to burn it, and has
not been able to. The player opens the room (the lock, the keeper's spare key lifted from her, or the shutter),
reads the body (a line to the keeper gated on Alchemy 40, or the same from Old Menas for a price), takes the
irons, and works a cast of six: the Dunmer wayhouse-keeper, whose mark is on most of the irons and who cooked the
supper; the ferryman; the pit-boss and his wife; a Dunmer trader whose raft of shackles is held at Fort
Willowmoth's gate and who was the clerk's carrier (D valley-of-storms, Fort Willowmoth; he is spoken of, and the
fort is not visited). The wayhouse-keeper can be named to Hleran on the irons alone, wrongly. The ferryman, faced
with an iron of his own line's stamp, does one of two things by the player's choice of words. Told he will be
named, he fights, a strong man with a boat-hook on his own landing. Offered the chance, he hands over the roll and
says "Then I will take the ferry", and a drowned man lies at the landing when the player has reported in
Stormhold. Then the roll is the player's. It can be read: the first name on it is the founder the order
venerates, and two of the house-marks are Keema-Tul's line and Asks-Twice's own. It goes, by a line, to one of
four: Asks-Twice, who burns it in front of the player and says why; Varon, who will read it out in the square;
Hleran, who pays well and now holds the west quarter and the Lamp House by the throat; or Old Menas, who lays it
in the mortuary with the unclaimed dead, true and no one's weapon. Whoever has it is how the house greets the
player afterwards.

**LB05, What the River Keeps.** Varon has gone south after the boat without leave, and has not come back.
Asks-Twice sends the player after both. At Sedgeweir "someone is pulling up the weir stakes at night and leaving
the crocodiles a way in" (D valley-of-storms, Sedgeweir): the player goes into the weir pool for what is caught
under the weir, a boatman's pole-shoe and a strongbox (the digest gives it). Shuxal may be standing on the bank,
working her Owing. At Ranvos Landing "a body washed up" (D valley-of-storms, Ranvos Landing): a boatman strangled
with tarred rope before he went into the water. Varon is in the roadhouse there with a Naga arrow through his
leg, ahead of the player and furious about it; he says what he saw and who he was following. At Julo "the
ferry-rope has been cut twice" and "a salt trader's body washed up at the paddies" (D lake-ulaish, Julo). The
truth (X): the lodger of LB01, Tallow, killed a crewmate over the crystal, was put off the boat for it, and has
gone to ground with the grave-robbers at the opened cairn on Julo's near bank; the salt trader knew his face and
died of it. Julo's grandmother, who "settles every quarrel", has settled both on the Naga band of the delta and
means to send her grandsons against them. The ground is deadly (D lake-ulaish, Julo) and warned of twice. The
player reads two bodies (Alchemy, or what is on them: the same rope), gets into the cairn camp by stealth, by a
hard fight or by the one robber who will talk and wants the lamp's quarry out of his camp, and has Tallow in
front of them at last. He fights if he is named to his face. He talks if he is shown the dead crewman's ring and
offered the river: the clutches, the stones, the tender at each nest, "a Vicecanon's stone on the mate's neck",
and that he never saw who gave it. Alive, he says he will go north and give himself to the lamps, and is found in
the cage at Stormhold; dead, his sea-chest key is on him. The grandmother is given a name: the Naga, or a man who
is dead or gone. A wrong one empties two places at her table by the time the player next comes from Stormhold.

**LB06, The Boat That Paid.** "A boat paid the toll at dusk and was never seen to leave the lake; a cellar under
the keep has been bricked shut since the Legion left" (D lake-ulaish, Fort Gysilla). The truth (X): the toll
rowers, "grandchildren of freed labourers", searched the boat and found a clutch under the sacks. One of them
killed both boatmen at the chain with a boat-hook. Captain Peradus Xil, of the Archein family that holds the keys,
found a Vicecanon's pass-stone on one of the dead, a cut amethyst on a cord, and understood that his garrison had
killed a magistrate's carriers. The bodies and the baskets went into the old cellar and its hatch was shut again.
The clutch was rowed that night to the village tree at Yalugo (D lake-ulaish, Yalugo: "a small village tree"), to
the house of the old cook who once fed the garrison, and the eggs are alive there. They are Stormhold's. The
player is a stranger on an islet of ten people who all know: into the cellar by the captain's key, by the
quartermaster's greed or by the grate on the water side; through the skeevers the record puts there; two bodies
killed from behind by something that is not a soldier's sword; and the stone. The rower, faced with it, says what
he did and that he would do it again. The choice of the name: to the captain, whose drill-book has one answer for
it; to the factor of Truowa's council, which "would pay to see the garrison replaced"; or to nobody. Whatever is
chosen, the captain keeps the fort: Truowa is given a grievance to shout. The choice of the eggs: the cook gives
the clutch into the player's pack, wrapped in sap-wet moss, to carry home to the pond and put into Waleesh's
hands; or it stays to hatch at Yalugo, as a clutch that belongs to the tree it hatches under. At Stormhold the
crystal-cutter says whose cut the stone is. Asks-Twice, shown it, tells the player to put it away and not to show
it in the house. The player may answer "You knew." She did not; she never looked, and she says so, and it is the
one time she does not ask anything twice. Keema-Tul, shown it, says: "A stone can be lost, lamp. Bring me a
tongue." She does not ask for it and does not take it.

**LB07, The Heron Post.** At Gaxu-Reet "a clutch is missing from the nest-pits; boats pass that did not come up
the river; the old sap-speaker will name who took it for a price" (D murkwood, Gaxu-Reet). The egg-thieves' boat
is tied up below the platforms with a crew of three and their pilot, on ground the record makes lethal: the
hardest place in the line, and one where nobody owes a lamp an answer. The pilot is the one of them who will talk,
to a stranger who holds the amethyst: he takes the player for one of hers. His case is the old custom's: the
tribes paid men like him to carry these same eggs for a thousand years, and all that has changed is who is
ashamed. He names the door and the woman. He can be parleyed with, robbed (his chest holds a second stone, his pay
and an Archon berth-pass for "the stairs under the shut school", which names the door to a player who finds him
already dead), or killed with his crew by a player strong enough. The sap-speaker's price is his death. Her secret
is that she handed him the clutch, because she believes in the giving and the young tenders do not; the player can
name her to the station's tenders or keep it.

**LB08, The Shut Door.** Archon, where a lamp has no standing and the harbour has cells. Gaxu-Reet's clutch is in
the locked hold of a lighter on the lower wharves, to be landed after dark. Three people each know a piece (a
wharfinger; a customs officer who has been told not to look at boats under a purple stone; and the fence, who
serves a known Night-Reed freely and refuses a lamp of rank), and one thing is seen only on the spot: the hold has
a second hatch below the waterline. The player takes the clutch out (the key from the lighter-man's belt, the
lock, or the swim, with breath from the harbour families' tree for any race) and it rides in the pack. Then the
eggs are the player's to place, each by a line to the person who would take them: north to a tender of Gaxu-Reet;
to the keeper of Archon's landward tree, to hatch as harbour folk; or to the townswoman who watches the sealed
school, who will see them round to the water. The player can go and look at both sides of the school. The door is
used like any door and does not open. On the water side, down the stairs the berth-pass names, is a grille with a
row of empty moss baskets behind it, set out to dry; they are counted in the message, and there are more of them
than the north has lost. Who is behind the door, the line never learns.

**LB09, The Name.** While the player is at Archon, Vicecanon Arvys Hleran dies in Stormhold. He is on Old Menas's
slab when the player returns, the Dunmer families want a name, and they have one: Varon, who quarrelled with
Hleran in the trading hall the day before, over the roll or over the clutch, and is in the cage on the arch. The
city's court is one woman. The truth (X): Hleran had worked out the clutches for himself and did not name her; he
asked a price, one court for both peoples, his. Keema-Tul put thickened Hist sap in his cup with her own hand,
quick and quiet, as her brother's order killed. The grand case is a closed run of three questions (who, with
what, why), each answered by a thing: the body, read on the slab (Alchemy 50, or Old Menas for a price); Hleran's
locked study, which holds the crystal he kept back and his own cut copy of her pass-stone; her house, which holds

## 3. The first three cases

Common to all three.

- **Asks-Twice** stands in the Lamp House for the whole line; **Keema-Tul** stands at the pond's wall in the
  square and **Arvys Hleran** in the trading hall of the Ayleid compound. The line cannot go on without these
  three: the death of any fails a running case with the faction's message, and between cases Asks-Twice (or, if
  she is the one dead, Varon) has a line that says the line of cases is over.
- **One working stage.** After the briefing each case sits at one working stage. What the player learns is a
  stored number or a plain fact (a thing held, a person dead), never a stage of its own, so leads can be followed
  in any order, mixed or dropped. A line that needs a thing checks that it is held, however and whenever it was
  got; taking a thing moves nothing and writes nothing.
- **One note.** Each case has one thing that is used, not taken (the stones, the nest, the body). Using it shows
  a message box, sets a number and writes one journal note, through a stage that opens at the working stage and
  closes when a name is given.
- **Giving the name.** The player names by choosing a line to the person who asked. Each name is gated on what
  convicts in that hearer's eyes, which is not always the truth. Too little gets "bring me more" in the hearer's
  own words and leaves everything open. The prompt of each naming line says that a name cannot be taken back.
- **A death moves no stage** except where the outline says so. Lines check who is alive.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are first person and past tense; they say
  what the player did and what named people said, never what it means. Thresholds are fixed. Each failed Speech
  check has its own written refusal and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms.

**What a new player is told, and when.** A player who holds the moss basket can open with it, before the

## 5. Read on paper

One round, on the first draft of sections 1 to 4: two players given only the story as a player meets it, the three
full cases step by step with pauses and the later arc in summary, and one reader given the ceiling, the quest form
and the document.

**What they said.**

- *The player who loves the lore* took the work at once ("a custom, not a guild pitch"), read the gap in the
  causeway unprompted, named the true hand every time, and would retell the line and join it over a thieves'
  guild. Hard to please on: knowing the patron's guilt from the second case, so that the pass-stone five cases
  later was treated as a reveal that was not one; ten cases of "name one of several", with the fourth and sixth
  reading as the plot paused; three opening cases in which the true name always hurts the weak; not being allowed
  to press Keema-Tul or Shuxal on who gave the order, to accuse Asks-Twice, to argue for Keema-Tul's life or to
  carry Stormhold's eggs home; Varon as a mouthpiece for one option; the Vandrels vanishing after the third case;
  a dead boy, Rilas, with no family and nobody told; a Dunmer magistrate who was only venal; and why an old and
  lawful custom needs a secret boat. The shut door at Archon was, to this player, the best thing in the line.
- *The impatient player* liked the gap, the crocodile, the barn, the tunnels and the theft of the eggs from the
  lighter, and found the play thin elsewhere: the second case "nine conversations, one pocket and one lock" with
  nothing that fights back; every case the same loop (ask round, find the object, show it, pick a name, get a dry
  line); too many names; openings that are three talks before anything happens; the plot of the third case in one
  lump of speech; nobody ever caught alive. Guessed the patron in the second case as well and resented acting
  ignorant. Found the shut door a let-down. Would not join over the thieves or assassins: "a lamp and regrets",
  no hideout or perks. Noticed that the dry stair skipped both the third case's fight and its clue.
- *The deliverability reader* found every mover, gate and switch buildable and no dead end in the first two
  cases, and found these not buildable as written: several journal notes that could fire in any order (the form
  loses the later ones, and with them the number that gated one name); markers forced onto things the player was
  meant to discover; a line gated on a barn having been entered; a line lost because it came after the quest's
  end; two dead people who had to be bodies the place holds; a switch that put a named boy home in an ending that
  says he is not there; a mother whose death left no line open; a mourning entry that claimed a request never
  heard; a number read in the third case and never set in the second; a flute still stolen after its owner said
  to take it; a death between cases that left the line silently unoffered; and Mysticism, which the form cannot
  gate a line on. In the later cases: a paper "burnt at any fire", eggs "set down" in view, an ending triggered
  by the player walking away, and a switch fired where the player stands.

**What changed, as one set.**

1. *The line no longer pretends the player does not know.* Section 2 now says when a careful player suspects the
   patron and lets them say so at once and be answered with the order's own rule: a suspicion is not a finding.
   The turn is recast from her name to the first proof she cannot take, the eggs alive, and the Keeper's "put
   that away". Keema-Tul and Shuxal can both be pressed; Asks-Twice can be accused and answers; the reason for
   the secrecy is said by Shuxal.
2. *Nine cases, and no two running that end alike.* The Gideon case is gone and its paper moved into the Tenmal
   room, in the killer's hands; the river case became a manhunt that ends with the lodger of the first case
   caught, alive or dead. The table of cases gained a column for how each ends: a village name, a cage, a person
   brought out, a killer faced, a man caught, eggs carried home, a pilot, a theft, the name.
3. *Something fights back in every case.* A quarter's bully sits on the lamp-lighter's step in the second case;
   the Tenmal killer and the lodger each fight if named to their face; the last case can end in the patron's own
   fight. Section 1 says so as the faction's rule.
