# The Lamp-Bearers: the line's story

The story of the detectives' faction of decision 0025
([0025](../../../../docs/decisions/0025-a-detectives-faction.md)): eight cases, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the province plan gives the line ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §3.2, §4, §7), and held to the rulings
that make fourteen stories one province ([consistency](consistency.md) §8, the Lamp-Bearers). A quest writer turns
each full outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world (`story/design/world/`; "D valley-of-storms,
Surkina" is that place's record or hooks in that region's file). Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is
`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation from them (decision 0032).
The places are not built yet, so a place is named by what the digest says it is, and everything else a quest needs
of it is a row of section 4, with how the player will know it. The faction had no quest rows in the old design: its
foundation is decision 0025, the `detectives` profile and the detectives' notes of the questline principles.

**Words used below.** *Vicecanon*: a magistrate of Stormhold; the city invented the office, and two sit at once,
one for each people. *The west quarter*: Stormhold's old Argonian quarter. *Egg-tender*: the keeper of a nest of
eggs. *Clutch*: one nest's eggs. *The Shadow*: the birth sign under which, by old custom, a hatchling was given
away to be raised a Shadowscale, one of the old kings' sanctioned killers, whom the north remembers as its judges; their
school at Archon has been shut for fourteen years. *The Owing*: the native sentence, months of owed work. *A lamp*: what people of the Stormhold
country call a Lamp-Bearer. *The Archwardens*: the state that keeps the old forts; people call them the Wardens.

## 1. The faction in a page

**The thing they want.** To be able to say what happened. Black Marsh has no courts above the tribe except Gideon's
courthouse and the Vicecanons of the north ([province-vibe](../../province-vibe.md) §2), and in 4E 201 the last
serving Shadowscale has just died (*state*:201 to :213; consistency T5). When a body is found, a nest is empty or
a stake is pulled, a village settles it by who is disliked. The Lamp-Bearers settle it by what can be held in the
hand.

**Where they come from (X).** Stormhold was the Dunmer slavers' foothold, and its western quarter was the quarter
of the Argonians who worked for them (`stormhold.md:6`, `:52` to `:54`). When Vicecanon Heita-Meen tried the Dres
slavers and hung them in cages on the Ayleid arches (`stormhold.md:67` to `:70`), her court had to know who had
been in the holds and who had put them there. The order's story is that its first members carried a lamp down into
the holds for her and said who was in them. The order has kept the lamp, the work and the name.

**Who they are.** A dozen people of both peoples in one room of Stormhold's old Ayleid compound, beside the town
mortuary (`stormhold.md:40` to `:41`), because the dead are brought there first.

- **Asks-Twice** (Argonian man of the west quarter, old), the Lamp-Keeper: the house's one keeper, an office and not
  a rank the player reaches. His teaching is the order's: *a lamp shows; it does not strike*. He holds that a
  lamp-bearer names and then stands back, and that the day the order chooses sentences it is one more hand
  reaching for the empty chair. He took the lamp forty years ago after naming his own clutch-sister for a theft,
  rightly, and has not been asked to a west-quarter hatching since. His fault is the other side of his rule: he
  has not looked at what standing back is paid with.
- **Varon Sedrys** (Dunmer man, third generation in the city, better in Jel than in Dunmeris), the player's peer
  and the rival inside the house. He holds that a name given quietly to a magistrate is a name the magistrate may
  drown, and that a finding belongs to the square. He is right more often than he is liked, and he acts on it:
  from the second case he is on the boat's trail by himself, and the player keeps finding him ahead of them, the
  worse for it. He keeps the house's pack: picks, oil and a lamp's small tools, sold to lamps only.
- **Old Menas** (Dunmer man), keeper of the mortuary next door, who is not of the order, lets its people read
  the dead on his slab and sells what an alchemist needs, to lamps and to nobody else.
- **The two Vicecanons**, who pay for every finding: **Keema-Tul** (Argonian woman, the west quarter's) and
  **Arvys Hleran** (Dunmer man, the Dunmer families'). "Several Vicecanons sit at once, one for each people" (D
  valley-of-storms, Stormhold); these are the two (X). Keema-Tul pays the rent of the Lamp House out of her own
  share of the crystal dues, and has for years. Hleran is dry, slow to cage anyone and careful of his own: he
  will not take a Dunmer on a boy's word, and he pays for the urn of a digger's boy nobody asked him to bury. He
  also keeps a little of the Vicecanons' crystal back for himself.

**How they work (X).** A finding stands on **the thing, the tongue and the name**.

- *The thing*: something held. What was taken, what was left behind, what the dead carry, what a liar keeps in a
  locked room. A lamp-bearer goes in after it: a lock picked, a key lifted from a belt, a cellar entered under its
  owner's eyes, a body read where it lies.
- *The tongue*: a person who says it, because they were shown the thing. A lie is not argued with; it is put
  beside the thing that refutes it.
- *The name*: said aloud by the lamp-bearer, once, to whoever asked for the lamp, with the thing put into their
  hand. There is no hearing and nothing is written. In the city the name goes to the Vicecanon of the named
  person's own people, so that no people is judged by the other (X, from "one for each people"); in a village to
  its reeve or tree-minder; among kin, to the kin. What is done then is theirs.

**What follows a name (X).** The west quarter, like every native place, has no cell: one of its people named
answers to Keema-Tul by the Owing, or by exile (owner-bar, "Truth and lore"). The Dunmer families kept one thing
from Heita-Meen's court, which was made for Dunmer and has never held anyone else: a single iron cage, standing
under an arch of the Ayleid compound and chained to it, beside the trading hall where their Vicecanon sits. A Dunmer named on a lamp's finding is
shown in it on Hleran's word while he decides, and no longer. It is never a sentence: Heita-Meen's own cages were
for a year "with exile to follow" (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:222` to `:225`), and the families' use of it now
ends in an Owing or in exile within days. So the cage holds one person, a Dunmer, from a naming until the player
has reported it, which is always before the next case begins, and never two (consistency P16). Nobody of the west quarter and no outsider is ever put in it.

Because a lamp's name is believed, a wrong name is a real ruin, and it stands. And a suspicion is nothing: every
quarter has those. That is the weight of every case in the line: the truth of each is fixed, the player's
conclusion is their own, the world acts on the conclusion, and the one name the player most wants to give cannot
be given until there is a thing to put in a hand. This is the line's own shape in the province (consistency S2):
every case ends on who is told, and what each telling gives and costs the player is in the tables of sections 2
and 3.

**Where a lamp counts.** In Stormhold and the farms and hamlets of its manner (Surkina and Tenmal, and far to the
south Julo, Luntoc and Yalugo: D province, Peoples: Shadowfen Border) a lamp is sent for and answered. Nowhere else.
The reed people of Jal-Veesa "refuse the Vicecanons' courts" (D valley-of-storms, Jal-Veesa), and a lamp who asks
about their pulled ward-stakes is told it is the reed people's business (province-plan §4). Helstrom's Root-Folk
owe a lamp nothing, the forts answer to the Wardens, and Archon has law of its own. The further the line goes from
Stormhold the less the lamp is worth and the more the work is done by nerve and skill alone.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **The Shadowscales' memory.** The old judges acted "with impunity" (*state*:205 to :207) and needed no proof;
  half the province thinks that was better, because it was quick and it ended feuds. A lamp is slow and ends
  nothing by itself.
- **The Night-Reed**, the thieves of Lilmoth, whose jobs are what the lamps are sent after. A thief says a lamp is
  a magistrate's dog. He is half right: see what the order hides.
- **The quarters themselves**, who would sooner settle a wrong by who is disliked, and who each know that the lamp
  is paid by the other quarter's Vicecanon too.
- **The Vicecanons**, the patrons, who want a name quickly more than they want the right one, and who decide what
  the square is told.

**What it pays and what it costs.** Each of these is something the player can go and use, and each is built by a
plain result of the quest form.

Each case also pays the Vicecanons' silver, fixed, and one thing of the trade from whoever the ending pleased (a
vanilla piece enchanted for Speech, Alchemy, Lockpicking, Sneak or Pickpocket under a name of ours; the record
writer names the template). It costs the player's ease: a name given wrongly is lived with, and is seen (a man in a
cage, a farm a hand short). It also costs a door or two: the order will not raise to Long Lamp a senior of the
Shadowscales or of the Night-Reed, and those two will not raise a Long Lamp (province-plan §4; consistency C27: a
gate on rank, which changes no stage).

**What it lies about.** Two things, and the line finds both.

1. *Whose lamp it was.* The first lamp-bearers knew the holds because they had carried the slavers' lamps into
   them. They were west-quarter collaborators who turned witness and were spared for it (X, from
   `stormhold.md:52` to `:54`). The order tells the second half of that.
2. *That it only shows.* Its house is paid for by one of the two people it names to, and a name given to her is
   hers to bury.

**Why a player wants in.** Each case is a break-in, a body, a liar and a decision, and the decision is theirs and
can be wrong. The cases join: the petty ones turn out to be the wake of one boat, the boat leads up to the person
who hired the lamp, and the player knows it long before they can prove it.

**The build leaning** (consistency K3). Speech first, and always as an act: a thing shown, a lie refuted, a person
leaned on, a name given. Alchemy second, to read what was done: what a body drank, what moss a basket is lined
with. Lockpicking third, because what people hide is behind a lock. Sneak and Pickpocket are ways, never the
leaning: a key on a belt, a room entered unseen. Fighting is never the whole of a case and is in every one:
something or someone fixed and hard, standing where the truth is (a crocodile in a drowned causeway, a quarter's
bully on a doorstep, the dead of a sunken hall, a culprit who would sooner fight than be named), with a fixed
reward, and nearly always with a way round. Nothing is levelled (decision 0035). The faction suits a talker first,
a reader of bodies second and an opener of rooms third; a fighter can finish every case. No step of the line
checks Mysticism (consistency K2).

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** By custom older than any state, a hatchling born under the Shadow was given away at birth
to be raised a Shadowscale (`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:61` to `:67`), and egg-tenders know each
clutch's hatching month and sign as they know its parents (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:74` to `:76`). The school at
Archon closed in 4E 187, and for fourteen years the tribes have had nowhere to send such clutches (*state*:214).
Two years ago Vicecanon Keema-Tul, whose clutch-brother was given and came home a judge the whole valley obeyed,
made a bargain with the last serving Shadowscale, through an Archon pilot, Olik-Tei: the north's Shadow clutches,
carried south, against the promise that in twenty years the north has judges again
([shadowscales](shadowscales.md) §2; consistency C1). The old judge meant it, and never asked how the clutches
were got. Nobody gives to a shut door, and the mothers would not have let them go, so she arranged for the
clutches to be taken. Olik-Tei's boats carry them by the back waters, past no toll post: they lift a causeway's
stones, pull a weir's stakes and cut a ferry's rope to get by, and their hands are paid in rough purple crystal
dug for her under the city. At each nest an old tender who remembers the giving hands the clutch out and says it
"sank into the roots", as a failed clutch does (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:77` to `:78`). The old judge died this
year. The baskets still go to the water side of his sealed school, and who takes them in there now is the
Shadowscales' story: this line never enters the school and never says.

At Stormhold's own pond the lie failed: a mother saw the empty nest before the tender could tell it, and cried
thief in the square. The quarters turned on each other. Keema-Tul then did the one thing that would calm them: she
sent for the Lamp-Bearers to find the hand that carried the eggs, meaning the finding to stop at the hand. The
line is the player following the boat instead.

**What the player knows, and when.** The line does not pretend its patron is above suspicion. A careful player
suspects her by the end of the second case, and the line lets them say so at once: asked "Who told Shuxal?",
Keema-Tul answers, "Find me the thing that says so, lamp, and I will hear the name, whoever's it is"; Asks-Twice
says a suspicion is what the quarters have. From then on the tension is the order's own rule turned on the
player: they are doing a guilty woman's tidying, case by case, and each thing they find she takes or thanks them
for. The turn (LB06) is not her name. It is the first thing that cannot be taken away, the eggs found alive, and
the keeper of the house telling the player to put the proof out of sight.

The plan's range offers a case each at Gideon and Thorn. The story did not earn them: the first reading found the
line too long at ten, and its one Gideon case was the plot standing still; the second found it long at nine, and
the Heron Post a second telling of the turn, so that case became the opening of LB08. Gideon is spoken of in LB04
and not visited. There are eight cases, and the number LB07 is not used.

**The ranks.** *Unlit* (on trial, LB01); *Wick* (LB01 done); *Lamp-Hand* (LB03); *Lamp-Bearer* (LB04); *Long Lamp*
(LB06; the senior rank, refused to a senior Shadowscale or Night-Reed with its own line). What each opens is the
table in section 1. There is one Lamp-Keeper, Asks-Twice, and no ending makes the player the next (consistency
S15, N46): the player ends the line as the one who knows.

**The days** (consistency T18). Each case counts from its own start. In LB01 the clutch was taken six nights ago
and the stones were lifted the night before that; LB02, which begins when LB01 is reported, says eight.

**The planned misjudgement** is LB04, where every thing in the room points at the wayhouse-keeper; and LB02 lets a
hasty player cage an innocent man with a true clue.

**Earlier choices** never change a later case's stages. They change greetings, single lines and who sells to the
player, read from stored numbers; only the finale's choice carries into the world after the line. The line
can be left at any rank: each case closes its own matter, and a player who stops before LB06 leaves a city that
believes its clutch sank.

**The house reacts.** After each case Asks-Twice, Varon and Old Menas greet with their own view of what the player
named and what it cost; a wrong name is said to the player's face by Varon and not at all by Asks-Twice, which is
worse. People of earlier cases come back: Shuxal stands at Sedgeweir in LB05; Ilver, if he is alive and above
ground, is one of the tongues of LB09; Waleesh is the one the west quarter's ending is given to. If a person the
next case cannot start without has been killed in between, the case is not offered, Asks-Twice greets with the
news, and the line of cases ends there.

### The later cases

Each is at arc level: the truth, the play, the choice, and a table of what each outcome gives and costs. The
tables' results are the quest form's own (a thing given, a skill raised, a container made the player's, a door
unlocked or locked, a person switched on or off away from the player, a selling line or another line gated on the
number the ending stores); a "unique" is a vanilla piece under our name with its template's fixed enchantment.
A gift is given by the stage its giver's own line moves to. Nobody carries the player anywhere: travel is the
boat network's own. Nothing is levelled and nothing is Nordic. From LB06 on, the cases stop asking who and start
asking whether she is wrong: every person the player meets after the turn has a view of the giving, and the
choices are about the eggs.

**LB04, A Room at Tenmal.** A traveller came to Tenmal's wayhouse by river with a sack of Dres slave-irons and
"has not left his room since" (D valley-of-storms, Tenmal). He is dead in it, behind a door barred from inside.
The truth (X): he was Junian Sek, a Lukiul clerk of Gideon's courthouse, where the old northern court's seized
things were carried when the Pact's court ended. He was selling each iron back to the family whose mark is on it.
The big stamps are the Dunmer slaving houses. The small second stamp, a lamp, marks the west-quarter crew that
brought the captive in; and he carried the proof of what the lamp meant, the roll of Heita-Meen's court: the
lamp-carriers who bore witness and were spared, by name and house-mark. It is old, and it still bites: the
house-marks on it are cut over doors in the west quarter today, and the quarter still counts, family by family,
who carried lamps. It is the line's one paper, and it is an outsider's (owner-bar, "The vibe of Argonia").
Tenmal's ferryman is of such a line, with kin among the reed people. He put a Morrowind poison in the clerk's
supper; the clerk felt it, barred his door and died; the ferryman went in by the shutter on the landing side and
took the roll, meaning to burn it, and has not been able to: it lies in his boat-chest at the landing. The player
opens the room (the lock, the keeper's spare key lifted from her, or the shutter), reads the dead man's cup and
plate (a line to the keeper gated on Alchemy 40, or the same from Old Menas for 100 gold), takes the irons, and
works a cast of six: the Dunmer wayhouse-keeper, whose mark is on most of the irons and who cooked the supper;
the ferryman; the pit-boss and his wife; a Dunmer trader whose raft of shackles is held at Fort Willowmoth's gate
and who was the clerk's carrier (D valley-of-storms, Fort Willowmoth; he is spoken of, and the fort is not
visited). The wayhouse-keeper can be named to Hleran on the irons alone, wrongly. The ferryman, shown an iron of
his own line's stamp, says what he did and stands on his own landing with a boat-hook. He speaks and is not
hostile: the player may attack him there, and his death is the player's choice; or the player says "Take the
ferry, then", at which he gives leave of his boat-chest, and when the player has reported in Stormhold he is gone
and his pole stands alone in the landing mud. Either way the roll is taken from the chest.

Then the roll is the player's own to lay down (consistency S2). It can be read: the first name on it is the
founder the order venerates, and two of the house-marks are Keema-Tul's line and Asks-Twice's own. There are two
places for it, each a thing the player uses with the roll held, and each owner says beforehand what laying it
there means. Varon argues for a third thing, reading it out in the square; the roll kept is the only way that
can still happen (LB09).

**LB05, What the River Keeps.** Varon has gone south after the boat without leave, and has not come back.
Asks-Twice sends the player after both. It is a chase, and each stop is one act. At Sedgeweir "someone is pulling
up the weir stakes at night and leaving the crocodiles a way in" (D valley-of-storms, Sedgeweir): the player goes
into the weir pool among them for what is caught under the weir, a strongbox (the digest gives it) holding a
boatman's pole-shoe and a coil of tarred rope. Shuxal, or Ilver, may be standing on the bank, working an Owing:
the weir-keepers take Owing hands from Surkina's reeve for stake-work and answer to no court for it (consistency
P15). At Ranvos Landing Varon lies in the roadhouse with a Naga arrow through his leg, ahead of the player and

## 3. The first three cases

Common to all three.

- **Asks-Twice** stands in the Lamp House for the whole line; **Keema-Tul** stands at the pond's wall in the
  square and **Arvys Hleran** in the trading hall of the Ayleid compound. Asks-Twice's death fails any running
  case with the faction's message; each case's table says who else it cannot lose, and until when. A case is
  offered only while those it needs are alive, and when one is not, Asks-Twice (or, if he is the one dead, Varon)
  greets with the news that the line of cases is over: a greeting, written with the dialogue.
- **The scene first, then one working step.** After the briefing the person who asked for the lamp sends the
  player to look at the thing itself (the stones, the nest), which is used: a message box and the case's first
  note. From there every lead is open at once and in any order. What the player holds is a thing in the pack;
  what the player has been told that a later line relies on is a stage, numbered in the order it can be met, so
  that no line puts words in the player's mouth that nobody has said to them.
- **A line moves a stage and does nothing else.** A door unlocked, a chest given, gold taken, a person switched:
  each is done by the stage a line moves to. A thing a person tells the player to take is placed where they say
  it is.
- **Giving the name.** The player names by choosing a line to the person who asked. Each name is gated on what
  convicts in that hearer's eyes, which is not always the truth. Too little gets "bring me more" in the hearer's
  own words and leaves everything open. The prompt of each naming line says that a name cannot be taken back, and
  before it the hearer says in plain words what each name would give and cost (the tables below).
- **A death moves no stage** except where the outline says so. A dead person is not named, and nobody dead is
  switched on anywhere: a line that leads to a second placement of someone is gated on them alive, with its twin
  for them dead.
- **A gift is given by the stage its giver's own line moves to.** What the hearer of a name gives is given when
  the name is given; what Asks-Twice gives, at the report. A trader who opens to the player is one selling line
  of theirs, offered from the ending that opens it; one who shuts is switched for a second placement of them
  that carries every other line of theirs and does not sell.
- **Nobody is switched where the player stands.** Every switch below is made by a line said indoors (the Lamp
  House, the trading hall) about people who stand outdoors or in another room.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are first person and past tense; they say
  what the player did and what named people said, never what it means. Thresholds are fixed. Each failed check
  has its own written refusal and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms.
- **How the player knows a place.** Each person who sends the player somewhere names it by what the game will
  call it and by something that does not move, and the objective's marker stands on it. Section 4 says, for every
  thing a case relies on, which of the player's four ways of knowing tells them what it is.

### LB03. Crystal for the Dark

Offered once LB02 has ended completed and while Ilver lives.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Asks-Twice, in four sentences: the Vandrels' eldest, Ilver, went under
the city for crystal a month ago and has not come up; he is an Argonian, hatched in the west quarter's nests
before his mother went to the farm, and carries his father's Dunmer name; nobody pays a lamp for a farm boy, and
he is sending one anyway, because that farm has had more ill luck in one month than he likes; the boy went down with Stone-Cough, who is found by the smelter at the tunnels' mouth, where
the smoke is. The objective points at Stone-Cough at once, and at the landing below the hall. Varon, if asked:
the tunnels are the old prison diggings, the crystal in them is the Vicecanons', and below the worked galleries

walk and is not required: Ilver is theirs, at home in neither quarter; his lamp has its handle bound with
saltrice twine; and she tells the player to take a leaf of the Hist shoot the family keeps in a pot by the
farmhouse door (D valley-of-storms, Vandrel Farm): "Show him this. He will know I am not angry."

**The hidden truth.** A month ago Ilver Vandrel went down the crystal tunnels to dig the farm's rent (D
valley-of-storms, Vandrel Farm: "a missing son who went to the Silyanorn tunnels for crystal"). The upper
galleries are picked clean. Stone-Cough took him as far as the stair above the sunken hall and turned back. Ilver
crossed the hall, which is full of the dead of Silyanorn, and reached the Ayleid well-room beyond it, where a
crew of outlaw diggers under a woman called Gall work the deep galleries (D valley-of-storms, Stormhold: "an
Ayleid well-room that outlaws use as a hideout"). Gall fed him and lent him tools against stones he has not yet
dug. A week after him a Dunmer boy of the city, Rilas, followed him down for the same reason. Ilver gave him his
own lamp and sent him back; the dead of the hall caught Rilas at the foot of the stairs, and his bones lie on
the landing there, on his digger's sack, with the lamp in it. Gall's best rough stones do not go to market. A
Vicecanon's factor, who comes "from the west bridge", takes them, leaves food and steel, and tells her which
coast men to pay in stone for work on the river. Before the clutch was taken one of those, Tallow, asked for a
dry roof near Surkina for a week, and Ilver named his father's barn, to put coin in his father's hand. He has
since heard what lay in it. He will not come up: he owes Gall, and he cannot look at his mother.
