# The Ulvarys house

The Ulvarys house is a Dunmer merchant family of Thorn that buys Owings and works the people under them for profit.
It cannot be joined and gives no quests: it is the enemy of [the Chainbreakers](chainbreakers-newworld.md), and this
page is its background.

**Words.** *The Owing*: the marsh's sentence, months of owed work in place of a cell. *A work roll*: the sheet that
names a person under an Owing, the months set on them and who holds them. *The great roll*: the house's own book of
every Owing it holds. *Catchers*: the house's hired fighters who bring back people who run.

## Where it comes from

Thorn was a Dres city. The Dres raid on Thorn made Dres a Great House, and Dres worked Thorn's saltrice fields with
Argonian slaves (`thorn.md:9-11`). Liberation began in those fields, when Heita-Meen killed a Dres councillor there
(`thorn.md:48-52`). Dres slavers worked the Thorn coast until the late Third Era
(`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md:676`). When Dres left, the fields passed to whoever was standing in them:
freed labourers' descendants and the ex-Archein families who had kept the accounts
(`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md:380-385`). Thorn still has a large Dunmer minority, many from families whose
ancestors stood on both sides of the coffles (`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md:387-389`).

The Ulvarys were one of those Dunmer families. **X** They were Dres's overseers in the fields, stayed when Dres
went, and held a share of the saltrice by being there when the title passed. They are not Dres, and in 4E 201 Dres
holds nothing in the province.

## Why the law lets it hold Owings

The house breaks no written law. Black Marsh has no court and no written code, and its sentence is the Owing
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:198-206`). In the north and east, whoever holds the estate or the crossing now sets the
months, and in Thorn's saltrice country that means the ex-Archein managers (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:84-89`,
`:132`). An Owing can be sold on, and nothing in custom forbids a buyer of any race (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:90-93`).

**X** So the Ulvarys buy. They pay the ex-Archein assessors and the toll-wardens for Owings that have grown past
paying, and they own the months from then on. The Vicecanons of Thorn take the house's taxes and its gifts, and
Thorn's guards look away (`thorn.md:6-8`). Because the house holds Argonian and Dunmer debtors under the same roll,
it can say it is not a Dunmer house holding Argonians, and some Argonian families who hold Owings of their own agree
with it.

## Who leads it

**Aldyne Ulvarys** is the matriarch. She keeps the great roll in her own hand and lives at the house's estate at
Julo, far from the city's eyes. She wants the family's fortune kept, and the Owing left as it is. She believes what
she says: that without the Owing the province has no law at all, and that what came after it would be worse
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:154-159`).

**Fenas Ulvarys**, her nephew, is the house's overseer in the Stormhold valley. He wants to be her heir and to be
taken seriously, and he is cruel to people who cannot answer back.

**Ah-Kesh** is the house's catch-captain, an Argonian of an ex-Archein family that set Owings at the crossings for
generations. He wants his pay and a name for never losing a runaway. The house can hire swords anywhere, but it
cannot replace him.

**Llaros Seloth** was the house's assessor for ten years and set many of the months in the great roll. The house put
him out for skimming. He now keeps the Chainbreakers' safe house in Thorn and lies to them about why he left.

## Where it sits

The house's seat in Thorn is its counting-house in the old Mages Guild hall, which has stood shut since the guild
dissolved and is now a saltrice store (Thorn's place record). Its country seat is the estate at Julo, a hamlet of
stone-footed farmhouses where the river braid enters Lake Ulaish, with a ferry and saltrice paddies. A Naga band
hunts the delta and crocodiles lie in the braid, so the estate is hard to reach (Julo's place record, strength D4).
Its factor keeps a stall in Stormhold's old Ayleid compound.

## How it makes the Owing pay

The house buys an Owing cheaply because it has grown past paying. It then works the debtor where labour is short and
paid labour dear: in the salt mine, the crystal pit, the saltrice paddies and the timber camps. It charges each
debtor for food, for a bed in a locked shed and for anything they break, so the months grow
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:94-96`). When a debtor dies, it charges the months to the household
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:97-99`). It sells months on to holders who need hands, and moves people between its
places by barge and raft. People held for years far from their Hist are washed out, and the house's workers are known
by it (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:112-116`).

## Its fighters

The house keeps catchers, swords and bows hired from both sides of the border, led by Ah-Kesh. Two or three stand at
each place that holds its debtors. It pays the wardens of some toll posts and the garrison of Fort Gysilla to hold
runaways for it. Its barges carry a crew of four and a master. At Julo it keeps its largest band, round Aldyne.

## Its kin

The Rothalen house ([rothalen-house](rothalen-house.md)), the Dunmer necromancers who hide behind a trading house in
Thorn, are kin of the Ulvarys by an old marriage. The two
houses do not speak. The Ulvarys say they deal in the living and want nothing of the dead.
