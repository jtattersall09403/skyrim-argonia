# The Thorn Ash-Reed: the line's story

The story of the Thorn Ash-Reed's five quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)), the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)) and the
province plan ([province-plan](province-plan.md) sections 1, 3.12, 4, 6 and 7). A quest writer turns each outline in
section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world (`story/design/world/regions/<region>.md` and
[province](../../world/province.md)); "D arnesian-jungle, Thorn" is that place's record or its hooks. Lore is
`file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is `extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own
extrapolation from the cited data and lore (decision 0032). No place here is built yet: a place is named by what its
record says it is, and every other physical thing a quest relies on is a row of section 4.

**Words used below.** *Saltrice*: Thorn's crop and its wealth (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn). *The coffles*: the chained
lines in which House Dres took Argonians out of Thorn (`topics/history-timeline.md:29`). *Dres*: the Dunmer Great
House that rose on that trade and held Thorn's fields (`thorn.md:9` to `:11`). *Archein*: the assimilated Argonian
landholding families who managed the fields and sold their own people (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:26` to `:28`),
and who keep the forts and the accounts now. *The Owing*: the native sentence, months of owed work instead of a
cell, which round Thorn can be sold on, grow and be inherited (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:68`, `:90` to `:99`,
`:132`). *Washed out*: the grey look of an Argonian kept for years from their own Hist (`:112`). *Hatching-stone*: a
small stone a worker carries from the village they were hatched in (D hatandros-bight, Coldbrine Mine, faith).
*Grave-stake*: the carved stake that marks an Argonian's dead. *Vicecanon*: Thorn's magistrate; there are two (X on
*state*:135, :380). *The Standing*: the Ash-Reed's founding act and its name for itself among its own (X, section
2). *Danger bands*: fixed in the world, never set against the player (D province, Danger; decision 0035).

## 1. The faction in a page

**The thing it prevents.** Thorn is the city the slave trade was built on. House Dres won its seat by a raid on
Thorn and worked the saltrice with the people it took (`thorn.md:9` to `:11`; `topics/history-timeline.md:29`). A
field slave of Thorn, Heita-Meen, killed a Dres councillor here and began the liberation (`thorn.md:48` to `:52`).
The trade came back under other names, was banned by Morrowind's own king late in the Third Era
(`topics/history-timeline.md:70`) and was still run in Arnesia after the ban (`topics/foreign-powers.md:43` to
`:44`). Then, inside two years, the Red Year broke Morrowind and Argonian armies went up the Tear road, sacked
Mournhold and were stopped only by House Redoran (`topics/history-timeline.md:80` to `:81`). In 4E 201 the frontier
is "cold" and "settled", the trade over it "real and unsentimental", and "grievance runs both ways and neither side
is acting on it" (*state*:485). Thorn is three parts Argonian and one part Dunmer, "many from mixed families whose
ancestors were on both sides of the coffles" (*state*:387 to :389). The fields "passed to whoever was standing in
them", and who holds title to which field is "the city's standing quarrel" (*state*:380 to :384; D
arnesian-jungle, Thorn). The Owing is at its worst here, and Dunmer and Argonian hands are held under it together
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:132`).

Nobody in Thorn is acting on the grievance, and the Ash-Reed is why (X). In a city like this, killing does not
start with an argument. It starts with a thing: a body found, a blade found, a name found, in the wrong hands on
the wrong day. The Ash-Reed goes and gets the thing first.

**Who they are and where they sit.** Two old women and whoever they can trust this year. **Uxa-Meen**, an Argonian
field-holder, descended from a freed field-hand as she tells it, sits in the Old Guild Hall, the shut Mages Guild
hall that is now the city's saltrice store (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn, look), where both peoples bring their crop
and so both may be seen going in. **Mirvasa Nethril**, the oldest Dunmer in Thorn, keeps the shrine of Saint Roris
(D arnesian-jungle, Thorn, gives and faith) and has not been inside the Hall in the player's lifetime. Roris is the
Dunmer trader whose killing by slaves in revolt began the Arnesian War (`topics/history-timeline.md:66`): a saint
to one people and a slaver to the other, with his shrine in the middle of the city. The two women agree on what
must be fetched and never on what to do with it. Uxa-Meen keeps. Mirvasa says burn. They send word to each other
by whoever is working for them, which now means the player.

The name is the two peoples: ash for the Dunmer, reed for the Argonians. Among themselves they say *the Standing*.
When the levies came up the Tear road after the Red Year (`topics/history-timeline.md:81`), field-hands of Thorn
stood in the doors of Dunmer houses and said "these are ours", and the houses they stood for were passed over (X:
the lore has the armies go north through this country and has a large Dunmer minority still here two centuries
on; somebody vouched). That is the story a recruit is told. Its other half is section 2's.

**How they work.** Quietly, by one hand at a time, and never as a crowd. The work is a retrieval or a removal: go
into the place that holds the thing (a mine's sealed shaft, a fort's store room, a field hand's hut, a dead town,
a wreck), past whoever keeps it, and bring it out before the people who would use it get there. Speech opens most
doors and Sneak the rest; a blade or a bow is for what lives in the places nobody else will enter (profile
`thorn-ash-reed`: Speech, Sneak, Archery, One-Handed). Then comes the part that is the line's own: the thing is in
the player's pack, several people have a claim on it, and the player's patron is only one of them. A sneak, a
talker, an archer and a fighter can each finish every quest; Lockpicking, Pickpocket, Illusion and Alteration each
open a way and none is required.

**Who stands against them, and why each side is arguable.**

- *The saltrice gangs*, the field-hands of both peoples, whose speaker is **Okan-Shei**. They say the Ash-Reed is
  the field-holders' broom: every time something comes to light that would make the holders answer, two old women
  have it fetched away. They are right about what the peace costs and wrong about how many of them would be alive
  without it.
- *The Sareloths*, a Dunmer trading house, whose head **Vaneth Sareloth** keeps the general store in the market (D
  arnesian-jungle, Thorn, gives) and has for most of her long life kept up her house's hunt for the line of a
  slave who ran. To Thorn's Argonians it is six generations ago. To a Dunmer it is her grandfather's brother.
- *The law*: Vicecanon **Teegla-Ru**, who would have everything the Ash-Reed has ever buried laid in the market
  square and judged; and Vicecanon **Arvys Tereval**, of a mixed Dunmer and Argonian house, who would not.
- *Each other.*

**What it pays and what joining costs.** Coin from Uxa-Meen's own crop, fixed; the run of her rice bin; in time
the things nobody else in Thorn may see. Joining costs the regard of the gangs, and it costs being lied to: the
patrons tell a new hand what to fetch and not always what it is.

**What it lies about.** That it destroys what it fetches. That the houses stood for were the ones with clean
hands. That it keeps the peace for both peoples alike.

**The Chainbreakers** (the plan's crossing, section 4). Uxa-Meen greets a known Chainbreaker coldly and says why:
"I know what you do in the south. Free one hand in these fields and forty ask why not them, and the managers
answer with the fort. I have kept that day from coming since before you could lift a pick. Work for me or do not.
Do not do your other work here." It is a greeting gated on that faction's rank and changes no stage. No quest of
this line frees anyone by taking them anywhere, and none is set on the Chainbreakers' ground.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** Each step is drawn from the cited record or lore; none is dated outside the lore's spine
(*state*:662 to :691).

1. In the last years of the Third Era, after Morrowind's king had banned the trade, the brig *Silt Bride* still
   carried captives out of Thorn for Dres (D padomaic-ocean, Wreck of the Silt Bride; `topics/foreign-powers.md:43`
   to `:44`). Her captain kept a log of every lot: which household sold, which bought, and who in the fields
   pointed out the hands to be taken. The sellers were Thorn's Dunmer houses and its Archein managers. The ones
   who pointed were field slaves set over other field slaves.
2. On her last run a woman of the Sareloth household's field, **Oleen-Gei**, slipped her shackle in the hold and
   got out as the brig cleared the bay. The *Silt Bride* went down on the shelf "with captives chained in the
   hold" (D padomaic-ocean, the same). Nobody knows whether Oleen-Gei opened her to the sea or only left her. The
   Sareloths' eldest son was aboard as supercargo and drowned. His house has no ash of him: he is one of the
   drowned dead who "still move in the cabin and along the lower deck", and to a Dunmer house an ancestor who
   walks unburied is a wound that does not close. Oleen-Gei hid inland and kept the shackle. Her line still has
   it.
3. The woman who had pointed Oleen-Gei out for the ship was the field-woman of the long field, **Uxa-Meen's
   foremother**.
4. The Red Year came and Dres was gone from Thorn. The levies came up the Tear road. The field-hands who stood in
   Dunmer doors did it for a price: a house that gave its fields to the one who stood for it was stood for, and
   both swore never to say who had bought and who had sold. The houses with the most land to give were the ones
   that had bought the most. The houses with nothing to give were not stood for. That is how the fields "passed to
   whoever was standing in them" (*state*:380 to :384), and why no title in Thorn can bear to be looked at.
   Mirvasa Nethril was a girl of fourteen in a house that was stood for. She is the last person alive who saw it.
5. The Ash-Reed has kept that bargain since, by fetching out of the world whatever would open it. Uxa-Meen keeps
   every such thing in the cellar under the Old Guild Hall, as her mother did, because a thing kept is a hold on
   whoever it shames, and the holds are what the peace is made of. Mirvasa gave over her own house's things to
   that cellar when she was young and has not gone into the Hall since.
6. Oleen-Gei's line, **Jasa-Gei** and her brother, cut rice under an Owing in the long field, which by Thorn's own
   rule is theirs: their foremother was the one standing in it. Uxa-Meen bought their Owing from the managers
   twenty years ago to keep them where no Sareloth may walk, and has never ended it, because ending it would make
   somebody ask why.
7. The log is in the stern cabin of the wreck, in the drowned captain's strongbox (D padomaic-ocean, the same,
   hooks). It names the buyers after the ban, the sellers, and the field-woman who pointed. "The Thorn Vicecanon
   wants it, and so do the families it names."

The data's own words carry the line's theme. Thorn says a Dunmer family is "hunting a runaway slave's
grandchildren" (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn, hooks). The Sareloths count in their own lifetimes, and by their count
two generations have passed. The people they hunt are the runaway's sixth generation and have only a piece of
iron and a story. One people remembers this as family and the other as carving, and the oldest woman in the city
simply remembers it. (X: that an elf of Thorn can be old enough to have seen the Red Year as a girl. The dossiers
do not state an elf's span; section 6 asks for the line to be added or the device changed.)

**The cast that carries it.**

- The mentor, **Uxa-Meen**: every quest. Kind first: she feeds the player from her own bin, pays on the day, and
  is the only holder in Thorn whose hands are never beaten. Her fault is found in TA03.
- The other keeper, **Mirvasa Nethril**: lines in TA01 to TA03, the giver of TA04. She dies after it.
- The peer, **Captain Cassia Ruleem** of Fort Thornmoth: an Archein who "answers to the Archwardens and dines with
  Thorn's Vicecanons" (D arnesian-jungle, Fort Thornmoth, people) and sends the Ash-Reed what she would rather not
  act on. TA01's aftermath, TA02, TA05.
- The rival inside is the two keepers' quarrel over keeping and burning.
- The antagonist outside, **Vaneth Sareloth**: met as a fair trader from the first day, heard of as the hunter in
  TA02, her hired men faced in TA03, herself in TA05.
- The gangs' speaker, **Okan-Shei**: TA02, TA03, TA05. He says aloud what the player may be thinking.
- The informant, Thorn's purse-cutter at the dockside tavern (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn, gives), who knows who
  carries which key and says so for coin.

*TA01* and *TA02* are section 3's full outlines.

**TA03, Whoever Was Standing in Them.** Uxa-Meen: three men out of Tear are walking the field huts "buying old
iron", and they are Vaneth Sareloth's. Somebody in the long field keeps a slave's shackle cut with an elf's house
mark. "I do not know who, and I have taken care not to. Find it before they do and bring it to me." The long
field lies outside the walls among the paddies (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn, look and gives), with a row of
field-hands' huts on its dyke (a need). The hands are washed out and wary and each knows a piece: Okan-Shei knows
which huts the buyers have already turned over; an old hand knows who never lets her door stand open; a child has
seen the iron. The shackle is in a locked box in Jasa-Gei's hut, the key on a cord at her neck: her word is won
by Speech and by what the player did in TA01 and TA02 (a line each for the truth told at the mine and the blades
given to the dyke), the box is an Adept lock in an owned hut, the key is a pocket to pick. Asked why she keeps it,
she tells the turn: the field-woman pointed, her foremother went to the ship, the field-woman's line holds this
field, "and I cut its rice, and I know whose hand you are". She will not give the iron to Uxa-Meen: "It is the
one thing that says who we are. The day somebody asks who stood in this field, that iron answers." The three
hunters stand at the dyke's far end, where the track leaves the field, and their leader speaks and is not
hostile: 500 gold for the iron or the name. They are fixed at the Dangerous band, hired blades from a harder
country than Thorn's paddies, and a player may fight them (the player's choice, by One-Handed, Archery, or
Illusion to set them on each other), talk them off with Speech 60 and the lie that the line died out at
Coldbrine, or leave them standing. The hands: the iron to Uxa-Meen (the line stays hidden, safe and bound; the
iron goes into the cellar); left with Jasa-Gei (she is safe only while the hunters are dead or gone); or sold to
the hunters (the Gei huts are empty when the player next passes, and the gangs spit). Whichever, Uxa-Meen can then
be faced with what Jasa-Gei said, and admits it: she bought their Owing to keep them where no Sareloth walks, and
has not ended it, "because the day I end it somebody asks why, and the answer is in a book under the sea. May it
stay there." **This is the turn**: the first two jobs were done for a peace whose keeper holds the wronged in her
own field. *Carries on*: `ta03.iron` (1 keeper, 2 Jasa-Gei, 3 hunters); who of the hunters is alive; rank 3. The
line's **misjudgement** is here too: a player who trusts the patron and fetches the iron as asked has taken from a
bound woman the only proof of who she is, and nothing in the quest stops them.

**TA04, The Mask in the Dead Town.** Mirvasa asks, not the Hall, and Uxa-Meen says only that the dead town is
sick ground and no business of hers. Mirvasa is dying and says so. She wants "a carved ancestor mask from the
household shrine" in the lower halls of Kharraluhl (D arnesian-jungle, Kharraluhl, hooks). Thorn's Dunmer lost
their own tombs to the Red Year and the armies, and leave food at Kharraluhl "for ancestors whose names they have
lost" (the same, people). The scavenger crew camped in its upper walls sells what it digs in Thorn's market, and
two of her house's young men have gone up with blades to stop them. One of the two stands at the ruin's edge,
apart from the crew, and can be spoken to; the crew is hostile on sight as the record has it. Below are the
flu-dead and, at the shrine in the deepest hall, the ghost of the household's head, all at the Deadly band: a
sneak goes round the crew and through the halls (Sneak, with Illusion to quiet the dead), an archer thins the
walls from the slope, a fighter needs Block and a good blade, and nobody is told it is safe. In the shrine's niche
are the mask and, wrapped beside it, a branding iron cut with the Nethril mark. The mask is not Velothi. Mirvasa
hid both there herself, at fourteen, in the year her house was stood for, in the one place no Argonian would
look; the crew is two rooms from finding them, and a brand with the shrine-keeper's mark on a market stall is
what she is truly afraid of. The hands: the mask to Mirvasa, always. The brand to Mirvasa (she asks the player to
drop it in the sea for her, and tells the Standing's other half as her last telling); to Uxa-Meen (the cellar);
or kept, to be shown in TA05. On the line that starts TA05, said in the Hall, Mirvasa is switched off at the
shrine and an urn of her ash switched on in its niche. *Carries on*: `ta04.brand`; rank 4; the mask is seen on
the shrine afterwards only if a placement of it is switched on there with the urn.

**TA05, What the Bride Kept.** With Mirvasa dead nobody living remembers, and the log is the only memory left.
Vicecanon Teegla-Ru has put a price on it and Thorn's divers are going down; two have not come up. Uxa-Meen wants
it first. Breath is had by any race: the Conclave of the One sells Alteration tomes to Adept and the apothecary
sells a diver's draught (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn, gives; a need for the draught). The swim from the shore of
painted shells (D padomaic-ocean, Wreck of the Silt Bride, faith) is "a short swim" past slaughterfish and can be
made without a fight. The stern cabin is a room of its own with a floor to stand on and air in it (a need; the
lead's ruling on drowned places), reached by the cabin hatch. In it stand the drowned captain and two of his
dead, at the Deadly band, one of them named when looked at: the drowned supercargo, with the Sareloth ring on
him. The log is in the captain's strongbox (a Master lock, its key on the captain). It is taken, never read as
play: its name is "The Log of the Silt Bride", and one message says what the player sees when it is opened. Then
five people have a case, each made by the one it concerns:

- *Vicecanon Teegla-Ru*: the law. Every title in Thorn reopened, the houses named put to an Owing or the road, as
  Heita-Meen caged the Dres slavers and then exiled them (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:221` to `:223`). It costs
  the peace: afterwards Dunmer doors in the market are shut, a gang stands at the quarter's gate, and Vicecanon
  Tereval is gone.
- *Uxa-Meen*: the cellar. Nothing changes, and that is her case: nobody dies. She pays most, and gives the
  cellar's key, and the player is the Ash-Reed's next keeper with everything that means.
- *Jasa-Gei* (if she is still in the field): the right. It is her title. Uxa-Meen, faced with the log in that
  hand, yields the long field and ends the Owing; Jasa-Gei stands at the field-house door afterwards. But the log
  names Oleen-Gei, and the Sareloths then know the line: this ending is safe for her only if Vaneth has the ring.
- *Vicecanon Tereval, for the houses it names*: fire. They pay the most gold in the province's north to watch it
  burn, and his case is not only fear: "Every name in it is dead but one, and she died this month. You would hang
  children for a book."
- *Okan-Shei*: the gangs. He would read it from the dyke to every hand in the fields. It is the truth told to the
  people it was done to, and it is the killing the line has spent five quests preventing.

Beside the choice, and open in every ending: the ring to **Vaneth Sareloth**. She has kept a niche empty for a
man who walks under the sea. Given him, she ends the hunt in her own words and for her own reason, and that is
the only ending in which Jasa-Gei holding the log is safe. Kept from her, or sold to her with the name, the hunt
goes on. *What each leaves, in play*: the law gives a house key in Thorn and the fort's and the Vicecanon's
regard, and closes the Dunmer traders' best prices; the cellar gives the most gold, the cellar and its hoard
(every thing the player handed Uxa-Meen is in it, with what three generations of keepers put there) and the
Hall's bed; Jasa-Gei gives a share of the long field (ownership of its rice bins and a bed at the field-house)
and the gangs as friends; the fire gives 3,000 gold and the Dunmer houses' prices and loses the gangs for good;
the dyke gives nothing but a named reaping-blade from Okan-Shei's own hand and a Thorn in which several people
the player has traded with are not where they stood. The finale is the plan's kind, *whose hands*, and each case
can be made without strain.

**What carries between quests.** Rank (Carrier, Quiet Hand, Door-Stander, Keeper's Hand); `ta01.end`, `ta02.hands`,
`ta03.iron`, `ta04.brand`, each read by greetings and single lines and never by a later quest's stages; who is
alive; the things held. The hub reacts: Uxa-Meen's greeting cools with each thing not brought to her and she says
which; Okan-Shei's warms with the same; Vaneth's prices are the same for everyone until TA05. **Abandoned at any
rank**, the line leaves a Thorn that is as it was: the peace holds because nobody finished looking.

## 3. The first two quests

Common to both.

- **Uxa-Meen** stands in the Old Guild Hall at Thorn for the whole line (a need). She must live: her death fails
  any running quest of the line with the faction's message. Nobody else is essential, and each outline says what
  a death closes. People stand where they are placed.
- **One working stage.** After the briefing each quest sits at a working stage at which every way in is open at
  once. A way's result is a door unlocked, a thing held or a person dead.
- **The thing is taken, and then it is carried to somebody.** The hand-in is a line gated on the thing being held,
  to whichever claimant the player chooses, and the report to Uxa-Meen ends the quest and, being said indoors,
  makes any switch the choice calls for.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense
  and say only what the player has just done. Every creature and person is at a fixed level. Every threshold is
  fixed, and a failed check has its own refusal and leaves another way open. Directions in words are written
  later, on the built places. "Night", "the rest-day" and "the turn of the watch" in anyone's mouth are their
  words only: nothing waits.

### TA01. The Barred Shaft

**What a new player is told, and when.** Thorn's people point a stranger who asks for work to "the two old women":
the boatman on the quay and the keeper of the dockside tavern (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn, gives) each say that
quiet work is had at the Old Guild Hall, the rice store, from the field-holder Uxa-Meen, and that the other one,
the Dunmer grandmother, is at Saint Roris's shrine. On first speaking to Uxa-Meen, before any job, optional lines
of two or three sentences each:

- *This house*: "The mages left it and the rice moved in. Both peoples carry their crop through that door, so
  nobody remarks on who comes to see me."
- *The Ash-Reed*: "Ash is the Dunmer, reed is us. When our armies went north after the Red Year, field-hands
  stood in Dunmer doors here and said 'these are ours'. We are what is left of that. Thorn has every reason to
  cut its own throat, and it has not, because when something turns up that would start it, somebody goes and
  fetches it before the wrong hand does."
- *Why you*: "You are nobody's cousin here and you owe nobody a season."
- *What it pays, and why you might not*: "Coin, on the day. The run of my bin. And why not: because the gangs
  will call you my broom, and some days they will be right."
- *Mirvasa Nethril*: "At the saint's shrine. Older than the walls. She will tell you to burn whatever you bring
  me. She has been telling my family that for four generations."

"Not yet" stays open and free. The job, in four sentences: behind a barred door in the old shaft of Coldbrine
Mine lie bonded labourers who died there in the Company's time and were never staked. Their kinswoman Wudan-Jei
has sworn to break the door on the next rest-day with half the gangs to carry, and the overseer, an Archein, will

hatching-stones, so they can be staked at last, and leave everything else where it lies. If there is something
in there I should know, I want to hear it before she does."

**The hidden truth.**

1. In the East Empire Company's time bonded labour cut Coldbrine's salt (D hatandros-bight, Coldbrine Mine,
   history). Brine broke into a side gallery of the old shaft where five were cutting. The overseer of the day, a
   Haleem, dropped the bar on the gallery's door from the outside with the five alive behind it, to keep the
   brine out of the lower shaft, where forty were at work. The Company wrote it down as a fall of rock and walled
   the shaft's end (the same, hooks).
2. The five cut at the inside of the door with their picks until they died. Their bones lie against it with
   their hatching-stones and five picks worn to the eye.
3. The keys of the mine pass in the Haleem family with the story (X on the same, people: "an overseer from an old
   Archein family who keeps the mine's keys"). **Quintus Haleem** knows what is behind the door and what it would
   mean said aloud in a city of freed labourers' descendants.
4. "Something has broken through the walled-off gallery wall from the other side" (the same, hooks): an old giant
   mudcrab from the brine pools. Its digging is the knocking.
5. **Wudan-Jei**, one of the mine's five hands, is of the line of one of the dead. "One of their descendants
   wants the bodies brought out and staked, and the overseer refuses." An Argonian who dies far from their tree
   and unstaked may never get home to it (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:118` to `:121`): to her the five are not
   history.

The mine's account has one wrong belief: the roof fell. *Why it is unsolved*: one family has the only key and
every reason. *The twist*: the door was barred on them; and the man who barred it had a reason a fair person can
weigh.

**The ways through.** The crux is the barred door; the gallery behind it holds one fight that can be had or

- *Round the back (One-Handed, Archery, Block).* Through the brine pools and the mine's own crabs to the hole the

- *In the gallery.* The old crab is one hard fight at a fixed level, in the room's middle. A bow has it from the
  doorway; a blade and a shield meet it; a sneak keeps to the door's side of the room, where the bones are, and

### TA02. A Crate Nobody Opened

**What a new player is told, and when.** Offered by Uxa-Meen once TA01 is done, in words that fit how it ended.
The job, in four sentences: the fort's quartermaster pulled a Dunmer carter's cart at the toll bar and found moon

**The hidden truth.**

1. A ring "moves moon sugar through Fort Thornmoth's gate" (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn, hooks), and "a guard lets a
   certain cart through untouched every few days" (the same, Fort Thornmoth, hooks). The guard is the gate-warden

   carts" (the same, people).
2. **Okan-Shei**, who speaks for the saltrice gangs, paid Mareth forty hands' season-ends to bring twelve chitin

   field-owners" (the same, Thorn, dangers): the blades are for the day the managers call an Owing on a child and
   the hands have only reaping hooks.
3. The Dunmer quartermaster, **Nevena Orvel**, who "keeps the store" (the same, Fort Thornmoth, people), is
   honest. She pulled the cart on a day Teeus was off the bar, put crate and sugar in "the locked store room"

4. **Captain Cassia Ruleem** has not opened it. A Dunmer drove it, so she believes it is arms for the Dunmer
   quarter, and by the Wardens' law she would have to search that quarter with an Argonian garrison. She is "a
   captain who wants an outsider to carry a word to Thorn without passing the gate" (the same, hooks): the word
   was to Uxa-Meen.
5. Mareth still waits at the toll bar. The ring will have his hide for the sugar and the dyke for the blades.
