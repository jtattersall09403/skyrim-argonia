# The Nisswo of the Turning Path: the line's story

The story of the Nisswo's line of quests, written with the
[story-line](../../../../.claude/skills/story-line/SKILL.md) skill from the
[province plan](province-plan.md) (section 3.7, the brief in section 7, and the crossings of section 4). The owner's
bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)) binds it.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world, [province](../../world/province.md) and the region
files beside it ("D kotherra-mangroves, Giovesse" is that place's record or its hooks in that region's file). Lore is
`file:line` under `story/lore/`; *sithis* is `topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md`; *state* is
`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation, drawn from the cited data and
lore (decision 0032). Nothing here describes a street or a room: what a quest needs a place to hold is a row of
section 4.

**Words used below.** *Sithis*: to Argonians, change itself, "First Creator and Final Destroyer"; they do not hold
Sithis to be evil (*sithis*:17, :157). *Nisswo*: a wandering priest of Sithis, who teaches people to let go; they
have no head and no agreed doctrine, on purpose (*sithis*:18, :23). *Shunatei*: "the pain caused by holding on too
tightly to that which has come to pass" (*sithis*:21). *Vastei*: change; *ku-vastei*, one who brings needed change
(*sithis*:21). *Grave-stake*: the carved post an Argonian is staked into the mud with, cut with the dead one's
whole story; a body left unstaked, or one whose stake is pulled, rises as a *bog blight*, a walking corpse
(*sithis*:123 to :129). *Hist*: the trees Argonian souls return to (*sithis*:130). *Lukiul*: the assimilated
Argonians of Gideon's country (`gideon.md`:68). *Kothringi*: the silver-skinned people of the west, dead of the
Knahaten Flu six centuries ago, for whom the river folk still hang chimes (`topics/lost-peoples.md`:22;
`tribes.md`:95). *Sul-Xan*: a Naga cult of the Blackwood that worships destruction and calls it Sithis
(*sithis*:48 to :53). *4E 48*: the year Gideon's Lukiul were killed and the dead overran the town
(`gideon.md`:91; *state*:339).

## 1. The faction in a page

**What they fight.** Not the dead: the holding on. Everything ends, and the pain is in the hand that will not
open (*sithis*:21). In the west of Black Marsh there is a great deal that has not been let go: a castle whose dead
gaoler still keeps his cells at the gate of a city (D kotherra-mangroves, Giovesse); dead Kothringi towns where
the river folk have hung chimes for six hundred years (D blackwood; D nha-enasa, Mnemosa); a shrine whose keeper's
ghost is still fed with flowers and wine (D kotherra-mangroves, Calyphe); and Gideon itself, whose Lukiul majority
died in 4E 48 and whose people have kept every Imperial habit since, as if to prove that nothing happened
(*state*:339; D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon). The Nisswo go to such places and bring the ending. The line's promise
is the plan's: walk into the places the dead will not leave, and be the one who ends it.

**Who they are, and where they sit.** The Nisswo keep no temple and answer to nobody (*sithis*:18). They are
welcome in almost every village and are called ku-vastei (*state*:218 to :222). The nearest thing to a house they
have in the west is one hut with a hearth among the squatters' huts of **Giovesse**, under the haunted castle,
"within arm's reach" of Gideon's north gate (D kotherra-mangroves, Giovesse; `gideon.md`:48). Gideon keeps Sithis
"in private" (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon, faith); the squatters "keep Sithis's name for the nights when something
moves on the rise" (D, Giovesse, faith). So the priests sit outside the wall, where they are wanted on bad nights
(X). Four of them matter:

- **Weaves-to-Burn**, an old woman, keeper of the hearth. She plaits reed mats all day and says she burns each one
  at dusk: the Nisswo make things in order to destroy them (*sithis*:19). She gives the jobs. She is patient, funny
  and sure, and she is hiding something under her hearthstone (section 2).
- **Otumeel**, a man of middle years who stands at the Gideon end of the plank causeway and speaks to whoever
  passes. He holds that an ending put off is a cruelty, and that mercy which leaves the thing standing is cowardice
  with a kind face.
- **Neema-Luh**, a woman who was a grave-singer before she was a priest (*sithis*:125). She sits by the hearth. She
  holds that an ending taken from someone is a theft, and that the Nisswo teach against holding, not for taking.
- **Jeer-Tei**, who never warmed himself at this hearth. He stands at Lilmoth, by the edge of the drowned quarter,
  and says there what the plan's crossing asks: that it is time to let go.

They disagree in front of the player, and that is doctrine: a village should hear many Nisswo and so more of the
truth (*sithis*:23). Most Nisswo only preach. That a priest should also put a hand to the ending is this hearth's
reading, born of the work it was lit for (X; section 2), and Jeer-Tei says plainly that it is not his.

**How they work, in the line's own skills.** A Nisswo's job has one shape. Go in through whatever danger the place
holds, at its fixed band; find what is holding the place (a thing, a person, a grief); and end it with your own
hands: a restless dead laid by Restoration or the blade; a keeper talked round by Speech, calmed by Illusion or
robbed by Sneak, Lockpicking and Pickpocket; a thing carried out and burned, sunk or staked at a named place,
before a witness who stands there. Restoration and Speech are the faction's own; Illusion, Alteration and
Conjuration answer a fear, a ward or a drowned way; any weapon will do for the unquiet. Mysticism is a Nisswo's
study and no gate is set on it, since the game cannot check it. No quest shuts out a fighter or a talker: each has
both ways.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- *The living who hold on.* Old Tavi feeds nine people on a haunted castle. A mother in Olvani has her son back,
  after a fashion. Gideon's Nibenese have tended a dead shrine-keeper for generations and are the better for it.
  The river folk's chimes are the last friendship anyone keeps with the Kothringi. Each of them is right about
  what the ending will cost, and the player is the one who brings it.
- *The Sul-Xan.* A Naga cult of the Blackwood who call Mehrunes Dagon the "True Egg-Child of Sithis" and hold "only
  the darkest, cruelest beliefs" (*sithis*:48 to :51). They still raid the dead towns (*state*:247; D blackwood).
  To them every destruction is holy. They are what the Nisswo would be with the mercy taken out, and their speaker
  in this line was a Nisswo of this hearth (section 2).
- *The Umbriel Witness*, at Lilmoth, who bring up the truth of 4E 48 and keep it. The Nisswo's doctrine is "the
  province's own counter-argument to its own obsession" (*state*:638 to :642). This crossing is talk only.

**What it pays and what joining costs.** The hearth pays nothing of its own. It passes on what the relieved
leave with it, in fixed sums. It gives a bed (the hut is the Nisswo's, and a member's to sleep in) and a black onyx
stone such as the Nisswo think on (*sithis*:43), which is replaced by a better one at each rank and carries a real
enchantment. Some endings also leave a keepsake or a skill book in the player's hand. Joining costs welcome: the
people an ending hurt remember who brought it, and say so. It closes no other faction to the player. A player of
any race is taken: "Sithis does not ask where you hatched."

**What it lies about.** That it keeps nothing.

**Why a player wants in.** Each quest is a haunted or grieving place to walk into, a fight or a way round it, a
person to face, and then one physical act that cannot be undone. The priests argue over every one, the player
decides, and the place is different when they come back.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** In order:

1. After the dying of 4E 48 the Lukiul dead of Gideon lay unstaked, too many for the living (`gideon.md`:91;
   *state*:339). Nisswo came to the north gate to help stake them, and lit a fire to work by. That fire is the
   hearth at Giovesse. It has not gone out since, and Weaves-to-Burn is its fifth keeper. A clutch that preaches
   wandering has kept one fire for five keepers' lives.
2. One of those first Nisswo, **Ah-Mota**, the first keeper's own clutch-brother, preached at the gate that the
   dead should be left as they lay: a stake is a hand that will not open, let the mud have them. Families
   listened. Their unstaked dead rose as bog blights and killed again (*sithis*:127). It was the Nisswo's own
   teaching, carried one step too far.
3. For that the first keeper did to him what is done to blasphemers: when he died she bound him to an urn and
   denied him return to the Hist (*sithis*:133). She scratched his name off it and put it under the hearthstone.
   Before he died he had cut his own grave-stake, and she kept that too, in the keeper's chest. It ends: "I taught
   at the gate that a stake is a hand that will not open. I saw what rose. I do not ask to go home. I ask not to
   be kept." The fire was kept so that the urn was never left. Each keeper has told the next, and nobody else.
4. So the house that teaches against shunatei has, at its centre, a grudge kept in a jar for five lifetimes.
   Weaves-to-Burn knows it. She thinks the punishment just, the hearth worth more than its secret, and herself too
   old to be the one who decides.
5. **Tsathra-Jei**, Lukiul-born, was to be the sixth keeper. Told of the urn, he called the hearth a liar and
   walked out. He went to the Sul-Xan diggers in the dead towns, who know how a soul is drawn out of what holds
   it: they set "soul snares" and feed them from graves, because "life energy clings to grave-stakes"
   (*sithis*:52, :129). They let him camp beside them because he shows them which graves are tended. He holds
   that Ah-Mota asked for an ending, not a homecoming, and that sending the man home sends his teaching back into
   the tree every Argonian drinks from.
6. The player ends things for the hearth, and learns at last that the hearth has not ended its own. The last thing
   in the line that will not let go is the faction, and then the player.

**The ranks.** *Hearth-Guest* (NI01 ended); *Path-Bearer* (NI03 ended); *Interpreter* (NI05 ended). Each brings a
better onyx; the old one is not asked back. The last title is the finale's: *Keeper* or none.

**The turn** is NI05. Until then Weaves-to-Burn is the one sure voice among quarrelling priests. Afterwards every
job she gave reads again: she sent the player to stake a stranger so that he could go home, to take a mother's
dead son out of her hands, to sink a village's silver, and all the while she kept a soul from going home under her
own fire.

**The misjudgement** is NI04. Otumeel, not Weaves-to-Burn, sends the player to end the ghost at Calyphe. A player
who does as told takes the shrine's best loot and finds out afterwards that she was holding the place's dead
down. It costs standing and does not end the line.

**What carries.** The rank; one stored number for how each quest ended; who is alive; what is held. Earlier
endings change greetings and single lines and never a later quest's stages. Where a later quest needs a person an
earlier one may have killed, its offer has a twin for them dead, and the outline says who or what stands in.

**The hub reacts.** After each quest the three at Giovesse, and Old Tavi, have a line of their own on what was
done, gated on the ending's number and on who is alive. Each ending also moves Otumeel's regard one way and
Neema-Luh's the other.

### The later quests

Rites that hand a carried thing over are lines to a person standing at the place, as in the first two quests. A
switch is made from indoors or from a place the needs promise to be far.

**NI03, What the Floor Gave Up.** *Place*: Umpholo, the river village round the dead Maran mission's chapel, where
the villagers have dug "a silver Mara amulet" from under the chapel floor, "a visiting Agaceph" wants it and "a
Sul-Xan scout" waits in the reeds (D blackwood, Umpholo). *The truth (X)*: the potter who found it means to sell
it at Slough Point for the price of a boat; the Agaceph, a Hirecan man whose people carried off the mission's
books long ago (D blackwood, Umpholo, history), will pay 300 gold to lay it with them; and half the village now
leaves food at the chapel for a mission that died in a month. Weaves-to-Burn says: sink it, before a dead foreign
temple has a second congregation. *What the player does*: gets the amulet out of the potter's strongbox (bought
for a fixed price, talked out of her by Speech, her fear of the reeds calmed by Illusion, the key lifted by
Pickpocket or the box picked, under a small village's eyes). Then it goes to the river, before the old fisher who
stands at the chapel landing; or to the Agaceph for his gold; or stays in the player's own pack, which
Weaves-to-Burn hears about; or is left in the box. In the reeds stands **Tsathra-Jei**, of the raiders' company and
so not attacked by the two Sul-Xan further in (hostile, Wild band; shot from the bank, fought or avoided). He
speaks, asks nothing, and gives a warning that makes leaving it a choice with a named cost: "If the silver stays
in that box my companions will come for it, and I will not stop them. Tell your keeper I said so. She burns reed
mats and keeps what matters." *What it leaves*: sunk, the potter has no boat and says so, and 150 gold comes from
the village elders who wanted the chapel quiet; sold, the gold, and Weaves-to-Burn's "moved, not ended"; left,
the stall is found robbed and burnt when the player returns (the stall and its strongbox switched from the
hearth, far away). *Carries*: Path-Bearer and its onyx; `ni03.way`. If Tsathra-Jei is killed here, the line goes
on without him: what he would have said is found (NI05), and Otumeel alone speaks for the snare (NI07).

**NI04, Flowers for the Keeper.** *Place*: Calyphe, the Kothringi shrine of Dibella sunk in mangrove roots, where
"the last shrine-keeper's ghost" stands at the altar and Gideon's Nibenese "tend it as a living shrine", with
diggers camped on the hummock beside it and a chime-keeper at the roots (D kotherra-mangroves, Calyphe). Otumeel
gives this one: a ghost fed for generations is shunatei with a priesthood; end her. *The truth (X)*: the Kothringi
dead and the ghost lights rose when Gideon's diggers began prising the carved stone out, and the head digger
sells cairn-silver to the Sul-Xan. The keeper is the only thing holding the dead from the landing. The
chime-keeper says so to anyone who asks before going in; so does a pilgrim at the landing. *What the player
does*: crosses the crocodiles and the Kothringi dead (Dangerous; a fight, Sneak, or Conjuration and Restoration
against the dead), and then either ends the keeper at her altar, as told, or turns on the camp: the head digger
faced down by Speech, robbed of the crate of carved stone, or fought, and the stone given to the chime-keeper,
who sets it back. *What each gives and costs*: the keeper ended, the offering chest under her altar is the
player's (the place's best, D) and Otumeel is warm; the shrine's blessing is switched off from the hearth, the
chime-keeper says the dead now walk to the landing, and the priestess of Dibella at Gideon thinks less of the
player and says why. The diggers ended, the blessing stands, the pilgrims leave 200 gold at the hearth, and
Otumeel says, once, that he sent the player at the wrong thing. *Carries*: `ni04.way`.

**NI05, The Chimes at Mnemosa.** *Place*: Mnemosa, the Kothringi burial terrace east of Gideon, a ring of
chime-posts the Naka-Desh keep in repair, where "the terrace's Kothringi dead ... rise at night", "a dig ...
disturbed the dead" and watchers "will shoot at a looter" (D nha-enasa, Mnemosa); and the Paatru hunters' camp a
short track away, who leave salt for the dead (D nha-enasa, Nul-Sakeesh Camp). *The quarrel*: Neema-Luh asks the
player to help the two Naka-Desh who tend the posts; Otumeel says the chimes are the sickness, six hundred years
of calling the dead by name, and should come down. *The truth (X)*: a Sul-Xan soul snare hangs on the great
tomb's own chime-post. Both priests are half right: the snare wakes the dead, and it was hung there because six
centuries of naming have made these dead the easiest in the west to call. *What the player does*: the two
watchers stand at the path, wary and not hostile. They will tell the safe way, and unlock the grate on it, to one
who earns it (a riddle traded, since the river folk love nothing else, `tribes.md`:95; Speech; or Illusion):
under the pool, with Alteration's water-breathing or the blessing Umpholo's shrine gives (D blackwood, Umpholo).
Anyone else crosses the terraces through the Deadly dead by weapon and Restoration, or by Sneak. In the great
tomb the player takes the snare's ring, and beside it finds a reed mat in the Giovesse plait, half finished. The
ring is broken at the Paatru's salt bowl, before the hunter who keeps it. *The chime*: taken down and handed to
the hunter with the ring, the terrace is said to lie quiet for good and the watchers turn the player away from
their stations; left, the watchers give their riddle-prize, a Kothringi silver chime with a real enchantment, and
everyone the player asks says the next snare will find Mnemosa as sweet. *The turn*: at the terrace's edge stands
Tsathra-Jei, who says almost nothing: he was of the hearth, the mat is his, and "Lift her hearthstone. I will not
tell you what is there. You would only have my word." At Giovesse the hearthstone is a thing the player uses,
under Weaves-to-Burn's eyes; under it is a sealed urn with a name scratched out. Only then does she speak, and
all of it: the fire, the first keeper, what Ah-Mota taught and what rose. "Ask one who never warmed himself
here." The hearthstone can be lifted from the end of NI03, on suspicion alone; a player who does so hears her
then, and Tsathra-Jei's line at Mnemosa reads that it is done. With him dead, the mat is the whole of the clue and
Neema-Luh names its plait. *Carries*: Interpreter and its onyx; the urn known.

**NI06, An Urn Somebody Loves.** *Places*: Lilmoth, where Jeer-Tei stands; and the Velothi shrine of Nyrath-Ven
by Archon, with "a ghost by the east niche that will not move until its ashes are carried to the sea", a niche
that "Archon's Dunmer families tend" (D nushara-bayou). Jeer-Tei answers at once and without a price: a bound
soul is loosed by its stake, and by something the looser would sooner keep; and he will not say what should be
done with another hearth's urn. Then he offers the same question where the player has no stake in it. *The
truth (X)*: the ghost was a ship's pilot of the Velothi who came by sea and died of the Flu inland; three Dunmer
families of Archon have carried offerings to his ash-urn for generations, and one old woman, Dralsi Rethan,
stands by the niche most days. He wants the sea. They want their ancestor where they can visit him. *What the
player does*: the niche is outside the shrine's Deadly lower rooms, and two people say so. The ash-urn is asked
for (Speech 50), bought with a promise the player can keep at once (an ancestor's mask from the lower rooms,
through the Deadly dead by weapon, Restoration or Sneak, to stand in the niche instead), or stolen from under her
eyes (Sneak, Lockpicking on the niche's grille). The ashes are given to the boatman at the mooring on the nearest
shore; or the urn is left. *What each gives and costs*: carried to the sea with her leave, the families' thanks
and the mask's worth; stolen, the ghost gone and Archon's Dunmer cold to the player; left, the shrine's blessing
(D) at its best price, since Dralsi gives a lasting token of the niche. Jeer-Tei has a line for each. *The
crossing* (province plan, section 4): asked about the Umbriel Witness, he says that a city which dives for its
grave every morning will drown in it, and that he respects them more than the people who built over it. He also
offers, apart from the line, two short rites on Lilmoth's southern island, where "the fishermen who hear the kits
at night want the tomb quieted" at Ashiit Den and "the farmers would pay to see the lights end" at Reknaa-iit
Mound (D althii-archipelago): each a wraith laid at Dangerous band and a grave-good carried out to the one who
asked. *Carries*: `ni06.way`; how a loosing is done.

**NI07, The Hearth.** Found on return: the hearthstone lifted, the urn gone and Otumeel's place by the causeway
empty (switched from far away, by the line that ended NI06). Weaves-to-Burn says the Sul-Xan came by night and he
went after them. Now she unlocks the keeper's chest and gives the player Ah-Mota's own stake to read. *Place*:
Meliton, the dead town above Hutan-Aru where "Sul-Xan diggers work the south side" under a dig-leader and
restless dead rise from the oldest cairns, Deadly (D blackwood, Meliton). The urn is in the diggers' haul and a
snare stands by the chime-hung thorn tree, humming (the data's "humming doll" hangs on it). On the Hutan-Aru
side, out of the band's reach and of its company, stand Tsathra-Jei and, beside him, Otumeel, who went to kill
him and stayed to listen. *The hard place*: the urn is got out through the hardest fighting of the line, by Sneak
into the haul, or with the key Tsathra-Jei gives to a player who will hear him out. *The giving up*: once the urn
is taken, Weaves-to-Burn and Neema-Luh are found waiting at the stake-ground by the causeway (second placements,
switched on far away), so that each ending's line is said outdoors and may change the hut. The player puts the
urn into one of three pairs of hands.

- **Loosed** (Neema-Luh): she drives his stake and breaks the urn at its foot. Her price is the rite's: something
  of the looser's own that they would sooner keep. The line is gated on holding the Interpreter's onyx, the one
  thing the hearth gave the player, and the stage takes it; without it she says "You have nothing of ours in your
  hand. Find your stone." The hearth is found cold, since a fire with nothing under it is only a habit;
  Weaves-to-Burn has gone on the road; the hut is a hut with a bed. The player loses the onyx and the faction its
  house, and is given what the keepers copied and never taught: three skill books, of Restoration, Speech and
  Illusion. "Ah-Mota's Grave-Stake" stands by the causeway.
- **Kept** (Weaves-to-Burn): she sets it back under the hearthstone. The player is the sixth Keeper, keeps the
  onyx and is given the Keeper's onyx, the strongest stone of the line, and the keeper's chest. Neema-Luh is
  found gone, and with her the hearth's teaching of Restoration; Jeer-Tei greets a keeper coldly.
- **Fed** (Tsathra-Jei, or Otumeel if he is dead): the urn goes to the snare. The soul is unmade. The player is
  given a jade mask of the Sul-Xan (*sithis*:51), strong and ugly. Weaves-to-Burn and Neema-Luh will not speak to
  the player as one of theirs again.

Each is argued by the one it concerns. Weaves-to-Burn, for keeping: he taught a thing that filled a city's gate
with walking dead, and the hearth is the one fixed door a mourner in the west can knock on; Tullo Mereo knocked
on it. Neema-Luh, for loosing: nobody unstaked goes home, and we said so to a dead eel-seller. Tsathra-Jei, for
the snare: read his stake; he asked not to be kept and did not ask to go home, and what goes home goes into the
tree. Otumeel, beside him: "Nine years I said end what stays too long, and slept on top of it. I do not know
whether this is ending it. I know the other two are not." If both men are dead, the third way is closed, and
Weaves-to-Burn says so.

## 3. The first two quests

Common to both.

- **Weaves-to-Burn** sits by the hearth in the Nisswo's hut for the whole line and gives each job. The line cannot
  go on without her until NI07: her death fails a running quest with the faction's message. Nobody else is
  essential. People stand where they are placed.
- **One working stage.** After the briefing every way through is open at once. A way ends in a thing held, a

**What a new player is told, and when.** Otumeel stands at the Gideon end of the plank causeway and speaks first:

**The hidden truth (X).** Castle Giovesse was a prison (`gideon.md`:48). When it was given up, its last gaoler
would not open a cell without a written release, and none came. The prisoners left in the cells died behind the
locks, and he at his post with his turnkeys. They still walk the cell block and he still keeps it (D, Giovesse:
"the ghost of the castle's last gaoler, in the cell block"). They are the dead of his roll and his watch, and
people say they will go when he goes. **Juvan**, a Lukiul, sold eels at Gideon's north gate and was jailed for an
unpaid gate-toll, the same toll the squatters pay now (D, Giovesse, present). He died in the third cell and was
never staked, so he cannot go home (*sithis*:123, :130). His shade stands inside the castle door and wants "its
name carved on a grave-stake by the north gate" (D, Giovesse, hooks). His wish is the name; his need is the bones
under it. The hamlet lives off all this: Old Tavi, whose family kept the castle's door when it was shut (D,
Giovesse, people), takes coin for walks to the castle's hall, where visitors meet Juvan and hear the keys walk
past the stair. Nobody pays to see an empty hall. End the gaoler and the walks end. Stake Juvan alone and she
still loses her best show.

### NI02. The Stake She Pulled

**What a new player is told, and when.** Weaves-to-Burn offers it once NI01 is ended, in four sentences: a fisher
of Olvani, Tullo Mereo, came to the hearth with his arm torn; the Mereos are Lukiul, Argonians with city names;
their son Caso drowned when the Aphon ferry did not come back, was staked at the shrine of the drowned, and now
walks in the family's smoke-shed; his mother Sila keeps the key and the stake. "A stake pulled is a dead thing
walking: we call it a bog blight." She names the choice before it is made: end it with Sila's leave, end it
without, or come back and say you will not. Asked, Otumeel says "End it. She has had her month", and Neema-Luh
"Bring her to it or leave her. Take it from her and you are a thief with a sermon."

**The hidden truth (X).** Olvani's ferry to Aphon Isle did not come back (D kotherra-mangroves, Olvani, hooks).
Of its dead only Caso Mereo came ashore. He was staked in the row at the shrine of the drowned, where there is "a
grave-stake for each one lost" (D, Olvani, faith). A month later his mother Sila pulled the stake by night, and he
rose (*sithis*:127). The Mereos shut him in their smoke-shed and hung racks of spoiled meat by its door, which
hide the scent of the living from a blight (*sithis*:128). Sila feeds him through the door, which has torn her
hands, and sings to him. He tore his father's arm to the bone when Tullo tried to put him down. Tullo will not go
back up to the house and stands at the shrine, by the hole. Sila's reason is her own: the others on the ferry
never came ashore and their mothers have a stake over nothing; she has this. The village knows, owes the Mereos
two boats' worth of kindness, and says nothing unless asked straight.

One round, three fresh readers, all on the version whose sketches first passed: a player who loves the lore and
