# The Nisswo of the Turning Path: the line's story

The story of the Nisswo's line of quests, written with the
[story-line](../../../../.claude/skills/story-line/SKILL.md) skill from the
[province plan](province-plan.md) (section 3.7, the brief in section 7, and the crossings of section 4). The owner's
bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)) binds it.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world, [province](../../world/province.md) and the region
files beside it ("D kotherra-mangroves, Giovesse" is that place's record or its hooks in that region's file). Lore is
`file:line` under `story/lore/`; *sithis* is `topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md`; *state* is
`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation, drawn from the cited data and
lore (decision 0032). Nothing here describes a street or a room: what a quest needs a place to hold is a row of
section 4.

**Words used below.** *Sithis*: to Argonians, change itself, "First Creator and Final Destroyer"; they do not hold
Sithis to be evil (*sithis*:17, :157). *Nisswo*: a wandering priest of Sithis, who teaches people to let go; they
have no head and no agreed doctrine, on purpose (*sithis*:18, :23). *Shunatei*: "the pain caused by holding on too
tightly to that which has come to pass" (*sithis*:21). *Vastei*: change; *ku-vastei*, one who brings needed change
(*sithis*:21). *Grave-stake*: the carved post an Argonian is staked into the mud with, cut with the dead one's
whole story; a body left unstaked, or one whose stake is pulled, rises as a *bog blight*, a walking corpse
(*sithis*:123 to :129). *Hist*: the trees Argonian souls return to (*sithis*:130). *Lukiul*: the assimilated
Argonians of Gideon's country (`gideon.md`:68). *Kothringi*: the silver-skinned people of the west, dead of the
Knahaten Flu six centuries ago, for whom the river folk still hang chimes (`topics/lost-peoples.md`:22;
`tribes.md`:95). *Sul-Xan*: a Naga cult of the Blackwood that worships destruction and calls it Sithis
(*sithis*:48 to :53). *4E 48*: the year Gideon's Lukiul were killed and the dead overran the town
(`gideon.md`:91; *state*:339).

## 1. The faction in a page

**What they fight.** Not the dead: the holding on. Everything ends, and the pain is in the hand that will not
open (*sithis*:21). In the west of Black Marsh there is a great deal that has not been let go: a castle whose dead
gaoler still keeps his cells at the gate of a city (D kotherra-mangroves, Giovesse); dead Kothringi towns where
the river folk have hung chimes for six hundred years (D blackwood; D nha-enasa, Mnemosa); a shrine whose keeper's
ghost is still fed with flowers and wine (D kotherra-mangroves, Calyphe); and Gideon itself, whose Lukiul majority
died in 4E 48 and whose people have kept every Imperial habit since, as if to prove that nothing happened
(*state*:339; D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon). The Nisswo go to such places and bring the ending. The line's promise
is the plan's: walk into the places the dead will not leave, and be the one who ends it.

**Who they are, and where they sit.** The Nisswo keep no temple and answer to nobody (*sithis*:18). They are
welcome in almost every village and are called ku-vastei (*state*:218 to :222). The nearest thing to a house they
have in the west is one hut with a hearth among the squatters' huts of **Giovesse**, under the haunted castle,
"within arm's reach" of Gideon's north gate (D kotherra-mangroves, Giovesse; `gideon.md`:48). Gideon keeps Sithis
"in private" (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon, faith); the squatters "keep Sithis's name for the nights when something
moves on the rise" (D, Giovesse, faith). So the priests sit outside the wall, where they are wanted on bad nights
(X). Four of them matter:

- **Weaves-to-Burn**, an old woman, keeper of the hearth. She plaits reed mats all day and says she burns each one
  at dusk: the Nisswo make things in order to destroy them (*sithis*:19). She gives the jobs. She is patient, funny
  and sure, and she is hiding something under her hearthstone (section 2).
- **Otumeel**, a man of middle years who stands at the Gideon end of the plank causeway and speaks to whoever
  passes. He holds that an ending put off is a cruelty, and that mercy which leaves the thing standing is cowardice
  with a kind face.
- **Neema-Luh**, a woman who was a grave-singer before she was a priest (*sithis*:125). She sits by the hearth. She
  holds that an ending taken from someone is a theft, and that the Nisswo teach against holding, not for taking.
- **Jeer-Tei**, who never warmed himself at this hearth. He stands at Lilmoth, by the edge of the drowned quarter,
  and says there what the plan's crossing asks: that it is time to let go.

They disagree in front of the player, and that is doctrine: a village should hear many Nisswo and so more of the
truth (*sithis*:23).

**How they work, in the line's own skills.** A Nisswo's job has one shape. Go in through whatever danger the place
holds, at its fixed band; find what is holding the place (a thing, a person, a grief); and end it with your own
hands: a restless dead laid by Restoration or the blade; a keeper talked round by Speech, calmed by Illusion or
robbed by Sneak, Lockpicking and Pickpocket; a thing carried out and burned, sunk or staked at a named place,
before a witness who stands there. Restoration and Speech are the faction's own; Illusion, Alteration and
Conjuration answer a fear, a ward or a drowned way; any weapon will do for the unquiet. Mysticism is a Nisswo's
study and no gate is set on it, since the game cannot check it. No quest shuts out a fighter or a talker: each has
both ways.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- *The living who hold on.* Old Tavi feeds nine people on a haunted castle. A mother in Olvani has her son back,
  after a fashion. Gideon's Nibenese have tended a dead shrine-keeper for generations and are the better for it.
  The river folk's chimes are the last friendship anyone keeps with the Kothringi. Each of them is right about
  what the ending will cost, and the player is the one who brings it.
- *The Sul-Xan.* A Naga cult of the Blackwood who call Mehrunes Dagon the "True Egg-Child of Sithis" and hold "only
  the darkest, cruelest beliefs" (*sithis*:48 to :51). They still raid the dead towns (*state*:247; D blackwood).
  To them every destruction is holy. They are what the Nisswo would be with the mercy taken out, and their speaker
  in this line was a Nisswo of this hearth (section 2).
- *The Umbriel Witness*, at Lilmoth, who bring up the truth of 4E 48 and keep it. The Nisswo's doctrine is "the
  province's own counter-argument to its own obsession" (*state*:638 to :642). This crossing is talk only.

**What it pays and what joining costs.** Nothing is paid by the hearth: the people the ending relieves pay what
they can, in fixed sums. The hearth gives a bed, a black onyx stone such as the Nisswo think on (*sithis*:43), and
from each ended place the player may carry off a keepsake with a real enchantment. Joining costs welcome: the
people an ending hurt remember who brought it, and say so. It closes no other faction to the player.

**What it lies about.** That it keeps nothing.

**Why a player wants in.** Each quest is a haunted or grieving place to walk into, a fight or a way round it, a
person to face, and then one physical act that cannot be undone. The priests argue over every one, the player
decides, and the place is different when they come back.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** In order:

1. After the dying of 4E 48 the Lukiul dead of Gideon lay unstaked, too many for the living (`gideon.md`:91;
   *state*:339). Nisswo came to the north gate to help stake them, and lit a fire to work by. That fire is the
   hearth at Giovesse. It has not gone out since, and Weaves-to-Burn is its fifth keeper. A clutch that preaches
   wandering has kept one fire for five keepers' lives.
2. One of those first Nisswo, the first keeper's own clutch-brother, preached at the gate that the dead should be
   left as they lay: a stake is a holding on, let the mud have them. Families listened. Their unstaked dead rose as
   bog blights and killed again (*sithis*:127). It was the Nisswo's own teaching, carried one step too far.
3. For that the first keeper did to him what is done to blasphemers: when he died she bound him to an urn and
   denied him return to the Hist (*sithis*:133). She scratched his name off it and put it under the hearthstone.
   The fire was kept so that the urn was never left. Each keeper has told the next, and nobody else.
4. So the house that teaches against shunatei has, at its centre, a grudge kept in a jar for five lifetimes.
   Weaves-to-Burn knows it. She thinks the punishment just, the hearth worth more than its secret, and herself too
   old to be the one who decides.
5. **Tsathra-Jei** was to be the sixth keeper. Told of the urn, he called the hearth a liar and walked out. He went
   to the Sul-Xan diggers in the dead towns, who know how a soul is drawn out of what holds it: they set "soul
   snares" and feed them from graves, because "life energy clings to grave-stakes" (*sithis*:52, :129). He wants
   the urn ended. They want it eaten. He has not yet seen that these differ.
6. The player ends things for the hearth, takes a keepsake from each, and is told at last that the hearth has not
   ended its own. The last thing in the line that will not let go is the faction, and then the player.

**The ranks.** *Listener* (NI01 ended); *Path-Bearer* (NI03 ended); *Interpreter* (NI05 ended). The last title is
the finale's: *Keeper* or none.

**The turn** is NI05. Until then Weaves-to-Burn is the one sure voice among quarrelling priests. Afterwards every
job she gave reads again: she sent the player to stake a stranger so that he could go home, to take a mother's
dead son out of her hands, to sink a village's silver, and all the while she kept a soul from going home under her
own fire.

**The misjudgement** is NI04. Otumeel, not Weaves-to-Burn, sends the player to end the ghost at Calyphe. A player
who does as told finds out afterwards that she was holding the place's dead down, and loses the shrine's blessing
and the good word of Gideon's temple. It costs standing and does not end the line.

**What carries.** The rank; one stored number for how each quest ended; who is alive; the keepsakes held. Earlier
endings change greetings and single lines and never a later quest's stages.

**The hub reacts.** After each quest the three at Giovesse, and Old Tavi, greet the player with their own view of
what was done. Otumeel warms to a player who ends things and cools to one who spares; Neema-Luh the other way.

### The later quests

**NI03, What the Floor Gave Up.** *Place*: Umpholo, the river village round the dead Maran mission's chapel, where
the villagers have dug "a silver Mara amulet" from under the chapel floor, "a visiting Agaceph" wants it and "a
Sul-Xan scout" waits in the reeds (D blackwood, Umpholo). *The truth (X)*: the potter who found it means to sell
it at Slough Point for the price of a boat; the Agaceph, a Hirecan man whose people carried off the mission's
books long ago (D blackwood, Umpholo, history), wants it laid with them; and half the village now leaves food at
the chapel for a mission that died in a month. Weaves-to-Burn says: sink it, in the river below the chapel, before
a dead foreign temple has a second congregation. *What the player does*: gets the amulet out of the potter's
strongbox (bought for a fixed price, talked out of her by Speech, lifted with the key by Pickpocket or the box
picked, under a small village's eyes), then gives it to the river at the chapel landing, before the old fisher
who stands there, or hands it to the Agaceph, or leaves it with her. In the reeds stands **Tsathra-Jei**, apart from the two Sul-Xan raiders further in (who are hostile, of
the Wild band, and can be shot from the bank or avoided). He speaks, asks nothing, and says what he came to see:
what the hearth's new hand does with a thing someone loves. *What it leaves*: sunk, the potter has no boat and
says so; kept, the stall is found robbed and burnt when the player returns (switched far away) and the silver is
seen again at Mnemosa. *Carries*: Path-Bearer; `ni03.way`.

**NI04, Flowers for the Keeper.** *Place*: Calyphe, the Kothringi shrine of Dibella sunk in mangrove roots, where
"the last shrine-keeper's ghost" stands at the altar and Gideon's Nibenese "tend it as a living shrine", with
diggers camped on the hummock beside it and a chime-keeper at the roots (D kotherra-mangroves, Calyphe). Otumeel
gives this one: a ghost fed for generations is shunatei with a priesthood; end her. *The truth (X)*: the Kothringi
dead and the ghost lights rose when Gideon's diggers began prising the carved stone out, and one of the diggers
sells cairn-silver to the Sul-Xan. The keeper is the only thing holding the dead from the landing. The chime-keeper
says so to anyone who asks before going in; so does a pilgrim at the landing. *What the player does*: crosses the
crocodiles and the Kothringi dead (Dangerous; a fight, Sneak, or Conjuration and Restoration against the dead),
and then either ends the keeper at her altar, as told, or turns on the camp: the head digger faced down by
Speech, robbed of the haul of carved stone, or fought, and the stone carried back to the altar. *What it leaves*:
the keeper ended, the shrine's blessing is switched off while the player is away, the dead are said to walk to the
landing, and the priestess of Dibella at Gideon will sell the player nothing; the diggers ended, the blessing
stands and Otumeel says, once, that he sent the player at the wrong thing. *Carries*: `ni04.way`.

**NI05, The Chimes at Mnemosa.** *Place*: Mnemosa, the Kothringi burial terrace east of Gideon, a ring of
chime-posts the Naka-Desh keep in repair, where "the terrace's Kothringi dead ... rise at night", "a dig ...
disturbed the dead" and watchers "will shoot at a looter" (D nha-enasa, Mnemosa); and the Paatru hunters' camp a
short track away, who leave salt for the dead (D nha-enasa, Nul-Sakeesh Camp). *The quarrel*: Neema-Luh asks the
player to help the two Naka-Desh who tend the posts; Otumeel says the chimes are the sickness, six hundred years
of calling the dead by name, and should come down. *The truth (X)*: a Sul-Xan soul snare stands in the great tomb
at the terrace head, fed from the graves. *What the player does*: gets past the watchers (a riddle traded, since
the river folk love nothing else, `tribes.md`:95; Speech; Illusion; or round them by Sneak), crosses Deadly ground
to the great tomb (through the dead by weapon and Restoration, by Sneak, or under the pool with Alteration's
water-breathing or the blessing Umpholo's shrine gives, D blackwood, Umpholo), takes the snare's ring out and
breaks it at the Paatru's salt bowl, before the hunter who keeps it. The chime on the great tomb may be taken down or left: both
priests are answered either way. At the terrace's edge stands Tsathra-Jei, who tells the player who he was, what
is under the hearthstone, and to go and lift it. At Giovesse the hearthstone is a thing the player uses; the urn
is there. Weaves-to-Burn then says all of it, and one thing more: "Ask one who never warmed himself here."
*Carries*: Interpreter; the urn known.

**NI06, Three Far Rites.** *Places*: Lilmoth, where Jeer-Tei stands; Ashiit Den and Reknaa-iit Mound on Lilmoth's
southern island, where "the fishermen who hear the kits at night want the tomb quieted" and "the farmers would
pay to see the lights end" (D althii-archipelago); and the Velothi shrine of Nyrath-Ven by Archon, with "a ghost
by the east niche that will not move until its ashes are carried to the sea" (D nushara-bayou). Jeer-Tei will not
say what should be done with another clutch's jar. He says how a bound soul is loosed (its stake, and what its
looser gives up), and sends the player to end three things first and see what they think then. One working stage,
any order. At Ashiit Den the wraith at the child's tomb is laid and a fox-tail charm carried out to the shore. At
Reknaa-iit the chieftain's wraith is laid, or the hoard left and his talisman alone taken and burned at the
farmers' fire. At Nyrath-Ven the ash-urn is taken from the east niche, which Archon's Dunmer families tend and
will not give up willingly (Speech, a theft or their leave bought), and emptied at the shore; the shrine's lower
rooms are Deadly and the niche is outside them. Each rite is done before the one who asked for it: a fisher on
the shore, a farmer at his fire, a boatman at the mooring. Each place offers a keepsake with a real enchantment: a bone
flute, a fox-bone talisman, a Velothi ash-bowl. Jeer-Tei, at the end: "Three graves, and three things in your pack
from them. You kept them. So did she." *The crossing* (province plan, section 4): asked about the Umbriel Witness,
he says that a city which dives for its grave every morning will drown in it, and that he respects them more than
the people who built over it. *Carries*: the keepsakes; what a loosing costs.

**NI07, The Hearth.** Found on return: the hearthstone lifted, the urn gone and Otumeel's place by the causeway
empty (switched while the player was far away). Weaves-to-Burn says the Sul-Xan came by night and he went after
them. *Place*: Meliton, the dead town above Hutan-Aru where "Sul-Xan diggers work the south side" under a
dig-leader and restless dead rise from the oldest cairns, Deadly (D blackwood, Meliton). The urn is in the
diggers' haul and a snare stands by the chime-hung thorn tree. Tsathra-Jei stands apart on the Hutan-Aru side and
speaks; Otumeel's pack lies in the trench. *The hard place*: the urn is got out through the hardest fighting of
the line, by Sneak into the haul, or by the key Tsathra-Jei will give for a promise he cannot enforce. *The
giving up*: the player ends it by putting the urn into one of three pairs of hands.

- **Loosed**: to Neema-Luh, who has cut his stake once Weaves-to-Burn gives up the scratched-out name, and who
  drives it in the stake-ground by the causeway, where it is found standing. The rite asks what the looser kept from the dead: the three keepsakes are
  taken. The hearth is found cold afterwards, Weaves-to-Burn gone on the road, the hut a hut with a bed. The
  player loses three enchanted things and the faction its house; every Nisswo met afterwards knows what was done.
- **Kept**: to Weaves-to-Burn, who sets it back under the hearthstone. The player is the sixth Keeper, keeps the keepsakes and is given the
  Keeper's onyx and the hut's chest. Neema-Luh is found gone. Jeer-Tei greets a keeper coldly.
- **Fed**: to Tsathra-Jei at Meliton, who carries it to the snare. The soul is unmade. Tsathra-Jei gives a jade mask of the Sul-Xan
  (*sithis*:51), strong and ugly, and says he thought he would feel something. No Nisswo will speak to the player
  as one of theirs again.

Each is argued by the one it concerns: Weaves-to-Burn for keeping (he taught a thing that killed hundreds, and the
hearth is the one fixed door a mourner in the west can knock on); Neema-Luh for loosing (nobody unstaked goes
home, and we said so to a dead eel-seller); Tsathra-Jei for the snare (a prison is not ended by moving the
prisoner). Otumeel, if the player finds him alive in the trench, says only that he no longer knows.

## 3. The first two quests

Common to both.

- **Weaves-to-Burn** sits by the hearth in the Nisswo's hut for the whole line and gives each job. The line cannot
  go on without her until NI07: her death fails a running quest with the faction's message. Nobody else is
  essential. People stand where they are placed.
- **One working stage.** After the briefing every way through is open at once. A way ends in a thing held, a
  person dead or a stage of its own; reports read who is alive and what was set.
- **A killing is the player's own.** No journal entry says how.
- **The rite has a witness.** The player ends a matter at a named place by a line to the person who stands there
  (Otumeel holds the post; a father holds his son's stake). The line is gated on the thing being held, the stage
  takes it and shows a line of words, and what then stands there is switched on later, from indoors. If the
  witness is dead, Weaves-to-Burn takes the thing and the journal says so. A rite that consumes nothing (a
  hearthstone lifted) is a thing used.
- Every objective has a marker. A place is found by its map marker and a named person's directions from something
  fixed; a thing by the name the game shows on it. Thresholds and levels are fixed. Each failed check has its own
  refusal and leaves another way open. "Dusk" and "night" are people's words; no step depends on the hour.

### NI01. The Gaoler Keeps the Door

**What a new player is told, and when.** Otumeel stands at the Gideon end of the plank causeway and speaks first:
"You. What are you carrying that is already dead?" His lines, each a sentence or two, before any job: *who are
you* ("Nisswo. Priests of Sithis, and before you reach for your knife: here Sithis is change, the first maker and
the last unmaker. Nobody in the marsh calls that evil"); *what do you do* ("We end what has stayed too long. The
dead who will not go, mostly. And the living who will not let them"); *where* ("The hut with the hearth, under the
castle. Weaves-to-Burn keeps it. It is named on its door"). In the hut, Weaves-to-Burn before the job: *what it
wants of me and why me* ("Someone with a strong arm or a steady voice, and no kin here to shame"); *what it pays*
("A bed, a stone to think on, and whatever the relieved can spare"); *why I might not* ("Every ending is someone's
loss. They will know your face"). "Not today" stays open and free. The job, in three sentences: a dead man called
Juvan stands inside the castle's door and asks everyone who comes for a grave-stake; go up and hear him; what else
is ended up there is the player's to judge, and Otumeel and Neema-Luh will each say what, and will not agree.

**The hidden truth (X).** Castle Giovesse was a prison (`gideon.md`:48). When it was given up, its last gaoler
would not open a cell without a written release, and none came. The prisoners left in the cells died behind the
locks, and he at his post. His dead still walk the cell block and he still keeps it (D, Giovesse: "the ghost of
the castle's last gaoler, in the cell block"). **Juvan** sold eels at Gideon's north gate and was jailed for an
unpaid gate-toll, the same toll the squatters pay now (D, Giovesse, present). He died in the third cell and was
never staked, so he cannot go home (*sithis*:123, :130). His shade stands inside the castle door and wants "its
name carved on a grave-stake by the north gate" (D, Giovesse, hooks). His wish is the name; his need is the bones
under it. The hamlet lives off all this: Old Tavi, whose family kept the castle's door when it was shut (D,
Giovesse, people), takes coin for walks to the castle, and Juvan is the first thing visitors pay to see. End the
gaoler and the walks end. Stake Juvan alone and she still loses her best show.

### NI02. The Stake She Pulled

**What a new player is told, and when.** Weaves-to-Burn offers it once NI01 is ended, in four sentences: a fisher
of Olvani, Tullo Mereo, came to the hearth with his arm torn; his son Caso drowned when the Aphon ferry did not
come back, was staked at the shrine of the drowned, and now walks in the family's smoke-shed; his wife Sila keeps
the key and the stake; "a stake pulled is a dead thing walking: we call it a bog blight". Otumeel, at the
causeway: "End it. She has had her month." Neema-Luh: "Bring her to it or leave her. Take it from her and you are

**The hidden truth (X).** Olvani's ferry to Aphon Isle did not come back (D kotherra-mangroves, Olvani, hooks).
Of its dead only Caso Mereo came ashore. He was staked in the row at the shrine of the drowned, where there is "a
grave-stake for each one lost" (D, Olvani, faith). A month later his mother Sila pulled the stake by night, and he
rose (*sithis*:127). The Mereos shut him in their smoke-shed and hung racks of spoiled meat by its door, which
hide the scent of the living from a blight (*sithis*:128). Sila feeds him and sings to him through the door. He
tore his father's arm to the bone when Tullo tried to put him down. Sila's reason is her own: the others on the
ferry never came ashore and their mothers have a stake over nothing; she has this. The village knows, owes the
Mereos two boats' worth of kindness, and says nothing unless asked straight.

**The choice, and each side's honest case.** *End it with her* (Neema-Luh): an ending she opens the door to is
hers, and she will be alive afterwards to bear it. *End it without her* (Otumeel): a mother's leave is not what
keeps a dead man from his tree, and every day behind that door is a day he is nowhere. *Leave it* (Sila, to the
player's face): "The sea kept the others and gave their mothers nothing. I have him. Who are you to count that as
less?" Tullo says what leaving costs: his arm, and his house.

**The endings and what each leaves.**

- *With her leave (90).* The stake stands in the row. The net-charm stays with the player, since Sila cannot
  look at it, and 100 gold comes from the Mereos. She stays. Neema-Luh is warm; Otumeel says it took too long and was right.
- *Without it (91).* The stake stands in the row. Tullo pays 150 gold. Sila is gone when the player returns, and
  Tullo says where. No charm. Otumeel is warm; Neema-Luh says "home and robbed; both are true" before
  Weaves-to-Burn can.
- *Left (92).* Nothing is paid. Tullo says he will not go back up to the house, and why. Otumeel will not use the player's
  name for the length of the next quest. Neema-Luh gives a Restoration skill book, "for the harder thing".

## 6. For the other lines

- **The Umbriel Witness.** Lilmoth's grave is talk only. Jeer-Tei stands on dry ground by the edge of the drowned
  quarter and says there that it is time to let go; his reason is his own (a city that dives for its grave every
  morning will drown in it), and he respects the Witness more than the council that built over the dead. Weaves-
  to-Burn says of the Witness that they are the only people in the province who are honest about holding on. No
  quest of this line is set in the drowned quarter or in any place of the Witness's. The Witness's patron may say
  of the Nisswo whatever that line wants; this line does not need it.
- **The Shadowscales.** Yes to SS06: Jeer-Tei is the Nisswo who stands at Lilmoth, and he will extend his
  protection to Geh-Meema if asked rightly. That line and its gates are the Shadowscales' to write. It fits him:
  he asks that something of the asker's own be given up to the water. He may be dead; SS06 then lacks that way.
- **The Sunken Archive.** NI03's Agaceph is a Hirecan man and no reader of the hall, as that line asks. Nothing
  here touches Yulca. SA01 leaves Umpholo untouched and this line relies on that.
- **The Chainbreakers.** The "travelling Nisswo" of BC06 is none of the four named here unless that writer wants
  Neema-Luh; if so she may be gone from the hearth (NI07, Kept).
- **Many-Root and the League.** Nothing here touches Gideon's Hist garden or its cutting. Where a soul "goes
  home" in this line, nobody says by which tree.
- **The main quest.** No quest here goes to Gideon South Watch or the relic dealer. The Unbound Root is one
  rumour in Otumeel's mouth: "There is talk of cutters who call their knives Sithis's work. We have a nearer
  heresy." Tsathra-Jei and the Sul-Xan know nothing of the Root, the Eye or the cult.
- **The local quests.** This line takes Olvani's lost ferry and its shrine of the drowned, Calyphe's hooks,
  Mnemosa's, Meliton's humming doll and trench, and Nul-Sakeesh's salt. Cutbank Camp's grave-stakes, Eurydike,
  Dorianth, Aithros and Hixinoag are left free.
