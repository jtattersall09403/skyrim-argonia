# The Rootworm Waykeepers: the line's story

The story of the Waykeepers' six quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)), the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)) and the
province plan ([province-plan](province-plan.md), section 3.9, its crossings in section 4 and the lead's rulings in
[nw-05](../../../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05-story-quest-adapt-1.md)). A quest writer turns each full
outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world (`story/design/world/`): "D ultherus-swamp,
Myroleon" is that place's record or its hooks in that region's file; "D people" is the roster of Helstrom. Lore is
`file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is `extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own
extrapolation from the cited data and lore (decision 0032). Places are named by what their records say they are.
Anything else a quest needs of a place is a row of section 4, and the outlines place people and things only by the
place and by those rows.

**Words used below**, for a reader new to the province. *Hist*: the sentient trees every Argonian is hatched
under. *Rootworm*: the great worm that swims the tunnels the Hist roots make between towns, and carries travellers
in its gut; it is called to a root pool and is never seen. *Post*: a place where the keepers keep a thing a
traveller steers by: a root pool, a fog-chime, a fire-basket on high ground or a lantern on the water. *Voice*: how
a post says which post it is: the count a chime is struck, the way a fire is screened, the count a lantern is
shuttered. *Tender*: the keeper of one post. *Chime-Keepers*: the Argonians of the Kothringi-named towns (Korydon,
Myroleon, Tydeos and the rest), who moved into the dead Kothringi's houses and kept their chimes. *The knot*: what
people of the interior call the Reed-Sail's raft-masters, from the mark on their cargo. *The Owing*: the native
sentence, months of owed work. *Danger bands*: Safe, Settled, Wild, Dangerous, Deadly, Lethal, fixed in the world
(D province, Danger).

## 1. The faction in a page

**What they keep.** The interior of Black Marsh has no roads worth the name. It has channels that look alike in
fog, one ridge of high ground, a lake of shoals, and under all of it the tunnels the Hist roots make, which the
rootworms swim (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:171`; `topics/material-culture.md:97`, "the Underground Express"). Between
Helstrom and the southern lakes a traveller steers by a handful of things that somebody keeps: the root pools where
the worm is called; the chime-posts that sound for boats in fog, of which Myroleon's "is the oldest working one in
the Ultherus country" (D ultherus-swamp, Myroleon); the watchfire on Aunike Crown, which "has burned ever since"
Tydeos was resettled (D aunike-ridge, Tydeos); the fire at Vesh-Aneel on "the only dry ground for hours" (D
murkwood, Vesh-Aneel); the lanterns at Nemeon and on Luntoc (D murkwood, Nemeon; D lake-ulaish, Luntoc). The
Waykeepers are the order that keeps them (X; province-plan 3.9). The name is from their oldest charge, the root
pools.

**Who they are.** Seven tenders, each at a post, and one walker who goes the round between them with oil, tin and
news (X). Their seat is the Keepers' House beside the root pool at Korydon's village tree, where the great chime
hangs that the town's elders strike at dawn and dusk (D ultherus-swamp, Korydon: gives, present). The First Keeper
is **Haxara-Tei**, a Chime-Keeper of Korydon. Their senior post is the root landing at Helstrom, where
**Cheeval-Xu** "keeps the root landing" and "knows each worm's temper and its route" (D people). They are
Chime-Keepers and Root-Folk together, which is rare: the order is older than the quarrel between the two (X).
Nobody pays them. The state's revenue is "tolls at crossings and customs at ports, nothing else" (*state*:704 to
:707), and the Chime-Keepers "deal with the Archwardens coldly" (D province, Peoples: Chime-Keepers). The towns
send oil, tin and food to the posts when they remember to.

**How they work (X).** Two rules, taught in one breath to anyone who joins.

- *The way is kept and never shown.* Every post has a voice. Myroleon strikes four. The Crown's fire is screened
  one way when the ridge is quiet and another when it is not. A pilot who knows the voices knows where he is in the
  dark, and a robber who knows them can stand in for a post. So the voices are never written and never told
  outside the order. They are taught by mouth and by hand, on a **notched tongue**: a small tin chime-tongue cut
  with a post's count, which a tender gives an apprentice to carry until the count is in the wrist and then takes
  back. The First Keeper wears the only whole set, seven old copper tongues on a cord, the **voice-string**.
- *Passage is a gift.* A keeper pilots a stranger through, walks them over or calls the worm for them, and asks no
  fare and no name. The traveller is never taught the way, only taken along it.

The work itself is a ranger's. A post that has gone dark is nearly always dark for a reason that is still there
when a keeper arrives, and it stands at the far end of ground that is more dangerous than the keeper is: the reed
maze of the Ultherus at the Dangerous band, the ridge and the lake's islets at Deadly, the forest hummocks at
Lethal (D, each place's law and danger). Keepers do not fight their way to a post. They go quietly, from the water
side, with a bow for the one thing that will not be gone round, and they learn to make a beast look elsewhere.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **Whoever answers the posts.** In the world's own data, lights are being lit that no keeper lit: a lantern "lit
  from the water side at night" at Nemeon, a lantern on Luntoc "answered from open water", a fire at Vesh-Aneel
  "lit three nights running without a keeper", a watchfire that "went dark one night and a stranger was found
  beside it" (D murkwood; D lake-ulaish; D aunike-ridge: hooks). The line is the finding out, and there is more
  than one hand (section 2).
- **Elder Naxil-Wo** of Korydon's chime-smiths (X; the record's elders "quarrel over who may tune the Hist-shrine
  chime"). He holds that every chime in the Ultherus should be tuned at the guild's forge and carry its stamp, the
  keepers' posts among them: "A voice nobody may know is a voice anybody may forge." He is right about that.
- **The knot.** The Reed-Sail's night boats steer by lights (province-plan 4, "the bought lights"). To a keeper
  that is theft of the way. To a raft-master, the keepers light the water and then say who may look.
- **Buys-by-Daylight**, a Lilmoth trader on Truowa's quay, who "wants a guard on the boat road to Helstrom" (D
  lake-ulaish, Truowa: hooks). She wants one honest thing: that a boat with a lawful cargo can run the lake and the
  channels by night without the knot's leave or the keepers' favour.
- **Many-Root.** The worm swims in the trees' roots, and the tree-minders think the roots are theirs to speak for
  (province-plan 3.9). Korydon's minder, Demeepa-Ru ([many-root](many-root.md), MR01), stands at the same pool as
  the keepers' house and has never been asked whether the tree minds.
- **The Naga at the pass** above Nacydes (D aunike-ridge, Nacydes) are not an argument. They are the reason the
  Crown has a fire.

**What it pays and what joining costs.** Little coin. A bed at every post, the keepers' own draughts and arrows,
and at each rank a keeper's thing (the record writer names the vanilla forms: gloves enchanted for Archery, boots
that muffle, a ring for Illusion). It costs long walks on ground that can kill, the knot's cold shoulder from the
day the player holds rank (section 6), and a rule the player will want to break: a keeper may not sell a showing
and may not teach a voice.

**What it lies about.** "Passage is a gift." At one post it has been sold for years, and the First Keeper takes
the coin (section 2).

**Why a player wants in.** It is the line that walks the interior: every job is a journey to a place most
travellers only pass, with something wrong at the end of it that is still there to be found. The player finishes
it knowing the ridge, the reed maze, the lake and the forest's edge as nobody else does, and holding a say in who
is safe on each of those ways.

**The build leaning.** Sneak first: every post is reached through ground above the player's band, and going
unseen is the craft. Archery second, the keepers' one weapon, for the single beast or robber that cannot be gone
round, from a rock or across water. Illusion third: a calmed spriggan, a muffled step, a frightened man steadied
(a line gated on the school stands in where a quest must know it). Alchemy for what a walker carries (poisons for
arrows, draughts to go unseen, and in WK03 a smoke that snakes will not cross). Speech wherever a tender, a watch
or a buyer must be talked round. A fighter can finish every quest, since nothing in the line is unkillable and
every fight has a fixed reward, but the fights are at the world's fixed bands and above a new character, which is
the point: the line teaches a player to get to the far end of bad ground alive. Nothing is levelled to the player
(decision 0035).

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** In order:

1. *The first sale.* Eleven years ago the towns' gifts stopped filling the oil-jars. Haxara-Tei, newly First
   Keeper, let the tender of Luntoc's lantern, the old pilot Skeeju, show his light for the knot's night boats on
   the dark nights, for silver. It was a showing and never a teaching: the knot learned no voice. The silver has
   kept seven posts in oil ever since. She has told no keeper but Skeeju.
2. *The pupil.* **Mereesh**, the walker, was her pupil and is the only other keeper who knows all seven voices. He
   carried the oil for twenty years and never asked where it came from, until four winters ago he carried a purse
   from Luntoc to Korydon and saw whose knot tied it.
3. *The second sale.* Mereesh decided that the first rule was already broken and the second already a toll. Two
   seasons ago he sold a whole set of notched tongues, cut by his own hand, to Buys-by-Daylight at Truowa for six
   hundred in silver, which re-tinned the chime at Myroleon and bought the Crown its winter's wood. He thought he
   was selling honest boats the night. She had the set cast in new tin for each pilot she hired.
4. *The copies.* Her pilots were robbed, drowned and bought. Within a season a copied string was in the chest of
   the reed-robbers who work the channels toward Blackrose, and another in a boat-robber's canoe on the lake. With
   a post's count in hand a robber does not need the post: he hangs a chime of his own, or lights the post's own
   lantern from the water side, and takes what steers onto him. Her own pathfinder died on Aunike Crown with a
   string in his satchel, having smothered the fire so that her mule-train could cross the ridge unseen by the
   Naga, and so let the snakes up onto the warm stones.
5. *What nobody sold.* The fire at Vesh-Aneel is lit by a hatchling, Tikaasi, who watched the dead tender for
   three years and was never sworn. He lights it wrongly and faithfully.
6. *The end of it.* When the player brings the trail home, Mereesh takes the copper voice-string from its peg by
   the great chime and goes up to the Crown with two of the trader's spears, meaning to carry it to Truowa and have
   it cast a hundred times, so that no keeper ever again decides who may read a light.

The decoys, each guilty of something else: Sheeva-Jul, who silenced her own post and is the one tender who says
aloud that the order is rotten; the Tumnatu, whom Korydon's elders already accuse of stealing a chime (D
ultherus-swamp, Tumnatu Cattail Camp; [many-root](many-root.md) uses the accusation as background, and this line
never settles it); and the knot, which buys lights and has killed nobody on these waters.

**The arc.** Six quests, each a different kind of job in a different kind of country.

- **WK01, The Post That Would Not Sound** (Korydon; Myroleon. The reed maze, Wild to Dangerous). *A walk to a
  silent post, and a thing fetched from under a beast.* The tender has silenced her own chime because something
  answers it. The player brings the answer back from a spriggan's hummock and decides what the post does. Carried
  on: the first rank; what Myroleon's post does (a number); who stands at its mooring.
- **WK02, No Name at the Pool** (Korydon's root pool; the robbers' mooring below Myroleon; Helstrom's root
  landing). *A raid for the truth, and the gift given or refused.* A man who will not give his name asks the worm.
  The player goes into the reed-robbers' mooring to learn what he is, and then says whether passage is still a
  gift. Carried on: the second rank; a notched tongue or none; what Tulaxi and the elders think of the player.
- **WK03, Cold on the Crown** (Tydeos; Aunike Crown. The ridge, Deadly). *A climb to a dead fire.* The watchfire
  has been cold since a stranger was found beside it, snakes hold the summit path, and the old tender will not
  climb. The player goes up, finds the stranger's satchel, and decides where the ridge's fire burns from now on.
  Carried on: the string of seven; where the fire is (a number); whether the old tender lives.
- **WK04, Answered from Open Water** (Uzu-Tegh; Truowa's quay; Luntoc. The lake, Deadly). *The turn.* A copied
  string taken from a boat-robber's canoe leads to the buyer, who names the seller, and to the one lantern the
  robbers never counterfeit, because the knot pays for it and the First Keeper takes the silver. Everything
  Haxara-Tei said about bought lights reads differently afterwards. Carried on: Luntoc dark or burning (a number);
  whether the player told her they know, and whether they told the tenders.
- **WK05, Three Nights Without a Keeper** (Vesh-Aneel. The forest's edge, Lethal). *A crossing of the worst
  ground in the line, to the one light in it that is innocent.* The player is sent to put out a false fire and
  finds a child keeping a dead tender's post. This is the line's misjudgement: the order was given for the wrong
  person, and a player who simply obeys it does harm. Carried on: who keeps Vesh-Aneel (a number).
- **WK06, The Voice-String** (Aunike Crown; Korydon). *The seller faced, in a hard place.* Mereesh waits on the
  summit with the order's copper string. What is done with him, and with it, is the ending.

Earlier choices never change a later quest's stages. They change greetings, single lines and who stands where,
read from stored numbers. Only the finale's choice carries into the world after the line.

**The ranks.** Oil-Carrier (after WK01), Tender (WK02), Keeper (WK04), Walker of the Round (WK06, in two of its
three endings). The player never becomes First Keeper.

### The later quests, at arc level

**WK03. Cold on the Crown.** Offered once WK02 has ended. *Told:* Haxara-Tei says the fire on Aunike Crown, the
peak above Tydeos, has been cold since its tender found a stranger dead beside the basket; Mereesh has gone ahead
with wood and oil and waits at Tydeos; the player is to walk the track north-west from Korydon (D ultherus-swamp,
Korydon: roads), get the fire lit and learn who the stranger was. "And do not trouble Luntoc on your way home.
Luntoc is well kept." *Truth:* section 2, step 4. The fire kept the giant snakes in their den below the summit;
with the basket cold they lie on the path (D aunike-ridge, Aunike Crown: "giant snakes denning below the summit
(x2)"; that the fire kept them down is X). *People:* at Tydeos, **Beem-Wazei**, the old watchfire-keeper (the
record's), who will climb again if told the Crown must burn, and will die on that path within the season; his
grandson **Chalureel**, the record's Archery trainer, who wants the fire brought down to the rock above the hamlet
where a tender can live to be old; Mereesh, a second placement, who gives a bundle of wood and, to a player with
Alchemy 30, the makings of a snake-smoke, and to anyone else three pots of it ready made. *The ways up:* past the
two snakes unseen (Sneak); from the rocks above the den with a bow (Archery); with the smoke, which is a named
thing whose use at the den's mouth switches nothing and is a line of Mereesh's that tells the player the snakes'
side of the path (Alchemy); or through them, a fight at the Deadly band whose reward is the den's own (the
record's bead-cairn treasure is left to the place). *The find:* a wet hide over the basket, and beside it a
satchel (a container, nobody's) holding a string of seven new-tin tongues, a pouch of uncut stones (the stranger
of Yalugo's hook, D lake-ulaish, Yalugo) and a token with Truowa's quay-mark. Taking the string moves the quest.
The carved stone beside the basket is the main quest's and is never used or spoken of (province-plan 4). *The
choice:* use the basket on the Crown (the fire burns where it always has: Vesh-Aneel and the pass can read it, and
the old man climbs), or take the fire-pan from the basket and set it in the low basket on Tydeos' rock (the old
man lives; from Vesh-Aneel the ridge is dark). A player who has killed both snakes may tell Beem-Wazei so, and
that is the only way the Crown burns and the old man is safe on its path: the death buys it. *Leaves:* by
Haxara-Tei's closing line, indoors at Korydon, the burning state of one basket is switched on; Beem-Wazei or
Chalureel stands as tender; under "Crown, snakes alive", Beem-Wazei is switched off when WK04 is reported and
Chalureel says where his pack was found. *Skills:* Sneak, Archery, Alchemy. *Retold:* lifting a wet hide off a
dead fire on a bare summit with the whole marsh below, and finding in a dead man's satchel every secret of the
order on a string of new tin.

**WK04. Answered from Open Water.** Offered once WK03 has ended. *Told:* Haxara-Tei counts the seven tongues and
says only two people living could have cut them, herself and Mereesh, and that he has not come in from the lake
round. A Root-Folk station on the lake, Uzu-Tegh, reports its own lantern "lit although no boat had come", and a
canoe "drifting with its paddles shipped" (D lake-ulaish, Uzu-Tegh). The player is to go there. *Truth:* steps 1 to
4. *Uzu-Tegh* (Deadly): the record's boat-robber at the drifting canoe has a copied string in the canoe's locker.
It is taken unseen from the water side, or after a shot from the islet, or over his body. Its tongues carry a
caster's mark the station's eel-trapper knows: "Truowa. The tin-caster on the quay." *Truowa's quay:*
Buys-by-Daylight speaks plainly to anyone who lays the string in front of her (the string held; no skill): what she
bought, from whom, for how much, how many of her pilots are dead, and one thing more: "Ask on Luntoc why no robber
ever counterfeits that lantern. Your order sells there too. It only minds when someone else does." No pilot of the
knot is named by her or by anyone (province-plan 4). *Luntoc* (Dangerous; reached by Yalugo's spare boat or by the
causeway of stones across the shoal, knee-deep, with the record's giant mudcrab in its reeds): Old **Skeeju**
stands by the lantern post. The lantern-chest is his and locked (Adept; his key on him: Pickpocket, Lockpicking,
or Speech 45 for his leave). In it is a purse tied with the knot. Taking it moves the quest. *The choice,* at the
lantern: take the lantern's wick-pan (Luntoc is dark: the knot's boats and honest boats alike cross the shoals
blind, and the island loses "the lantern toll" its record lives on), or leave it burning and carry only the
knowledge home. Then Haxara-Tei, who must be told something: "I know what Luntoc sells" or silence; her answer,
once, plainly: "Eleven winters of oil. Tell me which post I should have let go dark." A second line, to Tulaxi at
the pool, tells the tenders; each is a stage and a stored number, and the report is the ending. *Leaves:* Luntoc's
lantern in its dark or burning state (switched by the closing line at Korydon); Skeeju's greeting; the knot's
coolness to a Keeper from now on (section 6). *Skills:* Sneak, Archery, Lockpicking or Pickpocket, Speech.
*Retold:* being told by the enemy, on a sunny quay, to go and look in your own order's lamp-chest, and finding she
was right.

**WK05. Three Nights Without a Keeper.** Offered once WK04 has ended. *Told:* Haxara-Tei, shaken, says the fire
at Vesh-Aneel has been "lit three nights running without a keeper" (D murkwood, Vesh-Aneel: hooks), that its
tender Paheel has sent no word since the wet season, and that she knows whose insult it is. The order is hard and
she gives it in so many words: put out any fire there that a keeper did not light, and bring her the hand that lit
it. *The journey:* Vesh-Aneel is "the only dry ground for hours", on ground whose calmest way is Deadly and whose
fights are Lethal (D murkwood, Vesh-Aneel). From Tydeos the track runs north; the record's three giant spiders
and their matron hold "the last crossing". There is no way to fight through for anyone the line has made, and the
quest says so twice before the player goes (Chalureel at Tydeos; a dead hunter's pack at the edge of the crossing,
a container). The crossing is made unseen, by the water beside it, or under one of the Still Reed draughts.
*Truth:* step 5. Paheel went to the crossing to look for the party the station's guide lost (D murkwood,
Vesh-Aneel: hooks) and his pack hangs in the webs. The hatchling **Tikaasi**, a hunter's child, lights the post
every dusk as he saw it done, open and unscreened, which in the Crown's voice and Vesh-Aneel's says "the crossing
is clear". *The ways:* take the fire-steel from the signal post's box and tell the boy to stop (the order
obeyed); leave him lighting it as he does (kind, and wrong: the open fire goes on calling hunters to the
crossing); or fetch Paheel's own copper tongue out of the webs (Sneak, at the Lethal band's edge) and put it in
the boy's hand, which by the order's custom is a swearing, and which only the First Keeper may do. The station's
guide is never used: she keeps her post and her lines are the main quest's and the place's (province-plan 4).
*Leaves:* by the closing line at Korydon, the signal post dark, or burning; one hunter of the four gone from the
station under "left as it was" (told by the others: he read the fire and went to the crossing); Tikaasi's
greeting. Haxara-Tei, told it was a child: "Then I sent you to put out the only honest light I have heard of this
year." *And then:* the peg by the great chime is empty. Tulaxi: Mereesh came down the worm from Helstrom the night
the player left, took the string, and went out by the ridge track. *Skills:* Sneak, Illusion, Alchemy, Speech.
*Retold:* crossing the worst ground in the game without drawing a blade, to arrest a wrecker, and finding a boy
with a fire-steel who salutes.

**WK06. The Voice-String.** Starts when WK05 ends. *Told:* Haxara-Tei: he will go to Truowa by the ridge and
Kharaxos, and he will stop on the Crown, because every fire on the ridge can be read from there and he has never
passed it without stopping. "Bring me the string. Him I leave to you." *The hard place:* Aunike Crown, at the
Deadly band. Below the summit path stand two of Buys-by-Daylight's hired spears, ours, hostile, at a fixed level
of the Deadly band, with fixed kit worth the fight; the snakes are there if the player left them alive; the
lizard-keepers' path from Nacydes, which the main quest's need already asks of the place, goes round both (Sneak).
*On the summit* Mereesh stands by the basket, speaks, and is not hostile. He tells all of it without being asked:
the purse, the six hundred, the pathfinder, the pilot at Myroleon. "I sold them the night. I did not sell them
that." The copper string is on him (a thing he carries). *The ways to it:* his leave (Speech 70, or with no check
if the player told the tenders about Luntoc in WK04: "Then you already chose"); his pocket (Pickpocket); his body
(the player attacks; his death is marked as the player's choice). *The ending is what is done with the string:*

- **Kept** (to Haxara-Tei, in the Keepers' House). She re-voices every post within the season; whoever steered by
  the old voices, the knot's pilots, the trader's and the robbers alike, is blind until a keeper chooses to take
  them through. The order stays what it was, and stays in Luntoc's silver unless the player put that lantern out.
  *Gives:* the rank of Walker of the Round, the walker's boots (muffle) and the freedom of every post: each
  tender's chest is the player's to take from (a stage gives ownership of seven containers), a round worth walking.
  *Costs:* the knot's doors (section 6); and if Mereesh lives he is cast out, and stands afterwards on Truowa's
  quay, carrying crates.
- **Shown** (a line to Mereesh, alive, on the Crown: "Take it down to Truowa"). The voices are cast and sold on
  the quay to anyone. *Gives:* 1,500 gold and a fine bow from Buys-by-Daylight; her pilots' good word. *Costs:*
  the order casts the player out (the rank is taken; the tenders' regard falls; their chests stay shut); and the
  robbers can read the lights too, which Nemeon's boat-wife says in her greeting.
- **Broken** (the string put in the smelter at Korydon's chime-smiths' forge, which the record gives; using the
  smelter with the string held is the act). No one holds all seven again. Each tender keeps one voice and answers
  to nobody; there is no First Keeper to sell a showing and no walker to sell a string. *Gives:* the rank, each
  tender's parting gift when next visited (seven fixed gifts, one a skill book), Elder Naxil-Wo's stamp on every
  chime and his discount in nothing, since prices are not ours to change: his gift is a tuned silver chime. *Costs:*
  Haxara-Tei's house; she does not forgive it; and a post that falls silent now is nobody's to relight.

Mereesh dead, the Shown ending is closed, and Haxara-Tei dead, the Kept one; Broken is always open, so the line
can always end. After any ending, one line of hers or of his, whoever lives, asks the player the question the line
has been asking: "Is a light a gift, or is it just a light?" The journal does not rule on it. *Skills:* Sneak,
Archery, Speech, Pickpocket. *Retold:* standing on the highest ground in the interior with every light of the
marsh below, holding seven tongues on a cord, while a kind old man explains why he sold them.

## 3. The first two quests

Common to both.

- **Haxara-Tei** stands in the Keepers' House at Korydon for the whole line. She must live: her death fails any
  running quest of the line before WK06 with the faction's message. Nobody is essential. People stand where they
  are placed.
- **One working stage.** After its briefing each quest sits at a stage at which every way through is open. A way
  ends in a thing held, a person dead, a thing used, or a line that sets the stage which finishes the step.
- **A line only moves the quest to a stage**; whatever is given, taken, set or switched is done by that stage.
  Every switch is made by a line of Haxara-Tei's inside the Keepers' House, of things that are outdoors.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing, and the way is told in words as well. Journal entries are
  in the first person and the past tense and say only what the player has just done. Thresholds are fixed. Each
  failed check has its own refusal and leaves another way open.
- **Nothing sounds, burns or goes out in view.** The worm is never seen; calling it is told. A post the player
  strikes is a thing used and a journal entry. No step depends on the hour or the weather: "fog" and "night" are
  words people say.
- **Creatures.** The spriggan is the place's own and keeps its level. The reed-robbers are people of ours at a
  fixed level of the Wild band.
- **How the seat is found.** Korydon's boatwright at the channel landing and the tin trader on the square each
  have a line for a stranger, "work for someone who walks", which names the Keepers' House, says it stands beside
  the root pool at the village tree, and names Haxara-Tei. Cheeval-Xu at Helstrom's root landing has the same line
  for anyone who asks him about the worm: "The seat is at Korydon, by the pool. I can send you down to it."

### WK01. The Post That Would Not Sound

**What a new player is told, and when.** On first speaking to Haxara-Tei, before any job, five optional lines,
each two or three sentences. *The keepers*: "Between Helstrom and the lakes you steer by a few things that
somebody keeps: a pool where the worm is called, a chime in the fog, a fire on the ridge, a lamp on the water. We
are the somebody." *A voice*: "Each post says which post it is. Myroleon's chime is struck four times and then
rests. If you knew every post's voice you would never be lost in this marsh, and so we tell nobody." *The gift*:
"We take a stranger through. We do not ask a fare and we do not ask a name. We do not teach them the way." *Why
me*: "My walker is one man with one pair of knees, and three posts have sent bad word this season. You came here
across ground that kills people. That is all I need to know about you." *What it pays and why I might not*: "A
bed at every post, a keeper's things and a little silver. And you will walk alone to places where what went wrong
is still there." "Not yet" stays open and free. The job is four sentences: Myroleon is a hamlet of herb-gatherers
on the first channels toward Blackrose, across the marsh from Korydon's landing (D ultherus-swamp, Myroleon: 590 m
from the boat stop); its chime-post, the oldest in the Ultherus, has not sounded for boats since the last fog, and
boats from Blackrose are running blind; its tender, Sheeva-Jul, sends no word; "go on foot, find why, and see that
it sounds again." She gives thirty in silver for the road. Mereesh, the walker, stands in the same room and
answers one question for anyone who asks: what lies between, that a spriggan holds the hummock where Myroleon's
rare herbs grow and is passed "slowly, from the water side, or not at all", and that a post's count can be
changed, though only the First Keeper may order it.

**The hidden truth (X).** The reed-robbers who work the channels toward Blackrose bought Myroleon's count from a
keeper: four strokes and a rest, cut on a tin tongue, and struck for them once on a chime so that they had the
wrist of it. They had a chime cast in new tin by a tinker who stamps nothing, and hung it on a stake that stands
in the water at the edge of the hummock where "a spriggan guards ... the rarest dragon's-tongue" (D
ultherus-swamp, Myroleon: hooks). In the last fog Sheeva-Jul struck four for a boat coming up from Blackrose, and
the robbers' youngest struck four back. The pilot steered between the two sounds and ran onto the hummock. The
spriggan killed him. The robbers took the cargo from the water side, where the spriggan does not go, and Sheeva-Jul
poled the boat home herself: "a boat came up from Blackrose with a dead man aboard" (the same hooks). She has not
struck the post since. She told Korydon nothing, because the answer came in her post's own voice, and only a
keeper could have given that away. The robbers' ringer has since run (WK02), and the false chime hangs unrung.

### WK02. No Name at the Pool

Offered once WK01 has ended completed.

**What a new player is told, and when.** A traveller has stood at Korydon's root pool since before the player
first came to the town; the game names him "Nameless Traveller", and until this quest he says one thing to anyone:
"I asked the gift. I am waiting to hear whether it is still given." Haxara-Tei's briefing is five sentences. He
asked passage to Helstrom by the worm and gave no name, as is his right. The elders' watch say the boat he came in
was a Blackrose boat with its cargo gone, and that he is one of the reed-robbers; he says nothing. "The gift asks
no name. I am First Keeper and I will not have the worm called blind for a man who may have killed on my ways."
The robbers moor in the reeds below Myroleon, and the watch say they keep a boat-hand of their last boat alive
there. "Find out which he is. Then you say, since you will have looked." At the pool, three people each say their
part to anyone who asks: **Tulaxi**, who calls the worm at Korydon ("I would call it now. The day the pool asks a
name, it is a toll gate"); **Ruxheel**, a spear of the elders' watch ("A pilot is dead. The gift was never meant
for the ones who break the ways"); and Mereesh, inside the house, who knows how the mooring lies because he
passes it on the round: no watch on the water side, and the chest where they sleep.

**The hidden truth (X).** The traveller is the reed-robbers' ringer: the youngest of five, who struck the false
chime at Myroleon. He was told the boat would ground softly and be stripped. The pilot died. On the next boat the
band's leader decided that dead crews carry no tales: two were killed and the pole-hand, **Weeja**, was kept on a
rope to work. The ringer cut her rope half through, took the notched tongue the band had bought, and ran in the
stripped boat to the one road the band cannot follow. The band's leader and two more are out on the water (the
record's robbers "at the wreck of the Blackrose boat" off Orji-Hael, D ultherus-swamp, which this quest does not
go to); two keep the mooring. He will not say any of it at the pool, because every word of it is an Owing or a
spear.

## 6. For the other lines

For the consistency pass (nw-05 step 5). None of these changes another line's stages.

- **Reed-Sail.** The bought lights are honoured from this side: no keeper names a pilot, and no line of this
  story says who in the knot pays. The lantern that is paid for is Luntoc's, and its tender is Old Skeeju, the
  island's old pilot, whom [reed-sail](reed-sail.md) left to this line; whether that is the "third light" of RS03
  this story does not say, and RS03 reads the same if Luntoc is dark, since a passage's third light may be any
  lantern. From the rank of Tender, Haxara-Tei, Skeeju and Sheeva-Jul greet a player who holds rank with the
  Reed-Sail coolly, with a line that says why: "You steer by what we keep. I will not ask you whose lamp you
  answer." Buys-by-Daylight stands on Truowa's quay apart from Starless, Xal-Meema and Tibur; she is no friend of
  the knot and none of them speaks of her. Her hook ("a Lilmoth trader wants a guard on the boat road to
  Helstrom") is taken here.
- **Many-Root.** Demeepa-Ru, Ruleem, Jaseen and Meer-Zah are used as that story names them, and none has a stage
  in this one. Demeepa-Ru stands at the same tree as the pool; his one line for a keeper is that nobody has ever
  asked the tree whether it minds the worm. The elders named here are Elder Naxil-Wo of the chime-smiths and the
  watch's spear Ruxheel; the boatwright is left unnamed and has one line that names the Keepers' House. The
  accusation that the Tumnatu stole a chime is left exactly as that story has it: the false chime is unstamped
  new tin and is never said to be the stolen one. One ending of WK06 uses the forge's smelter; nothing is said
  about moving it.
- **The main quest.** At Helstrom nothing is said of the spring, the handler's household or the Root Talk.
  Cheeval-Xu keeps his one job in both stories and may be dead in either. On Aunike Crown this story uses the
  fire-basket, a satchel beside it, the snakes and the path from Tydeos; it never names or uses the carved stone,
  and it reads with the Naga scouts at the pass dead or alive. A player who reaches the Crown in MQ11 before WK03
  finds the basket cold under a hide and the satchel beside it, and may take the string early; WK03 notices. In
  WK06 two hired spears stand below the summit path from Tydeos; they are not on the Nacydes path. At Vesh-Aneel
  the guide is never used.
- **The Lamp-Bearers.** Nobody of this line goes onto Fort Gysilla's islet or speaks of its vanished boat.
  Luntoc's lantern is explained here and nowhere else.
- **The Chainbreakers.** Blackrose's boats are spoken of and never met in Blackrose. Okan-Shei and Dar-Meesei are
  boatmen of Blackrose who stand at Korydon and Myroleon only.
- **Local quests.** Orji-Hael's robbers "at the wreck of the Blackrose boat" are the rest of the reed-robbers'
  band, with their leader; this line leaves them to the place, and asks only that a local quest there not make
  them anything else. Nemeon's lantern "lit from the water side" is theirs too, and is told in a greeting. The
  hooks of Ixu-Maan's signal, Marzai's lantern and the lights at Nacydes and Nacydes Watch are released.
- **Travel.** A Keeper's free passage on the worm would be the right reward for the rank of Keeper; a price is
  not ours to change, so it is asked of the travel lane as a gate on rank.
- **Culture rows.** The Tumnatu and the tribeless Naga are spoken of and never met in this line; it asks for no
  row.
- **The timeline.** Nothing here is dated except "eleven winters" of Luntoc's silver and "two seasons" since the
  voices were sold. Nothing touches the trees, the spring or Vel-Sapha.
