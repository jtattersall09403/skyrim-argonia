# The Eye and the Root: the main quest's story

The story of the main quest's fifteen quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the [province plan](province-plan.md) gives it, under the lead's rulings in
[nw-05](../../../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05-story-quest-adapt-1.md) and the rulings of the
[consistency ledger](consistency.md) that make fourteen stories one province. A quest writer turns each outline in
section 3 into a record without inventing anything. It keeps the old line's name, treasure hunt, spies, people and
three endings ([main-line-outline](../main-line-outline.md)) and re-authors everything else for this world.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world ([province](../../world/province.md) and the region
files beside it; "D valley-of-storms, Nenria" is that place's record or hooks). Lore is `file:line` under
`story/lore/`; *state* is `extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation from
the cited data and lore (decision 0032). **C** is the consistency ledger, by its item ("C H2", "C 4.4"). Helstrom is
drafted and this story is fitted to it (C 2.2); the other places are not built yet: a place is named by what its
record says it is, people and things are placed only by the place and by a row of section 4, and nothing here
describes surroundings.

**Words used below.** *Hist*: the thinking trees every Argonian is hatched under and returns to. *Sap*: what a Hist
bleeds; drunk, it brings dreams the tree sends. *The Owing*: the marsh's sentence, months of owed work instead of a
cell. *Tree-minder*: the tender of a Hist. *Root-herald*: a town's gatherer of news and dealer with outsiders.
*Naheesh*: elders. *The Archwardens*: the thin successor state, which everyone calls *the Wardens*; the word means
nothing else here. *The Reed*: the Veiled Reed, the Wardens' spies, and nobody else. *The Unbound*: the cult that
cuts roots (the old design's Unbound Root); its mark is a cord cut through. *The Root*: the one place under Echo
Deep where the trees are joined. *Sick ground*: the ruins of dead peoples, which Argonians will not enter.
*Ward-stakes*: stakes set round sick ground to keep its dead in. *Naga*: tall tribeless Argonians whom no tree
claims. *Xanmeer*: an ancient Argonian stepped tower. *Mnemic Egg*: the seed a dying Hist leaves, holding all that
tree remembered.

## 1. The line in a page

**The mystery.** Someone is cutting the roots of Hist trees and carrying the sap away in jars, and the trees are
sickening: a young tree is dying at Vel-Sapha Stand and the spring of Helstrom's city tree has gone sour and been
sealed, so that the Root Talk, at which the tribes hear the trees, is late (D barsaebica, Vel-Sapha Stand; D
murkwood, Helstrom; [province-plan](province-plan.md) §1). Behind the cutting is an older question: what the Eye of
Argonia opens. Canon leaves the "priceless king's jewel of ancient Black Marsh" as perhaps "a key to the lost city"
(`eye-of-argonia.md:6` to `:8`). Here it is the key to the Root (X, kept from the old outline's Root of Accord): the
place where the trees are joined, which is not in the forest at all. The myth says the Hist's first land sank under
the southern sea but for one corner, and the divers of Bell Station say that in the trench they call Echo Deep "the
old Hist sings the drowned down" (D benthic-yobesh, history; Echo Deep). The first wood is still alive down there,
the living trees are all moored to it, and at the bottom of the trench stands "a sealed spire" (D benthic-yobesh,
Echo Deep) with the door in it.

**Who wants what.**

- **The Unbound** want every tree cut loose from the others. Their hands are tribeless Naga, paid in boats (D
  murkwood, Salt-Tooth Camp, "the one-eyed leader seeks a boat"; D barsaebica, Ushaan Camp; X for the buyer). Their
  leader, **Cuts-the-Old-Knot**, holds that the joining is a chain: a tree that is bound cannot shut out what
  another sends, which is how one mad tree at Lilmoth called a flying city down on everyone in 4E 48
  (`lilmoth.md:84`; `topics/hist-and-sap.md:19`; X for the mechanism). To cut the chain she needs a Mnemic Egg,
  which is "a gateway into the whole collective" and "a tool for severing" (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:150`), and the
  Eye to open the door. A tree must die to leave an Egg, so she is killing one. They speak of unbinding roots and
  of nothing else (C T16), and this story claims the cutting of one tree only (C C17).
- **The Collector**, Ennodius Vanin, a Nibenese buyer for the Cyrodilic Collections revived at Gideon
  ([province-vibe](../../province-vibe.md) §8), has hunted the Eye for twenty years. The Unbound call him
  Opens-the-Last-Door and he preaches for them. He no longer cares whom the jewel serves so long as he sees what it
  opens.
- **The Veiled Reed** is the Wardens' one small secret office, kept since 4E 48 to see that no tree turns again
  unnoticed (X; [province-plan](province-plan.md) §2). It has two lodgers in a tally-keeper's house in Helstrom and
  no hall. It wants the cutting stopped quietly and the jewel in the state's hand.
- **Helstrom's assembly** wants its sour spring unspoken until the Root Talk can be held (D murkwood, Helstrom).
- **The player** is a nobody off an Owing barge, of any race, on nobody's roll, which is exactly what each of the
  others can use.

**How it is played.** A hunt and a spy story on foot, by rootworm, by boat and under water. Every quest is a place
to get into and a thing to bring out, and every one has at least two ways through on different named skills: the
quiet way (Sneak, Lockpicking, Pickpocket), the spoken way (Speech, sometimes gold), the armed way (One-Handed,
Two-Handed, Archery, Block, Destruction, Conjuration), and often a knowing way (Alchemy, Restoration, Alteration,
Illusion). No quest shuts a build out: a fighter, a mage, a thief and a talker who relies on Speech and gold each
have a way through all fifteen (the talker's ways at MQ08, MQ12 and MQ14 are named there; C K4). None is won by
reading or carrying a paper. In Acts I and II no step the story requires meets a fight above the Wild band unless
another way stands beside it, and two people warn of every place above the band around it; from Act III the
fights are the place's own ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §2). Nothing is levelled (decision 0035). Breath
under water is had by any race as C 4.4 (7) lists.

**What the Reed lies about.** It says it listens. In truth it buys sap-dreams and keeps the dreamers, at Fort
Swiftmoth on the east coast: some came for the sap and the bed and would not leave if the door were open, and some
cannot leave ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §2). Its handler does not tell the player this, nor that the jar
the player carries south in Act I goes up-river to a dreamer to drink. No dreamer is in Helstrom (C H1): what the
tally-keeper's house hides is in its locked chest, empty jars of the cutters' make, which the Reed has bought
before.

**The three endings, and why players will split.** At the Root the player uses one of three things
([province-plan](province-plan.md) §2). None is the good one, and the tablet that tells the Root's history takes
no side: the trees gave their roots freely, and the kings chained them after a year in which trees closed their
roots on a dying neighbour and its people starved unheard (X). Each ending has its own advocate, whom the player
has reason both to trust and to doubt, and each gives a different kind of thing to play with afterwards (the
outcome tables are in section 2, under MQ15).

- **CUT** pours the Egg into the Root. Every tree is cut loose from every other. The sickness stops at once,
  because nothing passes between trees any more, and no tree can ever again call something down on the whole
  marsh. Nothing is left for the Reed to listen to, so no dreamer is ever kept again. The treeless and the
  tribeless are no longer lesser, since every tribe is now alone with its own tree. The cost: the Root Talk is over
  for good, the song from the trench stops, the dead tree's memory is spent, no tree answers a stranger, and the
  most feared power in the marsh is a cult that answers to the player. Its advocate is Cuts-the-Old-Knot, who
  carried the player out of a fire and killed a tree.
- **CLAIM** sets the Eye in the Root and closes the door to everyone else. The joining holds and one hand can shut
  a sick or mad tree out of it, so the sickness stops, Helstrom's spring runs sweet and the Root Talk is held as it
  always was. The Egg is not spent, and the player may carry it home to the stand whose tree died. The cost: it is
  the kings' chain again with the player as king; Helstrom's assembly sets a blood-price on the hand that took the
  seat; and the trees are still listened to. Its advocate is Never-Writes-Twice, who let a tree die and does not
  sleep.
- **MEND** gives the Egg back whole and reads the first consent into the Root, so that each tree may again refuse
  the joining. It is the only ending that undoes what the kings did. The cost is paid at once and in the
  player's sight on return: nothing is stopped by force. The tree the player knows best, Jal-Veesa's, leaves, and
  its pool gives no blessing and hears nobody; Helstrom's tree stays and its spring stays sour; the trees may now
  do again what made the kings chain them; the Unbound are not ended, hunt the player, and go on persuading trees
  to leave, and cutting; the dead tree's Egg goes to the Root and not home; the dreams the Reed buys are still
  there to be bought; and the Root Talk is held with empty places. Its advocate is Nesh-Deeka, who opened one door
  and made an enemy of the marsh by it.

A player who fears another 4E 48, or who has seen the cellar and wants no listener ever again, cuts. A player who
wants the marsh as it was, the spring sweet and the Root Talk held, and trusts their own hand, claims, and is the
only one who brings a dead tree's seed home. A player who thinks a bond is worth only what it may refuse mends,
and pays for it in trees, in a quiet pool and in an enemy left standing.

## 2. The arc

**The hidden spine (X throughout, on the cited lore), in the order it happened.**

1. When the first land sank, the trees that were left each gave a root to the drowned wood, freely, so that the
   drowned would be remembered and no tree be alone (D benthic-yobesh, history). That joining is the Root. A tree
   could close its root when it chose.
2. In the age of the xanmeers there came a year when trees closed their roots on a dying neighbour, and its
   people starved unheard. The priest-kings then had a jewel cut, the Eye, that holds the Root's door: with it a
   king could forbid a tree to close. The joining became a chain. The king's road to the door was marked on a
   height and on a tablet, and warded; the wards were kept by the ku-vastei, the order that became the Shadowscales
   (X on *state*:205 to :213). When the kings ended, the last sea-priest of Thul-Kesh shut the jewel in his Hist
   chamber and has kept the door since (D benthic-yobesh, Thul-Kesh Spire). The Root stayed as the last king left
   it: forbidden to close.
3. So a bound tree cannot shut out what another sends. In 4E 48 what Lilmoth's second tree called, the others
   could not refuse (`lilmoth.md:84`; C T4). The Wardens afterwards kept the Veiled Reed to listen for the next one.
4. Twelve years ago the Reed took in a young sap-speaker of the lake country who dreamed the same door every time.
   It kept her nine years in the cellar of Fort Swiftmoth, the keep it says is empty (D nushara-bayou, Fort
   Swiftmoth), knotting a tally cord for each dream it bought. Her listener, Nesh-Deeka, let her out three years
   ago and reported her dead. She cut her cord through and took a name from it; a cut cord is her people's mark.
5. Cuts-the-Old-Knot gathered Naga, whom no tree claims, and found the Collector, who knew of Lark of Rosgard, the
   masked thief who sought the Eye (`eye-of-argonia.md:9`), and of the East Empire Company's diving sphere (D
   benthic-yobesh, The Kelp Lantern). He taught her what the jewel is; she taught him what the door is.
6. This year her cutters bled the lone young tree at Vel-Sapha to death for its Egg. Its dying passes down the
   joining to trees that cannot refuse it: Helstrom's spring sours.
7. At Sedge-Kal Camp she paid two men of an Owing gang to burn the reed along the cutters' path. One asked for
   more. Her people fired the gang's huts with him inside, and she herself carried a fevered stranger, bought off
   the roll by the gang-master and lying unwritten in his hut, down to the barge. The stranger is the player.
8. The Collector lacked three things that say where the jewel lies, so he had a false Eye offered at Gideon to
   draw out whoever held them (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon). The player gathers all three, and he lets them.
9. Never-Writes-Twice, told of Vel-Sapha by the player's own report, sent nobody. A dead tree's Egg would be
   carried to the door, and the Reed's standing purpose is to put the state's hand on that door. Nesh-Deeka argued,
   was found to have freed dreamers, and was put in the cellar.
10. The Egg went down the creeks to the sea. The spire's outer seal opens to the dead, and an Egg is a dead tree:
    Cuts-the-Old-Knot is inside, at a door only the Eye opens, waiting for the one person who will bring it.

**The acts and their quests.** Fifteen quests. Each is a different kind of thing to do; scale climbs from one gang
to one tree, a city's spring, three coasts, the state's own house, and the Root. The turn is MQ10, two-thirds
through. Earlier choices change greetings and single lines, read from stored numbers, and what the choice itself
gave; they never change what a later quest asks. Only the ending carries into the world.

*Act I, the burnt camp (the north). Full outlines in section 3.*

- **MQ01 No Name on the Roll** (Sedge-Kal Camp). A culprit among a gang: get a fire-lighter's pay out of his
  kit-box by a witness, a word, a quiet hand or a knife. Carried on: the purse and its cut cord; who was named.
- **MQ02 The Cutters' Jar** (Jal-Veesa, Nenria). A thieves' camp on sick ground: bring out a jar of sap by stealth,
  by a fight or by turning the town's own youth. The player joins the Reed. Carried on: the jar; where the youth
  went.
- **MQ03 The Tree at the Edge** (Vel-Sapha Stand). A wary people, a wound, the crew that made it, and a truth to
  tell or keep. Carried on: the stand's place at the Root Talk; what the stand was told.
- **MQ04 The Sour Spring** (Helstrom). A forbidden draught taken in the oldest city, in front of its people.
  Carried on: rank in the Reed; the three leads.

*Act II, the hunt (three spokes from Helstrom, in any order). Nesh-Deeka gives all three leads at once and says
which is gentlest and which may kill.*

- **MQ05 The Glass Eye** (Gideon; Gideon South Watch). A dealer who "sold a Kothringi silver mask twice in one
  week" has the Eye of Argonia on offer (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon). The stone is glass. Its old setting, cut
  with a route cipher, is real and already sold on to the delta smugglers, and lies in "the smugglers' cache in the
  hidden cellar" under the leaning tower (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon South Watch). The Collector has left the
  player a courteous note with the dealer. The smugglers are Dangerous, so three ways stand side by side: in under
  the pier and out with the setting (Sneak with Lockpicking, or Pickpocket for the boss's key); as a buyer with the
  dealer's token, got by Speech or gold, to the one smuggler who talks before the crossbows do (Speech, gold); or
  by storm (weapons, Destruction), which the dealer and Gideon's watch both say has killed better hands. What it
  shows of the Unbound: the smugglers' last cargo up the delta was boats, bought for Naga with new-struck coin.
  Carried on: the setting; the note; the knowledge that someone is ahead.
- **MQ06 The Kelp Lantern** (Lilmoth; Bell Station; the wreck). Before the secession the Company lowered a brass
  diving sphere to reach the spires, and its "locked brass hatch" has never been opened (D benthic-yobesh, The
  Kelp Lantern). The key came down to Bell Station's winch-master, Sabina Lentus, granddaughter of the Company's
  salvage master (X), who will not lend it. It is lifted from her locker (Pickpocket or Lockpicking), talked or
  bought out of her (Speech, gold), or earned by killing the dreugh that has taken the station's lower bell-stage
  (D benthic-yobesh, Bell Station, "a bell hauled up with something clinging to it"; a fight on a floor). Then the
  dive: breath by Alteration, by Alchemy or by the sap of Lilmoth's young tree (D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth), the
  shoal swum round and never fought, the hatch opened from outside as a locked box. Behind it are the Company's
  lens-frame and a pilot's brass tag: "Lowered for the seventh. The Factor says the trench after." The Collector's
  hired diver is on the platform asking after the same key. Carried on: the lens-frame; the word "trench".
- **MQ07 The Thief Who Wore a Mask** (Blackrose; the Rose). The hardest, and Nesh-Deeka says so. Lark of Rosgard
  died a prisoner of the old fortress (X), and what the warders took off him lies in "the old warden's office" (D
  tulmek-washes, Blackrose Prison): his mask, scratched on the inside with seven towers and a mark on the
  outermost. The heirs are Deadly and hostile on sight; the Rose's own hook is "to break in, to bargain with the
  raj-kaal". One heir at the gate speaks, the gate-keeper the Chainbreakers' story names (C N33, P9). The bargain is
  carried by her, and it has three prices, each of which makes the player the Rose's guest: 800 gold; Speech; or
  the challenge the Rose keeps for strangers, "a sanctum path left open to test challengers" (D tulmek-washes,
  Blackrose Prison), which here is one champion who stands by the office door, hard and fixed, whose death is the
  price paid. The guest faction is the one the Chainbreakers' leave sets, so a player who already holds that leave
  is a guest here, and a guest made here is one there (C P9). If the heir at the gate is dead, no bargain is
  carried: her brother has none for whoever killed her, and the other two ways stand. The break-in is by a bastion
  the marsh has crept into, told of by the Blackguard deserter who trains on Blackrose's boardwalks (Sneak, then
  Lockpicking on the office and its case). Fighting the whole garrison is the place's own fight, and two people
  say it will kill. A Naga of the Unbound stands in the yard, come to buy the mask with boats and laughed at; he
  says his mistress will have the jewel from whoever fetches it. Carried on: the mask.
- *The three together.* A line of Nesh-Deeka's, gated on all three held, names the place: Thul-Kesh, the outermost
  of the seven drowned spires. The same line tells the act's news: the young tree at Vel-Sapha is dead, its people
  sing the mourning, and the cutters dug from its root what a dead tree leaves: a Mnemic Egg, which holds all that
  tree remembered and, poured where the trees are joined, would cut every one of them from the rest. Nobody was
  sent. Never-Writes-Twice speaks for the second time: "A tree was always going to die of this. Be glad it was a
  small one." This is the first stage of MQ08.

*Act III, the spies (the south-east sea and the east coast).*

- **MQ08 The Sea-Priest's Door** (Bell Station; Thul-Kesh Spire). A long dive to a dry temple. "Drowned priests'
  attendants in the outer galleries" stand on floors and are passed by Sneak or fought; the Collector's hired
  divers' kit lies among them. The talker's way is over their heads: the bell-tenders lower a bell "to the
  priest's stair", the spire's upper landing beyond the galleries, for whoever talks or pays the winch-master's
  crew into it (Speech, or gold; the bell is a door that her line unlocks). "A drowned sea-priest's ghost keeps
  the sealed door", which "opens only when the ghost is put to rest" (D benthic-yobesh, Thul-Kesh Spire). He
  speaks and does not attack. He asks whose hand comes for the king's jewel, and yields to Lark's mask held ("the
  thief I turned away sends his face"), to Speech, or to Restoration (a rite to sing him down): the line unlocks
  the door. Or the player strikes first and puts him to rest by force, and his death unlocks it. Taking the Eye
  from its stone setting shows the dream, as a message: a ring of trees standing in black water, a door nobody
  built, a singing. Carried on: the Eye, which cannot be lost.
- **MQ09 A Sermon Against Roots** (Seafalls). Nesh-Deeka sends her one deniable hand to the Unbound's own door with
  the jewel as bait. They gather where no root reaches, among people who "live too far from any tree to hear the
  Hist clearly" (D province, Peoples: Spray-Folk). The Collector preaches there, knows whose hand took his three
  pieces, and says nothing of it. The player needs the Unbound's mark, the cut cord that passes their boats: won
  from him in private talk (Speech), taken from the locked chest in his lodging (Pickpocket for the key, or
  Lockpicking), or had from the pilot-elder, Reads-the-White-Water, who took one off a drowned listener and will
  give it to whoever drives the raiders off her landing, a crew of no charter (D okuwun-rahei, Seafalls; a fight
  in the Wild band; C P10). If she is dead, her successor Nomu gives it on the same terms. A player who let Teemal
  go to the boats in MQ02 has a fourth way: a listener carries the boy's word and his cord for "the one she
  carried". His sermon says plainly what the Unbound will do: pour a dead tree's Egg where the trees are joined,
  and every tree is its own. Whatever the way, he then tells the player where to look, because he wants it seen:
  Fort Swiftmoth, inland of Archon. If he is dead, a letter to the Collections in his chest names the fort, and
  Cuts-the-Old-Knot stands alone at the end. Back in Helstrom, Ixtul-Bei's House holds only Never-Writes-Twice,
  who says Nesh-Deeka has gone up-river. Carried on: the mark; the fort's name; no handler.
- **MQ10 Dreams for Sale** (Fort Swiftmoth). The line's turn. The keep is "empty since the Legion left", with "a
  locked cellar door below the old garrison's strongroom" and a watch the coast takes for smugglers (D
  nushara-bayou, Fort Swiftmoth). They are the Reed's gaolers, and Deadly. In by stealth (Sneak; Lockpicking, or
  Pickpocket for the chief gaoler's key), by force, or by the old ex-privateer who keeps a room upstairs and knows
  the drain under the cellar (Speech, or gold). Below are pallets, dreamers, a tally cord cut through and hung
  above an empty pallet, and Nesh-Deeka. She says what the Reed is and whom she freed, and that the tree was left
  to die; she also says, before the player has met it, what she would do with the door under the sea if it were
  hers: give the trees back their no. Then the choice, which ends the quest, and which she and an old dreamer
  argue from either side (table below). Nesh-Deeka, staying or going, gives the last thing she knows: every
  dreamer dreams a carved stone on a height above lakes, the marks on it are warded, and the wards are Shadowscale
  teaching. If she has died in the cellar, the old dreamer says the same. Carried on: the gate open or shut;
  Nesh-Deeka freed, left or dead; nobody left to report to.

  **The cellar's gate.** Nesh-Deeka argues for opening it: "Nobody asked them. Some will die of it, and it will
  be their dying." The old dreamer argues for leaving it: outside he has no tree, no sap and no bed, and he has
  seen what the marsh does to the ones who walk out with the sap still in them. The chief gaoler, if he lives and
  can be spoken to from the drain, says what the fort is worth to a Hand of the Reed who leaves it be.

## 3. Act I's quests in full

Common to all four.

- **One working stage, and the stages its ways need.** After its briefing each quest sits at one working stage,
  at which every way through is open at once. A line only moves a stage, so a talked way has a stage of its own
  below the stage that the quest's one named thing sets when it is taken; what a stage gives, takes, unlocks or
  switches is written against it. A thing taken before its stage is noticed when the stage is reached.
- **Who must live.** Each quest names the people it cannot go on without. If one dies the quest fails with a
  journal entry and the main quest's message. Nobody stands in for anybody, so every journal entry names the
  person who truly spoke. Everyone else can die at any moment and the tables say what that leaves.
- **Nobody is made hostile by a line.** A person is placed hostile or speaks, never both; where a speaker can be
  fought, the player starts it.
- **Switches** are made only by a line spoken inside Vosh-Leera's house, about people and things outside it.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense,
  and say only what the player has just done. Skill thresholds are fixed. Each failed check has its own refusal
  and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms.
- "Night" and "dusk" in what people say are their words only; no step depends on the hour. A theft seen and a
  trespass are answered by Skyrim's own crime system under the place's law (D province, Power and law); no quest
  checks them.
- **Every quest has one way that needs no skill at all**, only legwork or a fight a careful new character can win,
  so that no build is stopped. The Naga of Act I are fixed below the Wild band's ten.
- **The player's own question is kept alive**: in each quest one person says something of the woman who carried
  the player out of the fire.
- Words: the people of Jal-Veesa are *the reed people* in every mouth and journal, and their own guards are
  *weir-keepers* and *stake-keepers* (C N43).

### MQ01. No Name on the Roll

**What a new player is told, and when.** The player comes out of the hold and Dralin Uveran speaks first. Four
sentences say where this is, what he is and what the player is: "Sedge-Kal Camp. The reed people cut here for
Stormhold, the city down the track. I am gang-master of the owing men. Your name is on no roll of mine." Then the
job, four sentences: his gang's huts burned in the night, and one of the gang, Counts-His-Luck, has coin he did
not have yesterday, in the kit-box he will not walk away from; a gang-master may not lay a hand on an owing man
before the Vicecanons' counter has come up from Stormhold (C C19); the player is on nobody's roll, so nobody
answers for what the player does; he wants the purse taken to Vosh-Leera at Jal-Veesa, the reed people's town
south-west by the track, "their root-herald, the one who deals with everyone who is not them. She will ask you
who lit it". The counter is glossed as he names it: "Stormhold's man, who counts heads and months". The chest in the
hold is the player's to empty. Four optional lines after it: *the Owing* ("there are no cells in the marsh; you
work off what you owe, and these men owe their months to Stormhold"); *the fire* ("one man, Tarries, did not come
out of his hut; ask the free cutters, one of them was up in the night"); *why I am aboard* ("somebody carried you
down here while my huts burned; you are not mine and I am not feeding you"); *what do I tell her?* ("name him.
One bought man costs me one man. A fire with no name on it is my gang's fire, and thirty men lose their hire").

**The hidden truth.** Three nights ago a border carter sold Dralin a fevered stranger off the roll, to be worked
without being written (X, on `topics/labour-and-bondage.md:90`, an Owing sold on). Dralin kept the purchase in his
own hut. Two of his gang, Counts-His-Luck and Tarries, had taken coin from a tall woman with a cut cord at her
wrist to burn the reed along the old channel, so that no crew would cut there (D valley-of-storms, Sedge-Kal
Camp, "a stranger who is paying for reed to be burned"). Tarries asked for more and spoke of the watcher. Last
night her people fired the gang's huts with Tarries in his. She carried the stranger out of Dralin's hut and laid
them in the barge. Counts-His-Luck saw it. An unwritten body is the one thing Dralin cannot let the counter find.

### MQ02. The Cutters' Jar

**What a new player is told, and when.** Vosh-Leera looks at the cut cord and says it is the third she has seen
this season, and that it is Naga pay: "tall folk whom no tree claims; they rob roads and take any coin". Before any
job, four optional lines: *a root-herald* ("I hear what comes up every road, and I speak for this town to people
who are not of it"); *the Hist* ("the tree in our pool; we are hatched under it and go back to it, and its sap,
drunk, sends dreams"); *sick ground* ("the white ruins of people long dead, whose dead still stand in them; we
stake them round to keep them in, and none of us will step inside the stakes"; nobody in Act I says why the
ground is sick, C T15); *the Veiled Reed* ("I sell some of what I hear to a few people in Helstrom, the old city
in the forest, who listen for a tree going wrong. Generations ago a tree at Lilmoth went mad and called a flying
city down on the whole marsh. They serve the Archwardens, whom everyone calls the Wardens, and the Wardens are all
the government this marsh has. They would pay for a hand that is on nobody's roll. Nobody here loves them, and I
have never seen what they do with what I sell"). The job: Geel-Masu the weir-keeper sees lights at Nenria, the
white ruin south of the town; someone is camped on its open top, on ground her own people will not enter, and
carrying jars out of it. She wants their jar. "Do not go under the arch. What stands there would kill ten of
you." Taking the job is joining: 50 gold, and "then you are the Reed's hand in the north until somebody better
comes". "Not yet" stays open and free, and she does not ask twice.

**The hidden truth.** Three Naga under Kharzal carry sap from the young tree at Vel-Sapha along the weir road and
keep it in Nenria's outer part, which no reed person will enter, until a boat's crew fetches it from the creek.
They pulled the ward-stakes on the dry side to make themselves a path, and so that the stake-keepers would think
the dead were stirring and keep further off (D valley-of-storms, Nenria, "ward-stakes pulled up one by one").
Teemal, a weir-keeper's son, carries the small jars to the creek for the promise of a place on a boat (D
valley-of-storms, Nenria, "a Jal-Veesa youth seen near the mound at dusk"). The hall's own dead are Deadly, stand
deeper in, and have nothing to do with any of it.

taps the tree and sells sap to passers (D barsaebica, Vel-Sapha Stand, "a sap-gatherer selling Hist sap"), and
have forbidden her the root. The crew is still moored at the creek landing below the stand, waiting for the last

**The ways through.** The hook is what the quest is after; the root is how most players learn to look for it.

- *By the creek without the roots (no skill).* Wuleen-Tei, asked what she says to the charge, has heard a boat on
  the creek below the stand at night; her line sends the player there as well.

you would walk in some day. She did not say whose side you would be on. Yes, we cut it, with the hook in that

### MQ04. The Sour Spring

**What a new player is told, and when.** Vosh-Leera's last line of MQ03 gives the way: Asheem-Lei will call the
rootworm at the pool, the beast of the roots that swallows a traveller here and lets them out at the foot of
Helstrom's city tree; in Helstrom, go down to the city landing and find the tally-keeper's house, the house
nearest it, whose door says Ixtul-Bei's House, and ask there for Nesh-Deeka, who "speaks to no stranger, and will
speak to one who carries a root-knot". Nesh-Deeka, shown the knot, has four optional lines: *Helstrom* ("the
oldest city; nobody has ever taken it; the elders and tree-minders rule it, and its guard, the levy, answers to
no outsider"); *the Reed* ("the tally-keeper, who writes down everything landed here, and his two lodgers, and a
few listeners like your root-herald; we serve the Wardens and the city takes us for boat people"); *the Root
Talk* ("when every elder of the forest comes here to drink at the city tree's spring and hear the trees speak
together; it is late this year and nobody will say why"); *what it pays and why you might not* ("coin, a chest of
your own in this house and a name nobody admits to; and if the levy takes you at the spring I never saw you").
The job: the spring at the foot of the city tree has gone sour, and the assembly has sealed it: the lidded urn
the worm set the player down beside. She cannot go near it; she is watched, and a guest of the Root Talk is not.
She wants a gourd of it to set beside the jar. She asks one thing first: "The woman with the cut cord carried you
out of a fire she lit. Do you know why? No. Nor do I, and I do not like it."

**The hidden truth.** Helstrom's tree is joined to the tree at Vel-Sapha, as every bound tree is to every other,
and cannot shut out its dying: the spring has soured since the heart-root was cut. The assembly has sealed the
spring, set a spear of the levy by it, "will not speak of its sour spring in open session" (D murkwood, Helstrom),
and put the Root Talk off. A water-carrier brings river water to the houses "in the weeks when the city spring is
left untouched", and the sap-seller drew a gourd of it to study before it was sealed (X, on D people, Ruxa-Neel,
Wujeel). The jar the herald sent is locked in the house's chest that night, among empty jars of the same make,
and goes up-river to a dreamer. Nobody says so.

## 5. Read on paper

### Round one

One round, on the first draft, all reading one version: two players given the story only as a player meets it,
Act I sheet by sheet and then the later acts in summary, asked after each what they thought was going on, whom
they trusted, what they expected and what they would choose, with the hidden spine and the endings kept from them
until they reached them; and one deliverability reader given the ceiling, the quest form, the build briefs and
the whole document.

**What they said.**

- *The player who loves the lore* (a talker and alchemist) was keen at every sheet, trusted the witness and the
  herald, distrusted the Reed from the back room onward, guessed the net-mender ran the house, and would retell
  the charm shown without a word, "what hatches under another tree is that tree's" and the stand's dead clutch
  being in the Egg. Told the truth at Vel-Sapha and was shaken by what it cost; opened the cellar gate "sick about
  it"; warned Sesk and lied to the officer; chose MEND with CLAIM the temptation. Hard to please on: words used
  before they were explained, and three kinds of "warden"; the player's own story dropped for three quests; the
  swerve from sour sap to a jewel at the end of Act I, with the Eye's purpose untold until Act IV; Act II as three
  fetches and one dive too many; Dralin's lie never answered; the Collector and Never-Writes-Twice thin before
  their big scenes; what pouring the Egg does told too late; a keeper that must be fought; MEND a little too
  plainly the wise ending, its cost late and on others.
- *The impatient fighter* was doing something inside a minute and stayed keen, would retell "the villain is the
  one who carried me out of the fire" and "the spies I worked for were farming dreams in a cellar", and made the
  same choices by another road: MEND, CLAIM the temptation, CUT respected. Hard to please on: the same unexplained
  words and near-identical titles; a new character's kit against three Naga; no fighter's way at the root gate, at
  the spring, at the Rose (where the fighter's way was the one called suicide) or in the last room, where the only
  way without Speech was to kill the woman who saved them; killing a frightened weakling as the fighter's only way
  in the first quest; most of the purse spent on a gourd.
- *The deliverability reader* found the people, locks, keys and fights buildable and none of the four outlines
  buildable as written. Blocking: a failure on "both dead", which no trigger can carry; stand-in speakers whose
  journal entries named the wrong person; MQ03's two stages reachable out of order; a seller who was hostile on
  sight; things taken early that skipped a briefing and the joining; "a jar" and "a gourd" that were several
  different things; a fallback resting on a purse already handed in; a switch made in sight when a stand-in spoke
  outdoors; a name that could be given for a youth never met; lessons no result can give; a guard "giving a
  container's contents". Also: no lockpicks anywhere, level-20 crocodiles at the starting landing, looks that may

  carriers missing in the later acts if the Collector, Nesh-Deeka or Never-Writes-Twice dies.
