# The Eye and the Root: the main quest's story

The story of the main quest's fifteen quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the [province plan](province-plan.md) gives it and under the lead's rulings in
[nw-05](../../../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05-story-quest-adapt-1.md). A quest writer turns each outline
in section 3 into a record without inventing anything. It keeps the old line's name, treasure hunt, spies, people and
three endings ([main-line-outline](../main-line-outline.md)) and re-authors everything else for this world.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world ([province](../../world/province.md) and the region
files beside it; "D valley-of-storms, Nenria" is that place's record or hooks). Lore is `file:line` under
`story/lore/`; *state* is `extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation from
the cited data and lore (decision 0032). The places are not built yet: a place is named by what its record says it
is, people and things are placed only by the place and by a row of section 4, and nothing here describes
surroundings.

**Words used below.** *Hist*: the thinking trees every Argonian is hatched under and returns to. *Sap*: what a Hist
bleeds; drunk, it brings dreams the tree sends. *The Owing*: the marsh's sentence, months of owed work instead of a
cell. *Tree-minder*: the tender of a Hist. *Root-herald*: a town's gatherer of news and dealer with outsiders.
*Naheesh*: elders. *The Wardens*: the thin successor state. *Sick ground*: the ruins of dead peoples, which
Argonians will not enter. *Ward-stakes*: stakes set round sick ground to keep its dead in. *Naga*: tall tribeless
Argonians whom no tree claims. *Xanmeer*: an ancient Argonian stepped tower. *Mnemic Egg*: the seed a dying Hist
leaves, holding all that tree remembered.

## 1. The line in a page

**The mystery.** Someone is cutting the roots of Hist trees and carrying the sap away in jars, and the trees are
sickening: a young tree is dying at Vel-Sapha Stand and the spring under Helstrom's city tree has gone sour, so
that the Root Talk, at which the tribes hear the trees, is late (D barsaebica, Vel-Sapha Stand; D murkwood,
Helstrom; [province-plan](province-plan.md) §1). Behind the cutting is an older question: what the Eye of Argonia
opens. Canon leaves the "priceless king's jewel of ancient Black Marsh" as perhaps "a key to the lost city"
(`eye-of-argonia.md:6` to `:8`). Here it is the key to the Root of Accord (X, kept from the old outline): the place
where the trees are joined, which is not in the forest at all. The myth says the Hist's first land sank under the
southern sea but for one corner, and the divers of Bell Station say that in the trench they call Echo Deep "the old
Hist sings the drowned down" (D benthic-yobesh, history; Echo Deep). The first wood is still alive down there, the
living trees are all moored to it, and at the bottom of the trench stands "a sealed spire" (D benthic-yobesh, Echo
Deep) with the door in it.

**Who wants what.**

- **The Unbound Root** wants every tree cut loose from the others. Its hands are tribeless Naga, paid in boats (D
  murkwood, Salt-Tooth Camp, "the one-eyed leader seeks a boat"; D barsaebica, Ushaan Camp; X for the buyer). Its
  leader, **Cuts-the-Old-Knot**, holds that the joining is a chain: a tree that is bound cannot shut out what
  another sends, which is how one mad tree at Lilmoth called a flying city down on everyone in 4E 48
  (`lilmoth.md:84`; `topics/hist-and-sap.md:19`; X for the mechanism). To cut the chain she needs a Mnemic Egg,
  which is "a gateway into the whole collective" and "a tool for severing" (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:150`), and the
  Eye to open the door. A tree must die to leave an Egg, so she is killing one.
- **The Collector**, Ennodius Vanin, a Nibenese buyer for the Cyrodilic Collections revived at Gideon
  ([province-vibe](../../province-vibe.md) §8), has hunted the Eye for twenty years. The cult calls him
  Opens-the-Last-Door and he preaches for it. He no longer cares whom the jewel serves so long as he sees what it
  opens.
- **The Veiled Reed** is the Wardens' one small secret office, kept since 4E 48 to see that no tree turns again
  unnoticed (X; [province-plan](province-plan.md) §2). It has a household in Helstrom and no hall. It wants the
  cutting stopped quietly and the jewel in the state's hand.
- **Helstrom's assembly** wants its sour spring unspoken until the Root Talk can be held (D murkwood, Helstrom).
- **The player** is a nobody off an Owing barge, of any race, on nobody's roll, which is exactly what each of the
  others can use.

**How it is played.** A hunt and a spy story on foot, by rootworm, by boat and under water. Every quest is a place
to get into and a thing to bring out, and every one has at least two ways through on different named skills: the
quiet way (Sneak, Lockpicking, Pickpocket), the spoken way (Speech, sometimes gold), the armed way (One-Handed,
Two-Handed, Archery, Block, Destruction, Conjuration), and often a knowing way (Alchemy, Restoration, Alteration,
Illusion, Mysticism). No quest shuts a build out, and none is won by reading or carrying a paper. In Acts I and II
no step the story requires meets a fight above the Wild band unless another way stands beside it, and two people
warn of every place above the band around it; from Act III the fights are the place's own
([province-plan](province-plan.md) §2). Nothing is levelled (decision 0035).

**What the Reed lies about.** It says it listens. In truth it buys sap-dreams and keeps the dreamers: some came
for the sap and the bed and would not leave if the door were open, and some cannot leave
([province-plan](province-plan.md) §2). Its handler does not tell the player this, nor that the jar the player
carries south in Act I is given to a dreamer to drink.

**The three endings, and why players will split.** At the Root the player uses one of three things
([province-plan](province-plan.md) §2). None is the good one, and the tablet that tells the Root's history takes
no side: the trees gave their roots freely, and the kings chained them after a year in which trees closed their
roots on a dying neighbour and its people starved unheard (X).

- **CUT** pours the Egg into the Root. Every tree is cut loose from every other. The sickness stops at once,
  because nothing passes between trees any more, and no tree can ever again call something down on the whole
  marsh. The treeless and the tribeless are no longer lesser, since every tribe is now alone with its own tree. The
  cost: the Root Talk is over for good, the song from the trench stops, the dead tree's memory is spent, and the
  most feared power in the marsh is a cult that answers to the player.
- **CLAIM** sets the Eye in the Root and closes the door to everyone else. The joining holds and one hand can shut
  a sick or mad tree out of it, so the sickness stops and the Root Talk is held. The Egg is not spent, and the
  player may carry it home to the stand whose tree died. The cost: it is the kings' chain again with the player as
  king. The Wardens' last officer will back the claim, and Helstrom will not forgive it.
- **MEND** gives the Egg back whole and reads the first consent into the Root, so that each tree may again refuse
  the joining. It is the only ending that undoes what the kings did. The cost is paid at once and in the
  player's sight on return: nothing is stopped by force. The tree the player knows best, Jal-Veesa's, leaves, and
  its pool gives no blessing and hears nobody; Helstrom's tree stays and its spring stays sour; the trees may now
  do again what made the kings chain them; the cult is not ended, and whoever of it lives goes on persuading
  trees to leave, and cutting; the dead tree's Egg goes to the Root and not home; and the Root Talk is held with
  empty places.

A player who fears another 4E 48 cuts. A player who has seen what a tree left to itself can suffer, and trusts
their own hand claims, and is the only one who brings a dead tree's seed home. A player who thinks a bond is
worth only what it may refuse mends, and pays for it in trees and in a quiet pool.

## 2. The arc

**The hidden spine (X throughout, on the cited lore), in the order it happened.**

1. When the first land sank, the trees that were left each gave a root to the drowned wood, freely, so that the
   drowned would be remembered and no tree be alone (D benthic-yobesh, history). That joining is the Root of
   Accord. A tree could close its root when it chose.
2. In the age of the xanmeers there came a year when trees closed their roots on a dying neighbour, and its
   people starved unheard. The priest-kings then had a jewel cut, the Eye, that holds the Root's door: with it a
   king could forbid a tree to close. The Accord became a chain. The king's road to the door was marked on a height and
   on a tablet, and warded; the wards were kept by the ku-vastei, the order that became the Shadowscales (X on
   *state*:205 to :213). When the kings ended, the last sea-priest of Thul-Kesh shut the jewel in his Hist chamber
   and has kept the door since (D benthic-yobesh, Thul-Kesh Spire). The Root stayed as the last king left it:
   forbidden to close.
3. So a bound tree cannot shut out what another sends. In 4E 48 what Lilmoth's tree called, the others could not
   refuse (`lilmoth.md:84`). The Wardens afterwards kept the Veiled Reed to listen for the next one.
4. Twelve years ago the Reed took in a young sap-speaker of the lake country who dreamed the same door every time.
   It kept her nine years in the cellar of Fort Swiftmoth, the keep it says is empty (D nushara-bayou, Fort
   Swiftmoth), knotting a cord for each dream it bought. Her listener, Nesh-Deeka, let her out three years ago
   and reported her dead. She cut her cord and took a name from it.
5. Cuts-the-Old-Knot gathered Naga, whom no tree claims, and found the Collector, who knew of Lark of Rosgard, the
   masked thief who sought the Eye (`eye-of-argonia.md:9`), and of the East Empire Company's diving sphere (D
   benthic-yobesh, The Kelp Lantern). He taught her what the jewel is; she taught him what the door is.
6. This year her cutters bled the lone young tree at Vel-Sapha to death for its Egg. Its dying passes down the
   joining to trees that cannot refuse it: Helstrom's spring sours.
7. At Sedge-Kal Camp she paid two men of an Owing gang to burn the reed along the cutters' path. One asked for
   more. Her people fired the gang's huts with him inside, and she herself carried a fevered stranger, bought off
   the roll by the gang-master and lying unwritten in his hut, down to the barge. The stranger is the player.
8. The Collector lacked three things that say where the jewel lies, so he had a false Eye offered at Gideon to
   draw out whoever held them (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon). The player gathers all three, and he lets them.
9. Never-Writes-Twice, told of Vel-Sapha by the player's own report, sent nobody. A dead tree's Egg would be
   carried to the door, and the Reed's standing purpose is to put the state's hand on that door. Nesh-Deeka argued,
   was found to have freed dreamers, and was put in the cellar.
10. The Egg went down the creeks to the sea. The spire's outer seal opens to the dead, and an Egg is a dead tree:
    Cuts-the-Old-Knot is inside, at a door only the Eye opens, waiting for the one person who will bring it.

**The acts and their quests.** Fifteen quests. Each is a different kind of thing to do; scale climbs from one gang
to one tree, a city's spring, three coasts, the state's own house, and the Root. The turn is MQ10, two-thirds
through. Earlier choices change greetings and single lines, read from stored numbers, and never a later quest's
stages; only the ending carries into the world.

*Act I, the burnt camp (the north). Full outlines in section 3.*

- **MQ01 No Name on the Roll** (Sedge-Kal Camp). A culprit among a gang: take a fire-lighter's pay off him by
  pocket, word or knife. Carried on: the purse and its knot; who was named.
- **MQ02 The Cutters' Jar** (Jal-Veesa, Nenria). A thieves' camp on sick ground: bring out a jar of sap by stealth,
  by a fight or by turning the town's own youth. The player joins the Reed. Carried on: the jar; the youth named,
  gone or dead.
- **MQ03 The Tree at the Edge** (Vel-Sapha Stand). A wary people, a wound, the crew that made it, and a truth to
  tell or keep. Carried on: the stand's place at the Root Talk; what the stand was told.
- **MQ04 The Sour Spring** (Helstrom). A forbidden draught taken in the oldest city, under its guard. Carried on:
  rank in the Reed; the three leads.

*Act II, the hunt (three spokes from Helstrom, in any order). Nesh-Deeka gives all three leads at once and says
which is gentlest and which may kill.*

- **MQ05 The Glass Eye** (Gideon; Gideon South Watch). A dealer who "sold a Kothringi silver mask twice in one
  week" has the Eye of Argonia on offer (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon). The stone is glass. Its old setting, cut
  with a route cipher, is real and already sold on to the delta smugglers, and lies in "the smugglers' cache in the
  hidden cellar" under the leaning tower (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon South Watch). The Collector has left the
  player a courteous note with the dealer. The smugglers are Dangerous, so three ways stand side by side: in under
  the pier and out with the setting (Sneak with Lockpicking, or Pickpocket for the boss's key); as a buyer with the
  dealer's token, got by Speech or gold, to the one smuggler who talks before the crossbows do (Speech, gold); or
  by storm (weapons, Destruction), which the dealer and Gideon's watch both say has killed better hands. What it
  shows of the cult: the smugglers' last cargo up the delta was boats, bought for Naga with new-struck coin.
  Carried on: the setting; the note; the knowledge that someone is ahead.
- **MQ06 The Kelp Lantern** (Lilmoth; Bell Station; the wreck). Before the secession the Company lowered a brass
  diving sphere to reach the spires, and its "locked brass hatch" has never been opened (D benthic-yobesh, The
  Kelp Lantern). The key came down to Bell Station's winch-master, Sabina Lentus, granddaughter of the Company's
  salvage master (X), who will not lend it. It is lifted from her locker (Pickpocket or Lockpicking), talked or
  bought out of her (Speech, gold), or earned by killing the dreugh that has taken the station's lower bell-stage
  (D benthic-yobesh, Bell Station, "a bell hauled up with something clinging to it"; a fight on a floor). Then the
  dive: breath by Alteration, by Alchemy or by the sap of Lilmoth's young tree (D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth), the
  shoal swum round and never fought, the hatch opened from outside as a locked box. Behind it are the Company's
  lens-frame and a pilot's brass tag: "Lowered for the seventh. The Factor says the trench after." The Collector's
  hired diver is on the platform asking after the same key. Carried on: the lens-frame; the word "trench".
- **MQ07 The Thief Who Wore a Mask** (Blackrose; the Rose). The hardest, and Nesh-Deeka says so. Lark of Rosgard
  died a prisoner of the old fortress (X), and what the warders took off him lies in "the old warden's office" (D
  tulmek-washes, Blackrose Prison): his mask, scratched on the inside with seven towers and a mark on the
  outermost. The heirs are Deadly and hostile on sight; the Rose's own hook is "to break in, to bargain with the
  raj-kaal". One heir at the gate speaks (a need the Chainbreakers share). The bargain is carried by her, and it
  has three prices, each of which makes the player the Rose's guest (her line sets a faction, and the heirs stand
  down): 800 gold; Speech; or the challenge the Rose keeps for strangers, "a sanctum path left open to test
  challengers" (D tulmek-washes, Blackrose Prison), which here is one champion met alone in the yard, hard and
  fixed, whose death is the price paid. The break-in is by a bastion the marsh has crept into, told of by the
  Blackguard deserter who trains on Blackrose's boardwalks (Sneak, then Lockpicking on the office and its case).
  Fighting the whole garrison is the place's own fight, and two people say it will kill. A Naga of the cult stands
  in the yard, come to buy the mask with boats and laughed at; he says his mistress will have the jewel from
  whoever fetches it. Carried on: the mask.
- *The three together.* A line of Nesh-Deeka's, gated on all three held, names the place: Thul-Kesh, the outermost
  of the seven drowned spires. The same line tells the act's news: the young tree at Vel-Sapha is dead, its people
  sing the mourning, and the cutters dug from its root what a dead tree leaves: a Mnemic Egg, which holds all that
  tree remembered and, poured where the trees are joined, would cut every one of them from the rest. Nobody was
  sent. Never-Writes-Twice speaks for the second time: "A tree was always going to die of this. Be glad it was a
  small one." This is the first stage of MQ08.

*Act III, the spies (the south-east sea and the east coast).*

- **MQ08 The Sea-Priest's Door** (Bell Station; Thul-Kesh Spire). A long dive to a dry temple. "Drowned priests'
  attendants in the outer galleries" stand on floors and are passed by Sneak or fought; the Collector's hired
  divers lie dead among them. "A drowned sea-priest's ghost keeps the sealed door", which "opens only when the
  ghost is put to rest" (D benthic-yobesh, Thul-Kesh Spire). He is fought, or he is answered: he asks whose hand
  comes for the king's jewel, and yields to Lark's mask held ("the thief I turned away sends his face"), to
  Speech, or to Restoration (a rite to sing him down). A wrong answer makes him strike. Taking the Eye from its
  stone setting shows the dream, as a message: a ring of trees standing in black water, a door nobody built, a
  singing. Carried on: the Eye, which cannot be lost.
- **MQ09 A Sermon Against Roots** (Seafalls). Nesh-Deeka sends her one deniable hand to the cult's own door with
  the jewel as bait. The Unbound Root gathers where no root reaches, among people who "live too far from any tree
  to hear the Hist clearly" (D province, Peoples: Spray-Folk). The Collector preaches there, knows whose hand took
  his three pieces, and says nothing of it. The player needs the cult's mark, the knotted cord that passes its
  boats: won from him in private talk (Speech), taken from the locked chest in his lodging (Pickpocket for the
  key, or Lockpicking), or had from the pilot-elder, Reads-the-White-Water, who took one off a drowned listener
  and will give it to whoever drives the Skeith raiders off her landing (D okuwun-rahei, Seafalls; a fight in the
  Wild band). His sermon says plainly what the cult will do: pour a dead tree's Egg where the trees are joined,
  and every tree is its own. Whatever the way, he then tells the player where to look, because he wants it seen:
  Fort Swiftmoth, inland of Archon. If he is dead, a letter to the Collections in his chest names the fort, and
  Cuts-the-Old-Knot stands alone at the end. Back in Helstrom the household holds only Never-Writes-Twice, who says Nesh-Deeka has gone
  up-river. Carried on: the mark; the fort's name; no handler.
- **MQ10 Dreams for Sale** (Fort Swiftmoth). The line's turn. The keep is "empty since the Legion left", with "a
  locked cellar door below the old garrison's strongroom" and a watch the coast takes for smugglers (D
  nushara-bayou, Fort Swiftmoth). They are the Reed's keepers, and Deadly. In by stealth (Sneak; Lockpicking, or
  Pickpocket for the chief keeper's key), by force, or by the old ex-privateer who keeps a room upstairs and knows
  the drain under the cellar (Speech, or gold). Below are pallets, dreamers, a knotted cord hung above an
  empty pallet, and Nesh-Deeka. She says what the Reed is and whom she freed, and that the tree was left to die. The
  choice is the cellar's inner gate: opened, everyone may go and some who go will drown in the marsh with the
  sap still in them; left shut, they are fed and kept. One old dreamer asks the player to leave it shut. Whoever
  is let out is gone when the player is next far away (switched by Xil-Veeka's first line at Lilmoth, in MQ11).
  Nesh-Deeka, staying or going, gives the last thing she knows: every dreamer dreams a carved stone on a height
  above lakes, the marks on it are warded, and the wards are Shadowscale teaching. If she has died in the cellar,
  the old dreamer says the same. Carried on: the gate open or shut; Nesh-Deeka freed, left or dead; nobody left to
  report to.

*Act IV, the marks and the root (Lilmoth, the ridge and the north again). Nobody gives orders now.*

- **MQ11 Marks Only the Eye Can See** (Lilmoth; Aunike Crown). Xil-Veeka teaches the ward-words by a line of his,
  which sets the quest's next stage: to one skilled enough to have crossed his floor unheard (a line gated on
  Sneak), to one who asks well (Speech) or to one who pays (gold). If he is dead, his chest holds them, and taking
  them sets the same stage. Then the ridge: the Crown is "a bare rock summit" on Deadly ground with "Naga parties
  out of the northern camps" on its path and giant snakes below the top (D aunike-ridge, Aunike Crown, Nacydes).
  Up through the pass by force; round it by the lizard-keepers' path from Nacydes (Sneak); or by the one scout
  below the pass who speaks, bought or talked round (Speech, gold), whose line makes the scouts stand down. The
  carved stone by the fire-basket can be used only from the stage the words set. It shows its marks as a message:
  where the wood is moored, the tablet under the vaults will say. Carried on: the reading.
- **MQ12 The Tablet Under the Vaults** (Gemin). The story comes back to the country where it began, and the
  stake-keepers of Act I greet the player on sick ground again. The Vaults "kept the ancient tablet for ...
  divining Murkwood's place", and hold "undead of the sunken vaults", a dread wraith and a lowest hall behind a
  swim (D barsaebica, Gemin). Through the halls by force (weapons, Destruction, Restoration against the dead), or
  by the drowned side passage that passes under the wraith's hall (Sneak; breath by Alteration, Alchemy or the
  blessing of Jal-Veesa's pool, D valley-of-storms, Jal-Veesa). The fragment, taken with the Eye held, shows its
  reading as a message, and it says three things. The forest moves because its heart is not in it: the wood is
  moored to the first wood, under the sea, at the bottom of the trench beyond the seventh spire. The mooring was a
  gift, and a tree could close its root. And the kings chained it after a year in which trees closed their roots
  on a dying neighbour, whose people starved unheard. The cult's grievance is real, and so was the kings' fear.
  Carried on: the fragment, with the first consent on it.
- **MQ13 The One Who Wants Out** (Ushaan Camp; a line in Helstrom). The sap road's northern end is a Naga camp, and
  "one of the Naga wants out of the camp and will open the stockade at night for a price" (D barsaebica, Ushaan
  Camp). By Sesk (Speech or 400 gold: her line unlocks the gate), by stealth, or by a Deadly fight, the player
  reaches the leader's hut and the empty cradle the Egg was carried in, and learns it went down the creeks to the
  sea a month ago. The camp also holds a Khajiit trader for ransom, whose pen can be opened. On the road south, in
  the Helstrom household, Never-Writes-Twice owns what she did at Vel-Sapha ("One sapling at the edge, against
  every tree in the marsh the next time one turns. I would do it again, and I do not sleep") and asks one thing:
  where the cutters camp. Told the truth, the Reed burns the camp and everyone in it, and it is found so; she pays
  with the Reed's last stores, which are what a diver needs. Told a lie, the camp stands and goes on cutting, and
  the player buys a diver's needs like anyone. A player who first told Sesk to go, and gave her the price of a
  boat, finds her gone before the fire; a trader left in his pen burns with the rest. This is the line's
  misjudgement: the player may act on a patron's word after learning what the patron is. If Never-Writes-Twice is

## 3. Act I's quests in full

Common to all four.

- **One working stage.** After its briefing each quest sits at one working stage, at which every way through is
  open at once. A way's result is a thing held, a person dead or a stored number. The one named thing each quest
  is after sets the next stage when it is taken, and its take is open only from the working stage on: taken
  earlier, it is noticed when the working stage is reached (the form's held check), so nothing is skipped.
- **Who must live.** Each quest names the people it cannot go on without. If one dies the quest fails with a
  journal entry and the main quest's message. Nobody stands in for anybody, so every journal entry names the
  person who truly spoke. Everyone else can die at any moment and the tables say what that leaves.
- **Switches** are made only by a line spoken inside Neeva-Tul's house or the boatman's house in Helstrom, both
  interiors, about people and things outside them.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense,
  and say only what the player has just done. Skill thresholds are fixed. Each failed Speech check has its own
  refusal and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms.
- "Night" and "dusk" in what people say are their words only; no step depends on the hour. A theft seen, a pocket
  bungled and a trespass are answered by Skyrim's own crime system under the place's law (D province, Power and
  law); no quest checks them.
- **Every quest has one way that needs no skill at all**, only legwork or a fight a careful new character can win,
  so that no build is stopped. The Naga of Act I are fixed below the Wild band's ten.
- **The player's own question is kept alive**: in each quest one person says something of the woman who carried
  the player out of the fire.
- Words: the town's own keepers of weirs and ruins are called *weir-keepers* and *stake-keepers* in everything
  the player reads, so that "the Wardens" means the state and nothing else.

### MQ04. The Sour Spring

**What a new player is told, and when.** Neeva-Tul's last line of MQ03 gives the way: Asheem-Lei will call the

no outsider"); *the Reed* ("three of us in this house and a few listeners like your root-herald; we serve the
Wardens and the city pretends we are boatmen"); *the Root Talk* ("when every elder of the forest comes here to
drink at the city tree's spring and hear the trees speak together; it is late this year and nobody will say

(X, on D people, Ruxa-Neel, Wujeel). Behind the locked door at the back of the boatman's house is a dreamer the

## 5. Read on paper

One round, on the first draft, all reading one version: two players given the story only as a player meets it,
Act I sheet by sheet and then the later acts in summary, asked after each what they thought was going on, whom
they trusted, what they expected and what they would choose, with the hidden spine and the endings kept from them
until they reached them; and one deliverability reader given the ceiling, the quest form, the build briefs and
the whole document.

**What they said.**

- *The player who loves the lore* (a talker and alchemist) was keen at every sheet, trusted the witness and the
  herald, distrusted the Reed from the back room onward, guessed the net-mender ran the house, and would retell
  the charm shown without a word, "what hatches under another tree is that tree's" and the stand's dead clutch
  being in the Egg. Told the truth at Vel-Sapha and was shaken by what it cost; opened the cellar gate "sick about
  it"; warned Sesk and lied to the officer; chose MEND with CLAIM the temptation. Hard to please on: words used
  before they were explained, and three kinds of "warden"; the player's own story dropped for three quests; the
  swerve from sour sap to a jewel at the end of Act I, with the Eye's purpose untold until Act IV; Act II as three
  fetches and one dive too many; Dralin's lie never answered; the Collector and Never-Writes-Twice thin before
  their big scenes; what pouring the Egg does told too late; a keeper that must be fought; MEND a little too
  plainly the wise ending, its cost late and on others.
- *The impatient fighter* was doing something inside a minute and stayed keen, would retell "the villain is the
  one who carried me out of the fire" and "the spies I worked for were farming dreams in a cellar", and made the
  same choices by another road: MEND, CLAIM the temptation, CUT respected. Hard to please on: the same unexplained
  words and near-identical titles; a new character's kit against three Naga; no fighter's way at the root gate, at
  the spring, at the Rose (where the fighter's way was the one called suicide) or in the last room, where the only
  way without Speech was to kill the woman who saved them; killing a frightened weakling as the fighter's only way
  in the first quest; most of the purse spent on a gourd.
- *The deliverability reader* found the people, locks, keys and fights buildable and none of the four outlines
  buildable as written. Blocking: a failure on "both dead", which no trigger can carry; stand-in speakers whose
  journal entries named the wrong person; MQ03's two stages reachable out of order; a seller who was hostile on
  sight; things taken early that skipped a briefing and the joining; "a jar" and "a gourd" that were several
  different things; a fallback resting on a purse already handed in; a switch made in sight when a stand-in spoke
  outdoors; a name that could be given for a youth never met; lessons no result can give; a guard "giving a
  container's contents". Also: no lockpicks anywhere, level-20 crocodiles at the starting landing, looks that may
  need art (burnt huts, a sickly tree, a scratched wall, a living root), a keeper that shut builds out, and
  carriers missing in the later acts if the Collector, Nesh-Deeka or Never-Writes-Twice dies.

The two players split on nothing that was meant to divide them at the end, which is a finding: both chose MEND.
They split as hoped on naming Counts-His-Luck and Teemal against their elders' arguments, and both were torn at
Vel-Sapha and at the cellar gate.

**What changed, as one set.**

1. *Stages rebuilt on one pattern.* One working stage and one named thing per quest, its take open only from the
   working stage and noticed if held earlier; nobody stands in for anybody, and each quest names who must live.
   That one change answers the out-of-order stages, the skipped joining, the false journal names, the "both dead"
   failures and the switch in sight. Dralin gives the job in his first words, so his death strands nothing.
2. *One thing, one form.* The jar stands on a crate and Teemal's is the same form; the draught is a dipper behind
   a door and Wujeel's gourd the same form; the hook lies on the boat and Rask sells its ownership; the guard
   unlocks a door. Rask's crew is wary until a line provokes it. The phial is given at MQ02's end and taken at
   MQ03's. Teemal can be named only once met. Lessons became books.
3. *A way without a skill in every quest, and a fighter's way where one was missing.* The witness in MQ01 (so
   nobody has to kill a frightened man), the charm in MQ02, the creek by Wuleen-Tei's word in MQ03, a crocodile's
   gall for the sap-seller in MQ04; a champion to fight alone at the Rose; the Egg yielded to the tablet's words
   with no Speech at the last door. The hold chest gained a two-handed blade, a shield and lockpicks.
4. *The keeper may be passed.* A drowned root-channel runs under its hall. This departs from the province plan's
   "fought on every path", because the owner's rule that any build can finish outranks it; it goes to the lead.
5. *Plain words sooner.* Dralin says what the reed people and a root-herald are as he names them; the judges'
   "counter" replaces the reeve; weir-keepers and stake-keepers leave "the Wardens" to the state; Lilmoth's tree
   and the rootworm are glossed where first met.
6. *The player's own story runs through Act I.* Teemal, Rask and Nesh-Deeka each speak of "the one she carried";
   Counts-His-Luck says how the player came to the camp; Dralin answers for it afterwards.
7. *The jewel is joined to the trees at the end of Act I.* Nesh-Deeka says what the dreamers dream and what the
   old songs say the Eye opens, so Act II is a race for a key whose use the player knows. Each lead now also
   shows the cult at work, and the Collector's sermon says what pouring an Egg would do.
8. *The patrons are met before they turn.* Never-Writes-Twice pays the player and questions them in MQ04 and

   things is.
9. *The endings' weights were moved.* The tablet no longer takes a side: it also tells why the kings forged the
   chain. MEND's cost is now present and seen, CLAIM's mercy is plainer, and the three are set out so that none
   is the wise one.
10. *Later acts have carriers.* A letter in the Collector's chest, the keepers' cords at Swiftmoth, a chest in the

    gained their one speaker in section 4, with the rows the reader found missing.
