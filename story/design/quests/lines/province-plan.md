# The province plan: where the stories live

Step 2 of [nw-05](../../../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05-story-quest-adapt-1.md). It decides where the
main quest and each of thirteen faction lines live in the new world, so that fourteen writers can each write one line
with the [story-line](../../../../.claude/skills/story-line/SKILL.md) skill into one province. It binds them on seats,
ground, crossings and the political map; inside those it leaves every story to its writer. The owner's bar
([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)) binds this page and everything written from it.

How to read the citations:

- **D** is the digest of the new world, [province](../../world/province.md) and the region files beside it
  (`story/design/world/regions/<region>.md`). "D murkwood, Helstrom" is that place's record or its hooks in that
  region's file; "D province, Peoples" is a section of the province file.
- Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is `extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`.
- **X** marks this plan's own extrapolation, drawn from the cited data and lore and never from nothing (decision
  0032).
- A place is named by what the digest says it is. Nothing here describes a street or a room. What a story will need
  a place to hold that its record does not promise is a row of "Needs of places" at the end.

**Words used below**, for a reader new to the province. *Hist*: the sentient trees every Argonian is hatched under
and returns to. *The Reed*: the secret order that watches the trees through dreamers. *The Unbound*: the cult of the
tribeless that cuts Hist roots. *The Root*: the place under the sea where the roots of every Hist meet. *The Eye*:
the old kings' jewel that lies there. *The Owing*: the native sentence, months of owed work instead of a cell. *Naheesh*: a tribe's
elders. *Tree-minder*: the tender of a Hist, a village's usual arbiter. *Ku-vastei*: one who brings needed change,
the old word for a Shadowscale arbiter. *Vicecanon*: the magistrate of the northern cities. *Archein*: the
assimilated landholding families of Imperial times, now keeping the forts. *The Wardens* (Archwardens): the thin
successor state, and in this plan nobody else; the tribe of Jal-Veesa is "the Sedge-Wardens" in full or "the reed
people". *Xanmeer*: an ancient Argonian stepped pyramid. *Raj-kaal*: a war-leader; the Rose's is a Naga. *The heirs*:
the prison-born families who hold the Rose. *4E 48*: the year the old ruling party called the flying city Umbriel
through Lilmoth's tree, and Lilmoth died of it. *Danger bands*: Safe, Settled, Wild, Dangerous, Deadly, Lethal,
fixed in the world (D province, Danger). Greek-sounding town names (Korydon, Nacydes) are the dead Kothringi's,
kept by the Argonians who moved in. Writers keep apart what the names blur: the Salt-Tooth Naga camp is never
shortened, and Reed-Sail's pirate branch is called the Skeith crews, not the Salt-Teeth.

## 1. The province as a stage

**What is at stake.** Black Marsh in 4E 201 is quiet, poor, inward and very dangerous, a patchwork of tribes,
river-ports and cities held loosely by a state "whose reach ends where the water gets deep" (*state*:25 to :31). The
Archwardens keep the old forts, the port customs and the name of a party nobody says aloud (*state*:62 to :68,
:701 to :707). Everything that matters runs through the Hist: eggs, names, souls and burial (D province, Peoples:
Root-Folk; D murkwood, faith). And in this world's own data the trees are not well. Helstrom's assembly says the
trees decide, and cannot yet say what ails them (X, on D murkwood, Helstrom). A young Hist at Vel-Sapha Stand is dying (D
barsaebica, Vel-Sapha Stand). The sap-jar at Yuva-Goli has gone cloudy (D hatandros-bight, Yuva-Goli). Tsonac's
dreams have turned bad (D arnesian-jungle, Tsonac). Clutches fail at Guxal and Tazhul, sap is slashed and stolen at
Tseli-Oon, and Meris-Keth's tree sickened and emptied its village (D arnesian-jungle, Guxal; D okuwun-rahei, Tazhul;
D murkwood, Tseli-Oon; D banujha-basin, Meris-Keth). In the same year the last known Shadowscale dies and the
province loses the last of its judges (*state*:201 to :222). The Root Talk, at which every naheesh of the interior
gathers at Helstrom to hear the trees (D murkwood, Helstrom; *state*:169), is late this year, because the tree-minders
cannot yet tell the tribes why the trees are sick (X). That is the stage: a people whose government is a rumour and
whose real authority, the trees, has begun to falter, in the year the old order's last servants die.

**The four tensions.** Every line pulls on at least one from its own side. They are what make fourteen stories one.

A fifth thing runs under all four and is the treasure hunt's ground: **sick ground**. The ruins of the dead peoples
(Ayleid, Velothi, Kothringi, Lilmothiit, the drowned xanmeer spires) are avoided by Argonians and worked by
scavengers and foreign buyers (D barsaebica, the region as a whole; D benthic-yobesh; *state*:749 to :757). What
Argonians will not enter is where secrets are safest, and the stories use that.

## 2. The main quest's journey: The Eye and the Root

The plot is [main-plot](main-plot.md), the one statement of it. This section says where it lives in the province:
the premise in a line, the people, the nineteen quests by place and region, the side quests, the endings and where
the line meets the factions. It decides no plot.

**The premise.** The player, a stranger of any race, works off an Owing cutting reed at Sedge-Kal Camp. The
gang-master strikes their name from his roll on an order he cannot refuse, and sends them to the root-herald of
Jal-Veesa, who brings them into the Reed. Hist trees are falling sick, one at a time. The trail leads to the
Unbound, who cut the roots, and to the Eye of Argonia, which lies at the Root under the sea. The player is never
chosen: the Reed combs the work gangs for strangers with no kin and no ties, and a freed stranger wants work, coin
and a place in the world.

**The Reed.** A secret order that says it protects the old ways of Argonian life. In 4E 48 a mad Hist at Lilmoth
helped the An-Xileel call Umbriel down (`topics/umbriel.md:26` to `:29`; *state*:684), and the Reed set itself to
watch the trees so that it would never happen again. It watches through dreamers, people locked in the cellars of
its forts and fed sap, whose visions show what any tree has seen (X, on `topics/hist-and-sap.md:15` to `:16`, `:31`
to `:32`). Its hold on the marsh rests on them. It is the plan's one hidden power, small and of the marsh (X); the
state's working offices at Stormhold and Archon know nothing of it (X).

**The Unbound.** A cult of the tribeless, led by Cuts-the-Old-Knot, that cuts Hist roots and carries the sap off in
jars. She was a dreamer in the cellar of Fort Swiftmoth for nine years, and wants the Root cut so that no dreamer
can ever see through the trees again. Its hands are tribeless Naga, whom no tree claims (`tribes.md:7` to `:12`; D
valley-of-storms, Karsh-Naal Camp; X for the cult). The Collector, Ennodius Vanin, an Imperial relic-hunter of the
kind the Cyrodilic Collections' revived buyers are at Gideon ([province-vibe](../../province-vibe.md) §8), hunts the
Eye for himself and sells what he learns to whoever pays (X).

**The Eye.** Canon leaves "the priceless king's jewel of ancient Black Marsh" open as perhaps "a key to the lost
city" (`eye-of-argonia.md:6` to `:8`). Here it was cut by the priest-kings from Hist amber (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:154`)
and lies at the Root, the place under the sea trench of Echo Deep where the roots of every Hist meet, behind a door
the last king sealed (X). Held there it can heal every tree or cut every root. A beast the kings set there guards
it. Every one of those facts is plot, so the plot's page holds them and this page does not repeat them.

**How the plot sits on the province.** Nineteen quests in three acts. Act I opens at the north edge, runs west by
the weir road and ends in Helstrom, the hub of the travel network (D province, Roads). Act II is a treasure hunt
that fans out across the province's coasts and drowned ground, each find pointing to the next, the places growing
more dangerous and the rewards growing with them. Act III is the sea floor and the door. Every quest has at least
two ways through for different builds; the danger rises by the act, to the bands the digest fixes (D province, Danger).
Drowned ground is built with floors to stand on, since Skyrim gives no attack while swimming (section 6).

**The side quests on the road.** Six riddle quests in Act II send the player off the main road to a place a giver
names without a marker: Mistwatch, an Imperial fort on Cape Percar (D cape-percar, Mistwatch); Kitsa Burrow, a
Lilmothiit ruin of the Althii Archipelago (D althii-archipelago, Kitsa Burrow); Va-Seeth Spire and Zeshul-Tha Spire,
two of the seven drowned spires (D benthic-yobesh, Va-Seeth Spire, Zeshul-Tha Spire); Anutasil, an Ayleid hall of
Blackwood (D blackwood, Anutasil); and the Kelp Lantern, a wreck in Benthic Yobesh (D benthic-yobesh, The Kelp
Lantern). Each pays a thing made for the fight with the guardian. The plot's page holds the givers and the rewards.

**Where the story is felt afterwards.** The three endings are shown by a vision and then found: the trees of
Vel-Sapha Stand and Guxal (D barsaebica; D arnesian-jungle, Guxal) stand dead if the player takes or gives the Eye;
the Reed turns on the player in two endings, and in the other Fort Swiftmoth's upper keep is the player's own; Karsh-Naal Camp
is the Unbound's stronghold in the third. No faction line states any of this ahead of the main quest.

**Where the main quest meets the factions.** Section 4 lists each crossing. In short: Helstrom is the handler's
quiet house beside Many-Root's hall; Blackrose Prison's gate is shared with the Chainbreakers; Gemin, Nenria and the
spires are the main quest's; and the Nisswo's, the League's, the Night-Reed's and the Archive's regions each hold
one side-quest ruin that their lines leave alone.

## 3. Each faction line's home

Thirteen lines, thirteen different seats. A seat is a place where people can stand and things can be kept. Skill
leanings follow `profiles.yaml` in the quest-write skill unless a change is stated. "Hooks to take up" are the
data's own; the best quests will be those hooks made the line's.

### 3.1 The Blackrose Chainbreakers

- **At heart.** They get the held out. Their oath, "Bones go home", covers the living and the dead: the freeing and
  the burial are one work ([chainbreakers](chainbreakers.md) §1). Nobody in the line is a villain and somebody
  always pays.
- **Seat: Blackrose** (Tulmek Washes), the lake city "whose people trust no institution" and through which
  "everything moving between Murkwood, Oliis Bay and Bramman's river passes" (D province, Peoples: Blackrose). A
  place that swallows people is where people who fetch them out would gather. The Rose, "a day's march off and not
  theirs", is their standing problem as it is the city's.
- **Hubs and range.** Blackrose and the lake villages (Lujei, Ka-Asei, Reedfall Farm); the Rose; Soulrest's quay for
  the Broker. The delta estates of the Althii islands for later quests.
- **Hooks to take up.** A passenger at Lujei Ferry "who must not be seen"; Sheh-Ara, emptied by the Rose's raiders,
  and its egg-stone; why "the Rose is not the city's"; the Blackguard watch post at Khariton; the steward at
  Mereweather hiding a kinsman who ran from Fort Reedmoth's keepers; grave-stakes to be raised at Zul-Etha and
  Oru-Neel (D tulmek-washes; D althii-archipelago, Mereweather Estate).
- **Skills.** Lockpicking, Pickpocket, Sneak; Speech second; a hard fixed fight third.
- **Its drama.** Jailbreaks and stand-offs with a cause, and always about a person: a key off a belt, a keeper
  faced down, a fetcher fought on a bank. Where the Night-Reed plan their way to a strongroom, the Chainbreakers
  walk into a household that knows them. A freed person is never led out: they say they will go, and are found
  later where they said.
- **Friends and enemies.** The Night-Reed respect the craft and despise the cause. The Thorn Ash-Reed, who know
  them by repute, fear what a freeing would start in the saltrice country. The Nisswo argue with them about debts that outlive their cause.
- **Promise.** Steal a person's life back, and meet whoever paid for it.
- **What it becomes here.** The story of last night stands. Its writer re-homes it: the same people and quests on
  the new Blackrose, each place named from the digest, the physical things moved to the needs table.

### 3.2 The Lamp-Bearers (the detectives, decision 0025)

- **At heart.** Proof instead of a writ. They are called when something is found: a body, an empty nest, a pulled
  stake. They are rivals to the assassins and the thieves because their cases begin with what those two leave
  behind.
- **Seat: Stormhold** (Valley of Storms). "The city invented the Vicecanon office to try slavers", and the Dunmer
  left it stone roads and light posts (D valley-of-storms, Stormhold). A court that tried slavers needed someone to
  go into the holds with a lamp and say who was in them: the order grew from that work and kept the name (X; the
  line's lore pass may change the name, not the root). Several Vicecanons sit at once, "one for each people", so a
  finding must convince both.
- **Hubs and range.** Stormhold; the causeway farms and weirs (Surkina, Vandrel Farm, Sedgeweir, Tenmal); down the
  river road to the Stormhold-manner hamlets of Lake Ulaish (Julo, Luntoc, Yalugo); Thorn, the other Vicecanon
  city; one case each in Gideon and Archon.
- **Hooks to take up.** The clutch stolen from the square's Hist pond, which the Vicecanons want found "before the
  quarters settle it themselves"; the causeway stones pulled out at Surkina; the weir stakes at Sedgeweir; the
  traveller at Tenmal with a sack of Dres slave-irons who "has not left his room since"; the body at Ranvos
  Landing; the salt trader's body at Julo and its cut ferry-rope; the boat that "paid the toll at dusk and was
  never seen to leave the lake" at Fort Gysilla; the missing clutch at Gaxu-Reet (D valley-of-storms; D lake-ulaish; D murkwood, Gaxu-Reet).
- **Skills.** Speech first; Sneak, Pickpocket and Lockpicking to get at what people hide; Alchemy and Mysticism to
  read what was done (the profile's leaning, kept).
- **Its drama.** Investigations that widen: a theft with one unknown, a missing person with a trail, a death among
  a closed cast, an old wrong reaching a respected name. The line's long case is the stolen clutches, which runs
  from the pond at Stormhold to a door in Archon (section 4).
- **Friends and enemies.** The Shadowscales and the Night-Reed, as rivals with teeth: senior rank in the
  Lamp-Bearers shuts senior rank in either. The Thorn Ash-Reed are wary allies. Vicecanons are patrons with
  something to hide.
- **Promise.** Be the one who can say what happened, in a country that has just lost its judges.

### 3.3 The League of Open Water

- **At heart.** People with no tree of their own, who want to belong to the marsh without begging a root for it.
  Open water is water no root reaches. The spirit kept is plural, civic and stubborn.
- **Seat: Soulrest** (Cape Percar), the most mixed city in the province, whose Argonians "must come into
  [Mine-Burner] country to hatch, name and bury, which gives [the tribe] a hold over the city that they use" (D
  province, Peoples: Mine-Burners, Soulrest). A league of the treeless would grow in the one city held to ransom by
  its hatching ground.
- **Hubs and range.** Soulrest; the Banujha Basin by the boat to Jarecai and past its watch posts; Gideon, whose
  planted cutting is the precedent everyone quotes (D kotherra-mangroves, Gideon).
- **Hooks to take up.** "A Soulrest egg-tender whose clutch must reach the Jarecai Hist"; the elders' "price for
  passage through the basin"; "a young warrior's trial at the burned mine"; Xal-Tarek's watchers, "hostile to
  Imperial-descended visitors"; the toll quarrels at Banu-Veeth and Xul-Banu; the "faction contest for the
  council" at Soulrest; the Banujha signs cut into Redgum Camp's best trees (D banujha-basin; D cape-percar).
- **Skills.** Speech first; Sneak second; Restoration (the profile, with Restoration raised).
- **Its drama.** Standing up to a stronger neighbour on its own ground. The player goes into the basin as the
  city's one bold hand: wins back a clutch a watch post has kept, takes the burned-mine trial that the tribe
  respects and no townsman has dared, finds what a war-leader wants more than the lever, robs the post that robbed
  the city. Nothing living is led anywhere and nothing is timed; what is carried is carried in the pack. The line
  turns on a temptation: a cutting of Jarecai's tree would free the city for ever, and taking one is what Gideon
  is accused of.
- **What it becomes here.** The registers, votes and charters of the old design go to the background. The League
  keeps its name, its people and its question, "who counts as Argonian?", and answers it with eggs and river
  passages instead of petitions.
- **Friends and enemies.** Many-Root hold that no tree is taken. The Mine-Burners are the antagonist with a case:
  the coast burned nothing; they did, and the lever is all they have. Reed-Sail carry for the League at a price.
- **Promise.** Go alone into the country that holds your city's eggs to ransom, and come back with something it
  cannot be made to pay for again.

### 3.4 Many-Root

- **At heart.** The trees do not speak with one voice, and someone must go and listen to each. Consent and
  stewardship, discovered in danger and decided afterwards.
- **Seat: Helstrom**, the tree-minders' hall (D province, What the world offers: arcane enchanter "the tree-minders'
  hall"; D people, Taleeka-Ma). The Root Talk is held here and "every naheesh in the interior travels to" it; the
  minders who stay between convocations and carry what the trees said out to the tribes are the Many-Root (X).
- **Hubs and range.** By rootworm and boat to troubled trees everywhere: Korydon, Tsonac, Uluo, Gideon, Guxal,
  Yuva-Goli, Meris-Keth, Lake Ulaish's islet stations, the Naga camp at Dry-Fang.
- **Hooks to take up.** Tsonac's tree-minder "who says the Hist's dreams have turned bad since a mason cut too deep
  under the mound"; the clutch at Guxal that has not hatched; the cloudy sap-jar at Yuva-Goli ("a fresh jar must be
  carried from Helstrom, or the foulness in this one must be found"); Gideon's cutting and the river tribe that
  says it was taken; the two families at Uluo who dispute who tends the pool; the egg-tender at Ixu-Maan who
  "claims the Hist spoke to her in the mud"; the honey at Wattle-Pool Farm that "tastes of sap and sends those who
  eat it to sleep"; Meris-Keth, the tree that was left to die; the
  Root-Folk sap-gatherer "held for ransom" at Dry-Fang Camp with the stolen sap-skins (D arnesian-jungle; D hatandros-bight;
  D kotherra-mangroves; D althii-archipelago; D murkwood; D banujha-basin).
- **Skills.** Alchemy first (sap and what fouls it), Restoration, Archery and Sneak for the country between,
  Alteration.
- **Its drama.** Each quest is one tree in trouble with a physical cause to be found in a dangerous place, and then
  a decision that costs a real person something. The patron's secret is which trees the minders have chosen not to
  save.
- **Friends and enemies.** The League (a tree is not taken); the Reed (who watch the minders, section 4);
  sap crews everywhere.
- **Promise.** Learn to read a sick tree as a hunter reads a trail, and follow it to the hand that did it.
- **A boundary.** The cutting of the roots and the Unbound belong to the main quest. Many-Root's trees fail from
  causes of their own, and its quests never name the Unbound or the Reed.

### 3.5 The Marsh Charter

- **At heart.** A native fighting tradition arguing about whether to take a foreign form
  (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:134`). Competence, contracts answered for and other answers than killing.
- **Seat: Noota Nara** (Chixathe Mesa), the village "where, in legend, the villagers once heated spear-tips in a
  dragon's own fire and killed it", with its master spear-smith and the dragon-spear beside its Hist, directly
  below Fort Chalkmoth, whose Archein captain "watches the Sarpa" (D chixathe-mesa, Noota Nara, Fort Chalkmoth).
  The village that kills monsters and the fort that would put it on a charter stand a short climb apart: the old
  premise is already on the map.
- **Hubs and range.** The mesa and its road. The working hub is Inoja, the clay-mound market town one boat stage
  down (D okuwun-rahei, Inoja), where contracts from Aroxhu's country and the coast are taken; the line stays in
  the east.
- **Hooks to take up.** The apprentice who "can make the dragonfire spear-tips again if someone brings her fire
  salts" (they are at Percar Scar); the chalk cart and driver lost on the western road; the caravan guards hanging
  on Hanaxi Roost's feather poles, and the Sarpa elder "who will parley for a gift of fresh fish"; the
  Stone-Sleeper clutch in Sahlpesh Cave, where an elder in the back chamber will talk; the wing-chief of Seth-Alaxh
  Roost and the Aroxhu spear-captain who "wants peace and needs an envoy"; the Sarpa raiders in Joxhi's pit scrub;
  the dead lord of Vorannuhl (D chixathe-mesa; D okuwun-rahei, Seth-Alaxh Roost, Joxhi).
- **Skills.** One-Handed, Two-Handed, Block, Archery, Smithing; Restoration to come home.
- **Its drama.** Hunts. Each is a named beast or band in its own lair, with a reason it is there, and a contract
  that understates something. The Sarpa war is the arguable heart: a people who "trade nothing" and a roost that
  will parley.
- **Friends and enemies.** The fort is patron and rival at once. The Sunken Archive hire the spears to reach ruins.
  The chartered pirates of Skeith are what a charter can turn a fighter into.
- **Promise.** Take the spear in the village that once killed a dragon, and earn a name the mesa's beasts and
  raiders know.

### 3.6 The Night-Reed

- **At heart.** A native thieves' company with a code: skill, restraint, no blackmail. It ends on one great heist.
- **Seat: Pusbottom in Lilmoth** (Althii Archipelago): the data's own "Pusbottom's thieves", canon's "ancient,
  gangrenous heart of Lilmoth" (D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth; `topics/guilds-and-orders.md:182`). Lilmoth is
  "commercially strong and openly hostile to the successor state" (D province, Peoples: Lilmoth), and its council
  cannot rob the Wardens. Its thieves can.
- **Hubs and range.** Lilmoth and the estates in planters' shells; the Warden forts of the islands; Archon for the
  fence and the last job; a job each in Soulrest and Thorn.
- **Hooks to take up.** The salvage chest in Silt-Orrel's cellar "and the family will not say whose it was"; the
  planters' strongbox behind Dunhallow's walled cellar; the bonded strongbox at Brackwater "that no key on the
  shore fits"; the officer's sword in Fort Sallowmoth's locked cage; the toll chests of Fort Reedmoth and
  Southwatch Keep, held "against Lilmoth's wishes"; the docks-only law "some officials still enforce" (D
  althii-archipelago).
- **Skills.** Lockpicking, Pickpocket, Sneak; Speech; a little Alteration and Illusion.
- **Its drama.** Heists that climb: a purse, a cellar, a fort's pay chest, and at last the customs strongroom in the
  East Empire Company's old buildings at Archon, the state's one real purse (D nushara-bayou, Archon; *state*:704).
  What the patron hides is who pays for the war on the Wardens (X).
- **What it becomes here.** The Tidal Palace and the Crown Ledger are gone with old Lilmoth. The grand heist is the
  customs house, and the question "who owns the night?" is answered by whom the takings arm.
- **Friends and enemies.** The Lamp-Bearers, by trade. Reed-Sail, over who moves stolen goods. The Chainbreakers,
  with respect.
- **Promise.** Rob the people who tax the water, each job bigger than the last, up to the state's own purse.

### 3.7 The Nisswo of the Turning Path

- **At heart.** Sithis as change, plural and public. They teach the letting go of what has passed, and they are
  right and wrong at once.
- **Seat: Giovesse** (Kotherra Mangroves), "a hamlet of squatters' huts at the foot of haunted Castle Giovesse,
  within arm's reach of Gideon's north gate" (D kotherra-mangroves, Giovesse). The Nisswo have "no central
  governance" and keep no temple (`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:18`). A clutch of them among squatters,
  under a castle whose dead will not leave, at the gate of the city that lost most in 4E 48 and has let go of
  least, is as near a house as they would allow themselves (X). West of them lie the dead Kothringi towns, the
  chimes the river folk still hang for a people nobody living met, and the Sul-Xan with their "Dagon heresy" (D
  blackwood, the region as a whole), which calls all destruction sacred: the Nisswo's dark mirror.
- **Hubs and range.** Giovesse and Gideon; Blackwood and Nha Enasa (Umpholo's dead mission chapel, Mnemosa,
  Meliton); then one rite each in a few haunted places further off.
- **Hooks to take up.** The ghost at Giovesse that "wants its name carved on a grave-stake by the north gate"; the
  ghost at Nyrath-Ven "that will not move until its ashes are carried to the sea"; the fishermen at Ashiit Den who
  "want the tomb quieted"; the lights at Reknaa-iit Mound; the humming doll at Meliton "the Naka-Desh will not
  discuss"; the Mara amulet under the chapel floor, the Agaceph who wants it and the Sul-Xan scout in the reeds;
  the dead who rise at Mnemosa; the hardwood at Cutbank Camp with grave-stakes among its roots (D
  kotherra-mangroves; D nushara-bayou; D althii-archipelago; D blackwood; D nha-enasa; D arnesian-jungle).
- **Skills.** Speech and Restoration first; Illusion and Mysticism; any weapon for the unquiet.
- **Its drama.** Rites and their cost. Each quest goes into unquiet ground, on land among the dead, and each rite
  asks that something be given up that someone living wants kept: a thing burned, sunk or staked by the player's
  hand. What it settled is told, and found when the player comes back. The enemy is a sect that has made destruction a dogma. The
  hard cases are the kind ones: a chime hung for the dead, a memorial, a grudge.
- **Friends and enemies.** The Umbriel Witness, their true opposite. The Chainbreakers, half allies. The Sul-Xan.
  The Unbound are their heresy seen from far off.
- **Promise.** Walk into the places the dead will not leave, and be the one who ends it.

### 3.8 The Reed-Sail

- **At heart.** The people who move everything: raft-masters, pilots, ferrymen and smugglers. Mutual aid on one
  side, a cartel on the other, and a pilot's word on the water is law.
- **Seat: Uluo** (Althii Archipelago), the one town of the Three Waters Tribe, ruled by its elder and "the tribe's
  council of raft-masters", who "run the back-water routes between Archon and the Lilmoth delta that the customs
  officers do not see"; it is "both neutral ground and a smuggler's door" (D province, Peoples: Three Waters;
  D althii-archipelago, Uluo). The compact is that council grown past its tribe: reed for the rafts, sail for the
  coast pilots of Archon and Seafalls (X).
- **Hubs and range.** Uluo, Archon, Truowa; the Nushara channels; Lake Ulaish; the Padomaic coast and Skeith Isle;
  later Bramman's river in the south-west.
- **Hooks to take up.** The elder's "cargo of saltrice carried to Archon past the customs post"; the back channel
  offered "for a favour"; the chain at Fort Reedmoth; Truowa's refusal of Fort Gysilla's toll; the Hollow Tide
  leader who "will hire a stranger to carry a cargo past Marshgate Watch"; the quarrel of the two Skeith camps;
  the quartermaster of Rainbreaker Keep who "knows which wardens took the pirates' money"; the Brindle Gull's strongbox that both camps claim; the Stormwidow's key and the cairn on the
  Grey Tooth; the pilot-elder of
  Seafalls and the ship "whose cargo does not match its master's story" (D althii-archipelago; D lake-ulaish;
  D skeith-isle; D padomaic-ocean; D nushara-bayou; D okuwun-rahei, Seafalls).
- **Skills.** Speech and Archery; One-Handed; Alteration for the water; Sneak for a night run.
- **Its drama.** Smuggling by water, played on foot and by swimming: a cargo swum through a back channel under a
  fort's wall, a strongbox raised from a wreck two crews are fighting over, a toll chain's lock, a rival's cache
  lifted from under his own lookouts, and a pirates' war in which the player picks a camp. The pirate branch (the Salt-Teeth) is the Skeith half of this
  line, as before.
- **Friends and enemies.** The Warden forts; the Night-Reed; the Waykeepers, whose lights someone in the compact
  has been buying (section 4).
- **Promise.** Learn the channels the customs men cannot see, and get rich or get even on them.

### 3.9 The Rootworm Waykeepers

- **At heart.** The way is kept and never shown; passage is a gift. A small order, vital and overlooked.
- **Seat: Korydon** (Ultherus Swamp), the Chime-Keepers' principal town, "on a ring of dry ground encircled by
  water between Helstrom and the southern lakes": a rootworm stop and a boat stop for Helstrom, Blackrose, Kharaxos
  and Truowa at once (D ultherus-swamp, Korydon; D province, Roads). It is the interior's second crossroads, and
  its people already keep the things a traveller steers by: at Myroleon "the chime-tender has gone silent and the
  boats run blind in the fog", and on the ridge a watchfire "has burned ever since" the town was resettled (D
  ultherus-swamp, Myroleon; D aunike-ridge, Tydeos). The order keeps all the interior's ways, root, fog-chime and
  fire (X). Its second post is the root landing at Helstrom (D people, Cheeval-Xu).
- **Hubs and range.** Korydon; Aunike Ridge; the Ultherus channels; Lake Ulaish; the five root pools.
- **Hooks to take up.** A pattern the data holds across the whole interior, of lights nobody lit: the watchfire at
  Tydeos that "went dark one night and a stranger was found beside it"; the stranger dead beside the fire-basket on
  Aunike Crown; the fire at Vesh-Aneel "lit three nights running without a keeper"; the lantern at Nemeon "lit
  from the water side"; the lantern on Luntoc "answered from open water"; the lights at Uzu-Tegh and Marzai; the
  signal at Ixu-Maan "with no visitor in sight" (D aunike-ridge; D murkwood; D lake-ulaish). Also the Naga
  "watching the pass" at Nacydes and the chime quarrel at Korydon.
- **Skills.** Sneak first; Archery, Illusion, Alchemy, Speech (the profile, kept).
- **Its drama.** Expeditions along the ways to posts where something is already wrong: a fire-basket cold on
  Deadly ground with what put it out still near, a lantern found burning with a stranger's oil in it and a boat's
  mark on the landing, a keeper dead at his post, and at the turn the discovery that someone inside the order sold
  a way. What the player finds is always already there; nothing is lit or answered in front of them. The worm is never seen; it is called, and the traveller is swallowed out of sight.
- **What it becomes here.** The line is re-scoped from the rootworm alone to all the interior's ways. The name
  stays, since the root pools are its oldest charge and Helstrom's landing its senior post. The change gives
  it ground of its own (Aunike Ridge, Ultherus Swamp, Lake Ulaish) and a mystery the data already holds.
- **Friends and enemies.** Reed-Sail; the Naga at the pass; Many-Root, who think the roots are theirs to speak for.
- **Promise.** Keep the lights of the deep marsh, and find out who else has been lighting them.

### 3.10 The Shadowscales

- **At heart.** An order with three bidders and no one left to issue a writ. Duty, consent and the difference
  between a tradition and a useful costume. Killing written small.
- **Seat: the sealed school at Archon** (Nushara Bayou): "the sealed Shadowscale school, which the Dark Brotherhood
  wants reopened and the townspeople watch, with a very old ku-vastei living near it" (D nushara-bayou, Archon;
  closed 4E 187, `topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:84`). The old one is the last who still serves, and is found
  dead at the line's start: canon's death of the last known Shadowscale (*state*:201). The few retired elders at
  Lilmoth (*state*:212) laid the knife down long ago and will not take it up.
- **Hubs and range.** Archon; Lilmoth, where "elders, including retired Shadowscales, settle disputes" (D
  althii-archipelago, Lilmoth); targets in the cities and the forts.
- **Hooks to take up.** The school and its watcher; the retired Shadowscale who trains at Lilmoth; the privateer
  crew "whose prize the customs officers refuse to call lawful"; the Archon pilot named in the Tidewarden's logbook;
  and the stolen clutches
  (section 4): the Black-Tongues have had "nowhere to send" Shadow-born hatchlings for fourteen years (*state*:214),
  and someone has begun to collect them.
- **Skills.** Sneak, One-Handed, Archery, Illusion, Alchemy.
- **Its drama.** Contracts in which the question is always who has the right to order this death, with a quiet way
  and a restrained way rewarded. The three bidders are the Brotherhood, the Black-Tongue keepers and the state.
- **Friends and enemies.** The Lamp-Bearers. The Night-Reed (no chartered Shadowscale leads thieves). The Nisswo,
  who are also called ku-vastei and do it without a knife.
- **Promise.** Be the last blade of an order that may not exist, with three masters bidding for your hand.

### 3.11 The Sunken Archive

- **At heart.** Who owns knowledge, and whose ruin it is. Magic here is "folk literacy, not a profession"
  (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:35`): no robes.
- **Seat: the book hall at Yulca** (Okuwun Rahei), the one town of the Agaceph, held by "a council of readers".
  They "read, keep books taken from dead missions, and are accomplished illusionists", and "will freeze an intruder
  with a spell before deciding what to do with him" (D province, Peoples: Agaceph; D okuwun-rahei, Yulca). The
  province's one native library is where its scholars would be, and the foreigners already come to it: "a visiting
  Dunmer scholar wants to buy the secret of the Agaceph's freezing spell".
- **Hubs and range.** Yulca and the Agaceph hamlets (Yina, Icalca, Xibcan, Hirecan); the Velothi halls of Okuwun
  Rahei and the mesa; one last journey, by Truowa and Helstrom, to Gandranen. The readers do not dig: they send
  the player, which is why they need one.
- **Hooks to take up.** The founders' carved raft-prow stolen from the hall, which the elders "will not say why they
  want back so badly"; the humming door at Sethiral; the vault at Anvadas that "opens only to the right family
  tablet"; the Ayleid hall the diggers broke into at Joxhi; the mission lamp in the drowned chapel below Hirecan;
  the ruin in the reeds "that no raft has found"; and at the end Gandranen, the library "that draws books from any
  time", with "a book that should not exist" (D okuwun-rahei; D chixathe-mesa, Hirecan; D barsaebica, Gandranen).
- **Skills.** Mysticism, Alteration, Enchanting, Illusion (raised, for the Agaceph), Conjuration.
- **Its drama.** Expeditions into ruins, where the danger is the dead and the difficulty is the living who hold the
  ground to be sick. Foreign buyers, the Synod and the College of Whispers among them
  (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:71`, `:104`), bid for what comes out. The hall's own argument is whether to become
  an institution at all.
- **What it becomes here.** The seat moves from a guild-like archive to the Agaceph's hall, and the Conclave of
  Baal, which the data has "long gone from Stormhold" (D barsaebica, Gemin), leaves the line. Gemin is the main quest's.
- **Friends and enemies.** The Sedge-Wardens, who guard the northern ruins "against looters"; the Marsh Charter;
  the Umbriel Witness, over the College.
- **Promise.** Go down into the halls Argonians call sick, and bring back what no living reader has seen.

### 3.12 The Thorn Ash-Reed

- **At heart.** Argonians and Dunmer of one border city, trying to stop an old wrong becoming a new killing without
  pretending it did not happen.
- **Seat: Thorn** (Arnesian Jungle), under its Vicecanons and saltrice field-owners. The data gives the whole
  matter: "two field-holding families who each say their grandmothers stood in the same saltrice field when Dres
  fell", "a Dunmer family hunting a runaway slave's grandchildren", and off the coast a slaver's log that "names
  the Dunmer families who bought captives from Thorn; some of them still trade there. The Thorn Vicecanon wants it,
  and so do the families it names" (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn; D padomaic-ocean, Wreck of the Silt Bride).
- **Hubs and range.** Thorn, Fort Thornmoth, Coldbrine Mine, the Velothi ruins of the bay (Kharraluhl, Ovezza), the
  wreck of the Silt Bride, Guxal and Tsonac's road.
- **Hooks to take up.** Those above; the walled shaft at Coldbrine Mine where bonded labourers died, whose
  descendant "wants the bodies brought out and staked, and the overseer refuses"; the Dunmer grandmother who wants
  "a carved ancestor mask from the household shrine" in Kharraluhl; the cellar door at Ovezza "barred from the
  inside" (D hatandros-bight, Coldbrine Mine; D arnesian-jungle).
- **Skills.** Speech; Sneak and Archery; One-Handed.
- **Its drama.** Feud-breaking. Each quest goes after a dangerous thing that would start the killing if the wrong
  side held it (a log under the sea, the dead behind a mine's barred door, the name of a family in hiding), into
  the place that guards it, and then puts it in someone's hands. The other side's hunters are in the place too.
- **What it becomes here.** The memorial debates, lists and the closing accord go to the background. A short line,
  five or six quests.
- **Friends and enemies.** The Chainbreakers, whose freeings in the saltrice country it dreads; the Lamp-Bearers;
  both peoples' hotheads.
- **Promise.** Go where neither people dares be seen going, and bring out what would have started the bloodshed.

### 3.13 The Umbriel Witness

- **At heart.** Inheritors, not survivors: nobody alive saw 4E 48 (*state*:611 to :630). They want what happened
  named, and are argued with, politicised and sometimes shrugged at.
- **Seat: Lilmoth, at the edge of the drowned quarter** (Althii Archipelago), "whose name locals will not say to
  strangers", with "the villas that salvagers dive to reach" and "the story its people tell of the mad tree before"
  the young one (D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth). It shares the city with the Night-Reed on purpose: both live off
  what old Lilmoth left, and each despises the other's reason.
- **Hubs and range.** Lilmoth and its drowned quarter; the southern island's estates and forts on the path Umbriel
  laid waste (D nushara-bayou, history).
- **Hooks to take up.** The drowned quarter and its villas; the sunken shrine of Xhon-Mehl, "which a diver reaches
  only as far as breath allows"; Silt-Orrel's beams and fittings "from the drowned Imperial quarter"; the unstaked
  dead behind Dunhallow's walled cellar; the young Hist with its two minders, whose sap gives the breath for the
  dives (D althii-archipelago).
- **Skills.** Mysticism and Alchemy; Restoration; Conjuration and Alteration (the profile, kept).
- **Its drama.** Salvage under water. Where the Nisswo work on land with a rite and the Archive in dry halls with
  a spell, the Witness dives. The player goes down for what proves who did what in 4E 48, against the
  College of Whispers, which wants "Umbriel's dead" for its own study (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:113`), and
  against a city that would sooner build over it. What comes up indicts the wrong people as often as the right.
- **What it becomes here.** The archive rooms and the public record go to the background. The Refuge at Deepmire
  has no place in this world and is not replaced. A short line, five or six quests.
- **Friends and enemies.** The Nisswo; the Sunken Archive; Lilmoth's merchant council, a patron with something to
  bury.
- **Promise.** Dive the drowned city for the truth of the day it died, before the necromancers get there.

### 3.14 Nothing merged, two re-scoped, four given new work

No line is merged or dropped. Two are re-scoped: the Waykeepers (to all the interior's ways, with a new seat at
Korydon) and the Sunken Archive (to the Agaceph's hall, without the Conclave of Baal). Four keep their name, people
and question but do different things: the League of Open Water, the Nisswo, the Thorn Ash-Reed and the Umbriel
Witness, each because its old quests were hearings, registers or debates (owner-bar, "The vibe of Argonia"). The
Night-Reed's last heist has a new target because its old one does not exist.

**How the lines end.** Not every line ends on a judgement of who is told. The writers are held to these kinds: the
Chainbreakers, a hard choice among people; the Lamp-Bearers, a guilty name too strong to speak; the League, a
temptation taken or refused; Many-Root, a patron's secret exposed or kept; the Marsh Charter, a hard place and a
side taken; the Night-Reed, the last lock; the Nisswo, a thing of the player's own to give up; Reed-Sail, a camp
chosen; the Waykeepers, the seller faced; the Shadowscales, a master chosen or none; the Archive, a hard place;
the Ash-Reed, whose hands; the Witness, a race lost or won against the College by what the player did earlier.

## 4. Where lines meet and where they must not

**The rule.** A line's choices never change another line's stages. A crossing shows as a greeting, a rumour, a
person's attitude, a price and at most one door open or shut. Each side of a crossing is true whichever line the
player plays, in any order or not at all.
