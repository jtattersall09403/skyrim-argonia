# The League of Open Water: the line's story

Written with the [story-line](../../../../.claude/skills/story-line/SKILL.md) skill from the
[province plan](province-plan.md) (section 3.3 and the League's brief in section 7), for the new world. The old
design ([40-factions](../40-factions.md), section 38) is the foundation: the League keeps its name, its people
(Oshu-Kai, Martius, Hisska) and its question, "who counts as Argonian?", and answers it with eggs, a burned mine
and a tree instead of registers, votes and charters.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world ([province](../../world/province.md) and
`story/design/world/regions/<region>.md`; "D cape-percar, Xal-Tarek" is that place's record or hooks). Lore is
`file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is `extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own
extrapolation, drawn from the cited data and lore (decision 0032). Places are not built yet: each is named by what
its record says it is, and anything else a quest needs of it is a row of section 4.

**Words used below**, for a reader new to the province. *Hist*: the sentient trees every Argonian is hatched under
and returns to. *Clutch*: one household's eggs of a season, carried in an egg-basket packed with wet moss. *Naming*:
a hatchling drinks its tree's sap and is given its name; it is of that tree ever after, whoever laid the egg
(`topics/hist-and-sap.md:55`, `:59`). *Lukiul*: an Argonian hatched outside the marsh, who can hear no tree
(`topics/hist-and-sap.md:108`). *Mine-Burners* (Banu-Jaleel): the tribes of the Banujha Basin behind Soulrest, who
burned the Empire's mines and timber concessions in 3E 427 (D province, Peoples: Mine-Burners; `soulrest.md:41`).
*The Owing*: the native sentence, a season of owed work instead of a cell. *A burned mouth*: a mine the tribes
fired in 3E 427; to them each is a shrine (D banujha-basin, Jarecai). *Danger bands*: Safe, Settled, Wild,
Dangerous, Deadly, Lethal, fixed in the world (D province, Danger).

## 1. The faction in a page

**The thing it wants.** Soulrest is a merchant city of 172 people with no tree. "The city has no Hist, so its
Argonians hatch, name and bury inland in Banujha country" (D cape-percar, Soulrest), under the tree at Jarecai, and
the Mine-Burner elders "take a toll from Soulrest Argonians who come inland to the hatching grounds and use the
grounds as a lever on the port" (D banujha-basin, Jarecai). Every village of the cape sends its eggs the same way:
Acra-Tiin "sends its eggs the long way round the cape by boat to the Banujha tree, and presses a shell into the
shrine's clay for each clutch that goes" (D cape-percar, Acra-Tiin). This season the elders have gone further
(X): the gate on the way to the tree is shut to the coast, the clutches already carried in wait outside it, and
the elders have named a price nobody on the coast has been let in to hear. The League of Open Water is the city's
answer. *Open water is water no root reaches*: its people are the treeless and the half-rooted, Argonians named
under a tree they must pay to visit, lukiul sailors, Khajiit, Bosmer and the old Imperial families who raise
Argonian young as their own. Being "lukiul or mixed-urban and dependent on a neighbour's tree" is "the condition
the League of Open Water exists to argue about" (`topics/hist-placement.md:102`). It wants its children to belong
to the marsh without begging a root for it.

**Who they are and where they sit.** A hall in the half-empty government quarter of Soulrest (D cape-percar,
Soulrest: "the government quarter is half empty and repurposed"; the plan's need). Three people keep it, and they
do not agree (X; the names are the old design's, `36-cast-roster.md`).

- **Oshu-Kai**, the Speaker (man). He sends the envoy out and hears what comes home. He wins the things the hall
  wants won. He holds that a city which must ask leave to have children is not a city, and that the asking will
  never end by asking.
- **Hisska**, the League's egg-tender (woman), named under Jarecai's tree and proud of it. She packs the city's
  clutches and has carried them inland for twenty seasons. She holds that the city's young are the tree's own and
  the toll is the wrong, not the tree.
- **Martius Calvo**, the steward (man), of an old Imperial family, who with his Argonian partner is raising a
  clutch. He holds that open water means no root at all: send the eggs by sea to Gideon's garden tree, pay a
  second city instead of a tribe, take nothing and ask nobody. He is the voice inside the hall that says no to
  Oshu-Kai.

**How they work, in the line's own skills.** The League has one tool, an envoy: one person who goes alone into
country that does not want them, with a gift or a threat in their own pack, and decides what to bring home.
**Speech** is first: every door in the basin has a keeper who can be talked round, and every check has its own
refusal and another way left. **Sneak**, with Lockpicking and Pickpocket behind it, is second: what a keeper will
not give is under their lock, on their own ground and under their law. **Restoration** is third, and the tribe
respects it more than talk: a watcher's wound closed, a burnt hand eased, the burned dead of a mine turned. A
fighter can finish every quest; a fight is always the dearest way, because the people fought are the people the
city must live beside. No quest shuts out a build: each has a bought way at a fixed price.

**Who stands against them, and why each side is arguable.** The Mine-Burners. In 3E 427 "the tribes of Black Marsh
started a series of uprisings that destroyed plantations and mines in Soulrest" (`soulrest.md:41`); the basin's
people did the burning and "have never been reconciled to the coast" (D province, Peoples: Mine-Burners). Their
case: the coast burned nothing and lost nothing but its profits; the tribe bled for that ground, and the tree is
the one thing the city cannot buy elsewhere. The lever is all that stops the coast coming back with picks: the
merchant council still cuts timber on the basin's edge by the tribe's leave (D banujha-basin, Redtimber Camp) and
scavengers already work the burned mouths for iron (D cape-percar, Ninth Shaft, Blackdrift). The city's case: a
hatchling named under Jarecai's tree is that tree's child, and no people has the right to sell a tree's children
back to their parents. The tribe is three voices as well (X): **Xeech-Tul**, the elder who speaks for the council
and will not let go of the lever; **Jaxa-Reem**, the war-leader, who wants one thing more than the lever; and
**Neeta-Xi**, the tree-minder who keeps the gate, who says the tree turns whatever egg is laid at its root and
has never asked who paid.

**What it pays and what joining costs.** Fixed gold from the hall's purse, a bed in the hall, a rank that opens
the hall's lines, and what the basin gives an envoy it has come to respect: the trainers at its posts, who teach
only a friend (D cape-percar, Xal-Tarek: "Expert Archery trainer, the watch leader, once the watchers are
friendly"). Joining costs the envoy their welcome with the people who hold that no tree is taken: Many-Root's
minders greet a known envoy coldly and say why ([many-root](many-root.md), section 1).

**What it lies about.** The League says it wants fair passage to the tree. Its Speaker wants a tree. The hall's
roof is paid for by a merchant-council factor who holds the old concession paper and means to reopen the gold
seams the day the tribe's lever is gone, and the Speaker knows it. He also lets the hall believe he was named
under Jarecai's tree. He was hatched on a ship off Senchal and has never heard a tree in his life.

**The build leaning.** Speech, Sneak and Restoration, with Lockpicking and Pickpocket for what is under lock, and
a weapon or Destruction as the honest dear way (the profile `league-of-open-water`, with Restoration raised as the
plan rules).

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X throughout, on the cited lore), in the order it happened.**

1. A cutting of a Hist can be planted and will grow (`topics/hist-placement.md:93`, `:139`). It costs the tree it
   is taken from: the cut is made at the heart-root, and for some years after fewer of the mother tree's eggs
   turn. "A tree asked for a cutting gives it; a tree does not count its own eggs. It is the people under it who
   bury the shells." This is Many-Root's truth ([many-root](many-root.md), section 2, 1) and it is used here
   unchanged.
2. Gideon has a tree because, in a hungry year, the elders of the river folk, the Naka-Desh, sold a cutting of
   theirs to Gideon's council for salt and iron; a walker of Helstrom made the cut; the river folk's clutches
   failed for twelve wet seasons; the elders never told their people, who remember a theft and teach every
   hatchling a riddle about it, and Gideon's families remember a gift ([many-root](many-root.md), section 2, 2;
   D province, Peoples: Naka-Desh). In Soulrest only the gift is known: "Gideon was given a tree."
3. In 3E 427 the basin's tribes burned the Soulrest concession. The Empire's paper for it, the grant of the Percar
   and Banujha workings, was not burned. It lay in the civil office of the old capital, and when the Legion sailed
   the office kept it, as it kept everything.
4. Some seasons ago **Factor Tsabhira** of the merchant council (the record's "Expert Speech trainer, a
   merchant-council factor") bought the grant out of the civil office. She has had the seams above the city
   walked and sampled. She cannot dig while the tribe can close the hatching ground: the city's Argonians, who
   would be her crews, would not lift a pick. She began to pay for the League's hall.
5. Oshu-Kai came to Soulrest as a ship's purser, hatched off Senchal, deaf to every tree. He took the Factor's
   coin with his eyes open. His bargain with himself: a city with a tree of its own and gold of its own begs from
   nobody, and the crews will be hired at fair wage, not bound as they were.
6. Last season a Soulrest clutch of Hisska's own did not turn. She sat at the tree's heart-root at Jarecai and
   asked it, in grief, for a tree her city would not have to pay for. This season a pale shoot stands at the
   heart-root. Neeta-Xi found it, knew what it was, built the root-hut's door shut on it and has told neither the
   elders nor Hisska. A tree asked gives.
7. Word of something reached the elders anyway: a shoot is not kept secret from people who sleep under the tree.
   Xeech-Tul shut the gate to the coast and named a price, to find out what the coast knows. That is the season
   the line begins in.
8. Oshu-Kai has had a planting kit made at Gideon (a grafting knife, a jar of binding-sap, a basket), and it was
   taken off a boat at the Banu-Veeth chain by watchers who do not know what it is for.

**The later quests, at arc level.**

**LW03, Three Baskets.** *Place*: Jarecai, "a fortified town on stilts over the Banujha waters", "tiered halls
behind a palisade of stakes", where "strangers from the coast are questioned and sometimes held" (D banujha-basin,
Jarecai); the gate on the way to the tree, with its keeper (the plan's need). *What a new player is told*:
Oshu-Kai, in the hall: an envoy with ash on them will be heard; three of the city's clutches have waited outside
the gate since it shut, packed in wet moss, and Hisska says a packed clutch keeps the season; go by the raft from
the harbour or by the road past Deshaa, be heard, and bring home the price. He offers the pack a second thing to
carry beside the salt: the merchant council's sealed word that the red-mangrove fee stops (a threat; the Factor's).
*What the player does*: at the council hall's hearth Xeech-Tul hears the nail named and asks what the coast
sends. The gift and the threat are each a line that takes the thing from the pack; each warms one voice and cools
another. He names the price: the timber camps gone from the basin's edge, the coast's paper on the burned ground,
and the season's clutches named as Jarecai's and raised in Jarecai. Then the tribe's way with an envoy: "One
basket goes in as the city's. You carry the city's voice. Say whose." The three are the households the player
has met in the hall since LW01: Hisska's own; Martius's, "to be raised by a man with no scales"; and Sharp-Oar's,
a lukiul sailor's, "the eggs of one the tree never knew". Each parent has argued their case at Soulrest, and
none asks for another's to be left out. The choice is a line. A second basket can go in by Neeta-Xi at the gate
(Speech 50, or the gift if it is still in the pack): "The tree does not know the toll." A basket can instead be
lifted from the gate's rack (Sneak, an Adept lock, the tribe's law and the Owing for a thief seen) and carried
home, where Martius sends it by sea to Gideon's tree. Jaxa-Reem, the record's war-leader, speaks last and apart:
"A lever is for people who mean to bargain for ever. Bring me the paper that says our burned ground is your
city's and I will burn it at a mouth, and there is no toll." *Skills*: Speech; Sneak and Lockpicking. *Carries
on*: three stored numbers, one a basket; gift or threat. *Moment*: three egg-baskets on a rack in the rain, and
an elder waiting for one name.

**LW04, The Paper That Did Not Burn (the turn).** *Place*: Soulrest: the civil office and a counting-house on the
wharf (needs). *What the player is told*: Oshu-Kai says the paper is the grant of the Percar and Banujha
workings and lies in the civil office under **Secretary Aulia Pontanian**, the last of the old apparatus; he
does not say he knows otherwise. *What the player does*: the office's press holds an empty sleeve and a
receipt-slip in the Factor's hand, got by Speech with the Secretary, who is glad to say who bought it, or by her
lock. The paper is in the strongbox of Factor Tsabhira's counting-house: her key (Pickpocket), the lock (Expert),
her clerk bought (300 gold), and the city's own law for a thief, with cells. In the box with the paper lie three
bags of fresh-broken gold ore, each tagged with a seam's name, and a smith's tally-stick for sluice-iron. Taking
the paper is the find. *The turn*: Oshu-Kai, shown the paper, tells all of it, for the first time saying "I":
where he was hatched, whose coin built the hall, and what he means to do. "Burn it and the toll ends for as long
as one war-leader lives and remembers. A tree does not forget us. Bring me my kit from the Banu-Veeth chain and
I will show you what Gideon was given." It makes the earlier quests read differently: every errand was also a
measuring of the lever. The player keeps the paper or gives it back; either is a line and a number. *Skills*:
Lockpicking, Pickpocket, Sneak; Speech. *Carries on*: the paper; the truth told to Hisska and Martius or not.
*Moment*: fresh gold ore in a strongbox, in a city whose mines burned two hundred years ago.

**LW05, The Chain at Banu-Veeth.** *Places*: Reek-Anu, the Naka-Desh river station near Naxan, where "a good
riddle gains passage" (D cape-percar, Reek-Anu); Banu-Veeth Post, "a stilt watch-post on the water with a lookout
platform and a long boat-landing, with a toll chain across the channel", "a heap of salt sacks ... under a
lean-to", whose six "are hostile to anyone from the coast", its fights at the Deadly band (D banujha-basin,
Banu-Veeth Post). *What the player does*: the pilot who carried the kit, **Otumi-Lar** of Reek-Anu, will speak to
someone who answers his people's riddle or gives one (Speech 40; or a gift of salt, which the river folk take:
D banujha-basin, Ukeel Station). He asks the riddle every hatchling of his people is taught: "What went out in a
basket and came home in sacks, and whom do we not thank?" He has seen the kit opened on the chain-watch's planks
and knows a grafting knife. He says what his grandmother counted: twelve wet seasons in which no egg turned
under the river folk's tree. He says where the kit lies, under the lean-to among the salt, and asks that it be
sunk in the channel. Then the post: past the watch to the lean-to and its chest (Sneak; an Expert lock or the
watch-leader's key), by the ferryman who doubts his leader (Speech 60 with the corded nail held, and the nail's
ending in LW02 colours his answer), or through six watchers at the Deadly band, the hardest fight of the line
with the post's own chest as its fixed reward. Where the boy of LW02 went to the chain, he is one of the six.
*Skills*: Speech, Sneak, Lockpicking; a weapon or Destruction. *Carries on*: the kit, brought to Oshu-Kai; and
whether the hall was told the count, by a line to Hisska and Martius. *Moment*: a river pilot asking a riddle to
which the true answer is the thing in the player's pack.

**LW06, Open Water (the finale).** *Place*: Jarecai's hatching ground: the gate, the nests under the tree, the
root-hut at the heart-root (needs; reserved to this line, [province-plan](province-plan.md) section 4). *What the
player is told*: Oshu-Kai puts the kit in the pack and says what it is for and what it will cost: "Some seasons
of their eggs. Some of ours, laid under that tree. Then never again." Hisska, told at last, says she asked the
tree and did not know it answered. Martius: "We said no root. Not a stolen one." *The ways in*: by Neeta-Xi's
leave (Speech 60, or free to an envoy for whom she let a basket in); unseen, over the stakes and through the
hut's Expert lock (Sneak, Lockpicking, the tribe's law); or by Jaxa-Reem's word, for an envoy who lays the paper
in her hand first. *At the heart-root*: the nests are there to be seen, the tribe's and the city's side by side,
and the shoot stands in the root-hut. Taking it is the cut. Each side is argued by the person it concerns, there
or on the way: Oshu-Kai for the city's children for ever; Neeta-Xi, who will not stop the hand, "It gave. It
always gives. Count the nests before you take what it gave"; Xeech-Tul, if asked openly, who refuses and says
so plainly, "You are the first to ask. That is worth something. The answer is no"; Jaxa-Reem for the paper and
an end to it.

- **The cutting taken.** The shoot is carried to Soulrest. Found afterwards, switched while the player is far
  away: a planted sapling in the hall's court, which gives the city a blessing of its own (Waterbreathing and a
  cure for disease, as Jarecai's gives: D banujha-basin, Jarecai), Oshu-Kai's fixed purse and the rank of Voice.
  It costs the basin: Jarecai's raft-master, trainers and tree-minder refuse the player by their lines, the
  posts' trainers close, the nests at the hatching ground show dull eggs among the bright, and Neeta-Xi says
  which are the city's. If the paper went back to the Factor, her survey stakes stand at Ashfall Cut.
- **The paper burned.** The paper given to Jaxa-Reem: she burns it at a mouth where the player is not, and the
  gate's lines open to the coast without toll. It gives the war-leader's teaching, the tribe's welcome at every
  post, the tree's blessing free and a Mine-Burner's burned-iron blade. It costs the hall: the Factor's coin
  stops, the hall's trader is gone, Oshu-Kai greets the envoy as the hand that chose a tribe's word over a tree,
  and the timber crew at Redtimber Camp, whom the envoy passed on the road, lose their leave to cut.
- **Neither.** The shoot left and the paper kept or returned. The city sends what clutches it can afford by sea
  to Gideon; the toll stands; the League keeps its envoy and gives no rank. Neeta-Xi unlocks the root-hut: "Then
  it will grow where it stands, and they will see it, and it will be theirs to answer."

No ending is free, and none changes another line's stages. *Moment*: a pale shoot the height of a hand, at the
root of a tree with two peoples' eggs under it, and a knife in the pack.

**The turn** is LW04: the errand for a tribe's price leads to the League's own strongbox. **The misjudgement** is
LW05: the post "that robbed the city" seized a kit it did not understand; the watch-leader the player may have
killed for it was guarding his tree better than he knew, and the ferryman says so.

**The rank ladder.** Wader (on taking the first job); Envoy (LW01); Ash-Envoy (LW02: the envoy the stakes of
Jarecai open to); Envoy to the Stakes (LW03); Deep-Water Envoy (LW05); Voice of Open Water (LW06, the cutting
taken, only).

**What carries between quests.** The rank, the stored numbers named in each quest, who is alive, and the things in
the pack: the cask of salt, the corded nail, the paper, the kit. Earlier choices change greetings and single
lines, and never a later quest's stages. **The hub reacts**: after LW01 Hisska presses a shell into the clay of
the hall's bowl; where Deekum was named, she does not, and says why. After LW02 Martius calls the envoy
"ash-hand" and asks whether it washes off. After LW04 the three no longer stand together. **The line survives
being left** at any rank: the gate stays shut or one basket in, and the city goes on paying.

## 3. The first two quests

The line opens when the player first speaks to Oshu-Kai. It is not in the plan's second slice
([province-plan](province-plan.md) section 5); its first two quests are outlined in full for when its places are
built.

### LW01. The Shell in the Clay

**What a new player is told, and when.** On first speaking to Oshu-Kai, before any job, four optional lines, each
two or three sentences. *The League*: "Open water is water no root reaches. This city has no tree. Its people
are named under another people's, or under none, and we are the ones who say they are of the marsh all the
same." *The tree*: "An egg laid here is carried inland to the tree at Jarecai, hatched there and named there.
The tribes who hold that ground are the Mine-Burners. They burned this city's mines two hundred years ago, and
they take a toll in salt or iron for every clutch." *Why you*: "Everyone in this hall is known at every post in
the basin, by face and by grudge. I need one person nobody there has yet refused." *What it pays and why you
might not*: "Coin, a bed, and my word in this city. And every Mine-Burner between here and the tree will
remember which side you carried for." "Not yet" stays open and free. Hisska, beside him, answers "Why must the
eggs go inland?" at any time: no tree here; hatch, name and bury under Jarecai's; a packed clutch keeps a
season in wet moss; and "the north has egg-thieves, I hear. Ours are not stolen. Ours are kept, by people we
can name." The job is four sentences: Acra-Tiin, the fishing village across the water, sent its clutch round the
cape by boat as it does every year; the Mine-Burner watch at Xal-Tarek, the stilt platform on the south shore,
boarded the boat and has kept the basket; **Deekum**, the village's boatman, carried it and is at Acra-Tiin's
landing; win the basket back, "and tell me how you did it, because how is what they will remember".

**The hidden truth (X).** The hatching toll is paid in salt or iron (D banujha-basin, Banu-Veeth Post: "a toll of
salt or iron"). Acra-Tiin lives by "fish and salt" (D cape-percar, Acra-Tiin) and had a poor salt year. Deekum
put in at Ninth Shaft on his way round the cape, a burned mouth whose headframe "draws salvagers for iron ties
and rail, and Xal-Tarek's garrison discourages the bolder ones" (D cape-percar, Ninth Shaft), and pulled
rail-iron to pay the toll with. A patrol from Xal-Tarek came on him at the portal; he ran for his boat; a
watcher, **Geem-Ra**, went through the rotten headframe after him and tore his leg open on the iron. The watch
boarded the boat off the shore and took what was in it: the iron and the egg-basket. Deekum swam for it and
nobody saw his face. **Bimeesh-Tul**, the watch-leader, keeps the basket in his chest as surety. To him a burned
mouth is a shrine, a village's eggs were a robber's cover, and his watcher may not walk again. He cannot go to

## 6. For the other lines

For the consistency pass (nw-05 step 5). None of these changes another line's stages.

- **Many-Root.** This line tells the truth of Gideon's tree exactly as [many-root](many-root.md) section 6 gives
  it: sold by the river folk's elders for salt and iron, cut by a walker of Helstrom, twelve wet seasons of failed
  clutches, a theft remembered by the tribe and a gift by the city. It adds nothing to Gideon and sets no quest
  there. LW05's river pilot asks the same riddle Hapu-Jeer asks at Teth-Kasa and gives the same count; he is a
  different man at a different station (Reek-Anu). This line adds one thing to the lore of cuttings (X): a tree
  that has been asked may put up a shoot at its heart-root, and taking the shoot is the cut and costs what a
  cut costs. Many-Root's hall is right, by this line's own ending, that Jarecai's people were never asked; here
  the envoy can ask, and is refused. If the watcher Marzeel carried the Listener's knife to Jarecai's elders
  (MR06), nothing here shows their answer. The hall's coldness to a known envoy is a greeting gated on League
  rank, which that line owns.

  cape's is stolen in this line: Hisska says so in one line, "ours are kept, by people we can name". The League
  knows nothing of the egg-thieves' pilot.
