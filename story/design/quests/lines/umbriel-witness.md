# The Umbriel Witness: the line's story

The story of the Umbriel Witness's six quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the province plan gives the line ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §3.13, §4, §7) and the lead's rulings on
it ([nw-05](../../../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05-story-quest-adapt-1.md)). A quest writer turns each
outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations. Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/` (*state* is
`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`; *umbriel* is `topics/umbriel.md`; *guilds* is
`topics/guilds-and-orders.md`; *loot* is `topics/ecology-encounters-loot.md`). **D** is the digest of the new world
([province](../../world/province.md) and the region files beside it; "D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth" is that place's
record or hooks in that region's file). **X** marks this document's own extrapolation from what is cited (decision
0032). The places are not built yet: a place is named by what its record says it is, and anything else a quest needs
of it is a row of section 4.

**Words used below**, for a reader new to the province. *Hist*: the sentient trees every Argonian is hatched under.
*4E 48*: the year Lilmoth's tree, gone mad, called the flying city Umbriel down on its own town; everything under
the city died and rose again as its army. That was 153 years ago, and nobody alive saw it. *The party*: the
An-Xileel, who ruled Lilmoth then; their offices are kept today by the Wardens. *Lukiul*: the party's word for an
Argonian who lived in the Empire's ways. *Grave-stake*: the carved post an Argonian is buried under, which tells the
dead one's whole story and pins them; an Argonian corpse without one does not lie still. *Name-charm*: the carved
charm a hatchling is given at its naming and wears for life (X; the Night-Reed's story has the same gift at a
naming, [night-reed](night-reed.md) NR01). *The College*: the College of Whispers, the Empire's licensed
necromancers.

## 1. The faction in a page

**What they want.** Lilmoth is "a smaller, lower, wholly Argonian stilt town over a mass grave" (`lilmoth.md:104`).
Its drowned Imperial quarter was never repaired, "because nobody will build there", and has a name that is not used
in front of strangers (`lilmoth.md:107` to `:108`; *state*:625). The Witness say the name: **the Unstaked** (X).
Every Argonian burial is "a xul-vaat carved with the dead one's whole story" (*loot*:139), and a corpse whose stake
is lost becomes one of the "dead-and-not-dead things" (*loot*:77). The thousands who died in 4E 48 got no stakes at
all, and "canon does not state what became of the horde" (*umbriel*:78): in this world some of it never left the
rooms it died in (X, on *guilds*:111 to :114, "Umbriel's dead still lie under the Murkmire mud"). The Witness go
down for what names a dead person, bring it up and carve the stake. A stake tells a whole story, so every stake
they raise says something about a family that is still alive.

**Who they are and where.** Inheritors, not survivors (*state*:622): a dozen divers and carvers whose
great-great-grandparents' accounts are "worn smooth by retelling and probably wrong in detail" (*state*:623). They
sit on dry ground at the edge of the drowned quarter (the lead's fifth ruling): the **Stake-Ground**, rows of
carved stakes with the black water behind them, the **Stake-House** beside it, a **Carving Shed**, and the
**Divers' Stair**, where the walk ends and steps go down into the water (all X, needs of Lilmoth, section 4).

- **Neexa-Tul**, keeper of the Stake-Ground, in her fifties ([40-factions](../40-factions.md) §41, kept). Her creed:
  the dead belong to their kin, not to the town. What comes up goes into the family's hands and the family chooses
  what is carved.
- **Old Nusa**, who taught every diver on the stair and is bored of Umbriel and says so (40-factions §41, moved
  here from the Refuge, which this world does not have: province-plan §3.13). "A hundred and fifty years. I have
  buried people I knew."
- **Carves-Twice**, the carver, who also carries the merchant council's stipend down from the upper walks (X). His
  creed: the Stake-Ground is the town's one honest boast, and a stake that shames a living house helps nobody dead.
- **Dives-Without-Asking**, the youngest diver, whose brother drowned in the quarter two winters ago and has no
  stake yet (X). Her creed: all of it comes up and all of it is carved, whoever it hurts. Hers is the only raw grief
  in the line, and it is new grief, not 4E 48's (*state*:628).

**How they work, in the line's own skills.** Breath first: Alteration's water-breathing, an alchemist's draught, an
Argonian's own gills, or the sap of Lilmoth's young tree, which its two minders give to anyone and which "gives
waterbreathing for 8 hours" (D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth). Then light, since the rooms are dark (Alteration, or a
torch once out of the water). Then the dead: nobody can fight in deep water, so a diver swims past what swims and
meets what stands only on a floor, with Restoration to turn it, Conjuration to set something between, Sneak and a
steady hand to take a charm from a neck that has not moved in a century and a half, or a hard fight. Then the
living, who are the harder part, with Speech. Mysticism is how a diver plays and never a gate: detection to find
what still stands behind a door, a Mark set at the stair before going under.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **The College of Whispers.** Canon: it "summons Daedra and practises necromancy" and had "the most up-to-date
  information on Umbriel", finding its dead "more akin to flesh atronachs" and deaf to "the same arcane stimuli"
  (*guilds*:106 to :109). Its interest in Argonia is "specific and worse", and "scholarly rather than genocidal and
  therefore harder to refuse cleanly" (*guilds*:111 to :118). Its buyer in Lilmoth is the record's own "Breton dock
  mage" (D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth: spell vendor), here named **Odile Marric** (X). The docks-only law against
  unlicensed foreigners is still enforced "in some quarters" (D, Lilmoth; `lilmoth.md:79` to `:83`), and the
  quarter's edge is one of them (X), so she cannot go where the Witness go. She pays divers. Above her is
  **Praeceptor Hadrian Scaeva** (X), who comes south at the line's turn. Their case: Umbriel is the only thing that
  ever raised an army nobody could stop, the Empire that could not stop it is weaker now, and a people who will not
  study what killed them will be killed by it twice. She adds, of the foreign dead: "They were Bretons. Ask yourself
  who a lizard's grave-post is for."
- **The merchant council**, which pays the Witness's stipend and has something to bury (province-plan §3.13). It
  hates the Wardens loudly because "it was the party's seat, and the party killed it" (`lilmoth.md:120` to `:122`),
  and the Stake-Ground is its standing accusation. Its voice in this line is **Shuvu-Meen**, matriarch of the
  Three-Knot lighters, who keeps the council's salvage (X; the Night-Reed names the council's Meer-Tulo, and the two
  stories do not need the two to meet: section 6). Her case comes in section 2.
- **The living**, family by family. A landholder with hatchlings asleep over a walled cellar. A sail-mender whose
  house has had its good name from a martyr for five generations.
- **The Nisswo**, in talk only (province-plan §4). Their Jeer-Tei stands on the dry ground by the quarter's edge
  saying it is time to let go ([nisswo](nisswo.md) §6); the doctrine is "the province's own counter-argument to its
  own obsession" and "sympathetic" (*state*:638 to :642). Neexa-Tul of him: "He says a town that dives for its grave
  every morning will drown in it. He may be right about the town. He has never had to tell a hatchling which stake
  is hers." Old Nusa: "He is the only one on this bank who wants nothing from the dead. I would share a pipe with
  him if he stopped pitying me."
- **The Night-Reed**, neighbours (province-plan §4). The house thinks the Witness sell grief; the Witness think the
  house sells the dead's spoons ([night-reed](night-reed.md) §1). Both are right.

**What it pays and what joining costs.** The diver's share: whatever comes up with no name on it is the diver's
(the record's own "salvage from the drowned Imperial villas", D, Lilmoth). Neexa-Tul's gifts are divers' things:
draughts and scrolls from the divers' chest, and vanilla jewellery enchanted for water-breathing, Alteration and
Restoration (the record writer names the forms). The cost: what the player brings up belongs to someone living, who
will not always thank them; and the dead down there do not lie still.

**What it lies about.** It says it carves what the water gives. It carves what a family can bear and what the
council will pay for; and its own first stake is a lie (section 2).

**The build leaning.** Alteration and Restoration first (breath, light, turning the dead), Conjuration and Alchemy
second, Sneak with Pickpocket for the quiet hand, any weapon skill or Destruction for the hard fight each delve
offers, Speech for the living. It suits a mage first, a careful thief second and a fighter third, and no quest
shuts any of the three out. Nothing is levelled (decision 0035): each delve's dead stand at fixed levels in the
world's fixed bands (D province, Danger).

## 2. The line's arc

**What is known and what is not.** 4E 48 is "not a mystery. What happened is known and documented; what is argued
about is blame, meaning and inheritance" (*state*:636). Everybody in Lilmoth can tell the player the canon: the
tree "was psychotic and had been controlling the An-Xileel", it summoned Umbriel, and "mind-controlled An-Xileel
and undead sea-creatures attacked lukiul Argonians and foreign races indiscriminately" (`lilmoth.md:92` to `:96`);
the party meant "to exterminate all foreign taint and 'assimilated' Argonians", and "the An-Xileel and the Wild
Ones intended to hide until Black Marsh was 'clean'" (`an-xileel.md:59` to `:61`). What nobody can tell them is
which of Lilmoth's houses did which. That is the line.

**The hidden spine (X), in order.**

1. The tree's singers were party men and women of Lilmoth, some of them in secret. Each wore a **singer's gourd**,
   a thumb-long gourd of the tree's sap stopped with wax, and drank from it before the "long, awful cacophonous
   chant" (`lilmoth.md:84` to `:86`). Whoever drank was the tree's and not their own (`an-xileel.md:64` to `:65`).
2. The party's chosen households in Lilmoth were to wait out the killing in the sunken shrine of Xhon-Mehl the
   Fisher (`lilmoth.md:53`), whose vault the party had pumped dry and warded as it did the Ixtaxh xanmeer
   (`an-xileel.md:52` to `:54`). The ward opened to a singer's gourd.
3. On the day, the foreigners were where the party's law had put them, on the docks (`lilmoth.md:79` to `:83`). The
   master of the Three-Knot lighters sold passage to those who could pay, in lead tallies stamped with his knots,
   and sent them up the cart road to wait in the cellar of an empty planter's house for a boat that he never sent.
   Then he took his own household down to the Fisher's vault. The dead found the cellar.
4. A servant of the party, a lukiul woman of Pusbottom, kept the vault's door. Forty of Pusbottom followed her down:
   the party's enemies, who "hid out in the Pusbottom district" (`an-xileel.md:50` to `:51`), and their hatchlings.
   She barred the door from inside. She lived, and so did the houses behind her.
5. Those who ran west along the road were shut into the cellar of the Legion's empty road post by the people of
   Karketa, who barred it on the living and the dead together, and so the village "was spared" (D
   althii-archipelago, Karketa). Karketa then "sent people to help the town's rebuilding" (the same record), and
   carved what it had done on its own post.
6. In the generations after, the vault's houses came back as "Karketa folk" and delta kin and made the council
   again (`lilmoth.md:118` to `:119`). The door-keeper came back first. She was the first to dive and the first to
   carve, and her telling, "I held the water-gate until forty were through", is the **First Stake**. The Witness
   began with her. Neexa-Tul is of her line. As a young diver she swam the Fisher's shrine "as far as breath
   allows" (D, Lilmoth: hooks), saw forty charms in the silt outside a door barred from within, and has told no one.
7. The council sells the College leave to take up the quarter's nameless dead, at so much a head, and that silver
   is the stipend that pays for the stakes. Carves-Twice carries it and knows.
8. The College wants a living sample of what moved the dead. The vault holds the singers' store of sap, the **Song
   Jar**. The College believes the 4E 48 dead will still answer it. Nobody in the line can show whether they do.

### The later quests

**UW03, The Barn at Seeku (the turn).** "A stranger asks to rent the barn and will not say why" (D
althii-archipelago, Seeku Farm), "a short walk from Lilmoth's tree". The stranger is Praeceptor Scaeva's man, and
the barn is the College's workroom (X). Dives-Without-Asking has gone to look and stands at the paddock gate,
outside the barn's reach, where she speaks: she has seen a dead thing carried in that had her brother's arm-ring.
*What the player does:* gets into a locked barn held by two hired blades (hostile, fixed at level 18) and one of the
quarter's dead standing roped in a stall. The door key is the farm family's, who took good coin and are frightened
(Speech, or 150 gold, or Pickpocket); the door picks at Adept (Lockpicking); the blades are fought, crept past
(Sneak) or drawn off by something conjured. The prize is in a locked travelling chest: **the College's licence**,
an outsider's paper and the only document in the line, sealed with three knots, giving leave "to take up the
unnamed dead of the old quarter at forty septims a head, payable through the carver at the Stake-Ground". The dead
thing in the stall wears the arm-ring. *The turn:* the silver that pays for the stakes is the price of the
unstaked, and the mark that sold six Bretons a boat in UW01 sells the College their bodies now. *The choice,* each
argued by the one it concerns: to Neexa-Tul, who asks for silence until the Fisher's shrine is dived, because
without the council's leave nobody dives it; to Dives-Without-Asking, who will nail it to the First Stake before
the whole town; or to Carves-Twice, who buys it back. His case is honest in the Witness's own terms: "Forty a head
staked a hundred with names. I sold what nobody will ever name to bury those we can." *Gains and costs:* Neexa-Tul
gives the third rank and a ring of Restoration; Dives-Without-Asking gives her brother's arm-ring (a fixed
enchantment for swimming) and the council's guards turn cold for the rest of the line; Carves-Twice pays 600 gold,
and the paper goes back to the College (`uw.college` +1). *The misjudgement:* a player who kills Carves-Twice for a
traitor finds in his shed the hundred stakes he paid for and no coin of his own.

**UW04, The Road the Living Took.** Fort Sallowmoth is roofless, its "cellar holds standing water", "holes in the
floor by the old armoury drop into the cellar" and "the squatters call the yard unlucky and do not sleep inside"
(D althii-archipelago, Fort Sallowmoth). Its hook is taken: "a squatter wants a lost kinsman found, last seen
heading for the fort". Dives-Without-Asking's brother went there looking for the road dead; the squatter's kinsman
went after salvage. The squatter speaks from the road short of the wall (a need, by the lead's fourth ruling) and
shows the broken corner. *What the player does:* crosses or creeps the yard (the record's own squatters), drops
through the floor, which cannot be climbed again, a point of no return said twice, and stands on the cellar's dry
shelf among the dead of the road: bog blights at fixed level 30 and two singers still wearing their gourds.
Restoration turns them; Sneak passes them; a fighter earns the armoury's worth below. Five **name-charms** lie
among them (a counted thing; the hand-in is gated on holding five), with the kinsman's satchel. The way out is the
flooded sally-drain to the marsh, or a Mark set before the drop and Recall (Mysticism). The cellar's own door is
barred on the outside with a boat's thwart cut with Karketa's fish. *Then Karketa:* the headwoman does not deny
it. Her village's "prayer-post carved for the drowned" (D, Karketa) says it, for anyone who reads posts: "We
barred the road post on forty living and the dead behind them. We are alive. Carve it." A village that never hid
its guilt, a short walk from a town that hides all of it. She asks one thing back: "Ask your keeper whose charm
hangs on the Fisher's door." *The choice:* the five stakes say who barred the door, or say only who died. *Gains
and costs:* the fourth rank; the two gourds (kept, given or sold: `uw.college` +1 if sold); Karketa's boats and
table open or shut to the player by what the stakes say.

**UW05, The Fisher's House (the race).** The sunken shrine of Xhon-Mehl, "which a diver reaches only as far as
breath allows" (D, Lilmoth: hooks), is the longest swim in the line and the council keeps it for itself. Beyond the
drowned nave is the vault, dry behind its door (the lead's third ruling). The door opens to a singer's gourd, which
is its key. *If the College has been sold little* (`uw.college` 0 or 1): the door is shut. The player needs a
gourd of their own (kept in UW02, taken in UW04, or one Neexa-Tul hands over with a reluctance the player will
understand an hour later) and passes the shrine's dead. *If it has been sold much* (2 or more): the door stands
open. Scaeva's two blades and three roped dead hold the vault floor (hostile, fixed at level 32); Scaeva himself
stands apart in the dry robing room and speaks, with the Song Jar already in a locked case beside him, and the
player gets it by a bargain he names, by his key lifted, or over his people. Either way the vault shows the same
things: forty name-charms in the silt outside the door; the bar on the inside, with one more charm hung on it,
Neexa-Tul's forebear's, and her telling cut into the bar twice, the first cutting struck through ("I shut the door
on forty"); and the singers' cups, each with a house-mark, three knots among them. The player brings up the **Song
Jar**, the **Three-Knot cup** and the **bar**. Whoever lost the race waits at the head of the Divers' Stair,
switched on by the taking of the jar far below: Scaeva's blades if the player was first, nobody if the College was.
A fight on a floor, or a line.

**UW06, What the Stakes Say.** No delve. Four people, standing where they have stood all line, each ask for the
three things, and the hand-over is the ending.

Shuvu-Meen's case, said only here: "My house sold six Bretons a boat that did not exist, and hid, and came back,
and built every walk you are standing on. Carve it. Then tell me who feeds this town the season after, and whose
harbour it is when the Wardens hear that Lilmoth's council were party men all along." Last, alone with Neexa-Tul,
a player who holds the bar may ask about the charm on it. She answers once: "I was nineteen and I had breath for
one look. I have carved four hundred true stakes on the strength of one false one. Tell me which you would pull."

## 3. The first two quests

Common to both.

- **Neexa-Tul** stands in the Stake-House for the whole line. She must live: her death fails any running quest of
  the line with the faction's message. Nobody is essential. People stand where they are placed.
- **One working stage.** After the briefing each quest sits at one stage at which every way is open. What a way
  achieves is a thing held or a person dead, or a stage of its own below the take that finishes the step.
- **The dead stand on floors.** Every creature the quests place is a person of ours at a fixed level, standing where
  the ground can be walked, behind the swim and never in it (the lead's third ruling). What swims is the place's
  own (slaughterfish, young crocodilians) and can be swum past.
- **Breath is told before it is needed.** Old Nusa, at the head of the Divers' Stair, says where breath is had: the
  young tree's two minders at the tree's wall, the herb-wife's draughts, a mage's own spell, an Argonian's gills.
- Every objective has a marker. Journal entries are first person, past tense, and say only what the player has just
  done. Thresholds are fixed. Every failed check has its own refusal and leaves another way open. "Night" in
  anyone's mouth is their word only.

### UW01. Six Behind the Wall

**What a new player is told, and when.** Neexa-Tul, before any job, four optional lines of two or three sentences:
*4E 48* ("The old tree went mad and called a city out of the sky. Everything under it died and got up again. Nobody
alive saw it; we have what our great-grandmothers' grandmothers said"); *the Unstaked* ("The drowned quarter. We
are buried under a stake that tells who we were, and it holds us down. Nobody down there has one, and some of them
have not lain down"); *the Witness* ("We go under for what names them and we carve it. What comes up with no name
on it is yours"); *why I might not* ("What you bring up belongs to someone living. They will not always thank you.
And the council pays for my chisels, which you should know before you admire them"). "Not yet" stays open and
free. The job: "Dunhallow, the rice estate up the cart road north-west. The family walled its cellar in my
grandmother's day. I think six people are behind that wall who have waited a hundred and fifty years to be called
something. Bring me what names them. Tseeba-Mei, who holds the place, will not want it opened. And the Breton on
the foreigners' dock, Odile Marric, has been asking after that cellar. She will offer you money. Hear her if you
like, and know what she buys."

**The hidden truth (X).** In 4E 48 a Breton chandler of the docks, his wife, two children, a clerk and a maid paid
the master of the Three-Knot lighters for passage. He gave them six lead tallies stamped with his knots, each
scratched with its bearer's name so that it could not be sold on, and sent them up the cart road to the empty
planter's house (D althii-archipelago, Dunhallow Plantation: "gone long before the secession, leaving the house to
rot") to wait in its cellar for a boat on the dyke. No boat came. The dead of the road found them. They put what
they had in the planters' strongbox before the end. Generations later a Lilmoth family "patched the roof, cleared
the dykes and planted again" (the same record), found what stood in the cellar and "walled up the old cellar, where
the planters' strongbox lies and the unstaked Breton dead are not at rest" (D, Dunhallow: hooks). The hatch in the
kitchen yard was kept, locked, so that the wall could be looked at. The cellar's drain still runs to the east dyke.

## 6. For the other lines

- **The Nisswo.** Lilmoth's grave is talk only, as their story asks ([nisswo](nisswo.md) §6). Jeer-Tei stands on
  the dry ground by the quarter's edge, which here is near the Stake-Ground; no quest of this line moves a stage by
  him or is set where he stands. What Neexa-Tul and Old Nusa say of him is in section 1.
- **The Night-Reed.** Silt-Orrel is not used, nor its beams and fittings. Dunhallow's cellar and its dead are taken
  here; its strongbox holds the tallies. This line names one more council member, **Shuvu-Meen** of the Three-Knot
  lighters, keeper of the council's salvage; she and Meer-Tulo need not meet, and if the consistency pass wants one
  councillor, the Three-Knot house can be his. Fort Sallowmoth is used by both lines: theirs is the armoury cage,
  this one's the cellar under it and the drain out, and neither needs the other's thing. The house's opinion of the
  Witness stands as written.
- **The main quest.** Xil-Veeka is not used. The young tree's sap is used for breath only, and nothing is said of
  its health or of any living tree's. The singer's gourd and the Song Jar hold the sap of the second tree, dead
  since 4E 48: this line says only what the College believes of it and never shows it do anything; nothing here
  touches the Root, the Eye or the Unbound Root. Bell Station, the Kelp Lantern, Thul-Kesh Spire and Echo Deep are
  not entered or spoken for.
- **The Sunken Archive.** The College's leader in the south is **Praeceptor Hadrian Scaeva**, and its buyer at
  Lilmoth is **Odile Marric**, the record's dock mage. Maelle Jurard answers to Scaeva. Either may be dead by the
  time she speaks of them; she should say "colleagues", as she does.
- **The roster lane.** The dead this line places are the unstaked Argonian dead the lore calls bog blights
  (*loot*:77) and the foreign dead of 4E 48; they need forms the world already holds that are not Nordic (decision
  0006), at fixed levels 7 to 32.
