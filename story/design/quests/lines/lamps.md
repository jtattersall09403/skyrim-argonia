# The Lampbearers: the plot

This page is the plot of the Lampbearers, the detectives, written on 2026-11-16 to the owner's notes in
[the factions overhaul](../../../../docs/phases/nw-05c-factions-overhaul.md) and revised on
2026-11-17 to his story explorer notes. The Lampbearers have two rivals, the Eelskins ([thieves](thieves.md)) and the
Shadowscales ([shadowscales](shadowscales.md)). Each of the three lines tells its own late story; the rivals appear
in it with the same leaders and the same seats. Every quest is set in a place whose source holds what the quest
needs, and a bracket says which source place gives it and how we know: what that source's buildings, interiors and placed pieces are. Everything is
inside the quest ceiling ([quest-system](../../../../.claude/skills/quest-system/SKILL.md)), with the blocks listed
in section 8.

**Words.** *The Lampbearers*: the investigators of Soulrest, who find out who did a crime; one of them is a
*Lampbearer*. *A case*: one crime the Lampbearers are asked to solve. *The arbiter*: the judge whom both sides of a
quarrel choose, here the old woman who hears the Lampbearers' cases. *The Owing*: the marsh's sentence, a counted
number of months or years of owed work in place of a cell. *Ku-vastei*: "those who bring needed change", the
Argonian name for the Shadowscales when they served as judges and enforcers. *The Shadowscales*: Argonian
assassins, given to the order at birth. *The Eelskins*: the thieves' guild of the south. *Hist*: the great
thinking trees of Black Marsh.

## 1. What the faction is

The Lampbearers are a small band of investigators in Soulrest who are paid to find out who committed a crime and
to prove it. A Lampbearer searches the scene, finds the evidence, questions the people, works out who did it and
brings that person before the arbiter.

The Lampbearers work from the Lamp-Hall, a stone house in Soulrest's half-empty government quarter, with a branch
in a house on the quay of Truowa (checked: Soulrest's source is Skyrim's Solitude, whose palace interiors give the halls that the half-empty government quarter, with the Lamp-Hall and its hearing room, is to be built from, an addition the place's record lists; Truowa's source is King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth, whose merchant manors give a house, and the quay is an addition the record lists). Their skills are Speech, Sneak and Lockpicking for getting at evidence that people
hide, and Illusion: Calm, Fury and Mayhem, with Throw Noise and Clairvoyance from the spells by use
([spell-groups](../spell-groups.md)). Their rivals are the Eelskins, whom they catch. The Shadowscales are rivals
too, since the Lampbearers are paid to solve their killings. Joining gives the player cases that pay in coin and rank, a bed in Soulrest, and
a hand-made reward at each rank.

**Why there are Lampbearers.** Soulrest has three powers that contest who rules it: the merchant council, the old
Imperial civil office and the river families (`soulrest.md:49-50`; the place record). Black Marsh settles a quarrel
before an arbiter that both sides choose (`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md:121-127`). Its other judges were the
Shadowscales, and their order is thought dead since the Archon school closed in 4E 187
(`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:61-67`, `:85`; `argonia-4e201-state.md` §2.4). **X:** the three powers of
Soulrest agreed to pay for investigators that none of them controls, with an arbiter whom all three accept. They
hire strangers, because every local belongs to one side. That is why a stranger is useful to them.

**Their history and their name.** After joining, the player can ask Marcella Dovi about both. She answers: "When
the Archon school shut, nobody was left to settle a killing but the killer's kin. Soulrest's three powers each put
up a third of our pay in 4E 189, so that none of them owns us. The first of us worked the government quarter's
cellars by lamplight, and the city started calling us the ones who bring a lamp into the dark. We kept the name."

## 2. The truth under the story

**What the player is shown.** Cases climb from a stolen pay chest to murder. Late in Act II the player finds three
killings done with one clean cut by people who leave nothing behind. The arbiter says the Shadowscales are gone and
the last of them died this year in Skyrim. Then Orrel-Sakka, the first voice of Lilmoth's merchant council, is found
dead on his own boat landing at Silt-Orrel Estate.

**What is true.** The Shadowscales never ended. The Shadowscale who died in Skyrim this year, Veezara, was real
(`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:4`, `:85`). The word that he was the last was spread by the order itself, as
cover. The Black-Tongue tribe never stopped finding Shadow-born hatchlings (`argonia-4e201-state.md:214-216`), and an
old Shadowscale, Ukka-Teel, the Eldest, trains them in secret at Ra'tal Den, a ruin of the Banujha Basin. The
arbiter, Teesh-Vaal, was a Shadowscale herself before she retired to judge (`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:65-67`).
She knows the order lives, and she hides it for a reason of her own: a foreign order of assassins wants the
order's hatchlings, and word that the order lives would show it where they are (**X**, from `argonia-4e201-state.md`
§2.4).

**The murder.** For a year, men of the washes have sold Shadow-born hatchlings for gold to Lucan Vetrix, an Imperial who
calls himself a rice buyer. Vetrix is the agent of the Dark Brotherhood's Listener at Cheydinhal, a foreign order of
assassins that wants the Archon school and the order's hatchlings for itself (`archon.md:48-52`;
`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:203-223`). The sellers took the hatchlings to Vetrix on the boats of Silt-Orrel, and
Orrel-Sakka took a share of every sale. The Eldest killed three of the sellers this year, and from the last of them
he learnt who carried the hatchlings. On the next dark night he waited by Orrel-Sakka's landing, where the master
went alone to meet the hatchling boat. He killed him with one cut. Orrel-Sakka tore the Eldest's brass medallion
from his neck as he died. Every Shadowscale wears one (`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:68`), and on its back is
cut its owner's own sign, a hook and an eye (**X**).

That same night an Eelskin thief, Drips-From-Eaves, was in the estate's old storehouse, emptying the salvage chest
that the family keeps there (the place's record). He heard the cry and went out. He found the body on the landing
and the brass in its hand, and he took the brass, since brass is worth something. He gave it to Swims-Without-Ripples,
the Eelskins' leader, as part of her share, and went to ground at Meris-Keth. She now holds the one thing that names
the killer.

## 4. Joining

**The rumours.** People in Lilmoth, Truowa, Blackrose, Archon and Mir-Xal speak of the Lampbearers when asked about
work, crime or Soulrest: "Soulrest pays strangers to poke into crimes. They call them the Lampbearers." The first
such line starts the small quest *The Lampbearers of Soulrest*. Its journal names the Lamp-Hall, and a marker shows
its door. Soulrest's harbour boatman takes the player there from Lilmoth, Archon or Truowa.

**Being let in.** The player signs on with Marcella Dovi in the Lamp-Hall. She asks only that the player has no kin
in Soulrest, and takes them on because they belong to no side. The small quest completes, and the first case begins
at once. There is no test beyond the first case, which a new character with a lockpick can solve.

**What shuts the door.** The Lampbearers' door shuts when the player is given the key to the Eelskins' hall at the
end of the Eelskins' 4, The Lampbearer's Man, or takes the brass at the end of the Shadowscales' 4, The Hatchling
Seller. *The
Lampbearers of Soulrest* then fails. In this line, the Eelskins' and the Shadowscales' doors shut at the end of
quest 4, when the player takes the rank of Flame. Before they take it, Marcella tells them what it costs: every
thief and every assassin in the south will know them, and a player who is already an Eelskin or a Shadowscale of the
lowest rank is put out of it.

## 5. The acts

The Lampbearers' line is fourteen cases in three acts. Act I is petty crime in towns and on farms. Act II is
killing, and the case structure starts to break. Act III is the murder at Silt-Orrel and the Shadowscales behind it.

**The case structure.** Each case in Acts I and II runs the same way, and Marcella explains it in quest 1:

1. The journal names the places of the case by area: "search the mate's room at the harbour inn", "speak to people
   in the quay tavern". Each marker leads to the area or the room, never to the thing or the person in it. Inside,
   the player looks and asks. Each clue is a named thing to take (the game shows its name when the player looks at
   it) or a line to hear, and the journal records each one as it is found.
2. The player reports to their handler (Marcella in Soulrest, or J'zahri at Truowa) and names a suspect. Then they
   name the suspect's motive, then the means, then the opportunity. Each answer is open only once its evidence has
   been found.
3. Every suspect has a motive and a means. Every suspect but the culprit has an alibi or a clue against them, which
   the player can find.
4. When the player has named all four, the screen fades. The player and the suspect stand before Teesh-Vaal in the
   hearing room. She goes through the case aloud. She either confirms it or shows the key evidence that the player
   missed.
5. *Right:* the culprit is given the Owing, the player is paid and the next case opens. *Wrong:* the player pays a
   fine in gold and must solve one of the rank's two spare cases before the next case opens. Three wrong at one
   rank and Marcella puts the player out of the Lampbearers for good.

**The spare cases.** The fine climbs with the rank: 25 gold at Wick, 75 at Flame, 150 at Lantern. Each rank has two
spare cases of the same shape, and a wrong answer opens the first one not yet solved. A spare case can itself be
failed, which counts as one more wrong answer.

- *Wick.* **The Smelter's Silver** (Soulrest): three ingots have gone from the harbour smelter overnight. The
  night man, the buyer who weighs them and the keeper's nephew are suspects; the nephew's boots carry the smelter's
  slag, and the silver lies in his bedroll.
- *Wick.* **The Toll Box** (Mir-Xal): the toll house's box was short at dawn. The suspects are the reeve's clerk, a
  carter whom the guards turned back and the guard who stood watch that night; his dice partner at the stage inn has
  the coin.
- *Flame.* **The Salt Pan** (Karketa): a Lilmoth family's new salt pan was fouled with lamp oil. The headwoman's son,
  the family's foreman and a netter who lost the pan for a debt are suspects; the oil flask under the foreman's hut
  is his own, bought to ruin the pan and sell the debt on (checked: Karketa's source is Keeba Hollow from King of the Murkmire, an Argonian village of huts with dock and lighthouse pieces, and the salt pans on the shore are an addition).
- *Flame.* **The Bad Shellfish** (Amalaja): a rice trader died after a meal of shellfish. The cook, a rival trader
  and the trader's wife are suspects; the rival bought nightshade from a Lilmoth herb-wife, and her stall remembers
  him (checked: Amalaja's source is Hjaalmarch Trading Post from Skyrim Trading Posts, four buildings with no interior, whose Nordic pieces are replaced by Argonian reed huts from Seekhat Yol, whose interiors give the seller's room; the Lilmoth herb-wife's stall is the apothecary of King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth).
- *Lantern.* **The Drowned Diver** (Lilmoth): a salvager was found drowned in the drowned quarter with his weights
  cut. His partner, a fence and his creditor are suspects; the partner's knife matches the cut cords, and the villa's silver lies in his boat (checked: Lilmoth's drowned quarter of Imperial villas is an addition of submerged ruin pieces that the place's record lists).
- *Lantern.* **The Boat beyond the Reef** (Karketa): a boat went out past the reef with a villager's father aboard
  and came back without him. The two crewmen and his son are suspects; one crewman's boat-hook has blood under its
  iron, and the son was mending nets in front of the village all night.

Danger climbs with the line: towns (D0), farms and wrecks (D2), ruins and ferries (D3), the old forts and
strongholds of the washes (D4) and, last, the dead village of Meris-Keth and Ra'tal Den (D5). Locks climb with it:
Novice locks in Act I, Adept and Expert locks in Act II, Master locks in Act III. Where the player may fight or
sneak, the journal says so. Every objective has a marker. Every far place is reached by the boats of Soulrest,
Lilmoth, Truowa, Archon, Blackrose and Mir-Xal and a short walk from the stop, and each case says which.

**Ranks and rewards.** Each is fixed and hand-made. The pay for a case is fixed gold and climbs from 50 gold in
quest 1 to 600 in quest 12, so the boats are paid for from the first case.

| Rank | Given after | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Wick | quest 1 | the Lampbearer's coat and hood (King of the Murkmire's fine marsh coat and hood, under our name), ten lockpicks, a bed in the Lamp-Hall |
| Flame | quest 4 | the Hearing Ring (Fortify Speech 15), and Marcella as an Expert Speech trainer |

### Act I: small crimes

**1. The Mate's Pay** (Soulrest, the old capital on the Topal Bay coast). Marcella signs the player on and gives them
a first case. A Khajiit ship's mate's pay chest was opened in his room at the harbour inn and emptied (checked: Soulrest's source is Skyrim's Solitude, whose inn interior with its rooms serves as the harbour inn and the crew's back room, and King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth gives a silver refinery that serves as the harbour smelter). The suspects
are the innkeeper's son, who owes money, the mate's shipmate, who quarrelled with him over a share, and the inn's
maid, who cleans the room. The journal says: "Search the mate's room at the harbour inn. Speak to people at the inn
and on the harbour." Markers lead to the room and to the harbour front.

- *In the room* the player looks at the emptied chest and finds a lockpick snapped off in its lock (a named thing:
  "snapped lockpick"). The journal: "Someone picked the chest, and broke a pick doing it."
- *At the inn* the innkeeper, asked about the night, says the room key never left his belt, which clears the maid.
  The mate says his shipmate sleeps in the crew's bunk room at the back of the inn.
- *In the bunk room* the shipmate's sea-chest has a Novice lock. The player picks it (Lockpicking) or comes in
  while the shipmate drinks in the front room (Sneak). Inside lie picks of the same make as the broken one. The
  journal: "The picks in the shipmate's sea-chest match the broken one."
- *On the harbour* the people the player asks send them to the smelter. The smelter's keeper says the innkeeper's
  son worked his night shift, if the player talks him round (Speech) or reads the shift tally nailed by his door,
  which names the son. The journal: "The innkeeper's son was at the smelter all night."

A new character with a lockpick can do all of it. *Leads on:* after the hearing, Marcella pays 50 gold, makes the
player a Wick and sends them to J'zahri at Truowa. The journal marks the Lampbearers' house on the quay, and
Soulrest's harbour boatman sails to Truowa.

**2. The Cloth on the Quay** (Truowa, the lake harbour on Lake Ulaish). Bolts of Lilmoth cloth have gone from a
warehouse on the quay. The council merchant who owned them blames Fort Gysilla's garrison, which has begun stopping
Truowa boats over the toll (the place's record). The suspects are the merchant, a garrison sergeant drinking in the
quay tavern and the quay's smuggler (checked: Truowa's source is King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth, whose taverna serves as the quay tavern and whose merchant manors serve as the merchant's house, while the warehouse and the smuggler's shed are made from the reed-pile quay buildings that the place's record lists as an addition). J'zahri's journal entry: "Search the warehouse on the quay. Ask in the quay
tavern. The merchant's house is on the council street." Markers lead to the warehouse, the tavern and the house.

- *The warehouse* has a Novice lock. The player picks it, or charms the watchman into opening it (Speech). Inside,
  the cloth's empty shelf and a cart track in the dust lead out to the smuggler's shed at the quay's end.
- *In the tavern* the keeper, asked, says the sergeant drank there all night. That is his alibi.
- *The smuggler's shed* is guarded by the smuggler himself. The player slips in while he sleeps (Sneak) or threatens
  him into opening it. The cloth lies inside, so the smuggler had it, and he says he paid for it.
- *The merchant's house* holds the receipt in his own hand for the cloth sold to the smuggler, in a desk the player
  reaches at night unseen (Sneak). The journal: "The merchant sold his own cloth to the smuggler and blamed the fort."

A player who freed Weeta-Lan in the Chainbreakers' 10, The Lantern on the Lake, has learnt from Weeta-Lan that the quay
smuggler keeps his goods in his shed at the quay's end. *Leads on:* J'zahri pays 100 gold and sends the player back
to Marcella, who has a case from the farms west of
Lilmoth. The journal marks Seeku Farm, a walk west from Lilmoth's harbour.

**3. The Missing Guar** (Seeku Farm, a guar farm west of Lilmoth; Lilmoth, the rebuilt stilt city). A guar has gone
from the farm's stockade, the second this season (checked: Seeku's source is Chillfurrow Farm from Skyrim, one farmhouse interior that serves as the low house, while the stockaded paddock and the barn are additions made from the reed fence and wall pieces of King of the Murkmire). Marcella teaches the player Calm before they
go: a stranger who rents the barn keeps a guard dog, and a Lampbearer calms a dog in place of killing it. The
suspects are the stranger in the barn, the farm's neighbour from Ixa-Ruu Farm, who quarrels over the boundary, and
the farm's hired hand, who has drink debts. The journal says: "Look over the farm and its barn. Ask in Lilmoth's
market who has sold guar." Markers lead to the farm and to Lilmoth's market walkways.

- *The barn* is behind the stranger's dog. The player calms it (Illusion) or kills it. Behind it the stranger hides
  only his sick kinswoman, who has the fever he feared the farm would turn out. The journal: "The stranger was
  hiding a sick woman, not a guar."
- *The neighbour*, asked at the farm's gate, says thieves took his own guar the same night, and the farmer confirms it.
- *The hired hand's bedroll* in the farmhouse holds a guar halter. The player searches it unseen while he works
  (Sneak). He keeps the stockade key, which the farmer tells the player when asked who opens the stockade.
- *In Lilmoth's market* the guar butcher remembers who sold him two guar, if the player pays him 20 gold or talks
  him round (Speech). He describes the hired hand.

*Leads on:* Marcella pays 100 gold and sends the player to a rice estate north-west of Lilmoth, where a man has died.

**4. The Walled Cellar** (Dunhallow Plantation, a rice estate north-west of Lilmoth). The family walled up the old
cellar years ago, with the planters' strongbox and the unrested Breton dead inside it (checked: the plantation's source is Cormar Crossing, a Dunmer farm from Journey to Baan Malur with five interiors, whose farmhouse serves as the great house and whose storehouse serves as the old cellar, behind a door that we wall up with placed boards and stone). Three
nights ago someone pried the boards loose and took the strongbox, and the family's old watchman was found dead at the
cellar door with his head broken. It is the player's first death. The suspects are a son of the rival family that
claims the estate's east fields, a tenant whose wife the watchman once had beaten, and the family's steward, who
owes a Lilmoth moneylender. The journal says: "Search the cellar and its door. Speak to the people of the estate."
Markers lead to the cellar door and the great house.

- *At the cellar door* the player finds splinters of the boards and a pry-mark, and inside the cellar the
  strongbox's dust ring on the floor. Two of the risen dead walk the cellar (the record's unrested dead). The player
  fights them or turns them (Restoration). The journal: "The thief came for the strongbox and knew where it stood."
- *In the great house* the family say the rival's son was at Lilmoth's night market that night, which a fish seller
  there confirms when asked. The tenant's neighbours say he sat up with his sick wife all night.
- *The steward's chest* in the great house has an Apprentice lock. The player picks it at night, or slips in by day
  while the family eats on the veranda (Sneak). Inside lie the planters' strongbox and a woodcutter's axe with board
  splinters in its edge. He set that night's watch himself. The journal: "The steward had the strongbox, and the axe
  that opened the cellar."

*Leads on:* Marcella pays 150 gold and offers the player the rank of Flame. She tells them what it costs (section 4).
Taking it shuts the other two doors. The next case is at Archon, and the journal marks the harbour; Soulrest's
boatman sails there.

### Act II: blood

From here the cases are killings. In quest 9 the structure begins to break.

**5. The Pilot's Bar** (Archon, the eastern harbour city; Wreck of the Tidewarden, a cog aground north of Archon). An
Archon pilot has been found drowned in the harbour. Marcella teaches the player Throw Noise before they go. The
suspects are the daughter of the Khajiit captain whose cog the pilot ran onto the bar years ago, a rival pilot, and
a fence on the lower wharves (checked: Archon's source is Journey to Baan Malur's waterside city of 162 interiors, whose dockhouses and general store serve the wharves and the fence's store, and King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth gives the stilt houses and the wharf sheds). The journal says: "Ask on Archon's wharves. Look over the fence's store on the lower
wharves. The Tidewarden lies on the beach north of the city." Markers lead to the wharves, the store and the wreck.

- *On the wharves* people say the captain's daughter swam in the harbour games that night, before twenty children,
  and that the rival pilot was out at sea.
- *The fence's store* is watched by a wharf watchman. The player draws him off with Throw Noise or waits for him to
  turn and slips past (Sneak). In the store is the pilot's knife, bloody (a named thing).
- *In the Tidewarden's hull* the captain's strongbox lies on the broken lower deck among mudcrabs (checked: the wreck's source is Pilgrims Trench from Skyrim, five wrecked ship hulls with no interior, so there is no ship's hold and the strongbox lies on the broken lower deck, open to the air). It has an Apprentice lock.
  Inside is the logbook that names the pilot and the fence's payments to him. The journal: "The fence paid the
  pilot to run ships aground, and the pilot wanted out."

*Leads on:* Marcella pays 250 gold and sends the player to Chasepoint, on the coast road east of Soulrest.

**6. The Lost Cart** (Chasepoint, a salvage and carting village east of Soulrest; Redgum Camp, a timber camp in the
Percar hills). A carter was found dead at the roadside and his cart is gone (checked: Chasepoint's source is Docks from Black Marsh & Valenwood, a town of 482 buildings whose ship pieces make the salvage yard and whose ship and cabin interiors serve as the inn and the yard smithy; Redgum Camp's source is Chillwater Mill from Wyrmstooth, a mill with lumber pieces whose one interior serves as the bunk house, and the log yard of red gum is added). The suspects are a logger at Redgum Camp
who lost money to him at dice, the carter's partner, who wanted the cart, and a salvager of Chasepoint, who wanted
its load of wreck brass. The journal says: "Ask in Chasepoint. Search the woods round Redgum Camp." Markers lead to
the village and to the camp's edge.

- *In Chasepoint* the partner says he was at the yard smithy all day, and the smith says the same.
- *At the camp's edge* the cart stands hidden among the red gums, with the salvager's two cousins beside it. The
  player fights them or slips past them by stealth and finds the brass fittings from the cart.
- *In the camp* the loggers' foreman will talk only if the player passes an Apprentice Speech check or shows him the
  brass fittings. He says the logger was on the saw that day, and that he saw the salvager's cousins come up the haul
  track with the cart.

*Leads on:* Marcella pays 300 gold and sends the player to Lujei, where a ferryman has gone missing; the boatman of
Mir-Xal sails nearest, and the journal marks the walk from there.

**7. The Ferryman** (Lujei, the shore village on the Blackrose channel; Lujei Ferry, the raft landing west of it).
The ferryman did not come home. The suspects are the island core's toll-taker, the ferryman's wife's kinsman and a
Blackrose boardwalk outlaw (checked: Lujei's source is Keeba Hollow from King of the Murkmire, an Argonian stilt village with nine interiors whose fisher's hut serves as the ferry families' cookhouse, and Lujei Ferry's source is Northshore Landing from Dragonborn, a dock of 24 buildings with no interior, with Argonian dock pieces from King of the Murkmire's Alten Meerhleel; the kinsman's house is one of Keeba Hollow's huts). The journal says: "Search the reeds round the ferry landing. Ask in Lujei." Markers lead
to the landing and the village.

- *In the reeds* by the landing lie the ferryman's body and a drowned traveller's purse. Marcella has taught the
  player Clairvoyance, and it leads them on from the body to the outlaw's hidden boat further in, where the purse's
  twin lies. The journal: "The outlaw robbed the traveller a day earlier, far from here."
- *In Lujei* people say the ferryman kept part of the toll. The toll-box in the ferry families' cookhouse is full,
  which clears the toll-taker. They say the kinsman owed the ferryman money and was on the raft that night.
- *The kinsman's house* has an Adept lock, and crocodiles lie in the channel between the house and the landing (the
  record's gar and crocodilians). In the house is his boat-hook, with blood under its iron.

*Leads on:* Marcella pays 350 gold and has a case in the burnt mines of the cape.

**8. The Burnt Cut** (Naxan, a logging village on the Soulrest coast; Ashfall Cut, a burnt open mine near it). A log-hauler of Naxan was found at the foot of the old vent shaft in Ashfall Cut (checked: Naxan's source is King of the Murkmire's Seekhat Yol, Argonian stilt houses with dock pieces, and its log landing is added; Ashfall Cut's source is Bilegulch Mine from Skyrim, whose one interior serves as the burned passages that the vent shaft leads down to, and the open cut, its gas vents and the grave goods at the shaft are added). He
went there for the grave goods that lie at the shaft. (Bramman's Brethren's 3, The Naxan Landing, robs the pay chest on the
same village's log landing.) The suspects are two men who went with him and a third who was not meant to be there.
The journal says: "Ask in Naxan who went to the cut. Search the cut and the vent shaft." Markers lead to the village
and the cut.

- *In Naxan* the men's kin name the two who went, and one says a third man followed them.
- *In the cut* the burnt overseer's corpse, risen, walks by the vent shaft (the record's fire-scarred undead). The
  player fights it or turns it with Restoration. At the shaft's top lies the second man's lamp, and in the cut a
  dropped knife marks the third man's way. In the first man's house in Naxan, his boots are scorched from the vent.
- *The third man* is in Naxan. Speech gets him to say what he saw, or his knife is enough.

*Leads on:* Marcella pays 400 gold, makes the player a Lantern and gives them the Truowa house. Her next case comes
from Mir-Xal.

**9. The Road Watcher** (Mir-Xal, the market town on the Soulrest-Blackrose road; Mudmoth, a ruined road station in
the washes). Travellers on the Soulrest road have been robbed and one killed, and Mir-Xal's toll-man swears it is
outlaws from Mudmoth (checked: Mir-Xal's source is the walled town of Fort Frostmoth from Bloodmoon Dungeons Restored, whose two interiors serve as the toll house and the guards' quarters, and King of the Murkmire's Alten Meerhleel gives the market house and a lodging house that serve as the stage inn). The journal says: "Ask in Mir-Xal. Search the road ditches south of the town." Markers lead to
the town and the road. A witness at the stage inn describes the robbers. A bloody toll token lies in a ditch by the
road. As the player takes the token, the toll-man learns that the Lampbearer is close. The journal marks Mudmoth for
the third clue. When the player reaches it, he is already there with the outlaws he pays, and they attack. Marcella
taught Fury before the case. Fury turns the outlaws on each other. The player can also fight through them, or shoot
down at them from the broken tower (checked: Mudmoth's source is Old Laboratory from King of the Murkmire, an Imperial ruin on the water with one interior that serves as the cellars where the outlaws sleep, and its outside already has a tower piece and ruin walls for the broken stone tower). The toll-man's body carries his share and a key to his Mir-Xal house, where the rest of the travellers'
things lie. There is no hearing: the culprit is dead or he yields, and the journal says what the Lampbearers then do.
*Leads on:* Marcella pays 450 gold and has a man who must be brought in alive.

**10. Come Quietly** (Fort Sallowmoth, a ruined fort on the Karketa-Lilmoth road). The case is solved before it
begins. A Lilmoth boat-builder strangled his partner, and the evidence is plain. He has fled to the squatters of
Fort Sallowmoth. Marcella wants him alive. The squatters' lookout stands on the north wall and the smugglers camp in
the yard (both in the place's record). The journal says the player may fight or go quietly, and the marker leads to
the fort's upper hall. In the armoury there (checked: Sallowmoth's source is Fort Hraggstad from Skyrim, an Imperial fort with two interiors, of which the upper hall serves as the barracks and armoury and the lower hall as the cellar) he speaks first. A hard Speech check (60) brings him to come quietly: the screen fades
and he is gone to Soulrest. If the check fails, or the player does not try, he attacks. A dead man pays the
Lampbearers nothing, so the reward is half of 500 gold. *Leads on:* Marcella has three deaths that look the same.

### Act III: the Eldest

**11. The Clean Kills** (Tarak Shan, a stormed Argonian stronghold east of the washes; Soulrest). Three men of the
washes died this year, each with one cut to the throat and nothing taken. One died under a fallen roof at Tailfall
Mound and one in his boat; the last died at Tarak Shan, where the dead men met. The journal says: "Search the halls
of Tarak Shan." The marker leads to the ruin. A feather-serpent dens in the great hall (checked: Tarak Shan's source is Teeth of Sithis from King of the Murkmire, an Argonian ruin of tiered halls with three interiors, of which the halls interior serves as the serpent's den and the two xanmeer interiors lie past it). The
player kills it, or slips past it, to the west xanmeer, where the last man died. There they find a hatchling's
practice blade, a brass chain link and the dead men's sale-notes. The notes say they sold Shadow-born hatchlings to
"the rice buyer of Lilmoth" and carried them on "the Silt-Orrel boats". Back in Soulrest, Teesh-Vaal tells the player
that the Shadowscales are gone, and the journal records that she would not look at the link. The player can make her
speak: an Expert Speech check (75), or showing her the link, the blade and the notes together. She admits that she
was a Shadowscale, that the order lives and that its Eldest is Ukka-Teel. She called Veezara the last to keep the
hatchlings from the Dark Brotherhood. She will not hear any case against them. *Leads on:* the next morning a runner from
Lilmoth brings word that Orrel-Sakka is dead. Marcella takes the case herself, pays 500 gold and sends the player to
Silt-Orrel, a walk north of Lilmoth's harbour.

**12. The Night at Silt-Orrel** (Silt-Orrel Estate, a rice estate north of Lilmoth; Lilmoth). The grand case. Two
Lampbearers and the family's guards watch the estate. Orrel-Sakka was found at dawn on his own boat landing, by the
woven reed post, and the family carried him into the planter's house (checked: Silt-Orrel's source is Cormar Crossing, a Dunmer farm from Journey to Baan Malur whose farmhouse interior serves as the planter's house and whose storehouse interior serves as the cellar with the salvage chest; the landing and its reed post are added from King of the Murkmire's dock and reed pieces). There are four suspects. Orrel-Sakka's cousin is back from his
service in an Archwarden fort and wants a share of the boats. Lucan Vetrix paid gold for the hatchlings that the boats carried. A thief robbed the estate that
night. The fourth is the killer. The journal says: "Look at the
body in the planter's house. Search the landing and the storehouse. Speak to the people of the estate." Markers lead
to the house, the landing and the storehouse.

- *The body*: one cut to the throat, and in the dead man's hand a torn brass chain whose links match the one from
  Tarak Shan. The medallion it held is gone. The journal: "The chain matches the link from Tarak Shan."
- *The landing*: blood on the boards by the reed post. Nothing else.
- *The storehouse*: its lock was picked, and the salvage chest inside stands empty. A thief was here that night.
- *The people*: the guard at the guardhouse says the master went down to the landing alone at midnight to meet a
  boat, as he did each dark of the moons, and that he heard the cry, and only then a door at the storehouse. The
  journal: "The thief came out after the killing, not before." The cook says the cousin went to Lilmoth to dice.
- *In Lilmoth* the keeper of the stilt-hall tavern says the cousin diced there till dawn. Vetrix's room there has an
  Expert lock. The player picks it, or lifts the key from the keeper (Pickpocket). In it lies the Listener's letter to
  Vetrix, which names the hatchlings he buys and the Silt-Orrel boats that carry them. A boatman at Lilmoth's harbour,
  asked, says he rowed Vetrix to the landing that night, and that they found the master already dead and rowed away.

The structure breaks again: Teesh-Vaal will not hear it, and the medallion is gone. Marcella makes the player a
Beacon and pays 600 gold. *Leads on:* the stolen salvage silver turned up with the boardwalk fence at Blackrose.
The journal says: "Ask the fence at Blackrose who sold him the silver." The fence names an Eelskin who went to
ground at Meris-Keth, for 200 gold or an Expert Speech check. The journal marks the dead village.

**13. The Witness in the Dead Village** (Meris-Keth, an abandoned stilt village of the Banujha Basin). Khajiit
moon-sugar smugglers store their goods in the dry halls, and the unstaked dead rise from the water under the
walkways (both in the place's record). Drips-From-Eaves hides in the headman's hall with the smugglers' captain
(checked: Meris-Keth's main source is an empty hamlet from Black Marsh & Valenwood with one Argonian house interior, which serves as the headman's hall; the tiers of stilt halls joined by walkways are added from the stilt huts of King of the Murkmire's Seekhat Yol). The journal says the player may fight
the smugglers or go quietly, and the marker leads to the hall. The player fights the smugglers, sneaks past them, or
buys their captain off for 500 gold. Drips-From-Eaves speaks first. He tells what he saw: a back on the landing, and
the brass in the dead man's hand, which he took and gave to Swims-Without-Ripples, his leader, as her share. As the
player leaves the hall, three Shadowscales wait on the walkways; they followed the same trail. Marcella taught Mayhem
before the case, and it sets the Shadowscales and the smugglers on each other. The player can also fight, or swim
away under the walkways. A dead Shadowscale's satchel holds a brass medallion of their own and a note in the
Eldest's sign: "The trial at Ra'tal Den, when the moons are dark." *Leads on:* Marcella says the last case is the
player's to finish, and the choice of how to finish it falls in the next quest.

**14. The Eldest** (Ra'tal Den, a Lilmothiit ruin of the Banujha Basin; Soulrest; Lilmoth, the Eelskins' hall under

the Eldest to confess (Speech 90); if he will not, he offers the frame himself, Marcella then offers the deal with

## 6. The last case, step by step, and its endings

**Step 1: Ra'tal Den.** Marcella says: "Teesh-Vaal has sent word to him under the old truce of the ku-vastei. He will
speak with you, once." The journal says: "Go to Ra'tal Den and speak with the Eldest." The marker leads to the den,
a walk west from Blackrose's landing through the Banujha Basin. While the truce holds, the Shadowscales' novices in the den
do not attack the player; the den's mudcrabs still do (checked: Ra'tal Den's source is Haj-Uxith from King of the Murkmire, a small Argonian ruin whose one interior serves as the dome's passages and hearth chamber, and the mudcrabs are placed in it). The marker then leads to the hearth
chamber. Ukka-Teel stands apart from his novices by the hearth and speaks first. He says why he killed: Orrel-Sakka
was selling the order's children to the Brotherhood.

**Step 2: the confession.** If the player holds the torn chain and has Speech 90, a line asks him to confess. He
confesses if the player promises that the Lampbearers will not hunt his hatchlings. The screen fades, and he is
gone to Soulrest. The line ends in the confession ending (below).

**Step 3: a failed check, or none tried.** Ukka-Teel refuses, and makes his own offer at once, since the player is
standing before him: "The thief's woman has my brass. Take it back from her strongbox and bring it here. Then tell
your Marcella that you found this in the same box." He gives the player Orrel-Sakka's council ring, which he took from
the body. "She will be named for it, and your case is closed. My order will owe you, and your Lampbearers will let
us work." The truce holds while the player leaves. The journal says: "The Eldest will not confess. He offers a
bargain: steal his brass from Swims-Without-Ripples, bring it to him, and tell Marcella that his ring lay in her
strongbox. Report to Marcella." The marker leads to the Lamp-Hall.

**Step 4: Marcella's deal.** In the Lamp-Hall Marcella hears the report. She does not hear of the ring unless the
player tells her, and a player who tells her ends the frame (the journal says so). She says: "Then we need the brass,
and we need the thief to say before Teesh-Vaal where it came from. Swims-Without-Ripples will want a price. Pay it."
The journal says: "Go to the Eelskins' hall under Pusbottom and get the Eldest's brass." The marker leads to the hatch
on Pusbottom's walkways (checked: Lilmoth's source is King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth, whose sewers have a hideout room that serves as the cellars under Pusbottom, entered through a hatch door that we set on the walkways). Marcella gives the player the hatch's key, which a Lampbearer took from a
thief last year.

**Step 5: the Eelskins' hall.** Behind the hatch, the Eelskins in the cisterns let the player through to the hall,
since Swims has sent word that the Lampbearer may come; a player who attacks them ends that leave, and they fight.
Swims-Without-Ripples speaks first and names her price for the brass and her word before the arbiter: the
Lampbearers drop every case against the Eelskins and take no new one. The player either:

- *agrees*: she gives the brass and her promise to speak at the hearing. The journal: "Take the brass to Marcella."
  This is the deal; or
- *refuses, or says nothing*, and takes the brass from her strongbox (a Master lock, or her key by Pickpocket), or
  from her body if they fight. A stolen brass is no proof, since no thief will say where it came from. The journal:
  "Take the brass to the Eldest." This is the frame.

**Step 6: where the brass goes.**

- *The deal.* The player gives the brass to Marcella. She sends two Lampbearers ahead to Ra'tal Den's mouth, where
  they are standing when the player arrives. With them, the player goes in and takes the Eldest in the hearth
  chamber. The truce is over; he and his three novices fight to the death.
- *The frame.* The player gives the brass to Ukka-Teel at Ra'tal Den, then shows Marcella the council ring and says
  it lay in the Eelskins' strongbox. She believes it, because the ring is real. The screen fades. The Lampbearers
  raid the hall and take Swims-Without-Ripples, and Teesh-Vaal gives her twenty years of the Owing for the murder.

**The confession.**

- *It gives:* the Lampbearers' highest rank and its reward, the Lamp of Truth (an amulet: Fortify Speech 25 and
  Illusion spells cost 25% less). Teesh-Vaal as a Master Illusion trainer. Neither rival turns hostile, since the
  Eldest's hatchlings are spared and the Eelskins were left alone. The Shadowscales' novices and the Eelskins'
  fences trade with the player.
- *It costs:* nothing in enmity, but only a player who has built Speech to 90 can take it. No faction owes the
  player a favour, and nobody holds the Eldest's brass to the order's account.

**The deal with the Eelskins.**

- *It gives:* Lamp-Master and the Lamp of Truth. The Eldest's own blade, the Hook-and-Eye (a dagger that absorbs
  health), from his body. The Eelskins' fences in Lilmoth, Blackrose and Archon buy from the player at the best price
  and never report a theft. Teesh-Vaal stays as arbiter.
- *It costs:* every Shadowscale attacks the player on sight. The Lampbearers take no case against an Eelskin from now
  on, so the Eelskins' small thefts in the towns go on, and Marcella's three paymasters cut her pay: the case pay for
  the line's repeating cases falls by half.

**The frame.**

- *It gives:* Lamp-Master, the Lamp of Truth and the Shadowscales' gift, the Quiet Cloak (Fortify Sneak 25 and
  Muffle). The Shadowscales trade with the player and never take a contract on them.
- *It costs:* every Eelskin attacks on sight. Their fences close. Teesh-Vaal, who knows the truth, will not sit for
  the Lampbearers again, so the Lampbearers lose their Master Illusion trainer. The player has sent an innocent woman
  to the Owing for twenty years, and the journal says so.

## 7. Rivals

- **The Eelskins** ([thieves](thieves.md)), the thieves of the south. Their leader is Swims-Without-Ripples, in their
  hall in the drowned cellars under Pusbottom in Lilmoth; their outpost is Corso Delling's hulk at the Blackrose
  boardwalks. In this line they appear as the thief at Silt-Orrel, the witness at Meris-Keth and the holders of the
  brass.
- **The Shadowscales** ([shadowscales](shadowscales.md)), the hidden order of assassins. Their Eldest is Ukka-Teel,
  who trains the novices at Ra'tal Den; their handler Hulee-Tah meets the order's people at the old ku-vastei
  Beem-Xal's house in Archon, beside the sealed school; their branch is Black-Tongue Brews in Lilmoth. In this line
  they appear as the clean kills, the killers at Meris-Keth and the Eldest.
- Each of the three lines tells its own late story. Only the leaders, the seats and the rivalries are shared.

### What it asks of the ceiling

Two blocks that the owner approved, each proven once before its first use:

- **The hearing after a fade.** When the player names the fourth answer, the screen fades. The player is moved to the
  hearing room, where the suspect is switched on. Teesh-Vaal speaks first. The simplest build: a fade, the player
  moved to a marker in the hearing room, the suspect's second placement switched on there.
- **The arrest after a fade** (quests 10 and 14, the confession and the frame's raid). A line ends in a fade, after
  which the person is switched off. The simplest build: the same fade, with the switch made while the screen is black.

The rest is inside the ceiling. Each answer to the handler is a line gated on a named thing held or a stage reached,
and it stores a number. Teesh-Vaal's verdict is a line gated on those numbers. A fine is gold taken. A wrong answer
starts the rank's next spare case, adds one to a stored count, and puts the player out of the Lampbearers when it
reaches three. The truce at Ra'tal Den and at the Eelskins' hall is a stage during which those people are not hostile;
attacking them sets the next stage. How the brass was got (given or stolen) is a stored number, and it gates which
person takes it.
