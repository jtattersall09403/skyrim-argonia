# The Night-Reed: the line's story

The story of the Night-Reed's six jobs, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the province plan gives the line ([province-plan](province-plan.md) §3.6, §4, §7). A quest writer turns each
outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations: **D** is the digest of the new world ([province](../../world/province.md) and the region
files beside it; "D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth" is that place's record or hooks in that region's file). Lore is
`file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is `extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own
extrapolation from the cited data and lore (decision 0032). The places are not built yet: a place is named by what
its record says it is, and every physical thing an outline relies on beyond that is a row of section 4.

**Words used below.** *Hist*: the tree a people is hatched under, named under and buried by. *The Owing*: the native
sentence, months of owed work instead of a cell. *The Wardens*: the thin successor state, which lives on tolls at
crossings and customs at ports and on nothing else (*state*:704 to :707). *Archein*: the old assimilated landholding
families who garrison the Wardens' forts; they kept Imperial names. *Pusbottom*: the oldest and lowest quarter of
Lilmoth. *4E 48*: the year Lilmoth's tree called down the flying city Umbriel and the town died; nobody alive saw
it. *A night-reed*: a reed cut by night from a bank somebody else claims, the delta's old word for a thing taken
from a toll-taker, and for the one who takes it (X). *The pot*: the house's common store, poured out to its own
(X). *A pledge*: a thing left with a lender or a toll-taker against a debt.

## 1. The faction in a page

**What they want.** The Night-Reed are thieves, and say so. They steal for gain and for each other. Lilmoth is
"commercially strong and openly hostile to the successor state" (D province, Peoples: Lilmoth), the Wardens hold
Southwatch Keep and Fort Reedmoth on its islands "against Lilmoth's wishes" (D althii-archipelago, the present),
and a ship that anchors below Southwatch pays the keep before it pays the city (D althii-archipelago, Southwatch
Keep). The merchant council cannot rob the Wardens. Its thieves can, and the town loves them for it more than is
good for them.

**Who they are and where.** A house of perhaps twenty in Pusbottom, canon's "ancient, gangrenous heart of Lilmoth",
where "the criminal underground operates" and where the old ruling party's enemies once hid
(`lilmoth.md:46` to `:47`; `topics/guilds-and-orders.md:182`), "repopulated, because slums always are"
(`lilmoth.md:109`). It is native, a house a Cyrodilic guild would know as a peer and never a branch of one
(`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:170` to `:195`). It sits in a den under a pawnshop on the lowest walk (D
althii-archipelago, Lilmoth: "Pusbottom's thieves"; "Fence: Pusbottom fence who buys stolen goods"). Its people:

- **Tilts-The-Jug**, the keeper (woman). She keeps the pawnshop, the door, the pot and the code. She is dry in both
  senses: she has never taken a drink, and people who hear her name think otherwise, which suits her. The name
  comes with the office (section 2).
- **Half-Rent**, who finds the work (woman). She plans every job and has never paid full price for anything,
  including, people say, her name. Generous, quick and funny; she pays the fines of the young ones out of her own
  share. Her egg-brother died in Southwatch's cells over an unpaid toll (X).
- **Bent-Spine**, the fence (man), in the den's inner room: slow, exact, and paying a little less than he quotes.
- **Silt**, the young climber (woman): fast, charming, sure she is the best hand in the house. She is not a traitor
  and never becomes one.
- **Sails-By-Morning**, the fence on Archon's lower wharves (woman, late fifties; D nushara-bayou, Archon: "Fence
  on the lower wharves"). She belongs to no house. The goods sail by morning; she has not left her den in years.

**How they work (X).** Two habits make the play.

- *Three tellings.* The house never goes on one person's word. Before a job a hand hears it from three people, and
  the house's saying is that one will be right, one will be right about last year, and one will remember half. The
  fourth telling is the place itself. The habit takes a different form in each job (section 2), and in one job
  there is nobody to ask at all.
- *Three thirds.* A take is split in three: the hand's third, the house's third (Half-Rent's, for tools, bribes and
  the next job) and the pot's third (Tilts-The-Jug's, poured out through the pawnshop to Pusbottom's hungry and to
  the kin of any hand who is caught).

**The code: three cuts a night-reed does not make (X, from the old design's "skill, restraint, no blackmail").**

1. *No blood.* A job that needs a death was badly planned. A robber who draws first is not a mark, and the house
   does not weep for bandits.
2. *Not from the hungry.* Rob those who take a toll and those grown fat on one. Never a working boat, a tool, an
   egg-basket or a naming gift.
3. *No sold silences.* What a hand learns in a house is not for sale: not to its owner, not to a magistrate, not
   to anyone.

And one debt: *a job taken is finished, and the client gets the thing that was named.*

**Who stands against them, and why each side is arguable.**

- **Southwatch Keep** and its captain, **Cassia Deemeel** (X; an Archein-line officer: D althii-archipelago,
  Southwatch Keep). Her eight keep a beacon lit on a reef coast, and the toll buys the oil and feeds nine families
  who hatch and bury at Lilmoth like everyone else. She "pays little attention to Lilmoth's politics" and cannot
  see why the town that sells her fish would sooner she drowned.
- **The Wardens' customs at Archon**, under **Receiver Hadrus Otheel** (X). The strongroom in the East Empire
  Company's old buildings is the state's one real purse (D nushara-bayou, Archon; *state*:704). His case: without
  it there is no state at all, and the last time there was none, the tree at Lilmoth ruled.
- **Lilmoth's merchant council**, whose guards "patrol the streets" (`lilmoth.md:63`). The councillor who matters
  is **Meer-Tulo**, master of a third of the harbour's lighters, who stands on the upper walks and can be spoken
  to (X; the plan gives this line the naming of Lilmoth's council). His case, said when he is faced after the
  turn: the town was killed by the party whose heirs now tax it, twice on every hull, and a council that cannot
  fight must pay those who can.
- **The Lamp-Bearers** of Stormhold, by trade: their cases begin where the house's jobs end
  ([lamp-bearers](lamp-bearers.md)). A thief calls a lamp a magistrate's dog.
- **Reed-Sail's back-water factor at Uluo**, who also buys stolen goods (D althii-archipelago, Uluo) and thinks
  whoever moves a thing owns the trade in it.

**Neighbours.** The Umbriel Witness sit at the edge of the drowned quarter and live, as the house does, off what
old Lilmoth left (province-plan §3.13). The house thinks they sell grief; they think the house sells the dead's
spoons. The Chainbreakers of Blackrose get a thief's respect and no more: "Fine hands. But nobody fences a person."
The retired Shadowscale who trains on the walks (named in [main](main.md)) has never come down the stair, and the
house will not be led by anyone who serves a writ.

**What it pays and what it costs.** The hand's third in coin, fixed per job, and Tilts-The-Jug's gifts: picks and
plain rings and gloves enchanted for Lockpicking, Pickpocket and Sneak (existing forms). Bent-Spine buys from any
member. A hand caught in Lilmoth owes as any thief owes under native law, and the pot keeps their kin. A hand
caught at a fort or at Archon sits in a Warden cell, since those keep foreign law (D althii-archipelago, Southwatch
Keep; D nushara-bayou, Archon). The Lamp-Bearers will not raise a senior Night-Reed to Long Lamp, and the house
will not raise a Long Lamp past its third rank (province-plan §4).

**What it lies about.** The house says the war on the Wardens is its own: thieves robbing toll-takers because
toll-takers are fair game. It is not its own (section 2), and one of the three cuts is being made for it at one
remove.

**What is unsettled about them.**

1. The pawnshop lends to the same poor the pot feeds, at the usual rate. Tilts-The-Jug sees no contradiction.
2. The toll-takers they rob are island families. A stolen pay chest is nine households' season, and the keeper
   says so plainly and takes it anyway.
3. The town's love is the house's safety, and it is bought with a share of what is stolen from the town's enemies.
   Take the enemy away and the house is only thieves.
4. The question the line asks is what a thieves' house owes the city it lives off. Half-Rent says service.
   Tilts-The-Jug says restraint, and the pot. Sails-By-Morning says nothing at all: be gone by morning.

**Why a player wants in.** Every job is a break-in with a different shape, heard three ways and done the player's
own way; the marks deserve it, mostly; the house is good company; and the last job is the province's treasury.

**The build leaning.** Lockpicking, Pickpocket and Sneak are in every job, because every prize is behind a lock,
under somebody's eye, and its key is on somebody's belt. Speech is the second way in every job: a keeper has a
price, a shame or a reason. Alteration and Illusion have a small honest part: two jobs have a way in under water
(Alteration's water-breathing, or the sap of Lilmoth's young tree and the blessing of Archon's, which give breath
to any race: D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth; D nushara-bayou, Archon; Silt says where both are before the first of
those jobs), and a quiet spell is as good as quiet feet. A fighter can finish the line, since the smugglers of
NR03 and the bandits of NR05 draw first and may be met with a weapon, but the house's best gifts go to the hand
that leaves nobody dead. Nothing is levelled to the player (decision 0035). No quest shuts out a talker; a player
with none of the three thief skills will find the line hard, and that is its nature.

**Ranks.** Lookout (after NR01), Cutpurse (NR02), Water-Rat (NR04), Night-Reed (NR05, from the keeper's hand: the
house's own name for a hand it trusts with the truth; refused to a Long Lamp, with her reason). There is no rank
of leader.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** In order:

1. Old Pusbottom hid the ruling party's enemies (`lilmoth.md:46` to `:47`) and kept a common pot: a great clay jug
   of coin, marked with a reed cut slantwise, poured out by an officer called the jug-tilter. In 4E 48 the tree
   killed the town (`lilmoth.md:92` to `:96`).
2. In the first generation after, a lookout's line of the old house dug the jug out of the drowned heart, found no
   house left to give it to, and settled an empty planter's shell north of the town with the salvage (D
   althii-archipelago, Silt-Orrel Estate: "salvaging beams and fittings from the drowned Imperial quarter"; its
   hook: "the family will not say whose it was"). A third of the jug bought boats. The rest stayed sealed in the
   cellar, and each landholder has told only the heir.
3. The house grew again in repopulated Pusbottom with a new pot, the old code and the old office. The keeper is
   still called Tilts-The-Jug; the present one took the name from her mother.
4. Some years ago Councillor Meer-Tulo, whose lighters pay Southwatch's toll on every ship they serve, began to pay
   Half-Rent. The house would rob only the purses he named, all of them the Wardens', and the council's guards
   would keep off the lowest walk. The house thinks the war on the Wardens is its own idea.
5. Two years ago he asked for more, and she gave it. The house's third now goes up the island road, through the
   bandits' dealer (D althii-archipelago, Fort Lowmoth: "the bandits' dealer"), to the band in the burnt shell of
   Fort Lowmoth, who pen Fort Reedmoth's patrols and take rice from Sal-Axan and Tal-Neesh Farm (D, Fort Lowmoth,
   people). A smaller part pays a garrison man at Southwatch to put the beacon out on chosen nights (D, Southwatch
   Keep: "the beacon lamp is found put out by hand"), so that ships strike, the keep looks useless and captains stop
   paying. Sailors have drowned. So the first cut is made at one remove, and the second: the hungry of the island
   road are robbed with the house's money.
6. That garrison man was **Kud-Lai** of Silt-Orrel, home this season with coin he cannot explain (D, Silt-Orrel
   Estate: "a cousin who left for the Warden fort has come back wanting a share of the boats").
7. Half-Rent also dips the pot's third. Tilts-The-Jug knows the pot is light and suspects Bent-Spine, who pays less
   than he quotes because Half-Rent takes her part before the goods reach him.

Half-Rent believes all of it is right. A house that only steals is a tick on the town; a house that breaks the
forts is worth feeding. She does not tell the keeper because the keeper would call it blood.

**NR03, No Key on the Shore.** East along the shore from Lilmoth stands the Company's old duty shed, with "a bonded
strongbox bolted to the shed floor that no key on the shore fits" and a smugglers' cache in the sea-cave below (D
althii-archipelago, Brackwater Waystation). The smugglers attack on sight and are Dangerous; Half-Rent and Silt
both say so first, and Silt says where breath for deep water is to be had. Half-Rent has three clients for one
box, and each has named one thing in it (kept from the older design's drowned lockbox): Councillor Meer-Tulo's
factor wants "the city's silver plate, taken in bond"; a Khajiit sloop-master wants "his own cargo seal"; a
fisher-woman of Nundi wants "her grandfather's pledge, a steering paddle with his knots". The three tellings are
theirs. The factor is right about the lock: a Master lock of Company make, and nobody living can cut a Company
key. The sloop-master is right about last year: the smugglers have moved from the shed to the cave since. The
fisher-woman half remembers that the last customs man never left, and that the tower came down on a night of
storm. What is learnt only on the spot: bones named Customs Man's Bones lie in the tower's rubble among the
snakes, and the key is on them. The ways: the key from the bones, or the lock picked; the shed reached past the
smugglers' one lookout by Sneak, by a quiet spell or by a fight, or by a trapdoor in its floor from the cave's
pool, a swim on held breath or water-breathing in which nothing hostile is met. The three things are named for
what they are: a Silver Plate with a Family's Mark, a Warden Customs Seal, a Carved Paddle. Bent-Spine, shown
each, says in a line what a fence sees: the plate was a family's and never the city's; the seal is the Wardens'
own, and a sloop that carries it passes its own cargo; the paddle is new wood cut to look old, because the pledge
was redeemed long ago and the family wants it twice. The debt says the client gets the thing named. Each thing
goes to its claimant, to another, to Bent-Spine for his fixed price or stays in the pack, each a line gated on
the thing held that sets a number; the job ends at Half-Rent's line "The box is settled", open once the box has
been opened, and Tilts-The-Jug says afterwards what the house thinks of each hand-over. The factor is served
first in Half-Rent's telling, and she is short with a player who gives his plate elsewhere. The sloop-master,
refused his seal, says: "Then learn what every sloop on this coast knows. Boats under a purple stone are not to be
robbed." (The rumour the Lamp-Bearers' line asks for; this line never explains it.) Bent-Spine quotes ninety for
the plate and pays sixty: "The planner's third comes off before I see it." Retold: three liars, a dead man's key,
and a customs seal in a thief's pocket.

**NR04, Pay Day at Southwatch.** The keep "takes a toll on ships that anchor below it" and its gate stores sell to
anyone (D althii-archipelago, Southwatch Keep), so a stranger may walk into the yard as a customer, and that walk
is the third telling. The garrison's season of pay has come by boat from Archon and sits in the keep in one great
sack under a lead seal, the Garrison Pay Sack. The spoken tellings: Kud-Lai, if he lives (right: four years in the
keep; the pay is in the room under the beacon stair and the captain carries its key); and Silt (half: "there is
a way up from the landing; I think it comes out in a store"). Two ways in: the gate, among men who know every
face, or the landing below and the stair in the rock, whose door has an Adept lock. The pay room's door has an
Expert lock and its key is on Captain Deemeel. Geem-Shah of NR01 sits by that door (a second placing of him,
switched for the first while the player takes the job in the den), and greets the player by how NR01 went. He can
be persuaded to open the door himself, a line that unlocks it: at Speech 40 if the player did right by him in
NR01, at Speech 65 if not. Before any of it the captain can be spoken to as a traveller, and she is the line's
antagonist met with her kindness first: she sells the player lamp oil at cost; she says the lamp has been found
put out by hand four times since her last lamp-man went home to Silt-Orrel; and she says Meer-Tulo's lighters
paid no toll that week and she cannot tell why. Half-Rent takes the sack whole, "to melt the seal off", and pays
the hand's third at once, 300 gold, as she always has. Her line also switches the world while the player stands
in the den: Renoit is gone from the harbour; a wrecked coaster's timbers lie on the reef below the keep; and on
the shingle is a body that can be searched, named Yves Renoit. The beacon was dark again. Afterwards the gate
stores' trader is gone from the yard, and the Karketa fishers at the gate say they have not been paid. Retold:
robbing the lighthouse, and then seeing what the dark does.

**NR05, The House's Third (the turn).** Tilts-The-Jug sends the player, in the pawnshop, where Half-Rent cannot
hear. She says what every player must know before going: the pot is light; a man who came to the house for help
lies drowned under a dark beacon; island silver with Southwatch's lead on it is being spent on the island road;
and there is nobody to ask this time, because anyone she asked would tell the one she suspects. She suspects
Bent-Spine. She names two places to look.

- *Bent-Spine's lockbox* in the den's inner room (Adept; his key on him): it holds melted lead and a few of
  Southwatch's seals, and no coin. A player may accuse him to her on that. This is the line's misjudgement.
  Accused, he is gone from the den (her line, spoken in the pawnshop, switches him off below), the house has no
  fence, and it was wrong. A player who later tells her so can have him back, at the price of his greeting.
- *"The one strongroom on the island I cannot see into"*: the iron door in the burnt shell of Fort Lowmoth, which
  "the fire did not reach" (D althii-archipelago, Fort Lowmoth). The band is Dangerous and attacks on sight; she
  and the dealer both say so; the keep's garrison "will not patrol its walls". The bandits' dealer stands apart on
  the island road and does not attack, since he buys from anyone. In: by Sneak through "gaps in the burnt floors
  [that] open onto the cellar", by a quiet spell, or by a fight a strong player can win; the iron door by the
  leader's key (lifted from him unseen, or taken from his body) or its Expert lock. Inside: a sack under
  Southwatch's seal with the same name as the one the player carried, crates of new steel, and casks of lamp oil.

Taking the sack moves the job. The dealer, spoken to with the sack held, says the rest in three sentences: who
sends the silver, what it buys on the road and what it buys at the beacon. The player then tells Tilts-The-Jug,
which ends the job and gives the rank of Night-Reed, and may also face Half-Rent, Meer-Tulo and Kud-Lai, each a
line gated on the sack held or the job done, each setting a number, none changing a later stage. Half-Rent does
not deny it: "Renoit paid a toll-taker twice and came to thieves for justice. Ask yourself what kind of town that
is. I am ending it." Tilts-The-Jug does not throw her out: "She is the best planner this house has had. I want
the last job done. If the Wardens' silver comes up my stair and not down to her table, she has no war to pay
for. That is the only way I know to stop her that sheds nothing." So both women want the customs strongroom
robbed, each asks for the take, and the player knows why. Retold: opening a bandit's strongroom and finding the
sack you stole.

**NR06, The Wardens' Purse.** Archon, where a thief has no friends in the guard and the harbour has cells. The
strongroom is in the Company's old counting-house on the waterfront, with the customs post on the quay beside it
(D nushara-bayou, Archon).

*The tellings.* Sails-By-Morning, right: a quay door, a cage, a strongroom door, and no key to either inner lock
in the whole of Archon. They are Company locks, which nobody living can cut. The cage key went west with a
Receiver the Wardens dismissed and was never got back; the strongroom key rides with the paymaster on his round
of the ports, so that nobody at Archon holds the purse alone, as Lilmoth's tree has two minders. She serves a
known Night-Reed freely and adds her one rule: boats under a purple stone are not to be robbed. Half-Rent, right
about last year: she has the silver in the cage, and it was moved behind the strongroom door after a scare. A
child of the harbour who plays the underwater games (D, Archon: "underwater games a visitor can join"), half:
"there is a grille under the Company stairs; my brother got his head stuck in it."

*The preparations* (kept from the older design's "two of four"). Half-Rent names three, all open at once, any or
none, each a small job of a different kind with its own keeper. They are legs of this one job, and each result is
a thing held.

- *Soulrest: the cage key.* Lucan Teeus (X), the dismissed Receiver, lives alone in the "half-empty government
  quarter" (D cape-percar, Soulrest). An empty quarter is the reverse of a tavern: nobody to see, and nobody else
  for a watchman to look at. His door (Adept), his chest (Expert), or his word: he sells the key for 200 gold to
  anyone who says the present Receiver will be shamed by it.
- *Thorn: the strongroom key.* Paymaster Nerva Shulm (X) is at Thorn's dockside tavern (D arnesian-jungle, Thorn),
  in a crowd, beside the purse-cutter who trains there and will say for a price which side he wears it. The line's
  one pure Pickpocket masterpiece; the other way is his clerk, who for Speech 60 says which room he sleeps in and
  where he hangs his coat.
- *Fort Sallowmoth: the commander's sword.* "A locked cage in the armoury still holds an Imperial officer's sword"
  (D althii-archipelago, Fort Sallowmoth). It was the last Legion commander's, and Receiver Otheel is of his line
  and ashamed of how it was left (X). The fort is Deadly, its squatters attack on sight, and three people say so.
  It is for a player who is strong or very quiet. Shown the sword, the Receiver takes it and unlocks the quay
  door himself.

*The ways in.* The quay door (Expert, in sight of the customs post; or unlocked by the Receiver for the sword);
the clerks' side door (Adept, in sight of the quay); the grille under the water stairs (Expert; a swim to a
flooded dock with a dry floor inside, nothing hostile in the water). *The two locks.* The cage (its key, or
Expert) and the strongroom door (its key, or Master). A player who made no preparation can still do it all on
skill. The customs men are the engine's guards, of the Dangerous band: seen, the player is a thief in a town with
cells. Nobody must die and nothing is fought for; who is dead afterwards is read by Tilts-The-Jug.

Behind the last lock are two named things: **the Customs Silver**, the season's bars in one strapped coffer, and
on a shelf of pledges, the things the customs took where a toll could not be paid (a steering paddle, an
egg-basket), **the Bell of Renoit's Coaster**. The state turns out to be a pawnbroker too. Taking the silver
moves the job. The bell is a small second choice: Tilts-The-Jug will send it to his crew, Half-Rent will melt it,
or the player keeps it.

*The ending is the silver's hand-over*, three lines in three places, each gated on the coffer held:

- **To Half-Rent, in the den: the war.** Her case: one season of the Wardens' own silver hires enough spears to
  take both forts, and the chain comes off the strait for good. What the other side winning would cost, in her
  words: "a pot poured out a cup at a time for ever, to people who stay poor." Her line switches, away from the
  den: Tilts-The-Jug gone from the pawnshop, and the pot with her; council guards on the lowest walk in new steel;
  more of the band at Lowmoth. Everyone on the islands says the forts will not see another season; the forts are
  not seen to fall (province-plan §4). Meer-Tulo, on the upper walks, sells the player the stilt house there (D,
  Lilmoth: "for sale to a trusted buyer") for a third of its price.
- **To Tilts-The-Jug, in the pawnshop: the pot.** Her case: the house owes the town one thing, to take only from
  those who can bear it, and a house that hires killers has nothing left to be but a worse fort. What Half-Rent
  winning would cost: "Renoit, again, every dark night." Her line switches Half-Rent off in the den below. The
  old jug, wherever it went, is spoken of: she will open a pot when Pusbottom is hungry and not before. Silt, in
  the den, tells the player to go up and look at the shelves, and her line empties them: every pledge sent home
  for nothing. The council's guards walk the lowest walk again. The forts stand and the tolls go on.
  Tilts-The-Jug gives her own ring and says that somebody must find the work now, and that she would sooner it
  were the player. It is said, and it is not a rank: the house has no leader, and now no protection.
- **To Sails-By-Morning, at Archon: gone by morning.** Her case: a thief owes a town nothing but to be out of it
  by first light, and both women in Lilmoth are building a government. She pays a fixed 3,000 gold. Her line
  locks the den's door and sets the player's rank to none; both patrons greet with one line each; the house goes
  on as it was, arming Lowmoth, since nothing was settled. The line can be left here, rich.

Whoever is given the silver, the Receiver, if he lives, has a different greeting afterwards: the Wardens will pay
their forts late this season or not at all, and he says who goes hungry first. Retold: no key in the whole city, a
grille under the stairs, and then standing in the province's treasury with a drowned man's bell on the shelf,
deciding what a thief is for.

**The hub reacts.** The den's greetings and the pawnshop's topics change at each rank and with each stored number
(who holds the old jug, who was served at Brackwater, whether Bent-Spine was accused, whom the player told at the
turn). Earlier choices never change a later job's stages. **Left at any rank** the world is whole: the house
steals, the forts stand, the beacon is sometimes dark.

## 3. The first two jobs

Common to both.

- **Tilts-The-Jug** stands in the pawnshop and **Half-Rent** in the den below it for the whole line. Either one's
  death fails any running job of the line with the faction's message. Nobody is made unkillable. People stand
  where they are placed; a person met in two places is two placings, switched while the player is elsewhere.
- **One working stage, one objective.** After the telling, each job sits at one working stage with one objective,
  whose markers are on the prize's keeper and on every person whose line can move the job; the journal names the
  tellers. What a teller says is a line and nothing more: no teller must be heard, and they can be heard in any
  order or not at all.
- **A take is a fact.** Taking the prize sets one stage, open from the job's hidden start. Every line that needs a
  thing checks that it is held, however and whenever it was got. A player who holds a prize before the job is
  given says so in a line and goes straight to the hand-in, with an entry that claims nothing they did not do.
  The hand-in lines are gated on membership as well, so nothing is handed in by a stranger.
- **A death moves no stage.** Lines check who is alive, and each job-giving line has a second form for a mark who
  is already dead.
- **Being seen is the game's own business.** No line or stage checks it. A theft from an owned chest is a theft
  with a bounty if seen; a bungled pickpocket turns the mark on the player; the job stays open.
- Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense and say only what the player has just done. Skill
  thresholds are fixed. Each failed Speech check has its own written refusal and leaves another way open. Things
  use existing forms. "Night" in anyone's mouth is their word only; no step depends on the hour.

### NR01. The Second Toll

**What a new player is told, and when.** **Silt** stands on Lilmoth's harbour landing, where the lighters set
passengers down (D althii-archipelago, Lilmoth: "Boat stop: lighter-men at the harbour"), and speaks first to a
newcomer: she has watched them come ashore, says they look at a quay the way she does, and says that
Tilts-The-Jug at the pawnshop on the lowest walk of Pusbottom buys anything and sometimes has quiet work. The
player knows the shop by the name on its door, **Tilts-The-Jug's Pledges**, and by the sign over it, a tilted jug.
A player who never meets Silt can walk into the shop, which trades with anyone, and ask the same question.

In the shop, before any job, Tilts-The-Jug has four optional lines of two or three sentences each: *the
Night-Reed* (what a night-reed is; "we rob the people who tax the water"); *the three cuts* (the code, in three
short sentences); *the thirds* (the hand's, the house's, the pot's, and what the pot is for); *why you, and why
you might not* ("Nobody on these walks knows your face. And a thief caught in Lilmoth owes for it in months, not
coin: the Owing. The pot will feed your kin; it will not work your months."). "Not yet" stays open and free. Then
the job, in five sentences. A Breton coaster-master, Yves Renoit, anchored below Southwatch Keep, the Wardens'
fort on the point, and paid its toll. The keep's toll-taker, Geem-Shah, came aboard and took it a second time.
Renoit came to the house. Geem-Shah is in Lilmoth this week, sleeping at the stilt-hall tavern, with the keep's
toll purse under its lead seal: bring the sealed purse, and touch nothing else of his. "It will cost him. A keep's
purse always costs the one who carried it. That is what the purse is for."

**The hidden truth.** Geem-Shah's clutch-child is to be named at Lilmoth's tree, as the garrison's all are (D,
Southwatch Keep: "the garrison hatches its young and buries its dead at Lilmoth"). He had no gift for the naming.
So he took Renoit's toll twice: the first into the keep's purse, as the law has it, and the second into his own.
Renoit saw one purse and believes both tolls went into it. Geem-Shah came to Lilmoth with the keep's week of
tolls, sealed, to buy its stores, and with Renoit's eighty in a plain purse on his belt. He has already spent some
of it on a small silver charm. The sealed purse is locked in the chest in his room, beside the basket for the
naming, because it is not his and he is afraid of it; the chest's key, the plain purse and the charm are on him.
So the purse the house wants is the keep's lawful money, the client's money is somewhere else, and the only thing
in the matter that was stolen for love is the one thing the code forbids taking. Tilts-The-Jug does not know
there are two purses. Renoit also says, when asked how he came to anchor so close, that the beacon was dark that
night.

### NR02. The Jug in the Cellar

**What a new player is told, and when.** The first time down the stair, **Half-Rent** has three optional lines:
*who finds the work* ("Tilts keeps the door. I keep us busy."); *the forts* (Southwatch on the point and Reedmoth

the old coin, which no fort has ever stamped, and she wants the thing itself: whoever holds the old jug can say
they are the house. She has not mentioned the job to the keeper.

## 5. Read on paper

**Round one.** Three fresh readers on one version: a player who loves the lore and reads every word; an impatient
sneak-thief who skims and wants to be doing something; and a deliverability reader with the ceiling, the quest
form and the whole document. The two players were given NR01 and NR02 a sheet at a time, as a player meets them,
and the rest of the line in summary; they were never shown the hidden truth. That version had seven jobs.

**What they said.**

- *The lore player* talked to every mark, served the captain and left the keep's purse, showed the token to the
  landholder and gave the jug to the keeper. They cared from the first job ("the code is taught in step 2 and
  examined in step 6, with no lecture between"), called the landholder's question about the keeper's name the
  best scene, and found Half-Rent's defence at the turn "the best a villain has had in a guild line". Their
  faults: the best-paid ending of NR01 hurts the toll-taker most and nobody remarked on it; Renoit's false belief
  was never corrected; in NR02 the token let them skip the burglary altogether, and the most interesting branch
  sat behind a Speech gate; the jug was paid "from the first pour" and then never poured; Half-Rent was flagged
  as suspect so early and so often that the turn was no surprise; her taking all the pay sacks was "so loud" that
  they wanted to refuse; the tools job was "padding" after the turn and gave no reason to go on working her plan;
  and the councillor was named and never faced.
- *The impatient player* went by the locks both times, never spoke to a mark, and so "skipped the story": no
  child, no naming gift, no account of the jug. They would have pocketed the charm "without a thought", and did
  not know whether the family's coin was fair game or whether they ought to have shown the token. They felt
  "slightly tricked" that the keeper ordered the keep's purse and the game then seemed to frown on it. They missed
  both beacon clues, which were spoken by people they would not stop for. They liked Brackwater, the keep and
  the last lock best, would skip the tools job as "three fetches in three towns", noticed the three-tellers shape
  wearing by the fourth job, and wanted something for the pot ending. Both would join, both gave the silver to
  the keeper, both hesitated at Half-Rent's answer, and both retold the bell on the pledge shelf.
- *The deliverability reader* found the skeleton buildable and eleven things the form would refuse, the chief of
  them: a line that gave a chest's contents less one item; one key held by two people, and locks at Archon whose
  keys were said to be on officers there and also abroad; a line that was open before an ending and after it;
  objectives that left out people whose lines move the job; a Speech threshold lowered by a number, and a man who
  walked off to count his stores; things switched in the speaker's own room; a dead man who was also standing on
  the harbour; several named things that were really three or ten; a label on a prompt that no line checked; and
  a journal entry that claimed a purse taken. It also listed places where the story spoke of prices, stock and
  leadership, which the form does not hold, and needs with no row.

**What changed, as one set.**

1. *The story reaches the hand that never talks.* The chest in NR01 now holds a Naming Basket beside the purse,
   and the charm is named for what it is. The keeper says before the job that the purse will cost its carrier and
   says so again when she takes it, so the keep's purse is fair game with its price known, and nothing frowns
   afterwards. Kud-Lai tells a creeping player that the old man knows their mark. The family's coin is in a box
   of its own, and the keeper takes it back from a hand that lifted it, by name of the cut. The wreck, the
   keeper's fear spoken at NR05's start and the sack in the strongroom carry the turn for a player who heard
   nobody.
2. *Half-Rent is harder to see coming.* Her line about the guards is gone from NR02 and the keeper's aside is
   about the family, not about her. She pays the hand's third at Southwatch as always and keeps the sack "to melt
   the seal off". The council's coin is seeded through the captain and the factor instead.
3. *The word no longer skips the cellar.* A persuaded landholder hands over his keys and makes the player go down
   and lift the jug; a failed persuasion leaves every other way open, and says so.
4. *The jug is not poured, and that is argued.* The keeper pays from the pot the house has, keeps the old one
   sealed, and admits Half-Rent's jibe may be right.
5. *Six jobs, not seven.* The three tools are now optional preparations inside the last job, each a small job of
   its own kind, so that nothing stands between the turn and its consequence. The rank of Night-Reed is given at
   the turn. The keeper says why she wants the last job done: it is her only bloodless way to stop the war.
6. *The councillor stands on the upper walks* and can be faced after the turn; the factor is his.
7. *The tellings change form* job by job: three people, a teller who sends the hand to the guards, three clients
   who lie, a yard walked as a customer, nobody at all, and three at Archon.
8. *Inside the form.* One key to a lock and one carrier: Kud-Lai unlocks the cellar by a line, and Archon's two
   inner keys are held abroad because nobody can cut a Company key, which also makes the Receiver's shame and the
   reason for the sword. The pay and the silver are each one named thing. Renoit, Geem-Shah and Silt have two
   placings each, switched from another room. Every switch is made by a line spoken somewhere else. The proofs at
   Brackwater are in the things' names and the fence's mouth. Speech at the keep is two fixed thresholds chosen
   by a number, and its result is an unlocked door. Objectives carry every person who can move the job. The
   lines that outlive an ending are separate lines with no stage. Prices and stock are gone: the fence pays less
   than he quotes in fixed sums, and a shut shop is a trader switched off. Leading is a thing said, not a rank.
9. *Section 4* gained the rows the reader found missing, and each says how it is known on sight.

stone stays unexplained, since it is another line's secret. The pot ending still pays no share: the keeper's ring

- Whether a player who goes by the locks now meets the naming in NR01 and the cuts in NR02 without being told to
