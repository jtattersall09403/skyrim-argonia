# The Sunken Archive: the line's story

**Status: to be rewritten.** This story rests on facts of the main quest's earlier plot that [the plot](main-plot.md) no longer holds; it is to be rewritten to the plot and is not yet. What in it is untrue, by section:

- Section 2 (The line's arc): it makes Xoh-Reesha, stake-keeper of Jal-Veesa, a woman the main quest names and may leave dead, but the plot's person at Jal-Veesa is Vosh-Leera, the root-herald, and it has no Xoh-Reesha.
- Section 4 (What each quest needs of its places): it stands SA06's leave at Jal-Veesa on Xoh-Reesha as the main quest has her, which the plot does not.
- Section 6 (For the other lines): it keeps SA06 away from Nenria, Gemin, Vel-Sapha Stand and Ushaan Camp as the main quest's places, but the plot uses Nenria, Vel-Sapha Stand and Gemin for its second, third and fifteenth quests and has no Ushaan Camp.

The story of the Sunken Archive's six quests and their two small riders, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the [province plan](province-plan.md) gives the line (its section 3.11, its brief in section 7, the crossings
and exclusions of section 4). A quest writer turns each full outline in section 3 into a record without inventing
anything.

How to read the citations. **D** is the digest of the new world: "D okuwun-rahei, Yulca" is that place's record or
hooks in [okuwun-rahei](../../world/regions/okuwun-rahei.md); "D province, Peoples" is a section of
[province](../../world/province.md). Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`. **X** marks this document's own
extrapolation, drawn from the cited data and lore and never from nothing (decision 0032). No place of this world is
built yet, so a place is named by what its record says it is, and every physical thing an outline relies on that the
record does not promise is a row of section 4.

**Words used below**, for a reader new to the east. *Agaceph*: the needle-faced, bright-scaled raft people of the
eastern lakes, the one people of Black Marsh who keep books. *Hist*: the sentient trees every Argonian is hatched
under. *Sick ground*: what the Hist peoples call the ruins of the dead peoples, which they ring with ward-stakes and
will not enter. *Velothi* (in full, Cantemiric Velothi): grey elves from the north, cousins of the Dunmer, who built
in black stone along the east and died out in the Knahaten Flu. *Ayleid*: the older elves who built in white stone
and kept Argonians as slaves. *The Stilling*: the Agaceph's name for the spell with which they freeze an intruder.
*The reed people*: the people of Jal-Veesa in the far north-west, whose stake-keepers guard the Ayleid ruins there.
*The Archwardens*, or the Wardens: the state; no people of this line is called a warden.

## 1. The faction in a page

**What they want.** Three peoples built in stone in the east of Black Marsh, and all three are dead: the Barsaebic
Ayleids, the Cantemiric Velothi and the priests of the Maran missions (`topics/lost-peoples.md:94`, `:119`;
`regions/middle-argonia.md:80`). Their halls stand half sunk in the marsh with their books, tablets and worked
stones still in them. Every Hist people calls those halls sick ground. The Agaceph call them the sunken archive:
shelves nobody has reached yet. Their saying is "We borrow. The dead are slow to ask for their books back."

**Who they are.** The Agaceph "read, keep books taken from dead missions, and are accomplished illusionists" (D
province, Peoples: Agaceph; `tribes.md:92`). Their one town is Yulca, a town of lashed rafts on a wide lake, held by
"a council of readers": five elders who sit together in the book hall (D okuwun-rahei, Yulca). They are no guild
and wear no robes: magic in Black Marsh is "folk literacy, not a profession" (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:35`), and
the readers are raft folk who happen to have a library, a scholars' enchanting table and the patience to teach for
coin (D okuwun-rahei, Yulca: gives). The eldest reader is **Yaxil-Ca** (X), dry, courteous and hard to surprise.
The readers do not go into the halls themselves. They send a **Borrower**: a pair of hands from outside who can get
past what guards a hall and bring out what is in it. The last Borrower, Ocuil, has not come back, which is why the
hall wants the player.

**How they work, in the line's own skills.** A dead hall is guarded three ways, and a school of magic answers each.

- *What was set to guard it*: wards that throw frost or fire at whoever comes near, doors that open to one stone or
  one tablet, water over the stair. Alteration answers these (Telekinesis lifts a ward's stone off its stand from out
  of reach, or draws a key off its base; Waterbreathing makes a long dive easy), with Mark and Recall, which Magic
  Expanded brings and which are a Borrower's way out of a hall whose stair has fallen in. Enchanting is the hall's
  third craft: a ward on a stand is a bound stone, and an enchanter can say what it holds, what it is worth and what a
  weave of marks is set to do; the stones a Borrower lifts are the charge the readers' table runs on.
- *The dead who never left*: Restoration turns them and wards the Borrower through a ward's fire. Conjuration
  brings help into a fight too big for one.
- *The living who got there first or stand in the way*: Illusion, the Agaceph's own school, sets squatters on each
  other, calms a watchman or walks past him unseen.

Mysticism has no skill number of its own in this game: its spells sit under Skyrim's five schools
(`docs/research/mods/lane-1.12-magic.md`, "Schools in Wind UI"). So Mysticism is played by casting its spells, and
every check a person makes in talk names Alteration, Conjuration, Restoration, Illusion or Enchanting. A quest
never checks that a spell was cast: a ward, a gap, deep water and the dead are built so that a school answers them,
and each also has a plain way (a lock picked, a fight, a run through the fire) that costs more.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **The Hist peoples**, who hold the halls to be sick ground and are right about what walks in them. The Fenlords of
  the Ayleids had their Argonian slaves "executed and raised as undead guardians" (`topics/lost-peoples.md:108`);
  the reed people's stakes round a ruin are "set facing in" (D barsaebica, Mirasel). To them a Borrower is a
  looter with better manners. Reader Hecal has a saying for it (X) which is hers and no people's, and which the line
  neither proves nor needs: sick ground is ground where Argonians were owned ([consistency](consistency.md) T15).
- **Tedryn Orvayn** (X), the "visiting Dunmer scholar [who] wants to buy the secret of the Agaceph's freezing
  spell" (D okuwun-rahei, Yulca: hooks), of Stormhold's Dunmer, who "keep their ancestors" (D valley-of-storms,
  Stormhold: faith). He holds that the Velothi halls are his people's graves, lying near a thousand years without a
  rite, and that he is the only living person asking after them.
- **The buyers.** The Synod has no hall in the province and works "through buyers, commissions and third parties",
  hunting "artefacts of great magical power" (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:80`, `:100`). Its buyer here is **Livia
  Semprone** (X), who pays for stone that still holds a charge and says so plainly. The College of Whispers
  "summons Daedra and practises necromancy" (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:106`); its scholar **Maelle Jurard** (X),
  who answers to the College's Praeceptor in the south, Hadrian Scaeva ([consistency](consistency.md) 4.4;
  [umbriel-witness](umbriel-witness.md) section 1), offers the readers a way to ask the dead directly, which tempts
  people who love books and horrifies everyone
  else.
- **The hall itself.** Reader **Ocan** (X) wants the buyers' coin, pupils who pay and a hall with stone under
  it: a school. Reader **Hecal** (X), whose pupil Ocuil was, wants the borrowing stopped and thinks the reed people
  are right. The old
  question of whether to become an institution at all (province plan 3.11) is these two, with Yaxil-Ca between
  them.

**What it pays, and what joining costs.** The hall pays in what it has, and in a mages' line that is spells, lasting
abilities and what a table can make: a Borrower's tools, given before the job that needs them (a tome of Telekinesis,
tomes of Mark and Recall, a ring of waterbreathing); the stores of the Borrower's Shelf; the use of its enchanting
table; and at each ending something taught that can be had no other way, up to the hall's own spell, the Stilling,
which it has never given an outsider (section 2, the ranks; each outline's table of endings). The cost: the halls are
Dangerous and Deadly ground (D okuwun-rahei, "What
ranges it"), fixed and never levelled to the player, and a Borrower is unwelcome wherever a Hist people keeps the
stakes. This is a line for a player who has grown.

**What it lies about.** The Agaceph say they came to empty halls and took what the dead had no more use for. They
did not come to Sethiral after its people died (section 2).

**The build leaning.** Alteration, Illusion and Enchanting ([consistency](consistency.md) 7.2), with Restoration as a
way through every hall of the dead and Conjuration as a gift or two. Enchanting is checked in talk in SA02, SA03
(twice), SA04 and SA05, is raised by four of the line's outcomes, and is what the lifted stones are for. A thief
(Sneak, Lockpicking) and a fighter can finish every quest by the plain ways, and each
plain way is paid: what a fighter kills leaves a hall safe to strip, and what a thief opens by the lock leaves its
wards standing. Speech is for the living claimants, and no quest is won by talk alone.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** Sethiral and Anvadas were two holdings of one Cantemiric Velothi kin (D okuwun-rahei,
Sethiral, Anvadas). The kin kept needle-faced lake people as bond-servants: boatmen, door-wards and copyists. They
taught them letters to copy the house's books, and one spell, with which a door-ward held a stranger still at the
threshold until the master came. When the Knahaten Flu reached the east (`black-marsh-province.md:79`) the kin at
Sethiral sickened and the bond-servants did not. The lord took his household down to the shrine in the lower hall,
which "served a Daedric power" (D okuwun-rahei, Sethiral: faith), and bargained for forty lives. The price asked was
five of the house's servants. The door-ward held her master still on the stair with his own spell; five servants
shut the lower door on the whole household, living and dying, and wove the spell into its bar. That weave is what
hums. They left on the house's rafts with the house's books, and they carved the five marks of the weave on the
prow of the first raft, with a bearing to the place in the reeds where they sank the lord's family tablet, which is
the only thing that opens the kin's vault at Anvadas. In the vault lie the bond-tablets by which the house held
them: the first five names the Agaceph of Yulca ever had, written by their owners.

The founders swore two things: the door stays shut, and the hall writes nothing of its own. The Agaceph have kept
other people's books ever since and never written one. Each eldest reader is told the whole of it by the last, and
each has sent outsiders into the halls so that no Agaceph would stand in front of a servant's collar cut for a
needle neck and know it. The Stilling, the tribe's pride, is a house-servant's spell. Their reading is a
copyist's. "They find the Hist loud" (D okuwun-rahei, Yulca: faith) because their founders were raised in a stone
house away from any tree.

Ocuil, the last Borrower and the first Agaceph to hold the post in living memory, traced the prow's carving as
every Yulca child does and saw that it was a bearing. He followed it to the drowned stones off Gaweja's point and
died there with the lord's tablet in his satchel. Yaxil-Ca knows the door's hum has changed and does not know what
that means, and she will not ask four other readers to help her re-weave a ward whose reason she would have to tell
them.

Earlier choices never change a later quest's stages. They change greetings, single lines and which gifts are given,
read from stored numbers. Only the finale carries into the world after the line. The later quests are given at arc
level; each will carry its sketch when it becomes a full outline. Their outcome tables are fixed here so that the
whole line's gifts can be seen side by side: the hall's signature spell is given in three forms across the last
three quests, and no player can hold all three without paying for each.

**SA04, The Humming Door.** *Place:* Sethiral, the black keep whose upper halls Yina's Agaceph sit and read in, and
whose lower halls are "shut behind a humming door" (D okuwun-rahei, Sethiral). *What the player is told:* Yina's
eldest, Ayaca, wants the door opened "without waking what lies behind" (D okuwun-rahei, Yina: hooks), because the
hum has changed; Yaxil-Ca, who has refused her for years, now tells the player to open it and to tell her alone
what is there. *What the player does:* the door answers the five marks, said with Ayaca at the door. There are four
ways to have them, each a line of Ayaca's that moves the quest to the quiet stage: Tedryn's reading, if he took his
rubbing in SA02 and lives (`sa02.copied`); the weave as the player and Ocan read it in SA02 (`sa02.weave`), or read
now off the door's own bar (Enchanting 60); the Agaceph's own school, which holds the marks to be five holds of one
illusion (Illusion 50); or Ayaca's guess, bought with Speech 40. Each failed check has its own refusal. The quiet
stage unlocks the door and switches the household's dead off in the lower hall, which is a separate inside; the
player walks a silent hall. Otherwise the door is a Master lock, and behind it the household's dead are awake:
skeletal guards and ancestor ghosts on Deadly ground (D okuwun-rahei, Sethiral: dangers), for Restoration, a
summoned ally or a weapon. Either way the player finds the inner face of the door scored by hands, the household's
dead lying at the door and not in their beds, and at the shrine five collars cut for needle necks. The lord stands
at the shrine and speaks first; he is not placed hostile. He takes the player for the door-ward's hand, come near a
thousand years late with the five; he tells the house's side in his own words (they were fed, lettered and of the
household; five for forty was a fair price), names the Stilling as his house's door-spell, and asks to be let up
the stair. He says what he will give for it before the player answers. *The turn:* Yaxil-Ca, told, says it
plainly: "We did not come to empty halls. We emptied one." Everything before reads differently: the kind truth she
shelved in SA01, the prow, her quiet at Joxhi, the rank of Copyist, and why a people of illusionists hires
strangers. *Skills:* Illusion or Enchanting first; Speech; Restoration, Conjuration or a weapon for the loud way;
Lockpicking (Master) for the door. *Carries on:* `sa04.lord` (held, ended, loosed) and a number for what the player
knows. The College's scholar arrives at Yulca's guest raft after this quest, with her offer and the news of a book.

*The choice at the shrine, and what each answer gives.* Ayaca says the first and third before the player goes
down; the lord says the second and third himself.

**SA05, The Right Voice.** *Places:* the drowned stones off Gaweja's point, where "nets bring up worked stones"
and "an Agaceph rafter has gone missing and the fishers found his raft empty" (D okuwun-rahei, Gaweja: hooks); then
Anvadas, whose "sealed vault door opens only to the right family tablet", carried off "into the marsh" (D
okuwun-rahei, Anvadas: hooks). *What the player does:* reads the prow's bearing with Yaxil-Ca, now that both know
what it is, and follows it to the stones: a dive to a sunk stone coffer on a drowned stair, crocodiles and
slaughterfish in the shallows (D okuwun-rahei, Gaweja: gives), under a ward on a stand that the founders set over it
as the Ayleids did at Joxhi. The lord's family tablet is in the coffer, where the founders sank it. Beside the coffer
lies Ocuil's satchel, which holds what his body would (a body cannot be placed): his own slate, scratched under water
in his last hour. He never got the lid up: the ward held him at it, and an Argonian does not drown. Hecal is the one
to whom the slate is carried, and with it in hand she tells what her
pupil worked out about the founders' locks (free to a player with Enchanting 40, who can follow it, and otherwise
two scrolls of a ward spell for the asking). On the bank waits the "stranger [who] digs the shallows by night", a
digger in Tedryn's pay, who is not placed hostile: he is paid off, talked round, walked past under Illusion, or
attacked by the player's choice. Then Anvadas, on Deadly ground: the drowned courtyard and its crocodiles, the
skeletal guards, and in the tower the household's steward, a ghost (D okuwun-rahei, Anvadas: gives, where the
record calls him its "warden ghost"). The Oxhu say "the tablets still answer the right voice" (D okuwun-rahei,
Anvadas: faith): the steward is not placed hostile. He stands and speaks first. To a player holding the lord's
tablet he asks in whose name they come and answers what he is asked; to one without it his only answer is a
refusal, and a player who presses him picks the fight. The lord's tablet is the vault's key. In the vault is a
small stone coffer with the five bond-tablets in it, one clay bundle that can be lifted out unread, and beside it
the vault's well, an open shaft of black water.

*The choice, made at the vault by the player's own act* ([consistency](consistency.md) S12): the bundle is read
there, or it is let fall into the well unread. Reading it closes the well: what has been read cannot be sunk.
Three people have argued the choice before the player ever reaches the vault, and none of them is handed
anything. Yaxil-Ca wants the five sunk where nobody will read them, and says what she will give for it. Hecal
wants them read, so that the first names the tribe ever had are said aloud at the next hatching, and says what
that will cost the player at the hall. Tedryn asks nothing of the choice. He wants only the kin's own tablets, which
nobody else does: "they were of the household, and I would sooner my cousins' house had heirs than none". The vault
stays open whatever is done, and if he lives he is found there afterwards among them (a second placement, switched on
by the report).

**SA06, A Book That Should Not Exist.** *Place:* Gandranen, "a library of magical halls that draws books from any
time, and a Fenlord's tomb crawling with undead slave-guardians", with "a book that should not exist" on its
shelves (D barsaebica, Gandranen). The journey is the plan's: by boat to Truowa and Helstrom and by rootworm to
Jal-Veesa (D province, Roads); the line passes through Helstrom and does nothing there ([consistency](consistency.md)
2.2). *What the player is told:* Maelle Jurard has been as far as the library's wards and says a book stands there
in the Agaceph hand; the Agaceph have never written one. *The hard place.* The reed people guard the ruin as sick
ground (D province, Peoples: Sedge-Wardens), and their weir at Teel-Fen sits on the creek that leads to it, where
"strangers come asking the way to Gandranen" (D barsaebica, Teel-Fen Weir: hooks). A barred gate stands across the
creek path at the weir, and its weir-keeper, **Tuzeel-Ma** (X), can be spoken to. The player gets past by leave, by
stealth or by a gift (province plan, section 4, "Whose ruin"). Leave is asked of the stake-keeper of Jal-Veesa,
**Xoh-Reesha**, a woman the main quest names and may leave dead ([consistency](consistency.md) N18, C7): she gives
it on a promise to carry nothing out, and her line unlocks the gate; if she is dead that way is shut, and Tuzeel-Ma
says so. Stealth is the gate picked, or the creek swum round it under Sneak or Illusion. The gift is the one they
would ask of nobody: that the Fenlord be ended, because the guardians who walk his tomb are Argonian dead who never
got home to a tree (X on `topics/lost-peoples.md:108`; [consistency](consistency.md) 4.4, grave-stakes). Inside,
every guard a hall can have stands at once: the library's wards on their stands (D barsaebica, Gandranen: dangers)

## 3. The first three quests

Common to all three.

- **Yaxil-Ca** stands in the book hall at Yulca for the whole line. She must live: her death fails any running
  quest of the line with the faction's message. Nobody else is essential. People stand where they are placed.
- **Out of the hall in three sentences.** Each briefing is short and gives the place, the way and the marker
  itself. Nobody else has to be spoken to before the player goes in: every other person on the way is there for a
  key, a warning or a better price, and can be walked past.
- **One working stage.** What the player achieves is a thing held, a person dead or a stored number, so the ways
  through can be mixed, done in any order or abandoned. The lines that carry a result to the person waiting for it
  set the stages.
- **A take or a use writes a journal note and nothing more.** Each is open from the stage that first names its
  thing as a target, and that stage sets the note at once if the thing is already held. A line that needs a thing
  checks that it is held, however it was got. A wall or an altar used before the job was given must be used again.
- **A death moves no stage.** Lines check who is alive. Where a person can be dead, the outline gives the line
  that stands in for theirs, and its speaker is a target of that stage.
- **A person found somewhere else afterwards is two placements**: one switched off and one on, far from the line
  that does it. The line is gated on the first being alive, so the dead do not turn up again.
- **Spells are the player's own.** No line or stage checks that one was cast. A thing drawn across a gap by
  Telekinesis is a thing taken; a dead man turned is a dead man passed.
- **A school must reach what it is meant to answer.** The dead a quest means Restoration to turn, and the living
  it means Illusion to sway, stand at fixed levels that the school's Adept-rank spells reach in this load order
  (Skyrim's own reach is level 16 for Repel Undead and 20 for Pacify and Rout before perks). The levels below are
  set on that rule and the quest writer checks them against the load order before the record. One named opponent in
  each place stands above that reach on purpose and is a fight or a thing to slip past. Skyrim's restless dead
  ignore Illusion, so Illusion is the answer to the living and Restoration to the dead.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense
  and say only what the player has just done. Thresholds, levels and rewards are fixed numbers. Each failed check
  has its own written refusal and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms; nothing new is modelled.
- "Night" and "dark" in what people say are their words only; no step depends on the hour.
- Danger is warned of twice before each place, by a person and by something found.
- **A line moves a stage, and the stage does the rest.** Every gift, price, lesson and switch below is a stage's
  result ([buildable](../../../../.claude/skills/story-line/buildable.md) section 2), and each outline's sketch, under
  its stage table, is that table as the quest form will hold it. Something that can happen at any point of a job (a
  captive freed, a stone lifted) is a minor quest of its own beside it, a rider, so that the job's own stages stay
  few.
- **Every ending says what it gives and costs in play**
  ([choices](../../../../.claude/skills/quest-write/principles/choices.md)): a spell, an ability, a thing, a chest, a
  teacher, a door. A spell or ability is a load-order record taught under our name; its form key is given, and what
  each does at its fixed strength is one of the checks owed in section 4.

### SA01. The Lamp Below

**What a new player is told, and when.** Yulca's raft ferry sets a guest down at the lake landing (D okuwun-rahei,
Yulca: gives), and the keeper of the raft kitchen says the readers are short of a pair of hands. On first speaking
to Yaxil-Ca in the book hall, before any job, four optional lines, each two or three sentences:

- *the readers*: "Five of us keep this hall. Any fisher in this country can freeze a thief where he stands. We are
  the ones who wrote down how."
- *the dead halls*: who built in stone here and how each died: the white-stone elves, three thousand years gone,
  who kept our kind as slaves and left one hall in the far north-west "that is said to fetch its own books"; the
  grey elves, northern cousins of the Dunmer who settled this coast, dead of a flu near a thousand years ago "that
  never touched us"; the
  priests of Mara "of swamp rot, in a month". "The tree peoples call those halls sick ground and stake them round.
  We call them shelves."
- *borrowing*: what the readers want of the player and why them: "We read. We do not dig. We send someone who can
  get past what the dead left on guard, and you came across a lake of crocodiles to ask."
- *what it pays, and why I might not*: teaching and tomes at the readers' price, the table, a share; and "the last
  who went down for us was Ocuil, three seasons a Borrower. His raft was found empty. The tree peoples are right
  about what walks in those halls."

"Not yet" stays open and free. The job is three sentences and two gifts. A keeper of books at Hirecan, Xocin, wants
her forebear's bronze hand-lamp from the cell under the old chapel downriver of her hamlet: down the chapel's stair
into the water, through a short drowned passage, up to a dry landing and a barred grille, with the last priest
still behind it. The lamp is Xocin's; "and whatever else is in that cell comes to me unread by Hirecan. She will
ask for it. I am telling you now so that you cannot say you were not told." She hands over a tome of Telekinesis:
"A Borrower's first tool. There are bars." With it goes a draught of waterbreathing for the passage. She also names
the choice that is coming and its terms: "She will pay you in her coin and I in mine, and each of us will shut a door
on you for the other's."

**The hidden truth (X).** In the Maran missions' day three priests kept an outstation chapel downriver of where
Hirecan now stands; the missions' people "all died of swamp rot in the first month" and "left behind some excellent
books" (`regions/middle-argonia.md:80`). An Agaceph woman, Tuma, had hired on to carry their lamp. She nursed all
three. The last, Brother Aurel, taught her letters in his last weeks, gave her the chapel's books "that Mara's word
might have one reader in this place", and, when the rot reached his mind, made her lock him into the cell under
the chapel with the lamp burning and take the key away. He wrote all of it in his daybook, with the words of his
own people's rite for the dead inside its cover, wrapped it in oilskin and set it by the lamp. She could not yet
read well enough to say the rite. She never went back, kept the key and never said why. Her family tells the
tribe's joke instead: that she took her wage out of the priests' shelves when they were past objecting. The river
has since taken the lower floor. Brother Aurel is one of the restless dead, because no rite of his own people was
said over him (X; `topics/guilds-and-orders.md:121` for the rite outsiders expect). Hirecan's people still read
the mission books "aloud at hatchings" (D chixathe-mesa, Hirecan: faith) and think it comedy.

**The ways through.** The dive is movement and wins nothing. At the grille, any one of:

1. *Through the bars (Alteration).* Telekinesis draws the lamp, and then the daybook, off the ledge and through the
  bars. The priest is never let out and never fought.
   It goes wrong as the spell goes wrong: a thing dropped short lies inside the bars within his reach.
2. *The key (Restoration or Speech).* Xocin will not hand her family's key to someone who would leave that door
   open behind them. She gives it to a player who can put the dead down or hold them off (Restoration 40), who
   talks her round (Speech 30), or who leaves 100 gold with her. Refused: "My forebear locked that door from
   outside. Whatever her reason was, it has not gone away."
3. *The lock (Lockpicking).* Adept, with him at the bars while the pick is in.
4. *Once the grille is open:* Restoration's Turn Undead drives him into the far corner while the player takes what
   they came for; Sneak, or Illusion's Muffle and Invisibility, pass him, since he is one and the cell is small;
   Destruction, a weapon or a summoned ally end him.

*The words.* A player who has taken the daybook can climb back to the chapel's upper floor and use the altar. The
words are said (they are shown), and Brother Aurel lies down: he is switched off in the cell below, and his folded
robe and amulet are switched on where he stood, and the grille's lock opens. This is open until the lamp is handed
over, and the daybook's last
line and the journal both say where the words are to be said. It is the only way to the amulet and the coffer that
needs neither a fight nor the key held against him.

### SA02. The Founders' Prow

Offered once SA01 has ended.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Yaxil-Ca's briefing: the founders' carved raft-prow, which hung in this
hall since Yulca was five rafts, was stolen (D okuwun-rahei, Yulca: hooks) by a Skeith crew that caches at
Mazurath, the black sea-hold on the cliff by Khiar, in the sea-cave under it. She has that from one of the crew,
whom the readers stilled and still keep on the guest raft. "The cave is reached from the sea. The crew will not
climb to the hold's upper tier, where there are ghosts and a fire that still burns, and its floor is rotten over
their heads. Bring the prow straight back. It is not for reading on the way, least of all by the grey elf on my
guest raft, who has asked me about it four times." She gives tomes of Mark and Recall: "A Borrower comes home when
the stair does not." She does not say why a carved board matters so much, and says that she is not saying.

**The hidden truth (X).** Tedryn Orvayn, a Dunmer of Stormhold, where his people have kept their houses and their
ancestor stones through every change of rule (D valley-of-storms, Stormhold: faith), came to Yulca to buy the
Stilling and was refused. On the hall's wall he saw the prow and knew its five carved marks for the door-ward of a
Velothi house, because the same five are cut on the lintel of the shut door at Sethiral. He asked the readers four
times what a house's door-ward was doing on a raft people's first prow, and was given tea. Then he let it be known
along the coast that he would pay well for the carved board in the readers' hall. A Skeith crew that caches at
Mazurath, where "a smuggling crew uses the lower cave" (D okuwun-rahei, Mazurath), took it. Their captain, seeing
how badly it was wanted, tripled his price; Tedryn cannot pay it, and the prow sits in the crew's cache. The crew
never climb to the upper tier, where the household's ghosts walk and "the spell-fire on the upper tier still
burns" (D okuwun-rahei, Mazurath: faith); they keep "a Khiar fisherman ... captive on the upper tier" (D
okuwun-rahei, Mazurath: hooks), in a barred side room off the stair, below the fire and out of the ghosts' walk.
The upper tier's floor is rotten "over a drop" (D okuwun-rahei, Mazurath: dangers), and the drop is into the back
of the sea-cave, behind the crew, beside the cache.

**The ways through.** The player must reach the back of the sea-cave, take the prow and get out again.

1. *From the top (the Borrower's way: Alteration, Restoration, Mysticism).* The landward track reaches the upper
   tier. The spell-fire is answered by Telekinesis (its stone lifted off from out of its reach), by a ward spell

   The drop is one way, and he says so. The player who cast Mark on the cliff-top takes the prow and Recalls out
   behind the crew's backs.
2. *From the sea (Sneak, or a weapon).* The cave mouth is reached by swimming (D okuwun-rahei, Mazurath: dangers).

   side; a fighter takes four, the captain last and hardest, and has the cache for it.
3. *Illusion, from either end.* The three crew are living men within reach of Frenzy, Fear and Calm; Muffle and
   Invisibility pass all four. The captain is above that reach: a frenzied crew turns on him, and he is still the
   one fight or the one man to slip.

no Mark set and the crew between the player and the sea, a ghost that follows down to the fire.

shame that he let it be known he would pay for the board, having asked four times first. Then he asks to take a
rubbing of the marks before it goes back, and says what he gives for it and what it will cost the player with the
eldest (the choice below).

### SA03. Under the Clay

Offered once SA02 has ended.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Yaxil-Ca's briefing: Joxhi is a clay-diggers' village of the mound people,
a walk north-west of the market town of Inoja; its diggers "have broken through into a buried stone hall of old
Ayleid make and will not go back down; the elder wants someone to look" (D okuwun-rahei, Joxhi: hooks). The way in
is a breach in the west wall of the clay pit. "The clay people do not send to us. He has. Go and see what is under
his village." At Joxhi one person speaks first, in one breath, on the path to the pit: Livia Semprone. "You are
the readers' new hand. I buy stone that still holds a charge, and I pay more than they will. Ayleid ward-stones
face inward: three thousand years of holding something in. Bring me what you lift." Yaxil-Ca's briefing names her as
Ocan's buyer, and Ocan, asked on the landing before the player leaves, says what an inward stone is (free to
Enchanting 30; 60 gold otherwise, with two ward scrolls either way). So the choice is named before the player goes
down, and nobody else need be spoken to.

**The hidden truth (X).** The red seam at Joxhi was first dug under Ayleid owners, who "enslaved Argonians as
labourers" in this country (`topics/lost-peoples.md:99`); the buried hall was the overseers'. The slaves pressed a
small clay figure into the hall's walls for each of their dead, because they were allowed no stake and no tree:
a few by the pit-side wall of the first gallery, hundreds in the undercroft. When the Ayleids fell, the overseers
who died in the hall did not lie quiet, and the last living ones set the hall's ward-stones to face inward and
shut the inner door on them. The village that grew on the spoil kept digging and kept the custom: Joxhi's diggers
"press a clay figure into the pit wall for any who died in a fall" (D okuwun-rahei, Joxhi: faith) and believe that
"a hatchling of the first Inoja families found the red seam" (D okuwun-rahei, Joxhi: history). The four diggers
who broke through saw, by their own lamp, their own grave-figures in the wall of a place no digger of theirs had
ever reached. That is why they will not go back down. They told the elder only that there was white stone.

Two ward-stones stand on stands in the first gallery and hold the hall's dead in the galleries beyond. The key to
the inner door lies on the base of the nearer stand, under its stone and inside the ward's reach. A careful
Borrower draws the key off by spell and leaves both stones standing. A greedy or hurried one lifts a stone: the
ward dies, the key is theirs for the picking up, the stone in their pack is worth more than Joxhi's clay, and a hall
of the dead under a living village has one ward the fewer. The nearer stand's stone is the story's inward stone; the
further ward is the place's own and no quest reads it. What the nearer ward holds is not in sight: two more of the
overseers, walled in beyond the frost. When the stone goes up through the breach they are found in the pit ahead of
whoever carries it.

**The choices, and each side's honest case.** *The stone* is made with a hand out towards a stand, and it is about
power. Lifted, it is the quick way to the key and a thing of great worth; and it always costs, because what it held
comes up into Joxhi's pit ahead of whoever carries it out, and goes for the people there: the grave-singer who could
have taught the Naming Song, the buyer who would have paid, the spear. Whoever of them dies is dead for the rest of
the game. Ocan and Livia have both said so before the player
goes down ("facing in, it holds something in"), and Jo-Reesh has said what he would like to go on saying. Livia's
case for buying it: the stone is wasted under a clay pit, her house will learn from it what nobody here can, and
she has hidden nothing about what it does. Ocan's, for the table: what is studied at the readers' table stays in
the east, and he will teach what he learns from it. Jo-Reesh's, for the slip-pit: no buyer will ever come to
Joxhi for another. Left standing, it keeps the hall shut on its dead and leaves the player a small key to draw
out by spell, or an Expert lock. *The telling* is smaller and has no lie in it. Jo-Reesh asks for a season's
silence: his diggers are proud of the figures, half of them would leave a pit they knew for a slave-pit, and
Thorn's buyers "who offer too much" (D okuwun-rahei, Joxhi: present) would have the seam inside a year. Ixha-Lei,
with the figure in her hand: those are dead who were never named at any fire, they are older than the village and
have waited longer, and a custom is not smaller for having been begun by people who had nothing else. Each says
what the other will withhold.

  gift is the ability alone, sinking a lifted stone gives what the village can spare, and the loosed dead are above a
  turning spell's reach
   and are said to attack the three people at the pit, any of whom then stays dead; the two takers at Joxhi deal
   only with a stone that came up through the breach.
3. *The Fenlord is a choice with two prices.* The College's spell is for keeping him; ending him no longer mends
   a broken promise; the reed people's gift is a spell of the line's own school, the Weir-Walk, and no Archery.
4. *SA05 is one clear dive and one clear act.* The lord's tablet is in the coffer; Ocuil's satchel holds his
   slate; Tedryn's wish is not a side of the choice.
5. *Small truths.* The Flu is near a thousand years gone. Hecal asks after Ocuil once, and says something new
   each time after. The wall of figures shows collars, so that the player can say what they have seen; Jo-Reesh
   speaks of the breach as it was when he sent. Ocan's school is said to begin with the book carried home.
6. *Every blocking fault is rebuilt*: the refusal; the gate on the second reading; a fetter to strike off in
   place of a door to use; the grave-singer stays at the pit wall and no switch touches her, and the elder's
   greeting has its twin for her dead; an ending of its own where both of Joxhi's speakers are dead; the rider's
   journal says only what was done; Livia's first line is SA03's and names the Synod; the fallback for the stone
   reads the one placed reference.

Not changed: that each trade is said before it is chosen (the owner's bar asks it, and one reader's wish for
surprise is one reader's); Yaxil-Ca's closing lines (two readers quoted them with pleasure); the Stilling as the
price of the names sunk (the reader who found it wrong was the one who paid it, which is the point).

**The stop rule, judged.** A round could retell the story, wanted the next chapter and split on every choice
meant to divide. The deliverability reader did not find every step buildable: all fourteen faults are answered
here, but by changes no fresh reader has seen, and two of them are new kinds of fault. The Fenlord's choice did
not split and has been re-priced unread. So the rule is **not met**. A third round must check: that a fresh
deliverability reader finds the rebuilt SA03 and its rider sound (the takers gated on the breach, the dead loosed
among people who can die); whether restraint and the table now pull against each other at Joxhi, with restraint still
paying nothing of its own; that the Fenlord kept is somebody's choice; and that SA05 is followed at a first reading.

## 6. For the other lines

Short notes, after the consistency ledger ([consistency](consistency.md), section 8, the Archive's list). None of
them changes another line's stages.

- **The Marsh Charter.** Joxhi's elder is Elder Jo-Reesh and the hired spear at the pit head is Gam-Kur, as MC02
  names them (N17, N22). He is "paid to the door" and never goes below. A lifted stone can kill him, the
  grave-singer or the buyer on the pit path, and can reach nobody at the kiln. The Sarpa in the pit scrub are the
  Charter's, and SA03 reads the same whether they are alive or dead (P6).
- **Reed-Sail.** The crew that caches at Mazurath is a Skeith crew, hostile on sight, with one captain, for whom
  Reed-Sail has no use (C45). Dives-Shallow is a crewman of no rank.
- **The main quest.** SA06 asks leave of Xoh-Reesha, the stake-keeper of Jal-Veesa, a woman, where the main quest
  leaves her, and reads sensibly with her dead (N18, C7). Its weir-keeper at Teel-Fen is Tuzeel-Ma, this line's to
  name (C21). No ending of this line makes the reed people hostile: their doors to the player are shut by this
  line's own stored number. SA06 goes to Gandranen only, never to Nenria, Gemin, Vel-Sapha Stand or Ushaan Camp,
  and passes through Helstrom without a word there. That sick ground is ground where Argonians were owned is Reader
  Hecal's saying and nobody's fact (T15).
- **The Lamp-Bearers.** Tedryn Orvayn is a private Dunmer of Stormhold with no office; they may know him by sight.
- **The Umbriel Witness.** The College of Whispers is an enemy both lines share. Maelle Jurard answers to
  Praeceptor Hadrian Scaeva and says "colleagues" (C48). If the Witness's writer wants it, the sale of the hall's
  book to her in SA06 may add 1 to `uw.college`, as every other sale to the College does; this line does not rely
  on it.
- **The Nisswo.** SA01 says only that the Agaceph took the missions' books and that one outstation's books were a
  gift; nothing in it touches Umpholo, and the Nisswo's Agaceph there is a Hirecan man and no reader (C49).
- **The world and travel.** A set-across from Yulca's landing to Gaweja's is with the owner (C60); the line does
  not need it.
- **Skill checks on magic.** No line of any quest here checks Mysticism (K2). Mark and Recall are given by name as
  a way, and count as the school their records sit under. The line leans on Alteration, Illusion and Enchanting,
  with Restoration as a way (K1, 7.2).
