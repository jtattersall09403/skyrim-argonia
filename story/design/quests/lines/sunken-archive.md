# The Sunken Archive: the line's story

The story of the Sunken Archive's six quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the [province plan](province-plan.md) gives the line (its section 3.11, its brief in section 7, the crossings
and exclusions of section 4). A quest writer turns each full outline in section 3 into a record without inventing
anything.

How to read the citations. **D** is the digest of the new world: "D okuwun-rahei, Yulca" is that place's record or
hooks in [okuwun-rahei](../../world/regions/okuwun-rahei.md); "D province, Peoples" is a section of
[province](../../world/province.md). Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`. **X** marks this document's own
extrapolation, drawn from the cited data and lore and never from nothing (decision 0032). No place of this world is
built yet, so a place is named by what its record says it is, and every physical thing an outline relies on that the
record does not promise is a row of section 4.

**Words used below**, for a reader new to the east. *Agaceph*: the needle-faced, bright-scaled raft people of the
eastern lakes, the one people of Black Marsh who keep books. *Hist*: the sentient trees every Argonian is hatched
under. *Sick ground*: what the Hist peoples call the ruins of the dead peoples, which they ring with ward-stakes and
will not enter. *Velothi* (in full, Cantemiric Velothi): grey elves from the north, cousins of the Dunmer, who built
in black stone along the east and died out in the Knahaten Flu. *Ayleid*: the older elves who built in white stone
and kept Argonians as slaves. *The Stilling*: the Agaceph's name for the spell with which they freeze an intruder.
*Sedge-Wardens*: the reed people of Jal-Veesa in the far north-west, who guard the Ayleid ruins there.

## 1. The faction in a page

**What they want.** Three peoples built in stone in the east of Black Marsh, and all three are dead: the Barsaebic
Ayleids, the Cantemiric Velothi and the priests of the Maran missions (`topics/lost-peoples.md:94`, `:119`;
`regions/middle-argonia.md:80`). Their halls stand half sunk in the marsh with their books, tablets and worked
stones still in them. Every Hist people calls those halls sick ground. The Agaceph call them the sunken archive:
shelves nobody has reached yet. Their saying is "We borrow. The dead are slow to ask for their books back."

**Who they are.** The Agaceph "read, keep books taken from dead missions, and are accomplished illusionists" (D
province, Peoples: Agaceph; `tribes.md:92`). Their one town is Yulca, a town of lashed rafts on a wide lake, held by
"a council of readers": five elders who sit together in the book hall (D okuwun-rahei, Yulca). They are no guild
and wear no robes: magic in Black Marsh is "folk literacy, not a profession" (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:35`), and
the readers are raft folk who happen to have a library, a scholars' enchanting table and the patience to teach for
coin (D okuwun-rahei, Yulca: gives). The eldest reader is **Yaxil-Ca** (X), dry, courteous and hard to surprise.
The readers do not go into the halls themselves. They send a **Borrower**: a pair of hands from outside who can get
past what guards a hall and bring out what is in it. The last Borrower, Ocuil, has not come back, which is why the
hall wants the player.

**How they work, in the line's own skills.** A dead hall is guarded three ways, and a school of magic answers each.

- *What was set to guard it*: wards that throw frost or fire at whoever comes near, doors that open to one stone or
  one tablet, water over the stair. Alteration answers these (Telekinesis lifts a ward's stone off its stand from
  out of reach, or draws a thing through bars; Waterbreathing makes a long dive easy), with the Mysticism spells
  that Magic Expanded brings, Telekinetic Grip and Mark and Recall, which are a Borrower's way out of a hall whose
  stair has fallen in. Enchanting reads what a found stone is set to do.
- *The dead who never left*: Restoration turns them and wards the Borrower through a ward's fire. Conjuration
  brings help into a fight too big for one.
- *The living who got there first or stand in the way*: Illusion, the Agaceph's own school, sets squatters on each
  other, calms a watchman or walks past him unseen.

Mysticism has no skill number of its own in this game: its spells sit under Skyrim's five schools
(`docs/research/mods/lane-1.12-magic.md`, "Schools in Wind UI"). So Mysticism is played by casting its spells, and
every check a person makes in talk names Alteration, Conjuration, Restoration, Illusion or Enchanting. A quest
never checks that a spell was cast: a ward, a gap, deep water and the dead are built so that a school answers them,
and each also has a plain way (a lock picked, a fight, a run through the fire) that costs more.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **The Hist peoples**, who hold the halls to be sick ground and are right about what walks in them. The Fenlords of
  the Ayleids had their Argonian slaves "executed and raised as undead guardians" (`topics/lost-peoples.md:108`);
  the Sedge-Wardens' stakes round a ruin are "set facing in" (D barsaebica, Mirasel). To them a Borrower is a
  looter with better manners. The line's discovery (X) is that they have a second reason they no longer remember:
  sick ground is ground where Argonians were owned.
- **Tedryn Orvayn** (X), the "visiting Dunmer scholar [who] wants to buy the secret of the Agaceph's freezing
  spell" (D okuwun-rahei, Yulca: hooks), of Stormhold's Dunmer, who "keep their ancestors" (D valley-of-storms,
  Stormhold: faith). He holds that the Velothi halls are his people's graves, lying six hundred years without a
  rite, and that he is the only living person asking after them.
- **The buyers.** The Synod has no hall in the province and works "through buyers, commissions and third parties",
  hunting "artefacts of great magical power" (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:80`, `:100`). Its buyer here is **Livia
  Semprone** (X), who pays for stone that still holds a charge and says so plainly. The College of Whispers
  "summons Daedra and practises necromancy" (`topics/guilds-and-orders.md:106`); its scholar **Maelle Jurard** (X)
  offers the readers a way to ask the dead directly, which tempts people who love books and horrifies everyone
  else.
- **The hall itself.** Reader **Ocan** (X) wants the buyers' coin, pupils who pay and a hall with stone under
  it: a school. Reader **Hecal** (X), whose pupil Ocuil was, wants the borrowing stopped and thinks the reed people
  are right. The old
  question of whether to become an institution at all (province plan 3.11) is these two, with Yaxil-Ca between
  them.

**What it pays, and what joining costs.** The hall pays in what it has: teaching and tomes at the readers' own
price, the use of its enchanting table, a share of anything sold, and fixed gifts that are a Borrower's tools (a
tome of Telekinesis, tomes of Mark and Recall, a ring of waterbreathing, and at the end the Stilling itself, which
the hall has never sold to an outsider). The cost: the halls are Dangerous and Deadly ground (D okuwun-rahei, "What
ranges it"), fixed and never levelled to the player, and a Borrower is unwelcome wherever a Hist people keeps the
stakes. This is a line for a player who has grown.

**What it lies about.** The Agaceph say they came to empty halls and took what the dead had no more use for. They
did not come to Sethiral after its people died (section 2).

**The build leaning.** Alteration and the Mysticism spells first, Illusion and Restoration second, Enchanting and
Conjuration third. A thief (Sneak, Lockpicking) and a fighter can finish every quest by the plain ways, and each
plain way is paid: what a fighter kills leaves a hall safe to strip, and what a thief opens by the lock leaves its
wards standing. Speech is for the living claimants, and no quest is won by talk alone.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** Sethiral and Anvadas were two holdings of one Cantemiric Velothi kin (D okuwun-rahei,
Sethiral, Anvadas). The kin kept needle-faced lake people as bond-servants: boatmen, door-wards and copyists. They
taught them letters to copy the house's books, and one spell, with which a door-ward held a stranger still at the
threshold until the master came. When the Knahaten Flu reached the east (`black-marsh-province.md:79`) the kin at
Sethiral sickened and the bond-servants did not. The lord took his household down to the shrine in the lower hall,
which "served a Daedric power" (D okuwun-rahei, Sethiral: faith), and bargained for forty lives. The price asked was
five of the house's servants. The door-ward held her master still on the stair with his own spell; five servants
shut the lower door on the whole household, living and dying, and wove the spell into its bar. That weave is what
hums. They left on the house's rafts with the house's books, and they carved the five marks of the weave on the
prow of the first raft, with a bearing to the place in the reeds where they sank the lord's family tablet, which is
the only thing that opens the kin's vault at Anvadas. In the vault lie the bond-tablets by which the house held
them: the first five names the Agaceph of Yulca ever had, written by their owners.

The founders swore two things: the door stays shut, and the hall writes nothing of its own. The Agaceph have kept
other people's books ever since and never written one. Each eldest reader is told the whole of it by the last, and
each has sent outsiders into the halls so that no Agaceph would stand in front of a servant's collar cut for a
needle neck and know it. The Stilling, the tribe's pride, is a house-servant's spell. Their reading is a
copyist's. "They find the Hist loud" (D okuwun-rahei, Yulca: faith) because their founders were raised in a stone
house away from any tree.

Ocuil, the last Borrower and the first Agaceph to hold the post in living memory, traced the prow's carving as
every Yulca child does and saw that it was a bearing. He followed it to the drowned stones off Gaweja's point and
died there with the lord's tablet in his satchel. Yaxil-Ca knows the door's hum has changed and does not know what
that means, and she will not ask four other readers to help her re-weave a ward whose reason she would have to tell
them.

one family's lamp to what the hall is. What ties them (X): each brings out proof that something a living Argonian
people thinks is its own was learnt in a dead master's house, until the proof is about the Agaceph themselves.

Earlier choices never change a later quest's stages. They change greetings, prices and single lines, read from
stored numbers. Only the finale carries into the world after the line.

**SA04, The Humming Door.** *Place:* Sethiral, the black keep whose upper halls Yina's Agaceph sit and read in, and
whose lower halls are "shut behind a humming door" (D okuwun-rahei, Sethiral). *What the player is told:* Yina's
eldest, Ayaca, wants the door opened "without waking what lies behind" (D okuwun-rahei, Yina: hooks), because the
hum has changed; Yaxil-Ca, who has refused her for years, now gives the player the prow and says to open it and
tell her alone what is there. *What the player does:* the door answers the five marks. The player says them with
Ayaca at the door: an Illusion check, or Tedryn's reading if he copied the prow in SA02, or Ayaca's own guess
bought with Speech. Said right, the household's dead lie still (they are switched off in the lower hall, which is
a separate inside) and the door unlocks; the player walks a silent hall. Otherwise the door is a Master lock, and
behind it the household's dead are awake: skeletal guards and ancestor ghosts on Deadly ground (D okuwun-rahei,
Sethiral: dangers), for Restoration, a summoned ally or a weapon. Either way the player finds the inner face of the
door scored by hands, the household's dead lying at the door and not in their beds, and at the shrine five collars
cut for needle necks. The lord stands at the shrine and speaks first. If the hall is quiet he takes the player for
the door-ward's hand, come six hundred years late with the five; he tells the house's side in his own words (they
were fed, lettered and of the household; five for forty was a fair price) and asks to be let up the stair. The
player answers with one of three lines: one that leaves him held (it locks the door again behind the player), one
that ends the talk in a fight (a hard fixed fight), or one that looses him, which is the same fight with a house's
thanks first. If Tedryn's reading opened the door, Yaxil-Ca learns from Ayaca that the prow was copied, and says
so. *The turn:* Yaxil-Ca, told,
says it plainly: "We did not come to empty halls. We emptied one." Everything before reads differently: the kind
truth she shelved in SA01, the prow, her quiet at Joxhi, and why a people of illusionists hires strangers.
*Skills:* Illusion first; Restoration, Conjuration or a weapon for the loud way; Lockpicking (Master) for the door.
*Carries on:* a number for what the player knows, and whether the lord is ended. The College's scholar arrives at
Yulca's guest raft after this quest, with her offer and the news of a book.

**SA05, The Right Voice.** *Places:* the drowned stones off Gaweja's point, where "nets bring up worked stones"
and "an Agaceph rafter has gone missing and the fishers found his raft empty" (D okuwun-rahei, Gaweja: hooks); then
Anvadas, whose "sealed vault door opens only to the right family tablet", carried off "into the marsh" (D
okuwun-rahei, Anvadas: hooks). *What the player does:* reads the prow's bearing with Yaxil-Ca, now that both know
what it is, and follows it to the stones: a dive to a sunk stone coffer on a drowned stair, crocodiles and
slaughterfish in the shallows (D okuwun-rahei, Gaweja: gives), and Ocuil's body beside it with the lord's tablet
in his satchel. His own slate is with him, scratched under water in his last hour: the founders wove their spell
into the coffer as they did into the door, it held him as he lifted the lid, and an Argonian does not drown. Hecal
is the one to whom the slate is carried. On the bank waits the "stranger [who] digs the shallows by night", a digger in Tedryn's pay, who
is paid off, talked round, walked past under Illusion or fought. Then Anvadas, on Deadly ground: the drowned
courtyard and its crocodiles, the skeletal guards, and in the tower "the household's warden ghost" (D okuwun-rahei,
Anvadas: gives). The Oxhu say "the tablets still answer the right voice" (D okuwun-rahei, Anvadas: faith): the warden is not placed hostile. He stands and speaks first. To a player holding the lord's
tablet he asks in whose name they come and answers what he is asked; to one without it his only answer is a
refusal, and a player who presses him picks the line that makes him attack. The tablet
is the vault's key. In the vault are the kin's own tablets and five small ones: the founders' bond-tablets.
*The choice, each side argued by the person it concerns:* Yaxil-Ca wants the five sunk where nobody will read
them; Hecal wants them read aloud at the next hatching, as the first names the tribe ever had; Tedryn wants
every tablet in the vault carried to his people's ancestor stones at Stormhold, the five with them, "because they
were of the household, and I would sooner my cousins' house had heirs than none". *Skills:* Alteration for the
dive, Illusion or Speech on the bank, Restoration against the dead, Speech with the warden. *Carries on:* who holds
the five; third rank; Ocuil's kin at Icalca told where the ruin in the reeds lies (D okuwun-rahei, Icalca: hooks).

**SA06, A Book That Should Not Exist.** *Place:* Gandranen, "a library of magical halls that draws books from any
time, and a Fenlord's tomb crawling with undead slave-guardians", with "a book that should not exist" on its
shelves (D barsaebica, Gandranen). The journey is the plan's: by boat to Truowa and Helstrom and by rootworm to
Jal-Veesa (D province, Roads). *What the player is told:* Maelle Jurard has been as far as the library's wards and
says a book stands there in the Agaceph hand; the Agaceph have never written one. *The hard place.* The Sedge-Wardens
guard the ruin as sick ground (D province, Peoples: Sedge-Wardens), and their weir at Teel-Fen sits on the creek
that leads to it, where "strangers come asking the way to Gandranen" (D barsaebica, Teel-Fen Weir: hooks). A barred gate stands across the creek path at the weir, and the wardens there can be spoken to. The
player gets past by leave, by stealth or by a gift (province plan, section 4, "Whose ruin"): leave from the
ruin-warden at Jal-Veesa, given on a promise to carry nothing out (his line gives the gate's key); the gate picked, or the creek swum
round it under Sneak or Illusion; or the gift
they would ask of nobody, which is that the Fenlord be ended, because the guardians who walk his tomb are Argonian
dead who never got home to a tree (X on `topics/lost-peoples.md:108`). Inside, every guard a hall can have stands
at once: the library's wards (D barsaebica, Gandranen: dangers) for Telekinesis and a ward spell, the slave-guardians
for Restoration (the wardens ask that they be turned and passed, not broken, and count the difference), a stair
that has fallen in behind the reading hall so that Mark and Recall are the easy way home and the tomb is the plain
one, and the Fenlord in his
tomb for whoever means to end him. The College's people are camped at the creek and want the Fenlord kept for
study (X on D barsaebica, Urvyn Farm: hooks; the College's side of this is shared with the Umbriel Witness, see
"For the other lines"). On the reading stand is the hall's own book. Its first leaf is the door, in the first
reader's hand. Its last written leaf is dated in a year that has not come and says the hall has stone under it and
forty who pay to sit, and that none of them is Agaceph. *What the player does with it:* reads it where it stands
and keeps a promise, or takes it and breaks one; and carries it home or does not. Ocan reads the last leaf as
the hall living for ever, Hecal as the tribe's death, Yaxil-Ca as the price of the door. If the book reaches the
hall the readers begin to write: a book of the hall's own stands open on the hall's landing afterwards, and
Yaxil-Ca offers the player her place on the mat. The player may refuse it. Taken, it costs the council Hecal, who
will not sit on the founders' mat beside an outsider and is found at Icalca afterwards. If the book stays on its
stand, the player can still tell the readers what the last leaf said: they hear it, half believe it, and go on
borrowing. Nothing
is voted on and nobody rules on which is better. *Skills:* every school of the line in its fullest use, and Speech
or Sneak for the wardens. *Carries on:* what the hall becomes; how the Sedge-Wardens greet the player.

**What the line leaves out of the first design, and keeps.** The Conclave of Baal has left the line and Gemin is
the main quest's (province plan 3.11). The escaped construct, the memory pool and the three charters are gone
([walk6-ceiling](../review/walk6-ceiling.md), "Sunken Archive"). Kept: the waterproofed book from a drowned room
(SA01), the ruin with two owners (SA03), the weight of a name on a tablet (SA05), the College at the door and the
Synod's purse (SA03 to SA06), a dead scholar's method (Ocuil), and the question of what the hall does with what it
knows, answered by a book in the player's hands and not by a vote (SA06).

## 3. The first three quests

Common to all three.

- **Yaxil-Ca** stands in the book hall at Yulca for the whole line. She must live: her death fails any running
  quest of the line with the faction's message. Nobody else is essential. People stand where they are placed.
- **Out of the hall in three sentences.** Each briefing is short and gives the place, the way and the marker
  itself. Nobody else has to be spoken to before the player goes in: every other person on the way is there for a
  key, a warning or a better price, and can be walked past.
- **One working stage.** What the player achieves is a thing held, a person dead or a stored number, so the ways
  through can be mixed, done in any order or abandoned. The lines that carry a result to the person waiting for it
  set the stages.
- **A take or a use writes a journal note and nothing more.** Each is open from the stage that first names its
  thing as a target, and that stage sets the note at once if the thing is already held. A line that needs a thing
  checks that it is held, however it was got. A wall or an altar used before the job was given must be used again.
- **A death moves no stage.** Lines check who is alive. Where a person can be dead, the outline gives the line
  that stands in for theirs, and its speaker is a target of that stage.
- **A person found somewhere else afterwards is two placements**: one switched off and one on, far from the line
  that does it. The line is gated on the first being alive, so the dead do not turn up again.
- **Spells are the player's own.** No line or stage checks that one was cast. A thing drawn across a gap by
  Telekinesis is a thing taken; a dead man turned is a dead man passed.
- **A school must reach what it is meant to answer.** The dead a quest means Restoration to turn, and the living
  it means Illusion to sway, stand at fixed levels that the school's Adept-rank spells reach in this load order
  (Skyrim's own reach is level 16 for Repel Undead and 20 for Pacify and Rout before perks). The levels below are
  set on that rule and the quest writer checks them against the load order before the record. One named opponent in
  each place stands above that reach on purpose and is a fight or a thing to slip past. Skyrim's restless dead
  ignore Illusion, so Illusion is the answer to the living and Restoration to the dead.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense
  and say only what the player has just done. Thresholds, levels and rewards are fixed numbers. Each failed check
  has its own written refusal and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms; nothing new is modelled.
- "Night" and "dark" in what people say are their words only; no step depends on the hour.
- Danger is warned of twice before each place, by a person and by something found.

### SA01. The Lamp Below

**What a new player is told, and when.** Yulca's raft ferry sets a guest down at the lake landing (D okuwun-rahei,
Yulca: gives), and the keeper of the raft kitchen says the readers are short of a pair of hands. On first speaking
to Yaxil-Ca in the book hall, before any job, four optional lines, each two or three sentences:

- *the readers*: "Five of us keep this hall. Any fisher in this country can freeze a thief where he stands. We are
  the ones who wrote down how."
- *the dead halls*: who built in stone here and how each died: the white-stone elves, three thousand years gone,
  who kept our kind as slaves and left one hall in the far north-west "that is said to fetch its own books"; the
  grey elves, northern cousins of the Dunmer who settled this coast, dead of a flu "that never touched us"; the
  priests of Mara "of swamp rot, in a month". "The tree peoples call those halls sick ground and stake them round.
  We call them shelves."
- *borrowing*: what the readers want of the player and why them: "We read. We do not dig. We send someone who can
  get past what the dead left on guard, and you came across a lake of crocodiles to ask."
- *what it pays, and why I might not*: teaching and tomes at the readers' price, the table, a share; and "the last
  who went down for us was Ocuil, three seasons a Borrower. His raft was found empty. The tree peoples are right
  about what walks in those halls."

"Not yet" stays open and free. The job is three sentences and a gift. A keeper of books at Hirecan, Xocin, wants
her forebear's bronze hand-lamp from the cell under the old chapel downriver of her hamlet: down the chapel's stair
into the water, through a short drowned passage, up to a dry landing and a barred grille, with the last priest
still behind it. The lamp is Xocin's; "and whatever else is in that cell comes to me unread by Hirecan. She will
ask for it. I am telling you now so that you cannot say you were not told." She hands over a tome of Telekinesis:
"A Borrower's first tool. There are bars."

**The hidden truth (X).** In the Maran missions' day three priests kept an outstation chapel downriver of where
Hirecan now stands; the missions' people "all died of swamp rot in the first month" and "left behind some excellent
books" (`regions/middle-argonia.md:80`). An Agaceph woman, Tuma, had hired on to carry their lamp. She nursed all
three. The last, Brother Aurel, taught her letters in his last weeks, gave her the chapel's books "that Mara's word
might have one reader in this place", and, when the rot reached his mind, made her lock him into the cell under
the chapel with the lamp burning and take the key away. He wrote all of it in his daybook, with the words of his
own people's rite for the dead inside its cover, wrapped it in oilskin and set it by the lamp. She could not yet
read well enough to say the rite. She never went back, kept the key and never said why. Her family tells the
tribe's joke instead: that she took her wage out of the priests' shelves when they were past objecting. The river
has since taken the lower floor. Brother Aurel is one of the restless dead, because no rite of his own people was
said over him (X; `topics/guilds-and-orders.md:121` for the rite outsiders expect). Hirecan's people still read
the mission books "aloud at hatchings" (D chixathe-mesa, Hirecan: faith) and think it comedy.

**The hidden truth (X).** The red seam at Joxhi was first dug under Ayleid owners, who "enslaved Argonians as
labourers" in this country (`topics/lost-peoples.md:99`); the buried hall was the overseers'. The slaves pressed a
small clay figure into the hall's walls for each of their dead, because they were allowed no stake and no tree:
a few by the pit-side wall of the first gallery, hundreds in the undercroft. When the Ayleids fell, the overseers
who died in the hall did not lie quiet, and the last living ones set the hall's ward-stones to face inward and
shut the inner door on them. The village that grew on the spoil kept digging and kept the custom: Joxhi's diggers
"press a clay figure into the pit wall for any who died in a fall" (D okuwun-rahei, Joxhi: faith) and believe that
"a hatchling of the first Inoja families found the red seam" (D okuwun-rahei, Joxhi: history). The four diggers
who broke through saw, by their own lamp, their own grave-figures in the wall of a place no digger of theirs had
ever reached. That is why they will not go back down. They told the elder only that there was white stone.

Two ward-stones stand on stands in the first gallery and hold the hall's dead in the galleries beyond. The key to
the inner door lies on the base of the nearer stand, under its stone and inside the ward's reach. A careful
Borrower draws the key off by spell and leaves both stones standing. A greedy or hurried one lifts a stone: the
ward dies, the key is theirs for the picking up, the stone in their pack is worth more than Joxhi's clay, and a
hall of the dead under a living village has one ward the fewer.

## 5. Read on paper

One round, on the first draft of sections 1 to 4. Two players were given only what a player meets, a sheet a quest
for SA01 to SA03 and then SA04 to SA06 in summary, and answered after each what they thought was going on, whom
they trusted, what they expected and what they would choose. One reader was given the ceiling, the quest form and
the outlines. All three read the same version, and nothing was changed until all had finished.

**What they said.**

- *The player who loves the lore* (a mage) cared most about Brother Aurel and his lamp-carrier, Jo-Reesh with his
  nineteen diggers, and Yaxil-Ca, whom she began by distrusting and ended by pitying; found Tedryn's "I would
  sooner my cousins' house had heirs than none" wonderful and appalling at once; could retell the line in three
  sentences; would join and wants SA04 above all. She guessed the outline of the turn at SA02 from "held, by
  five" and the five readers, and said what she had not seen coming still landed hard: the collars, "five for
  forty", and why Borrowers are outsiders. Hard to please on: the daybook dangling "the words" with no way to say
  them, "the sheet's biggest miss"; a choice that cost the player nothing; the same choice (a shelf, a people or
  an outsider) quest after quest; Tedryn's line in SA03 handing over the answer she was enjoying working out;
  tools given after the puzzle they solve; no quiet way to free the fisherman; "watch her face" with no face;
  nothing saying what the wards held in or what the diggers saw; the loose clay figure with no use; a season's
  silence with no sequel; the two other readers barely known and yet holding the ending; Ocuil a body with no
  account; the library that fetches books arriving from nowhere; a kept promise seeming to lock one ending; and
  three points of lore (Ayleid work is thousands of years old, not six hundred; a Dunmer at Stormhold in 4E 201
  wants a line; the Velothi of the east want a line).
- *The impatient player* (a sneaking spellsword) called Mazurath "a proper playground" and the best thing in the
  three, liked the frost gallery, the stone as a key and the wall of figures ("no dialogue, just looking, and it
  landed"), would join for the spell and the tomes and play SA04 and SA06 eagerly. Hard to please on: "two thirds
  talking and one third doing", with three talkers before each place and three cases after; one walking corpse
  being thin at his level; choices that were "flavour", the same rank and reward either way, three quests
  running; the Recall tome sitting in the cache behind the trick it enables and then given again; a Ring of
  Minor Illusion as "vendor trash"; names he could not tell apart; not knowing whether lifting a stone looses
  anything; the Enchanting check paying him for repeating the buyer; SA05 reading as a fetch inside a fetch; and
  Tedryn's SA03 line giving the turn away "flat".
- *The deliverability reader* found the stages, gates, hand-ins and endings of SA01 to SA03 buildable in kind and
  nine things that were not as written: a ward-stone cannot be a door's key; nothing reads "a stone left its
  stand"; SA03 stranded the player with both Joxhi speakers dead; a thing to take must be a target at the stage
  its take opens from; fallback speakers were not targets; with Xocin dead the way was untold and two journal
  entries were false; a person "moved" is two placements and the dead would reappear; switching people on the
  guest raft from the hall needs the hall to be its own inside, and two SA06 switches were in the speaker's own
  cell; and the Sedge-Wardens cannot stop anybody without a gate. Doubts, stated as doubts: that turning and
  swaying spells reach the levels set; that Telekinesis draws a thing through bars; that the ward on a stand works
  with a stone of our own; that hostile dead leave a neutral captive alone.

**What changed, as one set.**

1. *The words can be said.* The daybook now carries the rite, the chapel's altar stands over the cell, and using
   it lays Brother Aurel down. It is the mage's third way to what the cell holds, it is done by the player's
   hand, and it sets up SA04, where the right words make a whole household lie still.
2. *The choices are no longer one choice, and they bite.* SA01's costs the player a teacher either way, said
   before it is made. SA02's is framed and paid as what it is, an outsider getting the tribe's one made thing,
   with Tedryn's reading at the next door as its price. SA03's is now about power: a small key drawn out by spell
   against a stone worth a fortune, with the hall's dead at the breach and a spear's season charged to the player
   if they lift it and leave the dead walking. The lie to the elder is gone, and the grave-singer is told by the
   clay figure put in her hand.
3. *Out of the hall in three sentences.* Each briefing gives the place and the way and the marker; nobody else
   must be spoken to. Dives-Shallow, Xocin and Jo-Reesh are optional before the place; Livia speaks one breath;
   Tedryn's reading is two sentences.
4. *Tools before the job.* Telekinesis is given with SA01 and Mark and Recall with SA02; the cache and the
   rewards hold other things (a staff, a tome of Invisibility, a Ring of Waterbreathing, a tome of Detect Dead).
5. *The turn is kept for SA04.* Tedryn's SA03 line is cut. The seeds that stay are the ones a careful reader
   enjoyed finding.
6. *The other readers and Ocuil are people sooner.* Hecal and Ocan stand on the hall's landing and speak after
   every job, each with a stake: she asks after Ocuil, her pupil, and he names his buyer. Ocuil leaves an account
   of his own death. The library that fetches books is named in the first conversation. Yaxil-Ca's face is given
   when the prow comes home. The seat can be refused and its cost is explained; a kept promise still lets the
   player tell the readers what the book said.
7. *Everything the deliverability reader marked blocking is rebuilt*: a real key on the stand; stones read only
   as held or handed over, a number for each taker; stand-in speakers as targets and a line for Yaxil-Ca where
   both of Joxhi's are dead; takes opened from the stage that names their thing; two placements for each person
   found elsewhere, gated on the first being alive; the hall as its own inside with a landing outside it; a
   barred gate at Teel-Fen; a warden ghost who speaks and is never placed hostile; journal entries that hold with
   Xocin or the elder dead. Levels were lowered to what each school's spells reach, with one opponent in each
   place set above it on purpose, and the doubts that only the game can settle are listed in section 4 as checks
   owed before the record.
8. *Small truths.* Ayleid work is three thousand years old. Tedryn's people at Stormhold and the grey elves of
   the coast each get a line. Names were shortened so that no two start alike. The diggers saw their figures in
   the first gallery. The fisherman sits behind bars and is freed with a key or a pick.

Readers split, as meant, on Tedryn (used by both, trusted by neither, and one would speak for him), on the
grave-singer (one told her, one kept the elder's season) and on the lord (one would end him and one would not
say). Both gave the daybook to Hirecan, one uneasily: the cost now set on that side is for the next round to
weigh. Nothing was changed to settle a split.

**For a second round to check.** Whether the opening now reaches the chapel fast enough for an impatient player,
and whether one walking priest is enough of a first danger once the altar is there. Whether the three choices now
read as three different kinds, and whether SA05's and SA06's still feel like a fourth and fifth of one kind.
Whether the cost at SA01 reads as a real price or as a punishment for choosing, and whether readers now split on
the daybook. Whether SA03's key against stone is clear at the breach without the buyer's speech. Whether the turn
still lands with Tedryn's line gone, and whether it is now seeded too thinly for a player who skims. Whether Hecal
and Ocan are known by SA04. Whether SA05 plays as more than a fetch inside a fetch. Whether a fresh deliverability
reader finds the rebuilt stage tables sound when each named person is killed in turn and each thing is taken
first.
