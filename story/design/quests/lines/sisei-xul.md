# The Sisei-Xul: the plot

This page is the plot of the Argonian order of healers, makers and grave-singers. It was written fresh on
2026-11-16 to the owner's notes in [the factions overhaul](../../../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05c-factions-overhaul.md).
Its rival's plot is [the Rothalen house](rothalen-house.md): the two pages tell one war from its two sides. Every
quest is set in a place whose record and source place hold what it needs
(`python3 -m tools.newworld.place_card <place>`). Everything is inside the quest ceiling
([quest-system](../../../../.claude/skills/quest-system/SKILL.md)), with the additions listed in section 9.

**Words.** *Sisei-Xul*: the order's name, "sprout and death" in Jel. *Sisei* is the sprout, the first month of the
Argonian year after the sun (`topics/sky-moons-calendar.md:22`), and *xul* is death, as in *bok-xul*, "bowl of
death" (`topics/material-culture.md:249`) and Xul-Thuxis, "Snake-Means-Death City" (`regions/murkmire.md:63`). The
two words name the whole run of a life, from hatching to the grave, which the Jel calendar follows from Sisei to
Xulomaht, "the Deceased" (`topics/sky-moons-calendar.md:31-37`). *Hist*: the great thinking trees of Black Marsh.
*Tree-minder*: the one who tends a village's Hist (`topics/material-culture.md:226`). *Grave-singer*: the one who
tends the dead, stakes them and sings their last song (`topics/material-culture.md:232`). *Xul-vaat*: a grave-stake,
carved with the dead one's whole story and driven through the body into the mud
(`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:123-129`). *Bog blight*: what rises when a stake is pulled. *The Rothalens*:
a Dunmer trading house of Thorn. *The Owing*: the marsh's sentence, months of owed work in place of a cell.

## 1. What the faction is

The Sisei-Xul is the Argonian order of marsh medicine and the rites of the dead. Its members brew potions and
poisons, forge and enchant, heal the hurt, ease the dying who cannot be healed, and stake the dead so that nothing
can raise them.

The order is old and loose, as Argonian things are. Its mother hall is the tree-minders' hall in Helstrom, the
interior capital that no invader has taken. It has branches at Aroxhu, where its alchemists work beside the great
Hist of the Clay-Mound People, and at Tsonac, where the Stone-Sleepers' minders teach the flesh spells of stone.
Its skills are Alchemy, Smithing, Enchanting, Alteration and Restoration, with Destruction's poisons for a fight.
Our mods add no craft beyond Skyrim's three, so its making is smithing, alchemy and enchanting.

Its enemy is the Rothalen house of Thorn, which raises the Argonian dead and sells them north as thralls. To an
Argonian, a body pulled from its stake and made to work for a Dunmer master is the slave-coffles of Thorn over
again. Joining gives the player the order's tables, trainers and teachers, a spell or a recipe in most quests, and
at its end the house broken and its trade in the dead ended.

## 2. The truth under the story

**What the player is shown.** Graves are being opened. Stakes are pulled at night, the dead are gone, and bog
blights walk where they lay. A walled-up gallery of dead labourers at a salt mine is broken into from the far side.
Dead men who were never staked rise in the dark. The tracks lead to the Rothalens, a Dunmer house of Thorn that
trades in saltrice and buys old Velothi pieces from scavengers.

**Why the stakes.** Corpses rot fast in the marsh, so foreign necromancers seldom bother with it
(`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:136`). Life clings to a grave-stake, and a stake can feed necromancy
(`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:129`). The Rothalens' old master found that a body raised with its own stake
bound to it does not rot. So the house needs the body and its stake together, and an Argonian burial ground is a
crop to it. The salt mine's dead were never staked, but the rock-salt preserved them, which is why the house's diggers
went after them. **X**, from the two cited lines.

**Where the dead go.** The house binds the raised dead and ships them out of Thorn on its own ship, the *Saltrice
Queen*, to buyers across the Tear road. House Telvanni refused the Pact that ended Argonian slavery and kept taking
Argonians (`regions/thornmarsh-and-east.md:65`). The dead are property in a Telvanni tower and nobody there asks
where they came from. **X**, from the Telvanni line. The Barsaebic Ayleids did the same thing first: when their
Argonian slaves rose, their necromancer kings had them killed and raised as guards
(`topics/lost-peoples.md:108-109`).

**Who began it.** Morvayn Rothalen, the house's master, bought Argonian captives off the slaver brig *Silt Bride*
when he was young, before the Red Year. She sank off Thorn with her captives shackled in the hold
(`place_card "Wreck of the Silt Bride"`). Her captain's log names the families who bought from her, and the
Rothalens are among them. Morvayn learnt his art in a Telvanni tower and came home to Thorn to sell what he could no
longer sell alive.

**What the order hides.** Argonians bind souls too. A blasphemer is punished by being bound to an urn, so that his
soul is denied its return to the Hist (`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:130-134`). In the mother hall at
Helstrom there is such an urn. The Eldest of the order says it holds a Hist-poisoner. It holds Keel-Vaxa, an Archein
who sold the people of his own village to the Rothalens, and the Eldest bound him herself, out of grief: her
clutch-sister was among those he sold. His granddaughter, Neeja, now works for the house. The player learns this in
quest 8.

## 4. Joining

**The rumours.** In Thorn, Tsonac, Inoja, Aroxhu and Helstrom, people asked about healers, work or the dead speak of
the grave-singers. "If you can brew, the Sisei-Xul want hands. A kiln burst at Arorha, and their tree-minder there
has more burns than salve." The first such line starts **Find the Sisei-Xul**, and its marker sits on Heeba-Tsei,
Arorha's tree-minder.

**Being let in.** Quest 1 is the test the owner set: gather named ingredients, make a healing potion and give it to
the tree-minder. When the potters drink it, she offers the player a place.

**The door shut.** Find the Sisei-Xul fails, with a journal entry, when the player finishes **The Pulled Stakes**,
quest 4 of the Rothalen house's line, in which they pull the stakes of Tazhul's dead. The order then knows their
face.

## 5. The acts

The line is thirteen quests in three acts, as long as a guild line of Skyrim's. Act I teaches the order's work:
heal, ease and stake. Act II is the war of the stakes, which climbs from the order's own country into the house's
ruins and wrecks. Act III carries the war to the house and ends it.

Danger climbs with the line. Act I is played in the clay country's towns and on the shore of Hatandros Bight (D0 to D2). The world holds no
place of band D1, so the line opens at D2, on a shore road of mudcrabs and snakes that a new character can walk. Act II goes
into the Velothi ruins and the sea off Thorn (D3 to D4). Act III ends in the deepest ruin on the Chixathe Mesa (D5).
Skill checks climb with it: a beginner's potion in quest 1, an easy lock in quest 3, an expert lock and an Alteration of 50
in Act II, a master lock and Paralyze at the end.

Most quests begin with the player being taught one spell or recipe, and one step of the quest is built for it among
other steps that lead into each other. Every objective has a marker. Where the player may fight or sneak, the
journal says so, and the guards stand few and apart.

### Act I: the healers

**1. The Burnt Kilns** (Arorha, a clay-mound village on Hatandros Bight). A kiln has burst at the water's edge and
three potters lie burnt in the tree-minder's house. Heeba-Tsei, Arorha's tree-minder, speaks first at her door. She
teaches the player what two marsh plants do (the stage makes them know every effect of somnalius frond and swamp
fungal pod) and asks for three of each. Both grow along the shore road north of the village, among the mudcrab
swarms and giant snakes, and both are marked. The player fights the beasts, shoots them from the mounds or picks
round them. They make a healing potion at the tree-minder's table (Alchemy, any level) and bring it to her. She
gives it to the worst burnt potter and offers the player a place in the order. *Leads on:* Heeba-Tsei says a dying
man at the order's hall in Aroxhu has asked for a grave-singer and cannot wait. The journal marks him.

**2. The Painless Cup** (Aroxhu, the Clay-Mound People's mound-town). Nadras Velsa is an old Dunmer trader dying of
a wasting sickness on the guest mats of the moot-ground, where the order's alchemists keep him. No potion will save
him. The order's alchemist by the Hist teaches the player that imp stool and somnalius frond together make a draught
that brings sleep and then stops the heart. Imp stool grows on the mound terraces, and the player makes the poison
at the dye-and-sap table beside the Hist (Alchemy). Before he drinks, Nadras asks one thing: that he be staked like
an Argonian when he is dead, "so that Morvayn Rothalen's people never stand me up again". He was the Rothalens'
factor for forty years. He drinks, the screen fades and he is gone, and a fresh grave-stake stands below the mounds.
*Leads on:* his last words go into the journal. The alchemist hears them and sends the player to Coldbrine Mine,
where a Thorn woman wants her dead staked and the overseer refuses.

**3. The Walled Gallery** (Coldbrine Mine, a rock-salt working south-west of Thorn). Tzeeh wants her great-uncle and
the other bonded labourers who died in the old shaft's collapse brought out and staked. The overseer walled the
gallery up and keeps its key. The player is taught Turn Undead before they go. They lift the key from the overseer
(Pickpocket, easy), pick the wall door's lock (an easy lock) or go round by the brine pools, where the old giant
mudcrab has dug through. In the gallery the dead are risen. The rock-salt preserved the bodies, and someone has broken in
from the far side to raise them. The journal says the player may fight them or turn them. Past them lies the satchel
of a Dunmer digger the dead killed, with a soul gem and a stone carved with a spider in a circle. *Leads on:*
Tzeeh tells the player the spider in a circle is the mark the Rothalen house puts on its crates in Thorn's market.
Heeba-Tsei makes the player a Leaf-Hand and sends them to Akeeta-Sul at Tsonac, whose hamlets have graves the house
would want.

### Act II: the war of the stakes

**4. Under the Oldest Foundation** (Tsonac, the Stone-Sleepers' terraced city; Tselan, a stone hamlet on the river
west of it). Akeeta-Sul teaches the player Stoneflesh, the first of the Stone-Sleepers' stone spells (Alteration
25). Tselan's quarry gang has broken into a sealed Velothi chamber under the hamlet's oldest foundation, and one of
them went in. The elder there says he was a hired man nobody knew. Inside, the skeletons of the chamber are up, and
the hired man is prising a welkynd stone from the chamber's wall. He is the house's. The player fights the dead with
Stoneflesh on them, turns them or slips past, and kills the hired man or takes the stone while his back is turned
(Sneak). *Leads on:* the stone goes back to Akeeta-Sul. She admits, unasked, that she once sold the house Velothi
pieces. She sends the player to Tazhul, where two eggs have failed and the egg-minder speaks of a curse.

**5. The Pulled Stakes** (Tazhul, a Root-Folk egg station in the marsh west of Inoja; Joxhi, a clay-diggers'
village north of Inoja). The curse is the dead. Tazhul's stakes on the trap-line path have been pulled, and bog
blights walk the path. The egg-minder teaches the player Detect Dead (Alteration). The player lays the blights with
Turn Undead or weapons. With Detect Dead they see the drag-marks of bodies leading off the path toward Joxhi, where
the diggers broke into a buried Ayleid hall and will not go back down. The house's men have made the hall their
store of stakes. The journal says the player may fight them or go quietly. Two stand at the stair and one sleeps by
the stakes. The player takes the bundle of Tazhul's stakes (one named thing) by force or by stealth. *Leads on:* the
egg-minder takes the stakes back. A grave-singer's work follows, and Okan-Zeel asks for the stranger who did it. The
journal marks the tree-minders' hall at Helstrom.

**6. Grave-Iron** (Tsonac; Balormaak, a ruined Velothi town on the eastern mesa). Okan-Zeel sends the player back to
Tsonac to learn grave-iron from Bur-Meesh. A spike of it, driven through a raised body, holds it down. The stage
opens the recipe at his forge: two iron ingots and a pinch of salt from Coldbrine (Smithing 25). Skeletons walk the
cellars of Balormaak, and Ayiin's children have seen torches there at night. The house has raised a Velothi captain
in the bell-tower crypt to guard a cache. The player forges three spikes and goes in. Each skeleton they kill they
may spike, and the captain must be spiked before he stays down: the spike is a weapon the captain alone takes great
harm from. The journal says the player may fight through the cellars or sneak the silted ones. In the cache are
Velothi bronze and a letter of the house about a log in a wreck off Thorn. *Leads on:* Okan-Zeel makes the player a
Stake-Keeper. She has heard of the *Silt Bride*: if her captain's log names the Rothalens, the Vicecanon of Thorn
can close their door in the market.

**7. The Slaver's Log** (Wreck of the Silt Bride, a slaver brig off Thorn; Thorn, the grassland border city). The
order teaches Waterbreathing (Alteration 40). An Argonian player has no need of it. The brig lies a short swim off
Thorn's coast, ringed by slaughterfish. Her drowned crew and her shackled dead walk the hold. The player fights them,
or lays the shackled dead with Turn Undead and grave-iron, and takes the log from the drowned captain's strongbox,
whose key he carries (Lockpicking, adept, if the key is not taken). The log names Morvayn Rothalen as a buyer. The
player takes it to the Vicecanon's factor in Thorn's market. He reads it and says it is too old to hang a living
man, but enough to close the Rothalens' stall. *Leads on:* the factor shuts the stall the next day. The journal says
the house will answer. Okan-Zeel writes for the player to come to Helstrom at once.

**8. The Empty Niche** (Helstrom, the interior capital; Hiss-Bank Camp, a Naga camp on the western river bank). The
house has answered. While the player was in the sea, its people broke into the tree-minders' hall and took an urn
from its niche. Okan-Zeel says the urn holds a Hist-poisoner whose soul must never go free. The thieves have gone
west by river to Hiss-Bank Camp to wait for the house's boat. The Naga there are hired, and their camp is marked.
The player fights the camp or goes in quietly, past a chest lock of expert difficulty to the urn. Neeja waits apart
by the water. She is unarmed and speaks first. She tells the player whose soul is in the urn: Keel-Vaxa, her grandfather,
an Archein who sold his own village to the Rothalens and was bound by Okan-Zeel herself. She asks the player to let
her break it. The player keeps the urn or gives it to her. If they kill her, she drops nothing that the line needs.
*Leads on:* Okan-Zeel takes the urn back if the player brings it. Asked about Keel-Vaxa, she says what he was and
admits nothing else. The journal records that she lied about the poisoner. Either way she sends the player to the
order's enchanter in the hall.

**9. The Humming Door** (Helstrom; Sethiral, a ruined Velothi holding at Yina). The order's enchanter gives the
player a blade enchanted with Turn Undead and asks them to break it at the arcane enchanter in the hall. That teaches
them the enchantment (Enchanting 30). Sethiral's sealed door hums at night. The house has bound the household's
ghost behind it to keep its second stake store, and Yina's eldest will pay anyone who opens it quietly. The player
enchants their own weapon with Turn Undead and goes down past the ghosts and the river crocodiles. Behind the door the
ghost and the stakes wait. The player fights the ghost or turns it, takes the stakes and, from the store's keeper, a
key with the spider in a circle on its bow. *Leads on:* the enchanter knows the spider from the cellar door at
Ovezza, carved with the same sign and barred from the inside. The key opens a door below its drowned quay.

**10. The Barred Cellar** (Ovezza, a ruined Velothi town on the bay). The order teaches Circle of Protection
(Restoration 50). Ovezza's ghosts walk the square and slaughterfish fill the water over the quay. Below the quay is
the house's water door, which the key opens. Behind it is the house's hall, where its bound dead stand in rows and a
wraith keeps the inner door. The player raises the Circle at the stair's foot so that the bound dead cannot close on
them, and fights or slips past them to the wraith. In the hall's inner room are the house's papers of sale for the
dead, and a sealed letter of Morvayn's. It names the night the house will raid the order's grave-ground at Ixu-Maan.
*Leads on:* Okan-Zeel makes the player a Grave-Singer and goes to Ixu-Maan herself. She asks the player to follow
when they can, and the journal marks the outpost.

### Act III: the end of the trade

**11. The Night at Ixu-Maan** (Ixu-Maan, an egg-tending outpost south of Helstrom). The order teaches Poison Spray
(Destruction 50), as old marsh fighters used it. The raid has begun when the player arrives. The house's Naga from
Salt-Tooth Camp hold the nest-pit, and Dalvela Rothalen's bound dead walk among the grave-stakes. Okan-Zeel stands
at the egg pools with two grave-singers, placed there and fighting. The player clears the nest-pit, lays the dead and
kills the Naga scout-leader. If Okan-Zeel dies, the line goes on without her: the second grave-singer speaks in her
place. Dalvela is not here. Among the dead is a Rothalen captain carrying the *Saltrice Queen*'s sailing orders.
*Leads on:* the orders say the house's crew will sail a hold of bound dead north on the next tide from Kharraluhl, where they wait.
The journal marks both places.

**12. The Thrall Ship** (Kharraluhl, a ruined Velothi settlement north of the bay; Thorn). The order teaches Paralyze
(Alteration 75). Kharraluhl's upper walls hold the house's diggers and its lower halls hold the bound Argonian dead
in rows, waiting to be carried to the ship. The player fights or sneaks the walls, lays the bound dead or paralyses
the diggers who guard them, and burns the house's stake store at the household shrine. Then they go aboard the
*Saltrice Queen* at Thorn's quay. Its hold door has a master lock, and its key is on Ervyn Rothalen in the cabin.
The player picks the lock, paralyses Ervyn and takes the key, or kills him. In the hold the bound dead have no master
until the ship's binding-stone is broken. The player breaks it. *Leads on:* the hold's papers name the house's seat:
the hall under the well at Kaochalit. Neeja, if alive, is waiting on the quay and speaks first (section 6).

**13. Under the Well** (Kaochalit, a ruined Velothi settlement on the mesa south of Tsonac). The order teaches Sun
Fire (Restoration 75). A Dunmer herbalist keeps the outer tomb, and she is the house's watch. The lower halls hold
the flu-dead, and Morvayn has raised a priestess of Mephala to guard the hall under the well. He sits there with the
last of his house. The player comes in by the front, through the herbalist and the halls, or by the back way if Neeja
opened it. The fight is the line's hardest: the priestess, then Morvayn, who raises the fallen dead again until he is
killed. When Morvayn falls, every dead thing he bound falls with him. *Leads on:* the ending (section 7).

## 6. Neeja's offer

On Thorn's quay, after the ship, Neeja speaks first. She has left the house. She knows the back way into Kaochalit,
down the old well, and she will open it if the player gives her the urn to break. If the player gave her the urn in
quest 8, she opens the way without asking.

- **Give her the urn.** She breaks it and Keel-Vaxa's soul goes to the Hist. She opens the back way, so the player
  comes into the hall under the well with the herbalist and half the halls behind them. She leaves the marsh. In the
  order, the Eldest's lie is out: Okan-Zeel, if alive, gives up the Eldest's staff (section 7).
- **Keep it.** The player goes in by the front through every hall, the herbalist included. Neeja goes back to the
  house and stands in the hall under the well beside Morvayn as a battlemage, a second hard fight. Okan-Zeel keeps her
  office.
- **Neeja dead.** If the player killed her at Hiss-Bank Camp, there is no offer and the player goes in by the front.

## 7. The ending

Morvayn's death ends the house. The *Saltrice Queen* does not sail. The Vicecanon of Thorn takes the Rothalens'
stall and house in Thorn, and Ervyn, if alive, is put to the Owing. The bound dead fall and the order stakes them.
The threat to the living goes with it: no buyer across the Tear road has a source left, and the order's grave-singers
go back to every grave-ground the house opened.

| Outcome | It gives | It costs |
|---|---|---|
| **The urn broken** (Neeja's way) | the rank Eldest's Hand. The Eldest's staff from Okan-Zeel, who steps down: a staff with our custom enchantment, Sisei-Xul's Rest, which lays any undead it strikes and heals the bearer by part of the harm done. The tree-minders' hall becomes the player's home, with its enchanter and tables. Neeja's parting gift: Morvayn's own tome of Command Undead, taken from the house | Okan-Zeel leaves the hall. Her trainer's teaching of Restoration is gone, and the order's grave-singers are cool to the player for breaking a binding. Keel-Vaxa's soul is free, whatever he was |
| **The urn kept** | the rank Eldest's Hand. Okan-Zeel names the player her heir and gives them the same staff. Free training in Restoration and Alteration from her and Akeeta-Sul up to a set level | Kaochalit is fought through from the front with Neeja beside Morvayn. Her body gives her robes and tomes. The urn stays in its niche, and the order keeps what it did to one man |

In both, the player keeps every spell and recipe the order taught, and each branch's trainer gives cheaper training
from then on. If Okan-Zeel died at Ixu-Maan, the order's second grave-singer gives the staff and the hall in her
place.

## 9. What it needs, and what it changes elsewhere

### What it asks of the world

- **The bound dead:** Skyrim's draugr and skeletons, and the Argonian skeleton and zombie of King of the Murkmire's
  creature set under our names. A raised body wears its stake on its back (a xul-vaat mesh from Black Marsh &
  Valenwood, set as a worn item).
- **Grave-iron:** a forge recipe for an iron spike (a dagger mesh of ours under our name), with a script-free
  weakness: the raised captain of quest 6 and the risen of quest 13 take great harm from it, given as an enchantment
  on the spike.
- **The *Saltrice Queen*:** the ship interior of Thorn's first source, moored at the quay. Its hold and cabin are
  that interior's rooms.
- **The house's mark:** a spider in a circle, painted on crates and carved on a stone and a key bow (existing
  carved-stone and key meshes under our names).
- **Items:** the Minder's Staff and the Eldest's staff (Animated Armoury quarterstaff), Grave-Iron Claws (Animated
  Armoury claws), Sign of Cipactli's armour, Skyrim's urn, soul gems and welkynd stone, each under our names.

### What each quest needs of its places

### What it asks of the ceiling

- **A potion the player made, handed in.** In quests 1 and 2 the hand-in takes a potion or poison the player made
  with a named effect (Restore Health, Damage Health). The simplest build is one generic condition: the player holds a
  made potion carrying that effect.
- **A fade after which a person is gone.** In quest 2 Nadras drinks and the screen fades. He is switched off and his
  stake switched on. The owner asked for this fade in several factions' joins. It is built once and used here too.

Every other step is inside the ceiling. A taught spell is a stage's result. A step that needs it is passed by a
named thing taken or a named person dead, and the spell is the way through that serves it best.
