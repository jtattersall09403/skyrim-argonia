# The Sisei-Xul: the plot

This page is the plot of the Argonian order of healers, makers and grave-singers. It was written on 2026-11-16 to
the owner's notes in [the factions overhaul](../../../../docs/phases/new-worldspace-plan/nw-05c-factions-overhaul.md)
and revised on 2026-11-17 to his story explorer notes on the other lines. Its rival's plot is
[House Cardruhn](house-cardruhn.md): the two pages tell one war from its two sides. Every quest is set in a place
whose record and source place give what it needs. Each thing a quest asks of a place says in brackets which source
place gives it and how we know: what that source place's buildings, interiors and placed pieces really are, and which
pieces are added to it because the source has none.
Everything is inside the quest ceiling ([quest-system](../../../../.claude/skills/quest-system/SKILL.md)), with the
additions listed in section 9.

**Words.** *Sisei-Xul*: the order's name, "sprout and death" in Jel. *Sisei* is the sprout, the first month of the
Argonian year after the sun (`topics/sky-moons-calendar.md:22`), and *xul* is death, as in *bok-xul*, "bowl of
death" (`topics/material-culture.md:249`) and Xul-Thuxis, "Snake-Means-Death City" (`regions/murkmire.md:63`). The
two words name the whole run of a life, from hatching to the grave, which the Jel calendar follows from Sisei to
Xulomaht, "the Deceased" (`topics/sky-moons-calendar.md:31-37`). *Hist*: the great thinking trees of Black Marsh.
*Tree-minder*: the one who tends a village's Hist (`topics/material-culture.md:226`). *Grave-singer*: the one who
tends the dead, stakes them and sings their last song (`topics/material-culture.md:232`). *Xul-vaat*: a grave-stake,
carved with the dead one's whole story and driven through the body into the mud
(`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:123-129`). *Bog blight*: what rises when a stake is pulled. *House Cardruhn*:
a Dunmer trading house of Thorn, whose masters are necromancers. *The Owing*: the marsh's sentence, a counted number
of months of owed work in place of a cell (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:72`). *The Xeech-Leel*: "the People of the
New Beginning", the party that has ruled the province since the An-Xileel fell with Umbriel (lore §1). *The
Toh-Reel-Ka*: "the Secret Warriors", the Xeech-Leel's secret arm (lore §2.5). *Vicecanon*: a city magistrate, chosen
by the city.

## 1. What the faction is

The Sisei-Xul is the Argonian order of marsh medicine and the rites of the dead. Its members brew potions and
poisons, forge and enchant, heal the hurt, ease the dying who cannot be healed, and stake the dead so that nothing
can raise them.

The order is old and loose, as Argonian things are. It is the province's one native order of magic: the foreign
guilds are gone, but the tree-minders have always taught their pupils (**X**, from `gap-register.md` G4.10, whose rule
is about foreign guilds). Its mother hall is the tree-minders' hall in Helstrom, the interior capital that no invader
has taken. It has branches at Aroxhu, where its alchemists work beside the great Hist of the Clay-Mound People, and
at Tsonac, where the Stone-Sleepers' minders teach the flesh spells of stone. Its skills are Alchemy, Smithing,
Enchanting, Alteration and Restoration, with Destruction's poisons for a fight. Our mods add no craft beyond Skyrim's
three, so its making is smithing, alchemy and enchanting. None of its places is hostile to anyone.

Its enemy is House Cardruhn of Thorn, which raises the Argonian dead and sells them to northern estates as thralls. To
an Argonian, a body pulled from its stake and made to work for a Dunmer master is the slave-coffles of Thorn over
again. Raising the dead breaks no Argonian law, but a soul kept from its Hist is the Toh-Reel-Ka's business (**X**,
reconciliation item 6), so the Cardruhns work quietly and the order fights them itself. Joining gives the player the
order's tables, trainers and teachers, a spell or a recipe in most quests, and at its end the house broken and its
trade in the dead ended.

## 2. The truth under the story

**What the player is shown.** Graves are being opened. Stakes are pulled at night, the dead are gone, and bog
blights walk where they lay. A walled-up gallery of dead labourers at a salt mine is broken into from the far side.
Dead men who were never staked rise in the dark. The tracks lead to the Cardruhns, a Dunmer house of Thorn that
trades in saltrice and buys old Velothi pieces from scavengers.

**Why the stakes.** Corpses rot fast in the marsh, so foreign necromancers seldom bother with it
(`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:136`). Life clings to a grave-stake, and a stake can feed necromancy
(`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:129`). The Cardruhns' old master found that a body raised with its own stake
bound to it does not rot. So the Cardruhns need the body and its stake together, and an Argonian burial ground is a
crop to it. The salt mine's dead were never staked, which made them the easiest to raise, and a digger of the house
tried them first. **X**, from the two cited lines.

**Where the dead go.** The house binds the raised dead and ships them out of Thorn on its own ship, the *Saltrice
Queen*, north up the coast to estates that want hands that need no food. Its papers write each body as an Owing that
never ends, so the sale passes the crossings as a sale of Owings, the only trade in people the province has in 4E 201
(**X**, reconciliation item 2). The Barsaebic Ayleids did the same thing first: when their Argonian slaves rose,
their necromancer kings had them killed and raised as guards (`topics/lost-peoples.md:108-109`).

**Who began it.** Morvayn Cardruhn, the house's master, bought Argonian captives from the slaver brig *Silt Bride*
when he was young, late in the Third Era, when the Dres still raided the Thorn coast (lore §10.1). She sank off Thorn
with her captives shackled in the hold, and her captain's log names the families who paid him for them, the
Cardruhns among them . Morvayn learnt his art in a Telvanni tower and came
home to Thorn to sell dead what he could no longer sell alive.

**What the order hides.** Argonians bind souls too. A blasphemer is punished by being bound to an urn, so that his
soul is denied its return to the Hist (`topics/sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md:130-134`). In the mother hall at
Helstrom there is such an urn. The First Grave-Singer says it holds a Hist-poisoner. It holds Keel-Vaxa, an Archein
of the Thorn coast. In the 4E 160s he sold Morvayn Cardruhn the Owings of forty of his own village's debtors for
gold, and Morvayn sold the Owings on to House Ulvarys' factor at Thorn (**X**, reconciliation items 2 and 10). The
First Grave-Singer bound Keel-Vaxa herself, out of grief: her clutch-sister was among the forty. His granddaughter,
Neeja, now works for
the house. The player learns this in quest 8.

## 4. Joining

**The rumours.** In Thorn, Tsonac, Inoja, Aroxhu and Helstrom, people asked about healers, work or the dead speak of
the grave-singers. "If you can brew, the Sisei-Xul want hands. A kiln fire at Arorha burnt three potters, and their
tree-minder there has more burns than salve." The first such line starts **Find the Sisei-Xul**, and its marker
sits on Arorha's tree-minder's hut. Arorha has a boat stop on the Archon and Inoja runs.

**Being let in.** Quest 1 is the test the owner set: gather named ingredients, make a healing potion and give it to
the tree-minder. When the potters drink it, she offers the player a place. The test is easy by design: the
ingredients grow on a shore road of mudcrabs and snakes, and any character can make the potion at Alchemy 15.

**The order's history and its name.** Once the player has joined, Deegla-Tsei answers the topic *Sisei-Xul*: "The
tree-minders have always taught healing and the grave-song to whoever sat with them. When the Dres ruled this coast,
their slavers dug up our dead as well as taking the living, so the minders of Helstrom, Aroxhu and Tsonac began to
stake every grave and to teach each other. They named themselves for the two ends of a life: Sisei is the month a
hatchling comes out, and xul is death." (**X**, from the Dres slave coast, lore §10.1, and the calendar cited above.)

**The door shut.** Find the Sisei-Xul fails, with a journal entry, when the player finishes **The Pulled Stakes**,
quest 4 of House Cardruhn's line, in which they pull the stakes of Tazhul's dead. The order then knows their face.

## 5. The acts

The line is thirteen quests in three acts, as long as a guild line of Skyrim's. Act I teaches the order's work:
heal, ease and stake. Act II is the war of the stakes, which climbs from the order's own country into the house's
ruins and wrecks. Act III carries the war to the house and ends it. The line has one choice, met in two halves:
in quest 8 the player decides what to do with the urn, and in quest 12 Neeja makes her offer (section 6).

Danger climbs with the line. Act I is played in the clay country's villages and on the shore of Hatandros Bight (D0 to
D2). The world has no place of band D1 for this line, so it opens at D2, on a shore road of mudcrabs and snakes
that a new character can walk. Act II goes into the Velothi ruins and the sea off Thorn (D3 to D4). Act III ends in
the deepest ruin on the Chixathe Mesa (D5). Skill checks climb with it: a beginner's potion in quest 1, an easy lock
or pocket in quest 3, an adept lock in quest 7 and an expert lock in quest 8, a master lock and Paralyze at the end.

Most quests begin with the player being taught one spell or recipe, and one step of the quest is built for it among
other steps that lead into each other. Every objective has a marker on the area to search, never on the exact thing.
Where the player may fight or sneak, the journal says so, and the guards stand few and apart. The Act I quests pay
fixed gold (50, 75 and 100) for boat and rootworm fares. Every place is reached by short walks from a boat stop
(Arorha, Thorn, Tsonac, Inoja) or the rootworm stops at Aroxhu and Helstrom; each quest says how.

### Act I: the healers

**1. The Burnt Kilns** (Arorha, a clay-mound village on Hatandros Bight). A kiln fire at the water's edge has burnt
three potters, who lie on the bed-mats of the tree-minder's hut (Arorha's main source is Keeba Hollow in King of the
Murkmire, a village of mud huts with a tree-minder's hut interior, but that hut has no bed and no alchemy table, so
three bedrolls and an alchemy table are added from King of the Murkmire's other huts and camps, and the kilns are
added because the source has none). (Arorha's quay is where the Brethren's and the Topal Bay Company's lines set out
for Skeith Isle.) Deegla-Tsei, Arorha's tree-minder, speaks first at her door. She teaches the player what two plants
do (the stage makes them know every effect of swamp fungal pod and butterfly wing, both Skyrim's own and added along
the road; together they make a potion of Restore Health and nothing else) and asks for three of each. The journal says
"gather them on the shore road north of the village", and the marker covers the road. Mudcrab swarms and giant snakes
(Skyrim's mudcrabs; Curse of Cipactli's serpent) live along it. The player fights them, shoots them from the mounds or
picks round them. They make the potion at the tree-minder's alchemy table (Alchemy, any level) and bring it to her.
She gives it to the worst burnt potter, pays the player 50 gold and offers them a place in the order. *Leads on:*
Deegla-Tsei says that a dying man at the order's house in Aroxhu has asked for a grave-singer and cannot wait. Aroxhu
is a walk west, inland; the journal marks the order's house.

**2. The Painless Cup** (Aroxhu, the Clay-Mound People's mound-town). Nadras Velsa is an old Dunmer trader dying of a
wasting sickness in the order's house, where its alchemists tend him (Aroxhu's source is Lilmoth in King of the
Murkmire, a city of Argonian and Imperial buildings with 46 interiors, among them Healer Shuvip's Hut, which has two
alchemy tables, linen sheets and drying herbs and serves as the order's house). No potion will save him. The order's
alchemist by the Hist teaches the player that imp stool and nightshade together make a draught that stops the heart in
sleep (the stage makes them know both, Skyrim's own; together they make a poison of Damage Health). Imp stool grows on
the mound terraces (Skyrim's plant, added there because Aroxhu's record lists none), and the journal says "look on the
terraces". The player makes the poison at the alchemy table beside the Hist (Alchemy; Lilmoth has no great Hist, so
the Hist, its root pool and this table are added from Black Marsh & Valenwood's Hist pieces and King of the Murkmire's
alchemy table, as Aroxhu's record lists). Before he drinks, Nadras asks one thing: that he be staked like an Argonian
when he is dead, "so that Morvayn Cardruhn's people never stand me up again". He was the Cardruhns' factor for forty
years. He drinks, the screen fades and he is gone, and a fresh grave-stake stands below the mounds (the fade after
which a person is gone; the stake is a stand-in, Curse of Cipactli's carved Xanmeer post under our name, switched on
away from the player). The alchemist pays the player 75 gold. A player who freed Tsleeksa in the Chainbreakers' 7, Out
by Sea, can have three imp stools from her own sack instead of picking them. *Leads on:* his last words go into the
journal. The alchemist hears them and sends the player to Coldbrine Mine, south-west of Thorn, where a woman wants her
dead staked and the overseer refuses. Coldbrine is a walk north along the bight from Arorha, past Marshgate Watch.

**3. The Walled Gallery** (Coldbrine Mine, a rock-salt working south-west of Thorn). Tzeeh wants her great-uncle and
the other bonded labourers who died in the old shaft's collapse brought out and staked. The overseer walled the
gallery up and carries the key to its door (Coldbrine's older shaft takes its interior from Whistling Mine in Skyrim,
a mine by water whose one interior is timbered galleries, and the walled gallery is that interior's far end behind one
added locked door). The player is taught Turn Undead before they go. They lift the key from the overseer (Pickpocket,
an easy pocket), pick the wall door's lock (an easy lock), or go round by the brine pools outside, where the old giant
mudcrab has dug a hole into the gallery's far end (Whistling Mine has no second way in, so the hole is one door added
in the rock of the brine-pool hollow, built from the mine's own cave pieces). In the gallery the dead are risen (King
of the Murkmire's Argonian skeletons, under our name). A digger has broken in from the far side to raise them: his
side of this gallery is House Cardruhn's 1, The Demonstration. (The diggers locked in the mine's bunk shed belong to
House Ulvarys; the Chainbreakers' line frees them.) The journal says the player may fight the dead or turn them, and
"search the walled gallery". Past the dead lies the satchel of a Dunmer digger the dead killed, with a soul gem and a
silver token of a spider in a circle (a Skyrim amulet mesh under our name; the sign is in its name and the journal).
*Leads on:* Tzeeh pays the player 100 gold and tells them the spider in a circle is the mark the Cardruhns put on
their crates in Thorn's market. Deegla-Tsei makes the player a Leaf-Hand by letter and sends them to Akeeta-Sul at
Tsonac, a boat ride up the river from Thorn, whose hamlets have graves the Cardruhns would want.

### Act II: the war of the stakes

**4. Under the Oldest Foundation** (Tsonac, the Stone-Sleepers' terraced city; Tselan, a stone hamlet on the river
west of it). Akeeta-Sul teaches the player Stoneflesh, the first of the Stone-Sleepers' stone spells (Alteration 25).
Tselan's quarry gang has broken into a sealed Velothi chamber under the hamlet's oldest foundation, and one of them
went in (Tselan's sources have no interior but the elder's hut, so the chamber is a small interior added from the
Velothi tomb pieces of Redeyn Ancestral Tomb in Journey to Baan Malur, which are corridors, altars, ash pits and
bones). The elder says he was a hired man nobody knew. Tselan is a walk west along the river. Inside, the chamber's
skeletons are up (Skyrim's own skeletons, as the Velothi dead), and the hired man is prising a welkynd stone from the
chamber's wall (Skyrim's welkynd stone, set in an Ayleid welkynd holder from Black Marsh & Valenwood and added in the
chamber). He is the house's. The player fights the dead with Stoneflesh on them, turns them or slips past them, and
kills the hired man or takes the stone while his back is turned (Sneak). *Leads on:* the stone goes back to
Akeeta-Sul, who pays 150 gold. She admits, unasked, that she once sold Ervyn Cardruhn grave-goods for gold. She sends
the player to Tazhul, an egg station a long walk south of Tsonac, where two eggs have failed and the egg-minder speaks
of a curse.

**5. Drag-Marks to Joxhi** (Tazhul, a Root-Folk egg station in the marsh west of Inoja; Joxhi, a clay-diggers' village
north of Inoja). The curse is the dead. Tazhul's stakes on the trap-line path have been pulled (the stakes are the
Cipactli stand-ins, laid flat; Tazhul's main source, a small Argonian camp of huts and posts near Loreisian in King of
the Murkmire, has no interior, so the path is open ground and its trap line is added), and bog blights walk the path
(Curse of Cipactli's bog blight). The egg-minder teaches the player Detect Dead, so that they can see the blights
where they lie low in the reeds. The journal says "search the trap-line path". The player lays the blights with Turn
Undead or weapons. On the path north lies one dropped stake, and the diggers of Joxhi, a short walk on, tell the
player that strangers carry bundles into the buried hall the diggers broke into and will not go back down (Joxhi's
source is Root Whisper Village in King of the Murkmire, which has Ayleid ruin pieces and an Ayleid hall interior,
Vakka Bok, a hall of carved stone walls, balconies and busts that serves as the buried hall). Three of the
house's men camp in the hall: two watch the stair and one sleeps by the stakes. The journal says the player may fight
them or go quietly, and "search the buried hall". The player takes the bundle of Tazhul's stakes (one named thing) by
force or by stealth. *Leads on:* the egg-minder takes the stakes back and pays 150 gold. A grave-singer's work
follows, and Okan-Zeel asks for the stranger who did it. The journal marks the tree-minders' hall at Helstrom. A
rootworm runs to Helstrom from Aroxhu, a walk west of Tazhul.

**6. Grave-Iron** (Tsonac; Balormaak, a ruined Velothi town on the eastern mesa). Okan-Zeel sends the player back to
Tsonac to learn grave-iron from Gam-Meesh. A spike of it, driven through a raised body, holds it down. The stage opens
the recipe at his forge (Tsonac's main source, Skyrim's Windhelm, has a blacksmith's forge, anvil, grindstone and
armour workbench, which serve as it): two iron ingots and a salt pile from Coldbrine (Smithing; the salt pile is
Skyrim's ingredient, which Coldbrine's record gives). The spike is a dagger of ours under our name, with Skyrim's
silver-weapon keyword, so the undead take extra harm from it as they do from Skyrim's silver swords. Skeletons walk
the cellars of Balormaak, a walk east of Tsonac onto the mesa, and Ayiin's children have seen torches there at night.
The house has raised a Velothi captain in the bell-tower crypt to guard a cache (Balormaak's main source is a ruined
Dunmer and Imperial settlement with no interior, so the cellars and the bell-tower crypt are interiors added from the
Velothi tomb pieces of Redeyn Ancestral Tomb in Journey to Baan Malur, the silted cellars with water to the knee set
into them; the captain is the record's boss, Skyrim's skeleton under our name). The player forges three spikes and
goes in. The journal says the player may fight through the skeletons in the cellars or sneak past them in the silt.
The captain takes grave harm from a spike and little from anything else. In the cache are Velothi bronze and a letter
of the house about a log in a wreck off Thorn. A player who freed Huzei in the Chainbreakers' 1, The Salt Shed, can
get the sack of Coldbrine salt for the spikes from him at Thorn's west gate; Gam-Meesh also sells it. *Leads on:*
Okan-Zeel makes the player a Stake-Keeper. She has heard of the *Silt Bride*: if her captain's log names the
Cardruhns, the Vicecanon of Thorn can close their stall in the market.

**7. The Slaver's Log** (Wreck of the Silt Bride, a slaver brig off Thorn; Thorn, the grassland border city). The
order teaches Waterbreathing; an Argonian player has no need of it. The brig lies on the sea floor a short swim off
Thorn's coast, ringed by slaughterfish (the broken hull is Pilgrims Trench in Skyrim, a ship hull of 48 pieces on a
rocky shore, and the inside is Icerunner, another Skyrim ship, whose one real interior is a hold of crates, barrels
and hay with a table, benches and shelves at one end, which serves as both the hold and the stern cabin; the sea-floor
shelf, the shackles, the strongbox and the log are added). Her drowned crew (Skyrim's skeletons) and her shackled dead
(King of the Murkmire's Argonian skeletons, with Curse of Cipactli's shackles about the hold) walk the hold. The
player fights them, or lays the shackled dead with Turn Undead and grave-iron, and takes the log from the drowned
captain's strongbox in the stern cabin. The captain carries its key, or the player picks it (an adept lock). The log
names Morvayn Cardruhn as a buyer (a Skyrim journal under our name). The player takes it to the Vicecanon's factor at
the Vicecanons' hall in Thorn (Thorn's own source has only a ship interior, so the Vicecanons' hall is an interior
added from Imperial and Dunmer pieces, as Thorn's record lists). He reads it and says it is too old to bring a living
man before the Vicecanon, but enough to close the Cardruhns' stall (Thorn hangs no one: reconciliation item 12).
*Leads on:* the factor shuts the stall the next day: its trader and goods are switched off. The journal says the
Cardruhns will answer. Okan-Zeel writes for the player to come to Helstrom at once.

**8. The Empty Niche** (Helstrom, the interior capital; Hiss-Bank Camp, a Naga camp on the western river bank). The
house has answered. While the player was in the sea, its people broke into the tree-minders' hall and took an urn
from its niche (a King of the Murkmire Xanmeer urn under our name; Helstrom's own source has two interiors, one an
Argonian house, so the hall is an interior added from the Hist-root pieces King of the Murkmire's Lilmoth Roots is
built of, as Helstrom's record lists). This is where the line's choice begins, and the
player meets it in this order.

- *In the hall.* Okan-Zeel tells the player that the urn holds a Hist-poisoner whose soul must never go free. A
  raftsman saw the thieves pole west to Hiss-Bank Camp, where a tribeless Naga band waits for the house's boat. The
  journal says "recover the urn from the Naga camp at Hiss-Bank", and the marker covers the camp, a walk west along
  the river bank.
- *At the camp.* The Naga (Curse of Cipactli's Naga) are hired and attack strangers. The journal says the player may
  fight them or go in quietly. The urn is in a chest with an expert lock in the den of the band's seer (Hiss-Bank's
  main source is Brucas Leap Redoubt in Skyrim, a Forsworn camp of cave and mine pieces with bone-and-stick fences and
  one interior, which serves as the seer's den; the bank's huts come from the Argonian camp near Loreisian in King of
  the Murkmire). The player picks the chest or takes its key from the seer's body.
- *At the landing.* When the player takes the urn, Neeja's marker shows at the camp's added landing, where she waits
  apart from the band. She is unarmed and speaks first. She tells the player whose soul is in the urn: Keel-Vaxa, her
  grandfather, who sold the Owings of forty of his own people to Morvayn and was bound by Okan-Zeel herself. "If the
  Toh-Reel-Ka knew what your First Grave-Singer keeps in her niche, she would be the one in a cell." She asks the
  player to let her break it.
- *Give her the urn.* She breaks it (the Xanmeer urn's broken mesh, switched on in its place) and Keel-Vaxa's soul
  goes to the Hist. She gives the player her own tome of Conjure Familiar and says she will be on Thorn's quay when
  the player takes the house's ship. Okan-Zeel gives no reward when the player comes back empty-handed, and her regard
  falls.
- *Keep the urn.* Neeja goes back to the house. The player brings the urn to Okan-Zeel, who pays 300 gold.
- *Kill Neeja.* She drops her robes, her conjuration tomes and 100 gold. There is no offer in quest 12.

*Leads on:* asked about Keel-Vaxa, Okan-Zeel says what he was and admits nothing else. The journal records that she
lied about the poisoner. Whatever the player chose, she sends them to the order's enchanter in the hall.

**9. The Humming Door** (Helstrom; Sethiral, a ruined Velothi holding at Yina). The order's enchanter gives the player
a blade enchanted with Turn Undead and asks them to break it at the arcane enchanter in the hall. That teaches them
the enchantment (Enchanting). Sethiral's sealed door hums at night (Sethiral's source is Redeyn Ancestral Tomb in
Journey to Baan Malur, a Velothi tomb whose one interior of corridors, altars and ash pits has about sixteen doors,
among them a gate and two jail doors, so locking one of them splits it into the upper halls and the lower halls behind
the humming door; the hum is a sound we add). The house has bound the household's ghost behind it to guard its second
stake store, and Yina's eldest will pay 300 gold to anyone who opens it quietly. Yina is a walk south of Arorha's boat
stop. The player enchants their own weapon with Turn Undead and goes down past the ghosts in the upper halls (Velothi
ghosts, section 9) and the river crocodiles at the landing (Curse of Cipactli's). Behind the door the player finds the
household's ghost, the stakes and the store's keeper, a Dunmer hireling of the house. The player fights the ghost or
turns it, takes the stakes and, from the keeper, a key with the spider in a circle on its bow (a Skyrim key under our
name). *Leads on:* the enchanter knows the spider from the cellar door at Ovezza, carved with the same sign and barred
from inside. The journal marks Ovezza, a walk west of Thorn.

**10. The Barred Cellar** (Ovezza, a ruined Velothi town on the bay). The order teaches Circle of Protection
(Restoration). Ovezza's ghosts walk the market square and slaughterfish swim in the water over the drowned quay.
Behind the market, the cellar door with Mephala's spider carved on it opens to the key (Ovezza's source is Redeyn
Ancestral Tomb in Journey to Baan Malur, whose one tomb interior of corridors and altar niches serves as the barred
cellar and the house's hall, behind a door whose spider carving is added). Behind it is the house's hall, where its
bound dead stand in rows (the Argonian skeletons of quest 3) and a wraith guards the inner door (the record's boss, a
Velothi ghost). The player raises the Circle at the stair's foot so that the bound dead cannot close on them, and
fights the dead or slips past them to the wraith. In the hall's inner room lies a sealed letter of Morvayn's. It names
the night the house will raid the order's grave-ground at Ixu-Maan. *Leads on:* Okan-Zeel makes the player a
Grave-Singer and goes to Ixu-Maan herself. She asks the player to follow when they can, and the journal marks the
outpost, a walk south of Helstrom.

### Act III: the end of the trade

**11. The Egg Pools** (Ixu-Maan, an egg-tending outpost south of Helstrom). The order teaches Poison Spray
(Destruction, Magic Expanded), as old marsh fighters used it. The raid has begun when the player arrives. Naga hired
from Salt-Tooth Camp swarm the clay nest-pit (Curse of Cipactli's Naga; Ixu-Maan's source is a Nordic and Dwemer
homestead with one watch-hut interior and no nest-pit, so the clay nest-pit is added, as the record lists), and
Dalvela Cardruhn's bound dead walk among the grave-stakes (the Cipactli stand-ins). Okan-Zeel stands at the nest-pit
with two grave-singers, placed there and fighting. The player kills the Naga at the pit, lays the dead and kills the
Naga scout-leader (the record's boss). If Okan-Zeel dies, the line goes on without her: the second grave-singer speaks
in her place. Dalvela is not here. Among the dead is a captain of the house carrying the *Saltrice Queen*'s orders.
*Leads on:* the orders say the house's crew will ship a hold of bound dead north on the next sailing from Thorn, and
that the dead wait at Kharraluhl. The journal marks both places. A boat runs to Thorn from Inoja by Archon, or the
rootworm to Aroxhu and a walk north.

**12. The Thrall Ship** (Kharraluhl, a ruined Velothi settlement north of the bay; Thorn). The order teaches Paralyze
(Alteration 75). Kharraluhl is a walk west of Thorn. The house's diggers camp among its upper walls, and its lower
halls are full of bound Argonian dead in rows, waiting to be carried to the ship (Kharraluhl's main source is Sathil
in Solstheim Expand, a ruined Dunmer town of 327 buildings whose interiors include Sathil Manor and Sathil Ancestral
Tomb, which serve as the lower halls and the household shrine; the diggers are the record's scavenger crew). The
player fights the diggers or sneaks past them, lays the bound dead or paralyses the diggers who guard them, and takes
the house's bundle of stakes from the household shrine. Then they go aboard the *Saltrice Queen* at Thorn's quay
(Thorn's main source, Thorn in Black Marsh North, has one interior, a ship with a hold of barrels, a strongbox and an
apothecary's satchel, which serves as the *Saltrice Queen*, and the master lock on its door is added). Ervyn Cardruhn
stands on the quay with two of the crew, and the ship's door has a master lock whose key he carries. The player picks
the lock, paralyses Ervyn and lifts the key from him while he is held (Pickpocket), or kills him. In the hold the
bound dead rise against the player, and a binding-stone stands among them (a Skyrim black soul gem mesh under our
name). The player lays the dead and takes the stone. *Leads on:* the hold's orders name the house's seat: the hall
under the well at Kaochalit, on the mesa south of Tsonac. If Neeja is alive, she is waiting on the quay and speaks
first (section 6).

**13. Under the Well** (Kaochalit, a ruined Velothi settlement on the mesa south of Tsonac). The order teaches Sun
Fire (Restoration, Dawnguard's). Kaochalit is a walk from Tsonac's boat stop. A Dunmer herbalist tends a bench in the
outer tomb, and she is the house's watch (Kaochalit's source has only one interior, so the outer tomb with the
herbalist's bench is a small interior added from Redeyn Ancestral Tomb's Velothi tomb pieces). The lower halls are
full of the flu-dead, and Morvayn has raised a priestess of Mephala to guard the hall under the well (Kaochalit's
source is Redeyn Ancestral Tomb in Journey to Baan Malur, whose one tomb interior serves as the lower halls with the
hall under the well at its far end; the priestess is the record's boss, a Velothi ghost). He sits there with the last
of his house. The player comes in by the front, past the herbalist and through the dead in the halls, or by the back
way if Neeja opened it (Redeyn's interior has no second way in, so the back way is one locked door added at the well's
foot into the far end of the interior). The fight is the line's hardest: the priestess, then Morvayn, who raises the
fallen dead again until the player kills him. When Morvayn falls, the dead he raised fall with him, as Skyrim's raised
dead do when their caster dies. *Leads on:* the ending (section 7).

## 6. Neeja's offer

The choice that began at Hiss-Bank Camp ends on Thorn's quay, in quest 12. The player meets it in this order.

1. The player comes out of the *Saltrice Queen* with the binding-stone. Neeja is standing on the quay, by the marker
  the stage has put on her, and speaks first. She has left the house.
2. She knows the back way into Kaochalit, a door at the foot of the old well, and carries its key.
3. **If the player gave her the urn in quest 8**, she gives them the key without asking anything, and the journal says
  the back way is open.
4. **If the player kept the urn**, it is back in its niche at Helstrom. Neeja asks the player to bring it to her here.
  The journal marks the niche. The player takes the urn from the hall (a case with an expert lock, in the
  grave-singers' sight: taking it unseen is a Sneak and Lockpicking job; if a grave-singer sees it, it is theft and a
  bounty in Helstrom). If they bring it, she breaks it and gives them the key. If they tell her no, she goes back to
  Morvayn.
5. **If Neeja is dead**, nobody waits on the quay, and the player goes in by the front.

What each road leaves:

- **The urn broken.** Keel-Vaxa's soul goes to the Hist. The player comes into the hall under the well by the back
  way, with the herbalist and half the halls behind them. Neeja leaves the marsh. The First Grave-Singer's lie is out:
  Okan-Zeel, if alive, gives up the First Singer's staff (section 7).
- **The urn kept.** The player goes in by the front through every hall, the herbalist included. Neeja stands in the
  hall under the well beside Morvayn as a battlemage, a second hard fight. Okan-Zeel keeps her office.

## 7. The ending

Morvayn's death ends the house. The *Saltrice Queen* does not sail. The Vicecanon of Thorn takes the Cardruhns' stall
and house in Thorn, and Ervyn, if alive, is put to a long Owing in Thorn's saltrice fields. The order stakes the bound
dead it finds. The threat to the living goes with it: no estate in the north has a source of bound dead left, and the
order's grave-singers go back to every grave-ground the house opened.

| Outcome | It gives | It costs |
|---|---|---|
| **The urn broken** (Neeja's way) | the rank First Singer's Hand. the First Singer's staff from Okan-Zeel, who steps down: a staff with our custom enchantment, Sisei-Xul's Rest, which lays any undead it strikes and heals the bearer by part of the harm done. The tree-minders' hall becomes the player's home, with its enchanter and tables. Neeja's parting gift: Morvayn's own tome of Command Undead (Magic Expanded), taken from the house | Okan-Zeel leaves the hall and is switched off: her teaching of Restoration is gone, and the order's grave-singers' regard for the player falls for breaking a binding. Keel-Vaxa's soul is free, whatever he was |
| **The urn kept** | the rank First Singer's Hand. Okan-Zeel names the player her heir and gives them the same staff, and the stage raises their Restoration and Alteration by 5 each. Neeja's body gives her robes and tomes | The player fights through Kaochalit's halls from the front, with Neeja beside Morvayn. The urn stays in its niche, and the order keeps what it did to one man |

In both, the player keeps every spell and recipe the order taught. If Okan-Zeel died at Ixu-Maan, the order's second
grave-singer gives the staff and the hall in her place.

### What it asks of the ceiling

- **A potion the player made, handed in.** In quests 1 and 2 the hand-in takes a potion or poison the player made
  with a named effect (Restore Health, Damage Health). The simplest build is one generic condition: the player holds a
  made potion carrying that effect.
- **A fade after which a person is gone.** In quest 2 Nadras drinks and the screen fades. He is switched off and his
  stake switched on.

Every other step is inside the ceiling. A taught spell is a stage's result. A step that needs it is passed by a
named thing taken or a named person dead, and the spell is the way through that serves it best.

- *Place records:* Coldbrine Mine (the risen salt dead and the crab's hole), Tselan (the welkynd stone and the

  and the back way) take the rows above as promises.
- *The cast roster* takes the people of section 3, with the house's family name Cardruhn.
