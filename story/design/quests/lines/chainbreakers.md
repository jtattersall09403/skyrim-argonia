# The Blackrose Chainbreakers: the line's story

The story of the Chainbreakers' six quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)). A quest
writer turns each outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything. Lore is cited as
`file:line` under `story/lore/`; **X** marks this document's own extrapolation from it (decision 0032). Places are
those the game loads today ([census](../../../../docs/research/bmv/census.md)); form keys are Black Marsh & Valenwood's.

## 1. The faction in a page

**The wrong they fight.** Black Marsh has no jail of its own. Its sentence is the Owing: months of hard work, counted
in nushmeekos, assessed by an arbitrator, worked off and then never mentioned again
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:68`, `:75`, `:198`). Where it works it is kinder than a cell and Argonians will say so
(`:154`). In the south it has drifted. Whoever holds the estate, the crossing or the hiring office now does the
assessing (`:84`); an Owing can be sold on (`:90`), can grow with food, breakage and sickness faster than it is
worked (`:94`), and passes with the household when the debtor dies (`:97`). A person held for years far from their
Hist goes visibly grey, their tribe's colour and markings fading (`:112`), and one who dies there may never get home
to the tree at all (`:119`).

**Who they are.** The Chainbreakers are a few dozen people in and around Blackrose, the lake city that swallows
people (`blackrose.md:9`, `:80`), who get the held out. Their seat is a plain house on the town's north side, and
their patron is **Never-Sold**, who was born owing and discharged it
([cast roster](../36-cast-roster.md) §56). Their oath is three words, "Bones go home", and it covers the living and
the dead alike: the burial work and the freeing are one work (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:122`). Nobody above
them cares: the state's reach ends where the water gets deep ([province-vibe](../../province-vibe.md) §1), the town
keeps its own guard, who treat a theft as a theft whoever is robbed, and the town and the Rose share no government
(`topics/prisons.md:71`).

**How they work (X).** Never-Sold teaches that an Owing stands on three legs, and that it falls when any one is
taken away:

- **the count**: the holder's own reckoning of what is still owed, kept under lock. Writing it down is a habit the
  ex-Archein families and the prison-born took from the Empire, and it is theirs alone
  (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:84` to `:88`; province-vibe §5: "the ledger is the collaborator's tool"). An honest
  village Owing has no count, only witnesses who then forget. The Chainbreakers steal the count, swap it or carry
  it off;
- **the word**: the holder's or keeper's say-so that the debt stands. They talk it round, or buy it at the Owing's
  first price, which by custom closes it for good;
- **the hand**: whoever is paid to fetch a runaway back. They get past it, or break it.

Buying a person out is the quiet way and the oldest, and it stays in the background of every job. The play is the
other two legs: a key lifted from a belt, a lock in the dark, a dead man's satchel fetched out of a swamp full of
teeth, a keeper faced down, a fetcher fought on a channel bank.

**Who they are up against.** Nobody in the line is a villain, and everyone has a case
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:80` to `:82`, "none requires a villain").

- **The Tulm-Varo estate** (X, on gap QA3, `extrapolation/gap-register.md:141`): an ex-Archein family
  (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:26`) with a farm and a landing on Blackrose's north edge. The mistress winters in
  Gideon and is met only in talk. Her keeper, **Hulm-Tei**, holds the count. His case: the boat was truly broken,
  every line he wrote is true, forty people eat because the boats go out, and he was hatched owing on that estate
  himself.
- **The heirs of the Rose**: the prison-born families who hold the old prison a day's march south
  (`topics/prisons.md:23`, `:64`) and keep an outer post on Blackrose's east shore (X, gap QA4). Newcomers owe them
  for shelter (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:136`). Their case, spoken by **Third-Born Tsaxi** at the post and later
  by **Third-Born Xeekh**: the Rose is the only society four hundred people have. It fed them and it keeps the gate
  shut on the dead below (`topics/prisons.md:67`).
- **Ussa-Rekh, the Broker**, at Soulrest's dock, where a ship's berth is bought with somebody's months
  (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:135`). He bought people out for thirty years and now hires out the crews he freed.
  His case: every season's hire buys the next people out of Thorn, where it is worst (`:132`).
- **The fetchers and berth-masters** who are the hand: men doing paid work, who believe a runaway fetched back is
  kinder than what the holder does to those left behind.

**What it costs.** A Chainbreaker caught at a holder's lockbox is a thief under native law, and the sentence for a
thief is an Owing (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:200`). Somebody always pays for a freeing: a keeper, the hands who
stayed, a stranger further down the river, or the Chainbreaker's own purse. The line never offers a way out that
nobody pays for. The faction is poor: what coin it has goes out as first prices.

**What is unsettled about them.**

1. Their acts land on other bound people. In this line the keepers are owing too.
2. Buying people out puts coin in the holders' hands and a price on every debtor. Ussa-Rekh is what the Chainbreakers
   were thirty years ago (X): he founded the work, and he bought Never-Sold out.
3. They do not ask what a person is held for. One of the three at the Rose's post is held for a killing.
4. Never-Sold hides her own past (the line's hidden spine, section 2).
5. The Owing is native and it ends, which no prison does; take it away and the province has nothing
   (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:154` to `:158`). A Nisswo will say that a debt outliving its cause is a sin, and
   that so is a grudge nursed for generations (`:161`).

**Why a player wants in.** The jobs are break-ins, jailbreaks and stand-offs with a cause, each ends in a choice
the player has to live with, and each pays in something a thief can use. The faction is the underdog, its patron is
worth unpicking, and what the player steals is a person's life back.

**The build leaning.** Lockpicking, Pickpocket and Sneak are the faction's own skills, because the count is always
behind a lock, on a belt or under someone's eye. Speech is its second way, the persuader's, because the word can be
turned. Fighting (any weapon skill or Destruction, with Block or armour to live through it) is the third leg's
skill, and an honest one: every hand in the line is one hard, fixed opponent with a fixed reward, the swamp of BC02
and the dead of BC05 are a fighter's ground, and a death buys something the quiet ways cannot, at the price of a
life. Nothing is levelled to the player (decision 0035). Restoration and Alchemy have a small part wherever the
washed-out and the dying are tended. The faction suits a thief first, a talker second and a fighter third, and no
quest shuts any of the three out. Never-Sold's fixed rewards are thieves' things: picks, and vanilla rings and
gloves enchanted for Lockpicking, Sneak and Pickpocket (the record writer names the forms).

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** Never-Sold was the Tulm-Varo estate's keeper of the count before Hulm-Tei. She was hatched
owing there, taught to write small and exact, and kept the count for nine years. In her last two she wrote the first
lines on a boat-hand's leaf: four months for a broken boat, then the food, then a fever. Ussa-Rekh bought her out
nine years ago. She took a new name, broke with him in the year his buying turned into hiring, and has been freeing
people ever since, beginning as far as she can from the leaf she started. She tells no one. BC01 is her own first
line struck out by someone else's hand.

| The truth | Where it is seeded | Where it is found |
|---|---|---|
| Never-Sold kept the estate's count | BC01: her false leaf is "in a keeper's hand"; the first two years of the real leaf are in a smaller hand than the rest; Hulm-Tei says the keeper before him "wrote smaller than I do"; she knew of the keeper's leaf | BC03: her own bound count on the Broker's shelf |
| She wrote the first lines of Rows-For-Another's Owing | BC02: the old woman remembers "the girl who kept the count wept when she wrote my daughter down, and then she wrote it" | BC03: the bound count names the estate and the years |
| Ussa-Rekh founded the work and bought her out | BC02: Tsaxi calls the Chainbreakers "the Broker's children" and Never-Sold will not say why | BC03: Ussa-Rekh says it, and the book proves it |

**The arc.** Each quest is a different kind of job, so that the line never becomes a list of the same three doors.

- **BC01, Four Months for a Boat**: a locked box under a household's eyes. One debtor, one estate. Teaches the
  three legs on the smallest case and ends on the first cost: the keeper is owing too. Carried on: a rank, the
  journal and Rows-For-Another free or gone.
- **BC02, One Out of the Smoke**: an errand into a swamp full of beasts, and then a judgement. First touch of the
  Rose. The choice is among people and not methods, and one of them has killed. Carried on: who went free colours
  later greetings.
- **BC03, The Broker's Mercy** (the mid-line turn): a planned break-in of a house. The enemy is the faction's own
  founder, and his shelf holds the patron's secret. Everything in BC01 reads differently afterwards. Carried on:
  what the player knows, and whether they have told her so.
- **BC04, BC05, BC06** raise the scale from a household to four hundred people, then to the dead, then to what the
  Rose becomes, and they are the line's armed half: an aftermath walked into, a ruin of the restless dead, and the
  last lock.

Earlier choices never change a later quest's stages. They change greetings and single lines, read from stored
numbers. Only the finale's choice carries into the world after the line.

**BC04, Riot Without a Banner.** A rising is coming inside the Rose, among people who want different things: the
prison-born under Third-Born Xeekh, who want the hierarchy kept; the newcomers who owe them, who want it gone; and
those who want the gate to the dead levels opened
([40-factions](../40-factions.md) §39; `topics/prisons.md:64` to `:67`). The rising cannot be staged and is not. It
happens at the Rose, a day south, where the player never stands. The quest is what comes before it, in Blackrose:
three people have come north to the outer post, each needing one act that tips the rising their way, each a
different skill (a key lifted from Xeekh's own lodging; a blade-smuggler on the lake shore, one named and dangerous
fighter, killed; a gate-warden talked into looking away). The first act the player brings home is the one that
counts: a stored number holds it, and the other two askers then say they are too late. The player reports to
Never-Sold in her house; only then, with the player indoors, the post's people are switched for the aftermath. The
player walks back to find it: who is standing, who is not, a body to read, and each survivor's account of what the
player's act did. Nobody tells the player they chose rightly.

**BC05, Bones to the Dirt.** A stone prison of the old Empire stands empty of the living and full of Argonian dead
who never got home (`topics/prisons.md:87` to `:90`). Grave-Singer Ossu will not leave them there. No procession
walks: the player is the procession. Ossu stands at the burial ground; the named bones lie in the ruin among the
restless dead (a fight, a Sneak past or Restoration turned on them). One bundle is already gone: a holder's
toll-keeper at the crossing took it off the last carrier, because the dead still owe, an Owing passing with the
household (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:97`). He is paid, talked round, robbed of it or fought for it. Another
bundle turns out to be a keeper's bones, and the oath says only "Bones go home". At each hand-in Ossu greets the
dead by name and tells the player who they were. This is the line's dungeon. Host: a stone ruin the world already
holds; Black Marsh & Valenwood's Castle and its Cavern (`bmv.castle`, interior `18657D`, with its own Lost Castle
Guards and Wrathmen) fit, pending the place mapping, since White Rose itself has no ground yet.

**BC06, What the Rose Becomes.** After the rising the Rose rests on one thing, the key to the gate over the dead
levels, because whoever keeps that gate shut is owed by everyone who sleeps above it (X, from
`topics/prisons.md:67`). The finale uses every leg at once and with a weapon to hand: the gate key lies in a locked
box, the box key is on its keeper, and the keeper stands among armed heirs, in one place; the player gets it however
they can. If the place mapping confirms Black Marsh & Valenwood's own Rose interior (`01BlackRoseJail`), that is the
place and the player enters the Rose at last; if not, it is the outer post. Then four people in four places each ask
for the key, and the hand-over is the ending: to **Xeekh** (the heirs are recognised and keep their hierarchy); to
**Never-Sold** (the hierarchy is dispersed, the families stay as a people); to **Ossu** (the gate is staked and
sung, the levels become a grave that nobody rules); or to a travelling **Nisswo** (every reckoning at the Rose is
witnessed from now on, no Owing there may be sold or grown, and the custom spreads only where others take it up:
`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:158`). Every end keeps the heirs as a community and the Rose's own record of what
was done there ([40-factions](../40-factions.md) §39). Last, alone with Never-Sold, the player who knows her secret
answers one question of hers, "Will you tell them?": the truth and what is just come apart here, and the journal
does not rule on it. If the answer is yes she lays down her rank and offers the player her desk, which is the only
way the player comes to lead, and it costs the faction its founder twice over.

## 3. The first three quests

Common to all three.

- **Never-Sold** stands in the Chainbreakers' house in Blackrose for the whole line (Black Marsh & Valenwood's House
  interior `15635D`, entered by door `15647F` on the town's north side at 32,-14). She must live: her death fails
  any running quest of the line with the faction's message. Nobody is essential. People stand where they are placed.
- **One working stage, legs as numbers.** After the briefing each quest sits at one working stage. What the player
  achieves on a leg is a stored number or a plain fact (a thing held, a person dead), never a stage of its own, so
  the legs can be done in any order, mixed or abandoned. The lines that carry the result to the person who is
  waiting for it set the stages, and where two results are true at once the outline gives the order in which those
  lines are tried.
- **Taking a thing only writes a journal note.** Each take trigger is open from stage 10. A line that needs the
  thing checks that it is held, however and whenever it was got. A player who holds a thing before the job is given
  is offered the job with a line that says so.
- **A death moves no stage.** Lines check who is alive. Each job is offered only while its people live.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense and say only
  what the player has just done. Skill thresholds are fixed. Each failed Speech check has its own written refusal
  and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms; nothing new is modelled.
- "Night" and "dark" in what people say are their words only; no step depends on the hour.

### BC01. Four Months for a Boat

**What a new player is told, and when.** On first speaking to Never-Sold, before any job, four optional lines, each
two or three sentences: *the Owing* (with nushmeeko glossed: "a month of work nobody thanks you for"); *the grey
ones* ("the Hist is the tree a tribe is hatched under; our colour comes from it, and so does the way home when we
die"); *the Chainbreakers* (the oath, and the three legs in one breath); *what it pays and why I might not* (a
thief's share and Never-Sold's own gifts; and "caught at a holder's box, you will be owing them yourself"). "Not
yet" stays open and free. The job itself is three sentences.

**The hidden truth.** Eleven years ago Rows-For-Another, a boat-hand on the Tulm-Varo estate, holed one of its
boats and was assessed four nushmeekos. The estate's keeper wrote her leaf: first the four months, then her food,
then a fever. Every line since is true and the Owing has outgrown her. The Tulm-Varo trust no free hand with their
count: the keeper is always a debtor hatched on the estate, and whatever any leaf is short at the season's reckoning
is written onto the keeper's own leaf, the last in the box. So a leaf that vanishes, a leaf found "struck by
mistake" and a debtor who runs all land on Hulm-Tei; and if his own leaf is gone too, the shortfall is spread over
every other hand on the estate. Never-Sold knows this, because she was that keeper once, and does not say it.

**The people.**

**The legs** (all open at the working stage, in any order).

- *The count (Pickpocket or Lockpicking, with Sneak).* Lift the key from Hulm-Tei's belt as he stands at the door,
  or slip past him and pick the Adept lock, with the household in sight. Taking her leaf takes the false leaf from
  the player and writes the journal. It goes wrong as Skyrim's own law does: seen, the player is a thief on the
  estate's ground and the guard comes; a bungled pickpocket turns the keeper on the player.
- *The word (Speech, or what the box gave).* Hulm-Tei closes the Owing by custom, taking the first price, in one of
  three ways, each a line of his that sets "closed" and which way:
  1. *persuaded* (Speech 50): he strikes her leaf and writes the eleven years onto his own with his eyes open. "I
     have carried worse than a boat-hand." Below 50: "A true count is the only thing between me and the mud."
  2. *his leaf held*: "I have your leaf." He takes it back, strikes hers and says the damp had his. He is free of
     the estate tonight, and the shortfall will be shared among the dozen hands who stay.
  3. *the long purse*: the whole of what it has grown to, 1,000 gold and no first price. The estate is paid and
     nobody carries anything.
- *The hand (Speech, coin or a fight).* Brings-Them-Back looks away for Speech 35 or 150 gold (a number). Told
  "Then I go through you", he attacks, alone and hard. Bought, he will still know the way to her tree. Dead, nobody
  does.

or leave the box and clear the channel. For the swap: he wrote every line that held her. For facing him: he never
chose the box he keeps. For the channel: it costs nobody a leaf, and it costs her the way home, unless the fetcher
is dead.

  a season for her. They will never know why. I would have chosen the keeper."

  will get her colour back. I do not thank you for how."
- After any ending the player can say to Never-Sold "You knew about the keeper's leaf." "Every count has a last
  leaf. If I had told you, you would have stood in that store weighing a jailer against a boat-hand, with the box
  open."

afterwards working out that the patron knew.

### BC02. One Out of the Smoke

**What a new player is told, and when.** Never-Sold's briefing is short: three people are held at the old stone
houses on the east shore, here in town, by the families who run the Rose; the Rose is the old prison a day south
"and you are not going there"; one of the three can be got out; she wants the one held for words. Everything else
is Tsaxi's to tell on the spot, in answers the player picks: who he is, what "put under" means ("sent down below
the Rose, where the dead still walk; nobody comes up"), why only one, and where his reckoner has got to.

**The hidden truth.** Before the player came to Blackrose a fire went through the Rose's upper cells
([40-factions](../40-factions.md) §39). The heirs moved three of their held to the outer post under one
gate-keeper, Tsaxi, who counts heads and cannot read. Their singed leaves were to follow with the Rose's reckoner,
the only one who reads them. He came north by the wet road round the west of the lake and never arrived: he lies
dead in his camp in the western swamp, among the beasts that killed him, with the leaves in his satchel. Until
somebody reads those leaves the count is whatever Tsaxi says, and he can say that one of three died of the smoke.
Not two. Of the two younger held, one is there for words and one for a killing, and the killer will be put under
when the leaf is read. So both say they are the one held for words, and each can recite the words, because the
whole yard heard them. The old woman knows which is which and will not say, because the killing was done for her.

- **Walks-Far** (man) said in the yard that the gate to the dead levels should be opened and the Rose left. He
  owes three seasons for it.
- **Mends-Nets** (woman) drowned a prison-born shelter-reckoner, Otheel, in a net in the wash-pool, after he added
  a third winter to an old woman's roof-owing. She nurses that old woman's cough now.
- **Seven-Floods** (old woman), Rows-For-Another's mother, owes the heirs for a roof and always will
  (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:136`). The smoke is in her chest. She wants to die near her Hist, so that she gets

### BC03. The Broker's Mercy

**What a new player is told, and when.** Never-Sold's briefing: who Ussa-Rekh is to the docks (he bought hundreds
out and now hires them out), what a carved token is, and where the dock lies: on the coast road a short way east of
Lilmoth, where the map says Docks and its people say Soulrest's dock. She does not say what he is to her.

**The hidden truth.** For thirty years Ussa-Rekh bought Owings out and carved a token for each person, kept until

it bought twenty people out at once; he can name the year. Since then the seasons have had a way of becoming two.
He keeps nothing for himself. Heavy-Rope's crew have worked their season and one more, and their tokens are still
in his chest. The hire of this last season, which the berth-master Ra'Jhan pays when it is sailed, is promised to
the coin for eleven people held at Thorn, where it is worst (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:132`): their names are

## 4. Read on paper

One round, on the first draft of sections 2 and 3: two players given only the story as a player meets it, step by
step, and one reader given the ceiling and the outlines.

**What they said.**

- *The player who loves the lore* cared about Rows-For-Another, Seven-Floods, Hulm-Tei and the Broker, caught both
  seeds of the patron's secret unprompted, and would retell the keeper's own leaf and the book. Hard to please on:
  all three quests having one skeleton (a box, a pocket, a Speech check, a purse or a killing); the way out of BC01
  by the keeper's leaf costing nobody anything; gold buying the perfect ending cheaply, in BC03 with the Broker's
  own coin; a slip in the nine-year arithmetic; BC02 saying both that all three go and that two refuse; not knowing
  whose law a town guard keeps; Rows-For-Another never hearing of her mother; and the Rose teased but never entered.
- *The impatient player* enjoyed the two break-ins, was restless through BC02 ("standing in one room choosing a
  line"), solved its mystery in ten seconds from how the two spoke, found fighting "always the dumb option that
  punishes you", found rewards thin, and lost the thread in the opening topics and BC02's briefing (nushmeeko, the
  Hist, "put under", where the Rose is, a gift of coin "that will not work"). Would retell the book.
- *The deliverability reader* found the people, boxes, keys, gates and switch-offs buildable, and the stage tables
  not: each leg had a stage of its own, so doing legs out of order, a death at the wrong moment or a thing taken
  early broke them; BC03 had a dead end and no report; Ussa-Rekh could not hand over tokens that lie in his chest;
  several journal entries claimed what the player might not have done.

**What changed, as one set.**

1. *Each quest is now a different kind of job.* BC02's truth moved out of a second lockbox and into a dead
   reckoner's satchel in a swamp of beasts, so the line runs box, swamp, house. That one change answers the single
   skeleton, the talking room, the fighter with nothing to do and part of the thin rewards.
2. *Nobody gets out free, and a death buys something.* The keeper's-leaf way in BC01 now spreads the shortfall over
   the hands who stay; the persuaded keeper carries it knowingly; the purse is 1,000. A dead fetcher lets
   Rows-For-Another go home, which a bought one does not. BC03's purse is 1,000 and the Broker's chest holds almost
   nothing. Section 1 says it as the faction's rule.
3. *BC02's mystery no longer solves itself.* Both of the young held can recite the speech; the free clue is how the
   reckoner died, which points without proving.
4. *The stages were rebuilt on one working stage,* with legs as numbers, held things and deaths, takes as journal
   notes only, an order for ending lines, a report to Never-Sold in all three, and job offers gated on who is alive.
   BC03 follows BC02.
5. *Plain words sooner.* Four short opening topics with the Hist and nushmeeko glossed; BC02's briefing cut to
   three facts, the rest Tsaxi's; "put under" explained; whose guard and whose law said in section 1; the first
   price given with a reason.
6. *Small truths.* The smaller hand covers two years; Seven-Floods sends word to her daughter and her daughter can
   receive it; the ledger is named as a borrowed habit; each Never-Sold reward is a thief's tool; BC04 to BC06 say
   where the weapon comes out and where the Rose may be entered, and their three unbuildable phrasings were
   reworded.

**For a second round to check.** Whether BC02 now plays as an expedition with a judgement at the end, and whether a
player who skips the swamp still feels they did something. Whether the leaf-held way in BC01 reads as a real cost
or as a penalty for cleverness. Whether 1,000 gold reads as "possible, and dear". Whether the opening
topics are now short enough for the impatient. Whether the working-stage tables hold when a fresh deliverability
reader tries each order of legs. Whether readers still split three ways on whom to free.
