# The Eye and the Root: the plot

This page is the main quest's plot. It was rewritten on 2026-11-17 to the owner's story explorer notes of that day,
and brought up to his notes of 2026-11-18. Every quest is set in a place whose source place holds what the quest
needs. Where a quest relies on something major of a place, an item or a creature, the text says what was checked and
how we know: which source place has it and what that source holds, or which mod or Skyrim record gives the thing.
Everything is inside the quest ceiling ([quest-system](../../../../.claude/skills/quest-system/SKILL.md)), with the
additions listed in section 9.

**Words.** *Hist*: the great thinking trees of Black Marsh. *Sap*: what a Hist bleeds. Drunk, it brings visions
that the tree sends. *The scar*: the one place in a Hist's bark where its minder draws a little sap. *The Owing*:
the marsh's sentence, months of owed work in place of a cell. *The Xeech-Leel*: "People of the New Beginning", the
party that has ruled the province since the An-Xileel fell after Umbriel. *The Toh-Reel-Ka*: "Secret Warriors"
(*Toh*, secret, as in Xal-Toh, "Sacred Secret"; *Reel-Ka*, warrior), the Xeech-Leel's elite order of spies and
guards. *The Unbound*: a cult of the tribeless that drains Hist sap. *The Root*: the place under the sea where the
roots of every Hist meet. *The Eye of Argonia*: the lost jewel of the old priest-kings. *Archwardens*: the
Xeech-Leel's wardens, whose garrisons man the old Imperial forts, patrol the roads and collect the tolls. *Voh-Vastei*, *Toh-Wo*,
*Reel-Ka*, *Ka-Deelith* and *Toh-Nassa*: the Toh-Reel-Ka's ranks, lowest first ("Apprentice", "Secret-Speaker",
"Warrior", "War-Teacher" and "Leader of the Secret").

## 1. The premise

Black Marsh, 4E 201. The player is a stranger of any race, sent up the river on an Owing barge to cut reed at
Ashgate Landing, a reed-cutters' camp west of Loriasel. The game opens in the barge's hold, moored at the camp. The
door is locked. The rest of the gang sit there with the player, and none of them knows the newcomer.

The gang's cook, Okeeh, asks the player's name and their trade. The name is where the player names their character.
The trade is a choice of professions, as in Morrowind: each raises five skills by five levels, and one answer lets
the player pick their own five. When the talk ends, the door is unlocked from outside.

On the landing stands the gang-master, Dralin Uveran. He speaks first. The player's name was struck from his roll
this morning by an order he cannot refuse, before they cut a single reed. "You must have friends in high places,"
he says. He is sour about it. The woman who brought the order, Vosh-Leera, the root-herald of Jal-Veesa, a town up
the road, left word: she will have work for the player if they bring her some Sithis' beard.

The player is not chosen or special. The Toh-Reel-Ka comb the work gangs for outsiders with no kin and no ties,
because such a stranger makes a useful spy and a cheap one. The player learns this early and plainly. They want what
any freed stranger wants: work, coin and a place in the world. The Toh-Reel-Ka offer all three.

## 2. Why the trees matter

A Hist is a tree the size of a tower, with pale grey bark, roots that stand out of the water and a pool of eggs at
its foot. Every Argonian village is built round one. A hatchling licks its tree's sap on its naming day, and the sap
shapes its body for life (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:54-56`). The eggs lie in pools among the roots. If a tree's bond
with its eggs is cut, the unborn die (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:78-80`). A village whose tree dies has no future: no
egg in its pools will hatch again.

The trees are alive and aware. An Argonian with a hand on the bark hears them (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:106-107`).
Sap drunk brings visions of far places (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:31-32`). All the trees are joined at the root, so
what one knows the others can know (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:15-16`). A tree can also give a blessing to someone
its people trust.

The player is told all of this plainly in the first three quests, and can ask for more. Okeeh and Dralin answer
the topics *Hist*, *Jal-Veesa* and *the Owing* in quest 1: what a Hist looks like, that every village stands round
one and that the tall grey tree with a pool at its foot in the middle of Jal-Veesa is the town's. In quest 2 the
tree-minder, Weeltul, answers *Hist* again and *eggs*, *sap* and *the scar* once the player stands at her pool with
the moss, and shows the player the cooling egg-pool. Vosh-Leera answers
*the Toh-Reel-Ka*, *the Xeech-Leel* and *Umbriel*. *Umbriel* and *the mad tree of Lilmoth* are topics that most
people the player meets answer in a line or two of their own, because everyone in Black Marsh knows how the An-Xileel
used Lilmoth's tree to call the flying city down. The player also sees and uses what they are told: a sick tree, a
blessing from a tree and a cup of sap that shows them a real place.

## 3. The truth under the story

**What the player is shown.** Hist trees are falling sick one at a time. A healthy tree gives its minder a little
sap at the scar. A sick tree's scar closes and gives nothing. Its bark dries and darkens, and its egg-pool cools.
The tree-minders cannot find a cause. The Toh-Reel-Ka send the player to find it.

**What the player finds, in order.** At the first sick tree they see for themselves there is a grey paste in the
closed scar, and jars of sap hidden nearby. Someone drained the tree and then sealed it. A warden has heard the
drainers call themselves the Unbound, and a captive names their leader. Later the player finds bottles of the grey
paste among the Unbound's things. It is a paralysis poison, made to a recipe of their leader's. It numbs the scar
shut. A tree drained hard and then poisoned may die of it.

**The question the player is left with.** The Unbound want sap, and they take it. Why do they then seal the tree?
If their leader wanted the trees dead, she could kill them. The player writes this question in their journal and
carries it to the end. Cuts-the-Old-Knot answers it at the gate.

**What the Unbound want.** Their leader, Cuts-the-Old-Knot, wants the Eye of Argonia. The priest-kings cut the Eye
from Hist amber, the solid sap that the trees no longer make (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:154`). Canon leaves the
Eye's nature open (`eye-of-argonia.md:6-8`), so what follows is our extrapolation. The Eye sits at the Root, under
a drowned tower, behind a gate that the last king sealed. It is the knot that joins every root to every other. Used
where it sits, it can pour strength down every root and heal every tree. Lifted from its place, it parts every
tree from every other for good.

**Why she drains sap.** A beast guards the Root. Sap makes a fighter stronger (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:31-32`),
so she is brewing a draught strong enough to kill the beast. The player works this out from what they find, and
the Toh-Reel-Ka agree.

**Who rules, and what the Toh-Reel-Ka are.** In 4E 48 the An-Xileel used the mad Hist of Lilmoth to call the flying
city Umbriel down on the marsh, and its dead killed Lilmoth's people (`topics/umbriel.md:26-36`). The disaster broke
the An-Xileel, and no party of that name survived it (`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`, §1). The Xeech-Leel
took the province in the generations after by being everything the An-Xileel were not. Their first promise is that
no faction may speak alone with a tree, and they swear to govern in the open; the council of the Xeech-Leel sits at
Helstrom (§1). In the Void Nights of 4E 98 to 100 they made the Toh-Reel-Ka, so that no tree, and no person or group
allied with a tree, can bring another Umbriel (§2.5). Since 4E 187 the Toh-Reel-Ka have watched through dreamers:
people locked in the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth, fed sap and made to tell their dreams (§2.5, §11.2). A dreamer's
visions show what any tree has seen, anywhere in the province. That is how the Toh-Reel-Ka know so much, and the
Xeech-Leel's rule rests on it. The irony is the lore's own: the party that rose because one faction held secret
communion with one tree now holds, in secret, a communion with all of them (§1). The tree-minders and the Root Talk
do not know of the dreamers, and no tree-minder anywhere knows of them. Vosh-Leera serves the Toh-Reel-Ka without
asking what they do.

**What the Toh-Reel-Ka want of the Eye.** They want the Unbound stopped and Cuts-the-Old-Knot dead. They want the
Root found and the player to use the Eye where it sits, to heal the trees. They say that this saves the villages,
which is true. They do not say that a sick or sealed tree shows the dreamers nothing, so a healed tree is a tree
they can see through again. Then the Root's chamber is to be sealed for good, and the three seal-stones that open
its gate are to be "kept safe" by the Toh-Reel-Ka, so that nobody but them can reach the Eye again.

**Who she is.** Cuts-the-Old-Knot was a dreamer in the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth for nine years, until she found a
way out. She does not want the trees dead. She seals each tree that she drains so that the Toh-Reel-Ka draw no sap
from it for their dreamers, and so that their dreamers cannot see through it where she goes. She wants the Eye
lifted so that no dreamer can ever see through the trees again. She accepts that some trees will die when they are
parted. To her that is the price of a free marsh.

## 5. The acts

The main quest is nineteen quests in three acts, as many as Skyrim's. Act I sets up the mystery and the stakes.
Act II is the treasure hunt, the heart of the plot: each find points to the next. Act III is the gate, the twist
and the end.

Danger climbs with the plot. Act I is played in the settled north (bands D1 to D3) as one road onward from the camp, and ends
with the rootworm ride under deadly country to Helstrom, whose city is safe.
The hunt runs through dangerous country (D3) into deadly country (D4), and its last stone lies in one of the
province's worst ruins (D5). Section 9 lists the danger bands that the plot changes and how the player travels to
each quest.

Every quest has at least two ways through for different builds: melee of any kind, archery or magic,
alone or with stealth beside them. A few quests also have a spoken way, which no quest needs. Every quest has a way
for a player with no stealth. Every objective has a marker, and the marker shows the obvious way (the person with the
key, the front door); a quieter way round is mentioned in the journal or by a person and left for the player to find.
Where the player may fight or sneak, the journal says so, and it says what happens to a player who is caught and
whether the doors on the quiet way are locked. The journal only records what the player was told or saw, with a call
to action for the next step; nothing is learnt from it first. Where a person can be reached too soon or a step done
out of order, the quest either skips ahead cleanly or the person says why not and sends the player back; the notable
cases are named in each quest below.

The Toh-Reel-Ka's ranks come with the plot, each with a fixed reward. Gold comes with them, so that the player can
pay for boats and the rootworm from the start (fares are 15 to 45 gold):

| Rank | Given after | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Voh-Vastei ("Apprentice") | quest 5 | a set of light Argonian armour (from Sign of Cipactli, under our name), the order's traders and 200 gold for the road |
| Toh-Wo ("Secret-Speaker") | quest 10 | a rapier (from Animated Armoury, under our name) or a quarterstaff, as the player chooses, and 200 gold |
| Reel-Ka ("Warrior") | quest 13 | a cuirass and helm of the order's heavier make (Stormhold Warrior Armor, under our name), free beds in every Archwarden fort that has no bounty on the player and 300 gold |
| Ka-Deelith ("War-Teacher") | the three stones, at the report that opens quest 17 | the order's best: an enchanted pike or enchanted claws, as the player chooses, and the rest of the Stormhold Warrior set |
| Toh-Nassa ("Leader of the Secret") | the Use ending | section 8 |

Between ranks, the handler pays 100 gold at each report in Act II. A report that gives a rank pays the rank's
gold in its place.

### Act I: the sick trees

**1. No Name on the Work Roll** (Ashgate Landing, a reed-cutters' camp of the Owing in Barsaebica). The player
gives Okeeh their name and trade in the hold. As they finish, the bar is lifted from the door outside, and Okeeh
tells them they can go out onto the landing, where the gang-master waits. Dralin speaks first on the landing and frees
them. He tells them only this: Vosh-Leera of Jal-Veesa will have work for them if they bring her some Sithis' beard,
a grey moss that hangs on the woven weir at Reed-Sill Weir, on the road to the town. The player's pack holds two
lockpicks (Skyrim's). *Leads on:* Dralin's directions and a marker on the moss at the weir. The barge is Black Marsh &
Valenwood's own Imperial ship: its plugin has the hull as a building and a furnished hold as an interior cell, with
chests, loose things and furniture in it (section 9 gives the measures). Among the hold's things is plain gear enough
for a new fighter to face the weir's crocodiles: an Imperial sword and light shield, a set of Imperial light armour,
a hunting bow with twenty iron arrows and three potions of minor healing (Skyrim's, stocked by the place).

**A side quest here: What the Herald Sent.** Vosh-Leera sent things for the player with her order: a purse of 100
gold, a knife (Skyrim's iron dagger), an ordinary enchanted ring that keeps its wearer hale (Skyrim's Ring of Minor
Health) and two scrolls, one of firebolt and one of fast healing
(Skyrim's). Dralin does not see why he should hand them over, and he has "mislaid" the key to
his strongbox. Okeeh tells the player so. The player lifts the key from his belt (Pickpocket), picks the box
(Lockpicking) or talks him round (Speech). The lock is the easiest kind, Dralin's pocket is easy to pick and his
Speech check is 20, so a new character who chose any of the three skills, as a profession's skill or one of their
own five, passes. The main quest does not wait for it.

**2. The Dry Tree** (Reed-Sill Weir, a weir-wardens' camp; Jal-Veesa, a town of the Sedge-Wardens). The journal
sends the player to the moss first. Crocodiles lie in the shallows below the weir, where the moss hangs on its fences. The player kills them,
shoots them from the log walk or slips past them to pick the moss (Here There Be Monsters' hanging moss, as an
ingredient under our name); crocodiles that see the player attack. The journal then sends the player on to
Vosh-Leera's house in Jal-Veesa. She greets them: she is the root-herald, who speaks for the town's elders and its
tree to outsiders, and it was her order that struck their name. After a little talk she sends them to the tree-minder,
Weeltul, at the Hist pool, because the moss is for the town's sick tree and she gives work only to one who helps the
tree-minder first. The Hist is sick: its bark is dry and dark, its scar has closed and its egg-pool is cooling.
Weeltul takes the moss and makes a poultice that warms the pool but does not open the scar. She tells the player what
the town loses if the tree dies, and gives them the tree's blessing: for a time they breathe under water. An Argonian,
who already breathes water, is given the blessing of resist poison instead (the blessing that Jal-Veesa's record
lists for its tree). Weeltul then sends the player back to Vosh-Leera, who is waiting in her house. There,
where nobody else hears, Vosh-Leera tells the player whom she answers to: the Toh-Reel-Ka, the Xeech-Leel's secret
warriors, made after Umbriel so that no tree and nobody allied with one can ever turn on the marsh again. She tells
them in a few plain lines how the An-Xileel used Lilmoth's mad tree to call Umbriel down. Then she asks whether they
will work for the order. The choice is the player's. If they say yes, she pays them 50 gold. If they say no, the
journal records that the offer stands, and they can come back to her house at any time and say yes. *Out of order:* a player who takes the moss straight to Weeltul skips
ahead cleanly, and Vosh-Leera greets them at the report; a player who goes to Vosh-Leera without the moss is greeted
and sent back for it; Weeltul talks of the tree only once the player stands at her pool with the moss. *Leads on:*
once the player has said yes, Vosh-Leera has a second task for them there and then.

**3. The First Dream** (Jal-Veesa; Teel-Fen Weir, a weir-wardens' camp round a young Hist). Vosh-Leera gives the
player a cup of sap (Skyrim's potion bottle under our name). Tree-minders drink it, she says, to ask the trees a
question. When the player drinks it, the screen goes black and the vision is shown as words on the black screen: a
young Hist beside a platform of tents and a woven weir, and a reed hide beside a lone dead tree. The player tells
Vosh-Leera what they saw, in dialogue, line by line. She knows the place: Teel-Fen Weir, south of the town, where a
young tree grew from the roots of Jal-Veesa's own. The journal marks it. Heem-Jasa, the minder there, stands by her
tree on the platform. Its scar closed three nights ago. She shows the player the grey paste in it. The hide from
the vision stands west of the weir, with a marker. Three drainers wait there with jars of sap (Skyrim's clay jars
under our name). Heem-Jasa has heard them called the Unbound, which she takes to mean people with no tree and no kin.
The player fights them, shoots them from cover or passes them by stealth to take the note that lies with the jars
(a jar is theirs to take too). The hide has no door or lock; a player who is seen there has all three to fight. Back
on the platform, Heem-Jasa asks whether her tree will live. *Out of order:* met before Vosh-Leera sends the player,
Heem-Jasa says only that her tree is unwell and she does not tell its troubles to strangers; a player who finds the
hide before speaking to her skips ahead, and she shows them the scar when they come to her with the note.

- *The truth.* The player says it may die. Heem-Jasa thanks them for not lying. From then on she trains the player
  in Alchemy (Adept) and sells them sap, swamp fungus and eggshells.
- *A comfort.* The player says it will live. Heem-Jasa gives them three potions of Cure Poison (Skyrim's) on the
  spot. She trains and trades only with kin and with those who have been straight with her, so her training and her
  stall stay closed to the player.

*Leads on:* in the note, the crew's master orders them to bring their jars to Karsh-Naal Camp. The
journal sends the player there, and the camp is marked.

**4. The Sap Camp** (Karsh-Naal Camp, a camp of tribeless Naga in the Valley of Storms). The camp is the Unbound's.
Sesk, a Naga who drew sap for them, is caged at its west edge for trying to leave (Skyrim's long metal cage, bars and
door, placed in the camp's draft). When the player reaches the camp, the journal records what they can see: someone
locked in a cage at the camp's edge, away from the guards at the fire. The marker moves to Sesk. The player sneaks
over to the cage, walks or runs over openly, or fights the guards first; she never calls out, so a sneaking player
stays unseen. Spoken to through the bars, Sesk asks the player to free her: she knows who takes the sap and what it
is for, and will tell them once she is out. The lock is a poor one, she says, and the key is on the guard Jeeta-Sakka
at the fire. Markers then show the cage and Jeeta-Sakka. The guards are few and stand at the fire, away from the
cage, so a quiet player can reach it. The player
picks the cage (Lockpicking, a Novice lock), lifts the key (Sneak and Pickpocket) or kills the guards. A player caught
at the cage or at Jeeta-Sakka's pouch has all four guards to fight, and the key is then on his body. Sesk talks as soon
as her cage is open. She names the Unbound's leader, Cuts-the-Old-Knot, an Argonian woman who never stays where her
crews are. She says the sap is for a draught, and the draught is for something to do with "the Eye". She calls the
grey paste "the seal" and does not know what is in it. Then Sesk leaves the camp. *Leads on:* back in Jal-Veesa,
Vosh-Leera says this is more than a herald can judge. She sends the player to the order's handler in Helstrom and
marks the house. Helstrom lies in deadly country, so she tells them to ride the rootworm, and what it is: the "Underground
Express", a worm with clear flesh that races through the tunnels the Hist roots form under the mud and the swamp
water. It swallows a rider whole and digests so slowly that a rider could live in it for months; the rider watches
through its flesh for the bubbles of an air pocket and pushes up through its soft skin there to get off (UESP
Lore:Rootworm; `story/lore/topics/hist-placement.md` R5). Where the tunnels pass under a Hist's root pool there is
such a pocket, and a root-tender there takes the fare and shows riders where to go down (our travel network's
extrapolation). Jal-Veesa's root-tender is Weeltul, at the Hist pool in the middle of the town, and the fare is 25
gold. She
pays the player 100 gold. The journal marks Weeltul. *Out of order:* Vosh-Leera, asked for news before the camp,
sends the player to Karsh-Naal first. The rootworm is open to anyone who pays, as Morrowind's silt striders were, so
a player may ride to Helstrom early; Nesh-Deeka, met before anyone has sent them, says she takes no business from
strangers with no word from anyone she knows.

**The rootworm's stops.** Every rootworm stop is the root pool of a Hist, where the worm's tunnel passes an air pocket
under the roots, with a root-tender beside it who takes the fare
([the travel network](../../../../docs/design/travel-network.md): a rootworm route needs "a Hist with a root pool at
both ends"). In this plot the stops are Jal-Veesa's Hist pool (Weeltul), the root pool under Helstrom's city tree
(Cheeval-Xu) and Gideon's Hist garden. Helstrom's draft has its city tree (King of the Murkmire's young Hist) and its root-tender, Cheeval-Xu, beside it;
its root pool is owed to the place and is asked of its place lane (section 9).

**5. The Handler** (Helstrom, the oldest city). The quest opens with the ride. The worm comes up in the root pool
under Helstrom's city tree, where its root-tender, Cheeval-Xu, points the player to Nesh-Deeka's House, a quiet house south
of the city tree, down by the city landing where the rafts tie up, a short walk from the Boatmen's Lodge (the drafted
house 8, about 50 m from the raft-master's post).
Nesh-Deeka hears all that the player found and makes them a Voh-Vastei, the order's first rank. From now on the
player can ask her about the Toh-Reel-Ka's history and their name, and the Xeech-Leel's. She tells them what the Eye
is and where it lies: at the Root, behind a gate that the old kings sealed, in a place no living diver has found.
The old songs say a beast keeps the Root, older than the kings, and nobody living has seen it. The player says what
the draught must be for, and she agrees: Cuts-the-Old-Knot means to kill that beast. To prove the player, Nesh-Deeka sends them after an Unbound go-between, Keel-Raa, who lodges at the Boatmen's
Lodge, the inn by the raft landing, with a jar of stolen sap. He and his two hired men stand among the crates at the
far end of the lodge, not hostile, so that the player can talk to them. The player lifts the jar from his pack, buys
it from him for 100 gold or fights him and his men for it. A player caught at the pack has all three to fight, and
the jar is still in the pack after. They belong to a band whose members' wrongs Helstrom's levy does not punish
(Skyrim's faction flag that reports no crimes against its members), so attacking them is no crime. *Leads on:*
Nesh-Deeka gives the order's aim. The Root must be found before the Unbound find it, and the player is to use the
Eye where it sits to heal the trees. She has one lead: an "Eye of Argonia" is for sale in Gideon. She tells the
player that the rootworm runs from Helstrom's city tree to Gideon.

### Act II: the treasure hunt

Every find in Act II is a clue to the next. After each quest the player reports to the handler, who reads the
find and names the next place. Nesh-Deeka always knows a little more than she should about where the Unbound have
been, and lets it show in what she says. Only if the player asks how she knows does she answer "We listen" and change
the subject. The journal records that she knew and did not say how. What the Eye and the Root are is told once, in
quest 5, and the journal records it there. A player who forgets can always ask the handler "Remind me what we are
hunting, and why" (Nesh-Deeka, and Never-Writes-Twice from quest 10); nothing makes them hear it again.

**6. The Glass Eye** (Gideon, the relic-dealers' city; Gideon South Watch, an Imperial ruin). A dealer in Gideon's
market, Ovinius Calo, sells the Eye of Argonia (a flawless garnet of Skyrim's under our name). It is glass, and he
admits it for a few coins or a little Speech. He bought it from the smugglers of the South Watch, who kept the old
silver setting it came in. A player caught at his pocket pays Gideon's watch a fine or fights it, and he will still
talk. The player goes into the ruin's inner room and takes the setting (Skyrim's silver necklace under our name) from
the smugglers' chest, which has an Apprentice lock; their boss carries its key. They fight the smugglers and take the
key from the boss, or pass them by stealth and pick the lock (Sneak and Lockpicking); smugglers who see a quiet player
attack. Gideon South Watch is built from Old Laboratory, a small Imperial ruin on the water in King of the Murkmire
with one interior, which serves as the inner room where the chest stands. The Collector got there first and left the
player a courteous note in the chest. He took nothing: the setting was of no use to him. *Leads on:* a ship's name is scratched inside the
setting. Nesh-Deeka knows it. The *Pale Harvest* sank in Hatandros Bight with a Gideon dealer's whole stock aboard.
She marks the wreck. She already knows that two Unbound asked after the glass Eye in Gideon a month ago.

**7. The Pale Harvest** (the Wreck of the Pale Harvest, a shipwreck in Hatandros Bight). The wreck lies in shallow
water off Ysan's jetty, a short dive past slaughterfish. Air has stayed inside the hull. The drowned crew walk the
hold, and their captain stands beside his strongbox. The player fights them and takes the key from the captain, or
steals past and picks the strongbox's Adept lock; the drowned attack whoever they see. The wreck is built from
Katariah, the large Imperial ship of Skyrim, whose one interior is a hold full of crates and chests, entered here by a
door in the hull; the drowned crew are Skyrim's draugr. Inside is a keystone on a chain, of Kothringi
make (Here There Be Monsters' Kothringi keystone amulet; as the key to quest 9's door it is a key record that uses
the amulet's mesh). *Leads on:* Nesh-Deeka does not know the keystone. She knows that the Unbound lost the trail at
Gideon and have not found the wreck. The Chime-Keepers of Toklea keep what is left of Kothringi lore, she says. She
marks their town.

**8. The Drowned Street** (Toklea, a Chime-Keepers town in the Tulmek Washes; Zul-Etha, a drowned village). The
Chime-Keepers' elder, Hums-the-Long-Note, knows the keystone and names her price. Her family's grave-stake (King of
the Murkmire's carved grave-stake, as a thing to carry) lies in a chest in the drowned street of Zul-Etha, and she
wants it raised. Toklea is built from Toil's End, a Kothringi town in Here There Be Monsters whose one interior is
named the Kothringi Greathouse, where the elder stands. Zul-Etha is built from Seekhat Yol, an Argonian stilt village
of King of the Murkmire, its huts set low so that three roof peaks stand out of the water; the chest in the drowned
street is ours to add. Crocodiles lie on the roof peaks, with an old bull among them. The player kills them from a
roof or from range, calms them with Illusion or swims under them unseen to the chest, which is not locked; crocodiles
that see the player in the water attack. *Leads on:* the
elder says the keystone opens the tomb of the knight-captain at Eurydike. The Kothringi knights of Gideon were the
last to guard the kings' road to the Root. She marks the ruin. The player reports to Nesh-Deeka, who already knows
that the Unbound have been asking Gideon's diggers about Eurydike.

**9. The Knights' Tomb** (Eurydike, a Kothringi ruin in the Kotherra Mangroves). The knights' restless dead walk
the barracks, and a party of Unbound has got in but cannot open the sealed tomb. The player fights through the dead
and the Unbound, slips past them or turns the dead with Restoration; the dead and the Unbound attack whoever they see. The Unbound's packs lie by their fire short of
the tomb door, and the player searches them first. Among them are bottles of the grey paste (Skyrim's poison of
paralysis, under our name). The game names them for what they are: a paralysis poison. The journal asks the
question of section 3. With the keystone in hand, the tomb's door opens; nothing else opens it. Eurydike is built
from Driftshade Sanctuary, an Imperial ruin of Skyrim with two interiors: one serves as the barracks wing and the
other as the tomb, behind a stone door that we set to lock with the keystone as its key. Inside lies the knight-captain's bronze disc (King of the Murkmire's), stamped with a mine's mark. An
Unbound whom the player spares here can give the player a side quest (section 6).
*Leads on:* Nesh-Deeka knows the mark: Vicrium, a mine in the east.

**10. Down into Vicrium** (Vicrium, a mine of Okuwun Rahei). The miners work the upper galleries. After a roof fall
they shut the lower galleries behind a locked timber door that no pick opens, and the gallery-boss, Ah-Zish, carries
its key on his belt. The marker is on Ah-Zish. The player gets the key by Speech (40), by paying 100 gold or by
Pickpocket; a player caught at his belt pays a fine to his miners or fights them, and he will still deal. Ah-Zish
also mentions, as a grumble, the old lower adit on the creek bank, flooded and full of crocodiles, by which he thinks
the smugglers got in; it has no marker, and a player who finds it can swim in past the crocodiles. Vicrium is built
from two Skyrim mines: Gloombound Mine, one dry interior of timbered galleries, serves as the upper galleries, and
Whistling Mine, a mine by the water with its own interior and its own door out, serves as the lower galleries, its
door out as the adit. The timber door between them and the still water in the adit's first passage are additions,
listed in section 9. Smugglers have moved into the lower galleries. The player fights them or passes them by stealth;
smugglers who see the player attack. If Ah-Zish dies, his miners tell the player about the door and the strongbox,
and the key is on his body. Past the smugglers, in the knights' old strongbox, is an old parchment map (Skyrim's treasure map, under our name) showing seven
towers standing in the sea and a crag shaped like a tooth on an island. *Leads on:* Nesh-Deeka makes the player a
Toh-Wo, the order's second rank, and passes them to her officer, Never-Writes-Twice, who stands at the back of the
same house. Never-Writes-Twice tells her lie about Cuts-the-Old-Knot before the player asks, and warns them not to
listen to her. She knows the crag on the map: the Grey Tooth, the seamark of Skeith Isle, and she marks it.
*Out of order:* Nesh-Deeka, met before the player has been to Vicrium, sends them to the mine first. A player who
goes into the mine before the quest is given can take the map then: the door, the smugglers and the strongbox are
there from the start, and when the quest begins with the map already held, the journal sends the player straight to
Nesh-Deeka with it.

**11. The Grey Tooth** (the Grey Tooth, a crag on Skeith Isle; the Wreck of the Stormwidow, a beached galleon). The
Collector's hired men have camped at the foot of the Grey Tooth's goat-path. They, and the Unbound at the wreck, are
switched on when this quest starts, so a player of Bramman's Brethren who comes first meets neither. They have dug
in the small cave on its north face. The player fights them or passes them by stealth; the men attack a player they
see, and the cave has no door to lock. In the cave the player finds what the men found: an empty niche and their
master's rubbing of the stone that stood in it (a note under our name), which says that the Stormwidow's captain
carried the stone off to his ship. The crag is built from Mount Moesring of Dragonborn, a bare peak with no building
and no interior, so the small cave on its north face is an addition of ours from Skyrim's cave pieces, named in the
place's record. The quest leaves the cairn and its cache alone: they belong to Bramman's Brethren's line. At the wreck
on the beach, Unbound scouts have camped in the sand-filled hold and are searching its ballast crates. The player
fights them or slips past them; scouts who see the player attack, and the hold's door is not locked. In a crate lies
the kings' marker stone (a carved stone of King of the Murkmire's, under our name), which shows the seven drowned
towers with one marked. The wreck's hold comes from Icerunner, Skyrim's wrecked ship, which has two interiors: one
serves as the sand-filled hold and the other as the stern cabin. The quest uses the hold, never the captain's
strongbox in the stern, which is the Brethren's. With the scouts' things lies the recipe for the seal (a note under our name). Reading it teaches
the player to make the paralysis poison, and three bottles lie with it. A wounded scout lies on the beach below the
bow. She tells the player to ask what moans in the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth. If she is dead, a scrap of bark beside
her says the same. *Leads on:* Never-Writes-Twice reads the marker stone: the marked tower is one of the drowned
spires off Lilmoth. She says the divers of Bell Station will know which. Asked about Fort Chalkmoth, she says it is
a fort like any other. The journal sets a second objective: see the cellar for themselves. Quests 12 and 13 can be
done in either order.

**12. The Cellar Door** (Fort Chalkmoth, an Archwarden fort on the Chixathe Mesa). The player goes to see the
cellar on their own account, and it takes four stages. The fort is built from Fort Neugrad, an Imperial fort of
Skyrim with two interiors, which serve as the keep and as the prison below it, the cellar. Skyrim's own records for
Fort Neugrad include a jail key and a library key, so the fort has a locked prison and a locked room of its own; the
locked room serves as the quartermaster's store, and a door out of the prison is set as the trapdoor in the yard.

- *The captain's price.* The wall captain stands at the gate under a marker. He tells the player his terms when they
  ask. A Toh-Wo of the Toh-Reel-Ka may sleep in the barracks, but the keep is closed to strangers until the Sarpa
  raiders on the western approach stop robbing the chalk carts. The player kills the three raiders (King of the
  Murkmire's Argonian raiders, the records our ecology binds to the Sarpa), and the captain gives them the keep's
  key and 100 gold. The keep's door opens only to that key and cannot be picked. The journal also says a quiet
  visitor might find another way in: the trapdoor in the yard behind the barracks, out of the walls' sight, has no
  marker and is not locked, because the gaoler comes and goes by it. A player who goes that way (Sneak) and is seen
  in the yard or the guardroom is attacked by the garrison. If the captain is killed, the keep's key is on his body, and the player can take it and open the
  keep's door, or use the trapdoor; either way the garrison attacks whoever it sees inside.
- *The store by the stair.* The stair down to the cellar starts in the quartermaster's store, behind an Adept lock.
  The player picks the lock (Lockpicking) or lifts the store's key from the Dunmer quartermaster, Theyne Indarys
  (Pickpocket); a player caught at either is a thief to the garrison, which means a fine or a fight. A player with
  no stealth talks Theyne round (Speech 40) or pays him 150 gold, and he hands over the key. In the store, the player
  finds jars of Hist sap stacked by the stair, marked with Jal-Veesa's knot, though the fort has no Hist and its
  people go to Noota Nara's tree. The journal marks it as strange. The Imperial strongbox in the store is the
  player's to rob.
- *The gaoler.* At the foot of the stair, in the guardroom, a gaoler of the Toh-Reel-Ka, Tsleel-Vaku, stands before
  the inner door and speaks first: the cellar is closed to the player, Toh-Wo or not. A player with Speech 50, or
  one who casts an Illusion spell before him as a test, gets a slip from him: "They sleep, and they dream. That is
  all they do down there." He carries no key. Never-Writes-Twice has it.
- *The door.* The player tries the inner door. It is locked, and a message says that moaning comes from behind
  it. The journal records it.

The trapdoor comes up into the guardroom, so a quiet player meets the gaoler and the door in the same way. A player
who is seen in the keep without the captain's leave, or who attacks the gaoler, is a criminal there: the garrison
attacks them, and the door stays shut, because its key is not in the fort. *Leads on:* the journal turns the player
back to the hunt: to Bell Station, or to the three stones if quest 13 is done.

**13. The Kings' Gate** (Bell Station, a diving station in Benthic Yobesh; Teku-Sul Spire, a drowned tower). The
station's steward, Meer-Tasi, tells the player which tower the stone marks: Teku-Sul, whose sealed gate her divers
have never forced. She marks it. If she is dead, the head diver, Sees-Below, tells the player instead; if both are
dead, the divers left at the station name the tower. Neither leads the player down. The dive is long. The player needs the Hist's blessing, a spell of waterbreathing, a potion (the
steward sells them) or an enchantment of their own. Being Argonian is enough. Inside the tower there is air. The
drowned priests' attendants walk the east halls, and the player fights them, turns them with Restoration or slips
past them; they attack whoever they see, and the tower's doors are not locked. Past them stands a
great gate with three empty sockets (King of the Murkmire's crystal pedestals set in a gate of its Xanmeer pieces).
The ghost of a priest of the old kings, Sul-Xeeth, stands before it and speaks first, and the player can ask him
what the Eye is. He says that a seal-stone belongs in each socket. He names where each was taken when the last king
fell: the warden's vault in Blackrose Prison, where a thief who died in its cells had carried one, the flooded vaults of Gemin and the deepest hall of Vorannuhl. The tower is
built from Teeth of Sithis, an Argonian ruin in King of the Murkmire with three interiors named Teeth of Sithis East
Xanmeer, Teeth of Sithis Halls and Teeth of Sithis West Xanmeer: the East Xanmeer serves as the east halls, the Halls
as the gallery and the gate, and the West Xanmeer as the guardian's hall and the Root's chamber beyond the gate. *Leads
on:* the player goes back to Helstrom, where Never-Writes-Twice makes them a Reel-Ka, the order's third rank. Her
orders are to take all three stones before the Unbound do and bring them to her before anyone goes near the gate.
The journal marks all three places. They can be done in
any order, and are numbered here from the least dangerous to the most.

**14. The Thief's Stone** (Blackrose Prison, in the Tulmek Washes). A masked thief who sought the Eye died in the
Rose's cells, and his stone lies in the warden's vault. The Blackguards' heirs live in the prison under a Naga chief,
who carries the vault's key. The garrison is hostile to strangers: they attack on sight. The gate-captain stands
outside the gate, apart from them. Blackrose Prison is built from Cracked Tusk, an Imperial and Orc fort of Skyrim
with two keep interiors, which serve as the chief's hall and the drowned lower cells. The warden's vault is not in it
and is added as a locked room off the hall, from Skyrim's Imperial fort pieces. The player has three ways in.

- *At the gate.* The gate-captain stops the player and speaks first. The player pays a toll of 300 gold, persuades
  him (Speech 75) or duels him. If persuasion fails, only the toll and the duel are left. In the duel he yields when
  he is badly hurt. Each of these makes the garrison the player's host: it no longer attacks them.
- *Unseen.* A player who gets past the gate without being stopped (Sneak, or Illusion's Invisibility) is inside, and
  the garrison is still hostile. If a guard sees them, the guards attack, as they would any stranger.
- *By force.* If the player kills the captain of the gate or fights their way in, the garrison fights them.

Inside, there are three states. *A guest*: the garrison is friendly. The chief sells the vault key for 1,000 gold.
The player can also pick his pocket, or pick the vault's Expert lock at night. *Unseen:* the player picks the
vault's lock or lifts the key from the chief, and if they are seen, the garrison attacks. *An enemy:* the player
fights the guards in the yard and the chief in his hall, and takes the key from his body.

The Collector is in the hall as the chief's guest. He has offered a price for the stone and been told to wait. He
stands apart from the garrison and never fights. He wears a ring of Kothringi silver that the player can use (a
ring of Skyrim's, enchanted to fortify Lockpicking and Pickpocket by 15). In every state the player may talk to
him, lift the ring from his finger (Pickpocket, hard), kill him for it or leave him. Alive, he offers a side quest
(section 6) whose reward is made for the guardian. Dead, he gives the ring and his purse of 400 gold, and his side
quest is lost. *Leads on:* the journal still marks the stones that are left.

**15. The Vault's Stone** (Gemin, a flooded Ayleid ruin of Barsaebica). The second stone lies in the lowest hall,
under water. The restless dead walk the vaults, and a wraith guards the last hall. An Unbound rearguard is searching
the upper halls and has not got past the dead. The player fights, sneaks or turns the dead; the dead, the wraith and the
Unbound attack whoever they see, and no door in the vaults is locked. Gemin is built from Feren Tarn, a sunken Ayleid
ruin in King of the Murkmire with one interior, its lower levels set under water so that the lowest hall is drowned.
Beside the stone is a tablet that the kings cut (a carved tablet of King of the Murkmire's, under our name). It tells
the player, in plain words, why the kings sealed the Root: whoever uses the Eye where it sits sends strength down
every root, and whoever lifts it parts the trees. The kings trusted neither act to one hand. *Leads on:* the
journal marks the last stone.

**16. The Deepest Hall** (Vorannuhl, a Velothi ruin on the Chixathe Mesa). The third stone lies in a vault that a
bronze key opens. A band of Sarpa raiders (King of the Murkmire's Argonian raiders, the records our ecology binds to
the Sarpa) shelters in the outer yard, and one of them, Hesh-Var, carries the key. The player lifts it from him or
kills him for it; raiders who catch the player at his belt all attack. The vault's bronze door takes only its key. The
plague-dead of the old household walk the deep halls, and its lord, a wraith, guards the vault; they attack whoever
they see. Vorannuhl's deep halls are the Desecrated Ancestral Tomb from Caves of Morrowind, a large Dunmer tomb
of Redoran halls and rough cave passages, with the vault behind one of its doors, which is locked so that only the
bronze key opens it. It is the hardest place of the hunt, with its hardest fight or its hardest sneak. *Leads on:* whichever stone the player takes
last, the journal sends the player with all three to Never-Writes-Twice in Helstrom, who ordered them brought to her
first.

### Act III: the gate

**17. At the Kings' Gate** (Helstrom; Teku-Sul Spire). Never-Writes-Twice looks at each
stone and hands it back. She makes the player a Ka-Deelith, the order's fourth rank, and gives her last order. The
dreamers have not seen the Unbound near Teku-Sul, she says, so the player is ahead of them and the Unbound have not
found the gate. The player is to go now: open the gate with the three stones, use the Eye where it sits to heal the
trees and never lift it, take the stones back and bring them to her, so that the chamber is sealed for good. Then
the player is to find and kill Cuts-the-Old-Knot and rid the province of the Unbound. The player dives down again,
expecting an empty tower. An Unbound
rearguard waits in the east halls. Nobody warned the player of them: no tree grows under the sea, so the dreamers
saw nothing. The player fights them, slips past them or holds them off with magic. Before the gate stands
Cuts-the-Old-Knot. She got there first, but she has no seal-stones and cannot open it. She speaks first and tells the
player the truth about the Toh-Reel-Ka (section 7). The player can ask her about the cellar, the seal, what the order
wants and what each choice means. She tells them plainly what the Eye does used and lifted, and that if they use it
while she lives, its power runs through the sap she drank. The player then chooses: fight her here, or believe her.
*Leads on:* the gate, and the three stones the player carries.

**18. The Guardian** (Teku-Sul Spire, the hall beyond the gate). The player sets the three stones in their
sockets, and the gate opens. Beyond is a great hall where the Root's guardian waits: a hydra, old beyond memory. The
story does not explain it. The deep places of the world keep such things. It is highly venomous, and the air and
the water round it are poison. It fights in phases. When it is badly hurt its heads grow back, and heads that come
off crawl away and roam the hall as sentries. Its own music plays. If the player believed her, Cuts-the-Old-Knot is
already in the hall when they step through, and she fights beside them as a battlemage. The guardian's body holds
rich loot. *Leads on:* the door at the hall's far end.

**19. The Eye** (Teku-Sul Spire, the Root). In the last chamber the Eye of Argonia sits on a plinth among the
roots of every tree. The sounds that divers hear in the trench of Echo Deep nearby come up from here. The player
activates the Eye and chooses one of three endings (section 8).

## 6. Side quests on the road

side errand and that the Eye can wait. Each gives a riddle in place of directions. The place it names has no
marker until the player finds it. The riddle's own quest starts only when its giver gives it, so the journal says
that the player guessed right only for a player who was given the riddle; a player who wanders into the place
first finds only the place. The work there has markers as usual and uses the player's skills like any quest.

Each reward is made for the fight with the guardian. A player who does them all finds that fight easy. The
rewards serve every build: some protect, some heal and some attack.

## 7. The twist at the gate

Cuts-the-Old-Knot tells the player what the Toh-Reel-Ka are. They keep people in the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth, feed
them sap and make them tell their dreams. The moans were dreamers. She was one of them for nine years, and
Never-Writes-Twice kept the key. A dreamer sees what any tree has seen. Through its dreamers the order sees
everything that happens in the marsh.

She calls back to what the player saw. "How did your handler always know where to send you? Why was there sap in a
fort with no tree? Who did you think was behind the cellar door? And why did nobody warn you that we were here?"
She answers the question in the player's journal. She seals each tree that she drains so that the order draws no
sap from it for its dreamers, and so that its dreamers cannot see her coming. Under the sea there are no trees, so
she was never seen here. She never meant a tree to die of her seal, and some have.

She grants the order its reason, which is to stop another Umbriel. She also says what it costs, and what the
Xeech-Leel have become: a party that took the marsh by being not the An-Xileel, now ruling unseen through the trees
as the An-Xileel did. The healing they ordered is for the dreamers, she says. A sick tree shows them nothing, and a
healed one shows them everything. The stones they want "kept safe" mean that only they can ever reach the Eye. She
wants the Eye lifted, which parts the trees, ends the dreams and breaks the order. She admits that many trees will
die of it, perhaps three in ten, with the villages under them, and that she does not know which. The player then
fights her here or goes on with her.

**If they fight.** She drinks her draught and becomes a powerful battlemage, the hardest single fight before the
guardian. Her body gives spell tomes, her robes and staff (Skyrim's) and the rest of her draughts. The player goes on
alone. The third ending is closed.

**If they side with her.** She is already in the hall beyond the gate when the player steps through, and she
fights the guardian at their side. She does not follow the player: the stage that the player sets by opening the
gate switches her off at the gate and on in the hall while the game loads it.

## 8. The three endings

The player activates the Eye. A message lays out the three choices and what each will do. Each ending is shown as
a vision: one staged scene. The screen fades to black, the ending's music plays and the trees are shown changing.
What the scene cannot show (the cellar opened, the order's rage) is told in the messages that follow it. The
scenes are made in a later phase, and until then messages carry the whole vision. Then the player walks back into a
world that has changed.

**Use the Eye.** This is what the order sent the player to do. The player uses the Eye where it sits. Every tree is
healed and the Root stays whole. The gate's sockets give the stones back as the gate closes behind the player, and
the player carries them to Never-Writes-Twice.

- *The vision:* the trees across the marsh healing. Then, in words: every dreamer in the cellar seeing again, and
  the Xeech-Leel's rule over the province made fast for another age.
- *It gives:* the order's highest rank, Toh-Nassa, when the player gives the stones to Never-Writes-Twice. The keep of
  Fort Chalkmoth as the player's own house, with its trainer, smith, bed and benches. Free training from the
  order's members up to a set level. Stronger blessings from every Hist.
- *If Never-Writes-Twice is dead:* the player keeps the stronger blessings, but gets no rank and no house.
- *It costs:* if Cuts-the-Old-Knot is alive, the Eye's power runs through the sap she drank and kills her. Her body
  gives the battlemage's loot. Every Unbound and every friend of the Unbound attacks the player on sight. The
  dreamers stay in the cellar below the player's new house.

**Take the Eye.** The player lifts the Eye from its plinth. That act parts the trees. The player keeps the Eye.

- *The vision:* the trees across the marsh parting from each other, and about three in every ten dying. Then, in
  words: the Unbound opening the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth, and the order's people raging.
- *It gives:* the Eye, an amulet that fortifies health and magicka and resists poison, with a power usable once a
  day, the Eye's Gaze, which paralyses every foe in sight. The Unbound's highest
  rank, Knot-Breaker: one who has broken the old knot that tied every tree to every other. Their trainers and traders as friends. If Cuts-the-Old-Knot lives, she thanks the player.
- *It costs:* every member and every friend of the Toh-Reel-Ka attacks on sight. No Hist gives the player its
  blessing again. About three trees in ten are dead when the player next sees them: blackened, with their villages
  empty. Teel-Fen Weir's and Jal-Veesa's are among them.

**Give her the Eye.** Open only if Cuts-the-Old-Knot is alive. She lifts the Eye and keeps it.

- *The vision:* as in Take.
- *It gives:* the Unbound's highest rank, Knot-Breaker. Cuts-the-Old-Knot as an ally whom the player calls with one power and
  sends away with another, and who fights as a battlemage. When she is not called, she stands at the Karsh-Naal
  longhouse. Two strong spells that she teaches, Knot-Breaker's Storm and Root Ward, one of them a power usable
  once a day. The longhouse of Karsh-Naal Camp as the player's stronghold.
- *It costs:* as in Take: the order's enmity everywhere, no Hist blessings and the dead trees. The Eye is hers.

After the end the world stays open. The rewards are worth most to a player who goes on to the province's hardest
places, which a later piece of work names and marks (section 9).

## 9. What it needs, and what it changes elsewhere

### What it asks of the world

- **Danger bands**, so that a new character can play Act I. Each is an owner's overrule in
  `newworld/sources/danger/owner.json`: Ashgate Landing to strength D1 with its way in at D1 (mudcrabs and one young
  crocodile), Reed-Sill Weir to D1, Teel-Fen Weir to strength D2 (wary wardens, and three Unbound at the hide) and
  Karsh-Naal Camp to strength D3 (its Naga are the Unbound's hired guards, with no chief). Sedge-Kal Camp's overrule
  is gone with its part in the plot.
- **The barge** (checked and measured on 2026-11-17, not yet built). The ship is Black Marsh & Valenwood's Imperial
  ship: its exterior hull is the static `0ShipImperial` (`architecture\Ships\ImperialShip01Base.nif`) of
  `Black Marsh.esm`, 2,048 by 704 units by its bounds, 29.3 m long and 10.1 m in the beam (33.3 m by 16 m with
  masts and yards). Its hold is the interior cell `01dock3to4` ("Ship"), furnished with 25 containers, 128 loose
  things (potions, ingredients, armour, books, weapons and clutter) and 11 pieces of furniture, so a curious player
  finds things in the opening. It is given a door that locks from outside. Skyrim's Dainty Sload and Katariah are
  too big: their hulls are 63.5 m and 59 m long. At Ashgate Landing the river is 29 to 41 m wide within 200 m (29 m
  at its narrowest, 88 m north-north-east of the hut), and a pool 63 m west-north-west of the hut is 81 m across and
  about 3.8 m deep. The hull is moored in that pool along the east bank, bow toward the camp, with about 9 m of
  water clear on each side of the beam in the narrows. It is placed where the story needs it and set at the height
  that puts its deck the right height above the waterline; where the water is shallower than the hull is deep, its
  keel is simply sunk into the riverbed. The water is joined to the
  sea: a path over the water raster runs about 8 km from the landing by river, lake and marsh channel to the open
