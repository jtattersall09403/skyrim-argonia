# The Eye and the Root: the plot

This page is the main quest's plot. It was rewritten on 2026-11-16 to the owner's second set of notes. Every quest
is set in a place whose source place holds what the quest needs
(`python3 -m tools.newworld.place_card <place>` shows a place and its sources). Everything is inside the quest
ceiling ([quest-system](../../../../.claude/skills/quest-system/SKILL.md)), with the additions listed in section 9.

**Words.** *Hist*: the great thinking trees of Black Marsh. *Sap*: what a Hist bleeds. Drunk, it brings visions
that the tree sends. *The scar*: the one place in a Hist's bark where its minder draws a little sap. *The Owing*:
the marsh's sentence, months of owed work in place of a cell. *The Reed*: a secret order that says it protects the
old ways of Argonian life. *The Unbound*: a cult of the tribeless that drains Hist sap. *The Root*: the place under
the sea where the roots of every Hist meet. *The Eye of Argonia*: the lost jewel of the old priest-kings.
*Archwardens*: the province's rulers, whose garrisons hold the old Imperial forts.

## 1. The premise

Black Marsh, 4E 201. The player is a stranger of any race, sent north on an Owing barge to cut reed at Sedge-Kal
Camp. The game opens in the barge's hold, moored at the camp. The door is locked. The rest of the gang sit there
with the player, and none of them knows the newcomer.

The gang's cook, Okeeh, asks the player's name and their trade. The name is where the player names their character.
The trade is a choice of professions, as in Morrowind: each raises a few skills, and one answer lets the player
pick three skills themselves. When the talk ends, the door is unlocked from outside.

On the landing stands the gang-master, Dralin Uveran. He speaks first. The player's name was struck from his roll
this morning by an order he cannot refuse, before they cut a single reed. "You must have friends in high places,"
he says. He is sour about it. He sends the player to the woman who brought the order: Vosh-Leera, the root-herald
of Jal-Veesa, a town down the track.

The player is not chosen or special. The Reed combs the work gangs for outsiders with no kin and no ties, because
such a stranger makes a useful spy and a cheap one. The player learns this early and plainly. They want what any
freed stranger wants: work, coin and a place in the world. The Reed offers all three.

## 2. Why the trees matter

A Hist is a tree the size of a tower. Every Argonian village is built round one. A hatchling licks its tree's sap
on its naming day, and the sap shapes its body for life (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:54-56`). The eggs lie in pools
among the roots. If a tree's bond with its eggs is cut, the unborn die (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:78-80`). A village
whose tree dies has no future: no egg in its pools will hatch again.

The trees are alive and aware. An Argonian with a hand on the bark hears them (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:106-107`).
Sap drunk brings visions of far places (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:31-32`). All the trees are joined at the root, so
what one knows the others can know (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:15-16`). A tree can also give a blessing to someone
its people trust.

The player meets all of this in the first two quests, as things they see and use. They see a sick tree, dry and
dark, with its egg-pool going cold. Its minder tells them what the town loses if it dies. They take the tree's
blessing, which lets them breathe under water for a time. Then they drink sap and see a vision of a real place.

## 3. The truth under the story

**What the player is shown.** Hist trees are falling sick one at a time. A healthy tree gives its minder a little
sap at the scar. A sick tree's scar closes and gives nothing. Its bark dries and darkens, and its egg-pool cools.
The tree-minders cannot find a cause. The Reed sends the player to find it.

**What the player finds, in order.** At the first sick tree they see for themselves there is a grey paste in the
closed scar, and jars of sap hidden nearby. Someone drained the tree and then sealed it. A captive names the
drainers: the Unbound. Later the player finds bottles of the grey paste among the Unbound's things. It is a
paralysis poison, made to a recipe of their leader's. It numbs the scar shut. A tree drained hard and then
poisoned may die of it.

**The question the player is left with.** The Unbound want sap, and they take it. Why do they then seal the tree?
If their leader wanted the trees dead, she could kill them. The player writes this question in their journal and
carries it to the end. Nobody answers it until she does.

**What the Unbound want.** Their leader, Cuts-the-Old-Knot, wants the Eye of Argonia. The priest-kings cut the Eye
from Hist amber, the solid sap that the trees no longer make (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:154`). Canon leaves the
Eye's nature open (`eye-of-argonia.md:6-8`), so what follows is our extrapolation. The Eye sits at the Root, under
a drowned tower, behind a gate that the last king sealed. It is the knot that joins every root to every other. Used
where it sits, it can pour strength down every root and heal every tree. Lifted from its place, it parts every
tree from every other for good.

**Why she drains sap.** A beast guards the Root. Sap makes a fighter stronger (`topics/hist-and-sap.md:31-32`),
so she is brewing a draught strong enough to kill the beast. The player works this out from what they find, and
the Reed agrees.

**What the Reed is.** In 4E 48 a mad Hist at Lilmoth helped the An-Xileel call the flying city Umbriel down on the
marsh (`topics/umbriel.md:26-29`). The Reed set itself to watch the trees so that it would never happen again. It
watches through dreamers: people locked in the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth, fed sap and made to tell their dreams. A
dreamer's visions show what any tree has seen, anywhere in the province. That is how the Reed knows so much. Its
hold on the marsh rests on it.

**What the Reed wants of the Eye.** The Reed does not want the Eye moved. The Eye must stay where it is, locked
and guarded, or the trees are parted and the dreams stop. The Reed wants the Root found before the Unbound find
it, the gate's three seal-stones kept out of their hands and Cuts-the-Old-Knot dead.

**Who she is.** Cuts-the-Old-Knot was a dreamer in the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth for nine years, until she found a
way out. She does not want the trees dead. She seals each tree that she drains so that the Reed can draw no sap
from it for its dreamers. She wants the Eye lifted so that no dreamer can ever see through the trees again. She
accepts that some trees will die when they are parted. To her that is the price of a free marsh.

## 5. The acts

The main quest is nineteen quests in three acts, as many as Skyrim's. Act I sets up the mystery and the stakes.
Act II is the treasure hunt, the heart of the plot: each find points to the next. Act III is the gate, the twist
and the end.

Danger climbs with the plot. Act I is played in the settled north (bands D1 to D3). The hunt runs through
dangerous country (D3) into deadly country (D4), and its last stone lies in one of the province's worst ruins (D5).
Section 9 lists the four places whose bands the plot asks to be lowered.

Every quest has at least two ways through for different builds: melee of any kind, archery, magic, or stealth
paired with one of them. A few quests also have a spoken way, which no quest needs. Every objective has a marker.
Where the player may fight or sneak, the journal says so.

The Reed's ranks come with the plot, each with a fixed reward:

| Rank | Given after | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Reed-Hand | quest 5 | a set of light Argonian armour (from Sign of Cipactli, under our name) and the Reed's traders |
| Listener | quest 10 | a rapier of the Reed (from Animated Armoury, under our name) or a quarterstaff, as the player chooses |
| Watcher | quest 13 | a cuirass and helm of the Reed's heavier make (Stormhold Warrior Armor, under our name), and free beds in every Archwarden fort |
| Warden's Ear | quest 16 | the Reed's best: an enchanted pike or enchanted claws, and the rest of the Stormhold Warrior set |
| Reed-Master | the Use ending | section 8 |

### Act I: the sick trees

**1. No Name on the Work Roll** (Sedge-Kal Camp, a reed-cutters' camp in the Valley of Storms). The player gives
Okeeh their name and trade in the hold, and the door is unlocked. Dralin speaks first on the landing and frees
them. He names Vosh-Leera of Jal-Veesa, and the journal marks her house. The player's pack holds two lockpicks.
*Leads on:* Dralin's directions and the marker on Vosh-Leera's door.

**A side quest here: What the Herald Sent.** Vosh-Leera sent a purse and a knife for the player with her order.
Dralin does not see why he should hand them over, and he has "mislaid" the key to his strongbox. Okeeh tells the
player so. The key can be lifted from his belt (Pickpocket), the box picked (Lockpicking) or Dralin talked round
(Speech). The lock is the easiest kind, and Dralin's pocket is easy to pick, so a new character who chose either
skill can do it. The main quest does not wait for it.

**2. The Dry Tree** (Jal-Veesa, a town of the Sedge-Wardens; Reed-Sill Weir, a weir-wardens' camp). The town's
Hist is sick: its bark is dry and dark, its scar has closed and its egg-pool is cooling. Vosh-Leera tells the
player about the Reed, an old order that keeps the marsh's ways safe and has work for a stranger. First the
tree-minder needs Sithis' beard, a moss that hangs over the creek at Reed-Sill Weir. Crocodiles lie below the
weir. The player kills them, shoots them from the log walk or slips past them. The minder's poultice warms the
pool but does not open the scar. She tells the player what the town loses if the tree dies. The tree gives the
player its blessing: for a time they breathe under water. *Leads on:* Vosh-Leera asks the player back to her
house for a second task.

**3. The First Dream** (Jal-Veesa; Vel-Sapha Stand, a Root-Folk outpost in Barsaebica). Vosh-Leera gives the
player a cup of sap. Tree-minders drink it, she says, to ask the trees a question. The vision shows a young Hist
among stilt huts and a reed hide beside a lone dead tree. She knows the place: Vel-Sapha Stand. The journal marks
it. Heem-Jasa, the minder there, stands by her tree in the village. Its scar closed three nights ago. She shows the
player the grey paste in it. The hide from the vision stands outside the village, with a marker. Three Unbound
wait there with jars of sap. They are fought, shot from cover or passed unseen while the player takes a jar and
the note that lies with the jars. Back in the village, Heem-Jasa asks whether her tree will live. The player tells
her the truth or a comfort. *Leads on:* the note tells the crew to bring their jars to Karsh-Naal Camp. Reading
it marks the camp.

**4. The Sap Camp** (Karsh-Naal Camp, a camp of tribeless Naga in the Valley of Storms). The camp is the Unbound's.
Sesk, a Naga who drew sap for them, is caged at its edge for trying to leave. The journal says that the player may
fight the camp or go quietly, and markers show the cage and the guard who carries its key. The guards are few and
stand at the fire, away from the cage, so a quiet player can reach it. The player picks the cage (Lockpicking),
lifts the key (Sneak and Pickpocket) or kills the guards. Sesk names the Unbound and their leader,
Cuts-the-Old-Knot. She says the sap is for a draught, and the draught is for something to do with "the Eye". She
calls the grey paste "the seal" and does not know what is in it. *Leads on:* Vosh-Leera says this is more than a
herald can judge. She sends the player to the Reed's handler in Helstrom and marks the house.

**5. The Handler** (Helstrom, the oldest city). Nesh-Deeka hears all that the player found and makes them a
Reed-Hand, the Reed's first rank. She tells them what the Eye is and where it lies: at the Root, behind a gate
that the old kings sealed, in a place nobody now alive has found. The player says what the draught must be for,
and she agrees: Cuts-the-Old-Knot means to kill whatever guards the Root. To prove the player, Nesh-Deeka sends
them after an Unbound go-between who lodges in the city with a jar of stolen sap. The jar is lifted from his pack,
bought from him or won in a fight with him and his two hired men. *Leads on:* Nesh-Deeka gives the Reed's aim.
The Root must be found before the Unbound find it. The Eye must stay where it is. She has one lead: an "Eye of
Argonia" is for sale in Gideon.

### Act II: the treasure hunt

Every find in Act II is a clue to the next. After each quest the player reports to the handler, who reads the
find and names the next place. Nesh-Deeka always knows a little more than the player expects about where the
Unbound have been. Asked how, she says "we listen" and changes the subject. Each time, the journal notes that she
turned the question aside.

**6. The Glass Eye** (Gideon, the relic-dealers' city; Gideon South Watch, an Imperial ruin). A dealer in Gideon's
market sells the Eye of Argonia. It is glass, and he admits it for a few coins or a little Speech. He bought it
from the smugglers of the South Watch, who kept the old silver setting it came in. The player goes into the
ruin's rooms and takes the setting from the smugglers' cache by force or by stealth (Sneak and Lockpicking). The
Collector got there first and left the player a courteous note in the cache. He took nothing: the setting was of
no use to him. *Leads on:* a ship's name is scratched inside the setting. Nesh-Deeka knows it. The *Pale Harvest*
sank in Hatandros Bight with a Gideon dealer's whole stock aboard. She marks the wreck.

**7. The Pale Harvest** (the Wreck of the Pale Harvest, a shipwreck in Hatandros Bight). The wreck lies in shallow
water, a short dive past slaughterfish. Air has held inside the hull. The drowned crew walk the hold, and their
captain stands beside his strongbox. The player fights them, or steals past and picks the strongbox, or takes the
key from the captain. Inside is a keystone on a chain, of Kothringi make. *Leads on:* Nesh-Deeka does not know
the keystone. The Chime-Keepers of Toklea keep what is left of Kothringi lore, she says, and she marks their town.

**8. The Drowned Street** (Toklea, a Chime-Keepers town in the Tulmek Washes; Zul-Etha, a drowned village). The
Chime-Keepers' elder knows the keystone and names her price. Her family's grave-stake lies in a chest in the
drowned street of Zul-Etha, and she wants it raised. Crocodiles lie on the roof peaks that stand out of the water,
with an old bull among them. The player kills them from a roof or from range, calms them with Illusion or swims
under them unseen to the chest. *Leads on:* the elder says the keystone opens the tomb of the knight-captain at
Eurydike. The Kothringi knights of Gideon were the last to guard the kings' road to the Root. She marks the ruin.

**9. The Knights' Tomb** (Eurydike, a Kothringi ruin in the Kotherra Mangroves). The knights' restless dead walk
the barracks. A party of Unbound is already inside and has not been able to open the sealed tomb. The player
fights through both, slips past both or turns the dead with Restoration. With the keystone in hand, the tomb's
door opens. Inside lies the knight-captain's bronze disc, stamped with a mine's mark. Among the Unbound's packs
are bottles of the grey paste. The game names them for what they are: a paralysis poison. The journal asks the
question of section 3. An Unbound who is spared here can give the player a side quest (section 6). *Leads on:*
Nesh-Deeka knows the mark: Vicrium, a mine in the east. She already knew that the Unbound had been at Eurydike.

**10. Down into Vicrium** (Vicrium, a mine of Okuwun Rahei). The miners sealed their lower galleries behind a
timber door after a roof fall. The gallery-boss keeps the key. The player gets it by Pickpocket, Speech or gold,
or goes round by the flooded drift, where crocodiles lie. Smugglers have moved into the lower galleries. Past
them, in the knights' old strongbox, is a sea-chart cut on a slate: seven towers standing in the sea, and a
watchtower on an island. *Leads on:* Nesh-Deeka makes the player a Listener, the Reed's second rank, and passes
them to her officer, Never-Writes-Twice. She tells the player how Lilmoth's mad tree brought Umbriel. The Reed
"watches over" the trees, she says, so that it never happens again. She tells her lie about Cuts-the-Old-Knot.
The island on the chart is Aphon Isle, and she marks its tower.

**11. The Signal Tower** (Aphon Signal Tower, an Imperial ruin on Aphon Isle; Kelp-Mouth Cave, a sea cave on the
isle's east shore). The Collector's hired men hold the tower. They are fought, or passed by stealth. In the
tower's base is their dig: an empty hole and a rubbing of an old stone that names the cave on the east shore. In
the cave, Unbound scouts hold the dry outer chamber. The inner chamber is flooded and is reached by swimming.
There, in a chest, is the kings' marker stone. It shows the seven drowned towers with one marked. With the scouts'
things lies the recipe for the seal. Reading it teaches the player to make the paralysis poison, and three bottles
lie with it. A wounded scout sits apart from the fight at the cave mouth. She tells the player to ask what moans
in the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth. *Leads on:* Never-Writes-Twice reads the marker stone: the marked tower is one
of the drowned spires off Lilmoth. She says the divers of Bell Station will know which. Asked about Fort
Chalkmoth, she says it is a fort like any other. The journal sets a second objective: see the cellar for
themselves.

**12. The Cellar Door** (Fort Chalkmoth, an Archwarden fort on the Chixathe Mesa). The garrison lets a Reed
Listener in. A player who would not be seen can go in by the trapdoor in the yard. Either way, the stair leads
down to the old prison. At its door a Reed gaoler stops the player and speaks first: the cellar is closed to
them. Behind the door the player hears moaning. The journal records it. That is all they can do here for now.
*Leads on:* the journal turns the player back to the hunt, and to Bell Station.

**13. The Kings' Gate** (Bell Station, a diving station in Benthic Yobesh; Teku-Sul Spire, a drowned tower). The
station's steward tells the player which tower the stone marks: Teku-Sul, whose sealed gate her divers have never
forced. She marks it. Nobody leads the player down. The dive is long. The player needs the Hist's blessing, a
spell of waterbreathing, a potion (the steward sells them) or an enchantment of their own. Being Argonian is
enough. Inside the tower there is air. The drowned priests' attendants hold the east halls. Past them stands a
great gate with three empty sockets. The ghost of a priest of the old kings stands before it and speaks: a
seal-stone belongs in each socket. He names where each was taken when the last king fell: a thief's cell in
Blackrose Prison, the flooded vaults of Gemin and the deepest hall of Vorannuhl. *Leads on:* Never-Writes-Twice
makes the player a Watcher, the Reed's third rank. Her orders are to take all three stones before the Unbound do.
The journal marks all three places. They can be done in any order, and are numbered here from the least dangerous
to the most.

**14. The Thief's Stone** (Blackrose Prison, in the Tulmek Washes). A masked thief who sought the Eye died in the
Rose's cells, and his stone lies in the warden's vault. The heirs of the old garrison hold the prison under a Naga
chief, who carries the vault's key. The player has three ways in.

- *At the gate.* The gate-captain stops the player and speaks first. The player pays a large toll, persuades him
  (a hard Speech check) or duels him. If persuasion fails, only the toll and the duel are left. In the duel he
  yields when he is badly hurt.
- *Unseen.* A player who gets past the gate without being stopped (Sneak, or Illusion's Invisibility) is inside as
  a stranger nobody has noticed. If they are caught, the gate-captain's talk begins.
- *By force.* If the player kills the gate-captain, the whole garrison turns on them.

Inside, there are three states. *A guest* (the toll, persuasion or the gate-captain's yield): the garrison is
friendly. The chief sells the vault key for a great deal of gold. The player can also pick his pocket, or pick the
vault's hard lock by night. *Unseen:* the player picks the vault's lock or lifts the key from the chief. *An
enemy:* the player fights the yard and the hall, and takes the key from the chief's body.

The Collector is in the gatehouse as the chief's guest. He has offered a price for the stone and been told to
wait. He stands apart from the garrison and never fights. In every state the player may talk to him, rob him,
kill him or leave him. Nothing the plot needs is his. If he lives, he offers a side quest (section 6). *Leads on:*
the journal still marks the stones that are left.

**15. The Vault's Stone** (Gemin, a flooded Ayleid ruin of Barsaebica). The second stone lies in the lowest hall.
The restless dead hold the vaults, and a wraith guards the last hall. An Unbound rearguard is searching the upper
halls and has not got past the dead. The player fights, sneaks or turns the dead. Beside the stone is a tablet
that the kings cut. It tells the player, in plain words, why the kings sealed the Root: whoever lifts the Eye
parts the trees. *Leads on:* the journal marks the last stone.

**16. The Deepest Hall** (Vorannuhl, a Velothi ruin on the Chixathe Mesa). The third stone lies in a vault that a
bronze key opens. Winged Sarpa raiders hold the outer yard, and one of them carries the key. The plague-dead of
the old household walk the deep halls, and its lord, a wraith, keeps the vault. It is the hardest place of the
hunt, with its hardest fight or its hardest sneak. *Leads on:* with three stones in hand the player is made
Warden's Ear, the Reed's fourth rank. Never-Writes-Twice says that the Unbound are camped at the gate with picks
and fire. If they break through, the player must be able to follow, so the player is to carry the stones. Her
order is to kill Cuts-the-Old-Knot at the gate.

### Act III: the gate

**17. At the Kings' Gate** (Teku-Sul Spire). The player goes down again through an Unbound rearguard in the east
halls, who are fought, slipped past or held off by magic. Before the gate stands Cuts-the-Old-Knot. She got there
first, but she has no seal-stones and cannot open it. She speaks first and tells the player the truth about the
Reed (section 7). The player then chooses: fight her here, or believe her. *Leads on:* the gate, and the three
stones the player carries.

**18. The Guardian** (Teku-Sul Spire, the hall beyond the gate). The player sets the three stones in their
sockets, and the gate opens. Beyond is a great hall where the Root's guardian waits: a hydra, old beyond memory.
Nobody in the story explains it. The deep places of the world keep such things. It is highly venomous, and the air
and the water round it are poison. It fights in phases. When it is badly hurt its heads grow back, and heads that
come off crawl away and roam the hall as sentries. It has its own music. If the
player believed her, Cuts-the-Old-Knot is already in the hall when they step through, and she fights beside them
as a battlemage. The guardian's body holds rich loot. *Leads on:* the door at the hall's far end.

**19. The Eye** (Teku-Sul Spire, the Root). In the last chamber the Eye of Argonia sits on a plinth among the
roots of every tree. The sounds that divers hear in the trench of Echo Deep nearby come up from here. The player
activates the Eye and chooses one of three endings (section 8).

## 6. Side quests on the road

Through Act II, people the player meets give side quests off the main road. Each giver says plainly that it is a
side errand and that the Eye can wait. Each gives a riddle in place of directions. The place it names has no
marker until the player finds it. When they get there, the journal says they guessed right. The work there has
markers as usual and uses the player's skills like any quest.

Each reward is made for the fight with the guardian. A player who does them all finds that fight easy. The
rewards serve every build: some protect, some heal and some attack.

| Who gives it | Where the riddle leads | The reward, against the guardian |
|---|---|---|
| Vosh-Leera, after quest 5 | Mistwatch, an Imperial ruin on Cape Percar | an amulet that turns most of the guardian's venom |
| Sesk, if freed in quest 4 | Kitsa Burrow, a Lilmothiit ruin of the Althii Archipelago | scrolls of a paralysing spell, enough to hold the guardian three times |
| the Toklea elder, after quest 8 | Va-Seeth Spire, a drowned tower | a spell tome: a spell of ours that burns the guardian far worse than it burns anything else |
| a spared Unbound, after quest 9 | Anutasil, an Ayleid ruin of Blackwood | a quiver of arrows that slow what they strike |
| Bell Station's steward, after quest 13 | Zeshul-Tha Spire, a drowned tower | scrolls of a storm that strikes every head in reach at once, the roaming heads among them, and potions that cure its venom quickly |
| the Collector, if left alive in quest 14 | the Kelp Lantern, a wreck in Benthic Yobesh | a blade that bites deeper into the guardian than into anything else |

## 7. The twist at the gate

Cuts-the-Old-Knot tells the player what the Reed is. It keeps people in the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth, feeds them
sap and makes them tell their dreams. The moans were dreamers. She was one of them for nine years, and
Never-Writes-Twice held the key. A dreamer sees what any tree has seen. Through its dreamers the Reed sees
everything that happens in the marsh.

She calls back to what the player saw. "How did your handler always know where to send you? Who did you think was
behind the cellar door? Why did you think the old ways were good ways?" She answers the question in the player's
journal. She seals each tree that she drains so that the Reed can draw no sap from it for its dreamers. She never
meant a tree to die of it, and some have.

She grants the Reed its reason, which is to stop another Umbriel. She also says what it costs: everyone's freedom,
so that the Reed can rule unseen. She wants the Eye lifted, which parts the trees, ends the dreams and breaks the
Reed. She admits that many trees will die of it, with the villages under them. The player then fights her here or
goes on with her.

**If they fight.** She drinks her draught and becomes a powerful battlemage, the hardest single fight before the
guardian. Her body gives spell tomes, her robes and staff and the rest of her draughts. The player goes on alone.
The third ending is closed.

**If they side with her.** She is already in the hall beyond the gate when the player steps through, and she
fights the guardian at their side. Nobody follows the player: the stage that the player sets by opening the gate
switches her off at the gate and on in the hall while the game loads it.

## 8. The three endings

The player activates the Eye. A message lays out the three choices and what each will do. Each ending is shown as
a vision. The trees changing across the province are a short film that we make in a later phase. What a film
cannot show (the cellar opened, the Reed's rage) is told in the messages that follow it. Until the films are
made, messages carry the whole vision. Then the player walks back into a world that has changed.

**Take the Eye.** The player lifts the Eye from its plinth. That act parts the trees. The player keeps the Eye.

- *The vision:* the trees across the marsh parting from each other, and about three in every ten dying. Then, in
  words: the Unbound opening the cellar of Fort Chalkmoth, and the Reed's people raging.
- *It gives:* the Eye, an amulet with strong enchantments and a power usable once a day. The Unbound's highest
  rank. Their trainers and traders as friends. If Cuts-the-Old-Knot lives, she thanks the player.
- *It costs:* every Reed member and every friend of the Reed attacks on sight. No Hist gives the player its
  blessing again. About three trees in ten are dead when the player next sees them: blackened, with their villages
  empty. Vel-Sapha Stand's and Jal-Veesa's are among them.

**Use the Eye.** The player uses the Eye where it sits. Every tree is healed and the Root stays whole.

- *The vision:* the trees across the marsh healing. Then, in words: the Reed's hold on the province made fast for
  another age.
- *It gives:* the Reed's highest rank, Reed-Master. The keep of Fort Chalkmoth as the player's own house, with its
  trainer, smith, bed and benches. Free training from Reed members up to a set level. Stronger blessings from
  every Hist.
- *It costs:* if Cuts-the-Old-Knot is alive, the Eye's power runs through the sap she drank and kills her. Her
  body gives the battlemage's loot. Every Unbound and every friend of the Unbound attacks the player on sight. The
  dreamers stay in the cellar below the player's new house.

**Give her the Eye.** Open only if Cuts-the-Old-Knot is alive. She lifts the Eye and keeps it.

- *The vision:* as in Take.
- *It gives:* the Unbound's highest rank. Cuts-the-Old-Knot as an ally whom the player calls with one power and
  sends away with another, and who fights as a battlemage. Strong spells that she teaches, with a power usable
  once a day. The longhouse of Karsh-Naal Camp as the player's stronghold.
- *It costs:* as in Take: the Reed's enmity everywhere, no Hist blessings and the dead trees. The Eye is hers.

After the end the world stays open. The rewards are worth most to a player who goes on to the province's hardest
places, which a later piece of work names and marks (section 9).
