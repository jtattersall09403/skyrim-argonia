# The Blackrose Chainbreakers in the new world: the line's story

The story of the Chainbreakers' six quests, re-homed from Black Marsh & Valenwood's Blackrose to the new world's
Blackrose and its country. It is the story of [chainbreakers](chainbreakers.md), which met the owner's bar and whose
first three quests were built and played on paper, grown into new ground: the same hook, people and turns, every
quest re-seated in a place the digest describes, and whatever the new ground changes, changed. It is written inside
the quest ceiling ([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)), the owner's bar
([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)) and the province plan ([province-plan](province-plan.md), sections 3.1, 4 and 7).
A quest writer turns each full outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations. **D** is the digest of the new world ([province](../../world/province.md) and the region
files beside it): "D tulmek-washes, Reedfall Farm" is that place's record or its hooks. Lore is `file:line` under
`story/lore/`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation from what is cited (decision 0032). A place is named by
what its record says it is; anything a quest needs there that the record does not promise is a row of section 4.

**Words used below**, for a reader new to the province. *Hist*: the sentient trees every Argonian is hatched under
and goes home to at death. *The Owing*: the native sentence, months of owed work in place of a cell. *Nushmeeko*: a
month of work nobody thanks you for, the unit an Owing is counted in. *The count*: a holder's own written reckoning
of what a debtor still owes. *The Rose*: the old Imperial prison south of Blackrose, held by *the heirs*, the
families born inside it, under a Naga *raj-kaal* (war-leader). *The island core*: the walled middle of Blackrose,
whose holders rule the city by possession. *Put under*: sent down into the Rose's drowned cells, where the dead
still walk.

## 1. The faction in a page

**The wrong they fight.** Black Marsh has no jail of its own. Its sentence is the Owing: months of hard work, counted
in nushmeekos, assessed by an arbitrator, worked off and then never mentioned again
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:68`, `:75`, `:198`). Where it works it is kinder than a cell and Argonians will say so
(`:154`). In the south it has drifted. Whoever holds the farm, the crossing or the hiring berth now does the
assessing (`:84`); an Owing can be sold on (`:90`), can grow with food, breakage and sickness faster than it is
worked (`:94`), and passes with the household when the debtor dies (`:97`). A person held for years away from their
Hist goes visibly grey, their tribe's colour and markings fading (`:112`), and one who dies there may never get home
to the tree at all (`:119`).

Blackrose makes that last cruelty its own. The city's Hist "lies drowned in the lake, reached only by boat and dive",
and "the island core controls hatching and naming"; Lujei's elders "pay a share of the crossing tolls to the island
core as the price of that burial right" (D tulmek-washes, Blackrose; Lujei). So a bound hand on the lake goes grey
within sight of her own tree's water, because nobody will pay the core's due to pole her out to it (X).

**Who they are.** A few dozen people in and around Blackrose, the lake city "whose people trust no institution" and
through which "everything moving between Murkwood, Oliis Bay and Bramman's river passes" (D province, Peoples: the
Island of Blackrose), who get the held out. A place that swallows people is where the people who fetch them out
gather. Their seat is a plain house on the city's north side, and their patron is **Never-Sold**, who was born owing
and discharged it ([cast roster](../36-cast-roster.md) §56). Their oath is three words, "Bones go home", and it
covers the living and the dead alike: the burial work and the freeing are one work
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:122`). Nobody above them cares. The island core's guard keeps the toll and the core's
own walls, and treats a theft as a theft whoever is robbed; the lake villages keep their own elders; the Rose is "a
day's march off and not theirs" and has no law at all (D tulmek-washes, Blackrose; Blackrose Prison).

**How they work (X).** Never-Sold teaches that an Owing stands on three legs, and that it falls when any one is
taken away:

- **the count**: the holder's own reckoning of what is still owed, kept under lock. Writing it down is a habit the
  delta's rice families took from the Lilmoth buyers who advance them seed, and the prison-born took from the
  Empire, and it is theirs alone (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:84` to `:88`; province-vibe §5: "the ledger is the
  collaborator's tool"; D province, Peoples: Lilmoth and the Murkmire Coast; the Warden Marches). An honest village
  Owing has no count, only witnesses who then forget. The Chainbreakers steal the count or carry it off;
- **the word**: the holder's or keeper's say-so that the debt stands. They talk it round, or buy it at the Owing's
  first price, which by custom closes it for good;
- **the hand**: whoever is paid to fetch a runaway back. They get past it, or break it.

Buying a person out is the quiet way and the oldest, and it stays in the background of every job. The play is the
other two legs: a key lifted from a belt, a lock picked under a household's eyes, a dead man's sealed chest fetched
off a stranded raft among crocodiles, a keeper faced down, a fetcher fought on a track. A freed person is never led
anywhere. They say where they will go, and are found there afterwards.

**Who they are up against.** Nobody in the line is a villain, and everyone has a case
(`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:80` to `:82`, "none requires a villain").

- **The Tulm-Varo of Reedfall Farm** (X on D tulmek-washes, Reedfall Farm): the rice family on the channel bank
  between Blackrose and the Rose, who sell to the city and the Lilmoth road. The family is five under one stilted
  roof; the hands who work its far paddies toward Tei-Kur are bound to it. Its elder, **Meema Tulm-Varo**, is met:
  the boat was truly broken, the rice a hand eats is real rice, one paddy is failing and the Lilmoth buyers forgive
  nothing. Her keeper, **Hulm-Tei**, holds the count, and was hatched owing on that ground himself.
- **The heirs of the Rose**: the prison-born families who hold the old prison (`topics/prisons.md:23`, `:64`; D
  tulmek-washes, Blackrose Prison), "many generations deep", under the Naga **Raj-Kaal Druxeth** (X: the name), who
  "resent the word prison". Newcomers owe them for shelter (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:136`). Their case, spoken
  by **Third-Born Tsaxi** at the gate and later by **Third-Born Xeekh** in the yard: the Rose is the only society
  four hundred people have. It fed them, and it keeps the drowned cells shut on the dead below
  (`topics/prisons.md:67`; D: "the risen inmates in the drowned lower cells").
- **The island core**, under **Toll-Holder Nomu-Tei** (X: the name), which sells the way to the drowned tree: every
  hatching, naming and burial on the lake pays it. Its case: the dive is deep, the boats and the divers are the
  core's, and a tree nobody guards is a tree the Rose's raiders would sell water from.
- **Ussa-Rekh, the Broker**, on Soulrest's quay, where a ship's berth is bought with somebody's months
  (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:135`). He bought people out for thirty years and now hires out the crews he freed.
  His case: every season's hire buys the next people out of Thorn, where it is worst (`:132`).
- **The fetchers and berth-masters** who are the hand: people doing paid work, who believe a runaway fetched back is
  kinder than what the holder does to those left behind. Reedfall's fetcher is also the watchman who keeps the
  crocodiles off its paddies at night (D tulmek-washes, Reedfall Farm: "the family keeps watch at night").

**What it costs.** A Chainbreaker caught at a holder's box is a thief under native law, and the sentence for a thief
is an Owing (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:200`); at Soulrest, under foreign law, it is a cell (D cape-percar,
Soulrest). Somebody always pays for a freeing: a keeper, the hands who stayed, a stranger further down the river, or
the Chainbreaker's own purse. The line never offers a way out that nobody pays for. The faction is poor: what coin
it has goes out as first prices.

**What is unsettled about them.**

1. Their acts land on other bound people. In this line the keepers are owing too.
2. Buying people out puts coin in the holders' hands and a price on every debtor. Ussa-Rekh is what the
   Chainbreakers were thirty years ago (X): he founded the work, and he bought Never-Sold out.
3. They do not ask what a person is held for. One of the three at the Rose's gate is held for a killing, and the
   Rose's reckoner died at Lujei Ferry because somebody on the lake cut a cable.
4. Never-Sold hides her own past (the line's hidden spine, section 2), and at Reedfall the holder knows it.
5. The Owing is native and it ends, which no prison does; take it away and the province has nothing
   (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:154` to `:158`). A Nisswo will say that a debt outliving its cause is a sin, and
   that so is a grudge nursed for generations (`:161`).

**Why a player wants in.** The jobs are break-ins, jailbreaks and stand-offs with a cause, each ends in a choice
the player has to live with, and each pays in something a thief can use: a bed and a door on the lake, a key, a
holder's own box. The faction is the underdog, its patron is worth unpicking, and what the player steals is a
person's life back.

**The build leaning.** Lockpicking, Pickpocket and Sneak are the faction's own skills, because the count is always
behind a lock, on a belt or under someone's eye. Speech is its second way, the persuader's, because the word can be
turned. Fighting (any weapon skill or Destruction, with Block or armour to live through it) is the third leg's
skill, and an honest one: every hand in the line is one hard, fixed opponent with a fixed reward, and a death buys
something the quiet ways cannot, at the price of a life. Each of the first three quests can be done by each of the
five. Nothing is levelled to the player (decision 0035): each danger sits at its place's fixed band (D province,
Danger), and the line climbs them, from Reedfall's settled ground to the Rose's Deadly yard. Restoration and Alchemy
have a small part wherever the washed-out and the dying are tended. Never-Sold's fixed rewards are thieves' things.

**What other lines make of them.** The Night-Reed respect the craft and despise the cause: "Fine hands. But nobody
fences a person" ([night-reed](night-reed.md) §1). The patron of the Thorn Ash-Reed greets anyone of Chainbreaker
rank coldly and says why ([thorn-ash-reed](thorn-ash-reed.md) §1): the first quest gives the rank that greeting
reads. The Nisswo argue with them about debts that outlive their cause.

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** Never-Sold was Reedfall Farm's keeper of the count before Hulm-Tei. She was hatched owing
on that ground, taught to write small and exact, and kept the count for nine years. In her last two she wrote the
first lines on a boat-hand's leaf: four months for a broken boat, then the food, then a fever. Ussa-Rekh bought her
out nine years ago. She took a new name, broke with him in the year his buying turned into hiring, and has been
freeing people ever since, beginning as far as she can from the leaf she started. She tells no one. Meema Tulm-Varo
knows exactly who she is, and says only as much as stings. BC01 is Never-Sold's own first line struck out by
someone else's hand.

| The truth | Where it is seeded | Where it is found |
|---|---|---|
| Never-Sold kept Reedfall's count | BC01: she describes the box she has "never seen" to the hasp; she says "take hers, and do not read what lies under it"; the first two years of the real leaf are in a smaller hand than the rest; Hulm-Tei says the keeper before him "wrote smaller than I do"; Meema says "ask the one who sent you who taught my keeper his letters" | BC03: her own bound count on the Broker's shelf |
| She wrote the first lines of Rows-For-Another's Owing | BC02: the old woman remembers "the girl who kept the count wept when she wrote my daughter down, and then she wrote it" | BC03: the bound count names the farm and the years |
| Ussa-Rekh founded the work and bought her out | BC02: Tsaxi calls the Chainbreakers "the Broker's children" and Never-Sold will not say why | BC03: Ussa-Rekh says it, and the book proves it |
| Somebody on the lake cut Lujei Ferry's cable under the Rose's reckoner | BC02: the ferry-keeper says "the cable did not fray", and that her poleman has not come home | BC04: he is one of the three who come to Blackrose |

**The arc.** Each quest is a different kind of job, in a different kind of place, so that the line never becomes a
list of the same three doors. Danger climbs by the world's fixed bands (D province, Danger).

| Quest | The kind of job | Where | Band of its fight | Carried on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BC01 Four Months for a Boat | a locked box under a household's eyes | Reedfall Farm; afterwards Lujei Ferry | settled ground; one fixed fighter of the Wild band | the first rank; `bc01.settled`; where Rows-For-Another is |
| BC02 One Out of the Smoke | a dead man's chest fetched from among crocodiles, then a judgement among three people | the Rose's gatehouse; Lujei Ferry | Dangerous (the ferry's crocodiles); Deadly for whoever attacks the gate-keeper | `bc02.out`; who stands where |
| BC03 The Broker's Mercy (the turn) | a planned break-in of a house in a foreign port | Soulrest's quay and timber wharves | one fixed fighter above the Dangerous band, under a city's guard | the second rank; `bc03.settled`; `bc03.told` |
| BC04 Riot Without a Banner | three askers, three acts in three places, and an aftermath walked into | Blackrose's boardwalks; Khariton, Sheh-Ara or the Rose's gate; then the gate | Deadly | `bc04.side`; who stands at the gate |
| BC05 Bones to the Dirt | the line's dungeon, done three times over: the dead fetched home | Oru-Neel, Zul-Etha, Mereweather Estate; Lujei; Blackrose's landing | Deadly | the third rank; `bc05.stakes` |
| BC06 What the Rose Becomes | the last lock, in the hardest place, and a choice among four people | the Rose's gate and yard | Deadly | the ending; `bc06.key` |

Earlier choices never change a later quest's stages. They change greetings and single lines, read from stored
numbers. Only the finale's choice carries into the world after the line. **The ranks**: Hand (after BC01), Key
(after BC03), Breaker (after BC05), and Never-Sold's desk, which only one answer in BC06 gives.

### The later quests, at arc level

**BC04. Riot Without a Banner.** Offered once BC03 has ended. *Told:* a rising is coming inside the Rose, among
people who want different things: the prison-born under Third-Born Xeekh, who want the hierarchy kept; the newcomers
who owe them for shelter, most of them lake people the Rose's own raiders emptied out of Sheh-Ara (D tulmek-washes,
Sheh-Ara: "the Rose's raiders emptied it"), who want it gone; and those who want the drowned cells opened and the
Rose left ([40-factions](../40-factions.md) §39; `topics/prisons.md:64` to `:67`). The rising cannot be staged and
is not. It happens inside the Rose, where the player is not. The quest is what comes before it. Three people have
come north and stand on Blackrose's boardwalks, each needing one act that tips the rising their way, each act a
different skill in a different place:

- *for the prison-born*, Xeekh's sister-daughter: the heirs' own watch captain at Khariton (D tulmek-washes,
  Khariton: "a Blackguard watch post of three outlaws", Deadly) is landing blades for the newcomers on the silted
  channel. Kill him, the line's hardest fight so far and its richest chest; or empty his chest of the blades behind
  his back (Sneak, Lockpicking);
- *for the newcomers*, the missing poleman of Lujei Ferry, who cut the cable under the reckoner (X): his people
  inside will walk out only if they have somewhere to be a people. Bring Sheh-Ara's egg-stone, "the stone every
  Sheh-Ara hatchling was named over" (D tulmek-washes, Sheh-Ara), from the leaning minder's platform to the elder at
  Lujei. A large crocodile lies under that platform (Deadly; "the Lujei road above is the only safe approach"): it
  is swum round, crept past or killed;
- *for those who want the cells opened*, a friend of Walks-Far: Tsaxi's key to the gatehouse's inner door, lifted
  from her belt (Pickpocket), or her eye talked away from it (Speech).

The first act brought home is the one that counts: its stage sets `bc04.side`, and the other two askers then say
they are too late. The player reports to Never-Sold in her house; only then, with the player indoors, the Rose's
gate is switched for the aftermath. The player walks back to find it: who stands at the gate beside Tsaxi and who
does not, a strongbox broken open, and each survivor's account of what the player's act did. Nobody tells the player
they chose rightly. *Skills:* a weapon skill or Destruction; Sneak and Lockpicking; Pickpocket or Speech. *Places:*
Blackrose; Khariton; Sheh-Ara; Lujei; the Rose's gate. *Gains and losses in play, by side:* the watch captain's
chest and his arms, and the Rose's gate opened to the player as a guest (the Rose's forge and its quartermaster, a
fence: D tulmek-washes, Blackrose Prison, "once the gate is opened to the player"); or a bed and a friend's table
among the Sheh-Ara families at Lujei and the heirs' gate shut to the player by bargain; or Tsaxi's key and her
regard lost for good.

**BC05. Bones to the Dirt.** Offered once BC04 has ended. *Told:* **Grave-Singer Ossu**, who is Lujei's
tree-minder and "carries the dead out by boat to the drowned Blackrose tree" (D tulmek-washes, Lujei), will not
leave the lake's bound dead where they lie. No procession walks: the player is the procession. Three of the dead,
three places, three kinds of nerve:

- a bound field-hand's grave-stake standing in the salt paddy at Oru-Neel, where a wamasu lies (D tulmek-washes,
  Oru-Neel; Deadly): fought, or crept past and the stake drawn (Sneak);
- a runaway of the Broker's hired crews, dead of fever in the rice-barn at Mereweather Estate, whose steward hid
  him from Fort Reedmoth's keepers and cannot carry him out past his own master's sentries and dogs (D
  althii-archipelago, Mereweather Estate; its hook): a jailbreak for a dead man (Sneak, Lockpicking on the barn; or
  the steward's master talked round, Speech);
- a carved stake snagged in the drowned street of Zul-Etha under an old bull crocodile (D tulmek-washes, Zul-Etha;
  Dangerous). Its carving names an overseer of the old carting road, a keeper of bound carters. The oath says only
  "Bones go home".

At Blackrose's landing the island core's canoe-warden holds the third bundle back: the dead still owe the core its
burial due, an Owing passing with the household (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:97`). He is paid, talked round,
robbed of it or fought for it. At each hand-in Ossu greets the dead by name and says who they were, and the player
chooses whether the overseer goes to the tree with the hands he drove. *Skills:* all five of the line's. *Gains and
losses in play:* the wamasu's ground and the granary's locked bin at Oru-Neel; Ossu's gift of the drowned tree's
water (the record's blessing, Waterbreathing, as bottled draughts a stage gives); the core's regard lost if its
warden is robbed or killed, and with it the core's smith and trader as friends.

**BC06. What the Rose Becomes.** Starts when BC05 ends. After the rising the Rose rests on one thing: the key to
the iron hatch in its yard, over the drowned cells, because whoever keeps that hatch shut is owed by everyone who
sleeps above it (X, from `topics/prisons.md:67`). The finale uses every leg at once, in the line's hardest place:
the hatch key lies in a locked box by the forge in the Rose's yard (D tulmek-washes, Blackrose Prison: "the
Blackguards' forge in the yard"), the box's key is on its keeper, and the keeper stands among armed heirs of the
Deadly band. Tsaxi's line at the gate makes a Chainbreaker of the third rank the Rose's guest, so that the yard is
walked and not fought through; the key is then lifted, picked for, talked for or killed for, and killing for it
turns the whole yard. The Chainbreakers go no further than the yard: nothing below it is entered, and the hatch is
never opened in view. Then four people in four places each ask for the key, and the hand-over is the ending:

- to **Xeekh**, in the yard (the heirs are recognised and keep their hierarchy);
- to **Never-Sold**, in her house (the hierarchy is dispersed and the families stay as a people);
- to **Ossu**, at Lujei (the hatch is staked and sung, and the cells become a grave that nobody rules);
- to **Nisswo Okeeh-Ra** (X: a walker of the Turning Path, none of the four that line names), at Lujei Ferry's
  landing post (every reckoning at the Rose is witnessed from now on, no Owing there may be sold or grown, and the
  custom spreads only where others take it up: `topics/labour-and-bondage.md:158`).

Every end keeps the heirs in the Rose as a community (province-plan §4): what the Rose "becomes" is told, and shown
by who stands at the gate beside Tsaxi, switched on by the hand-over's stage from far off. *Gains and losses in
play, by hand-over:* the Rose's yard, forge and fence open for good and the heirs friends (Xeekh); the key to
Never-Sold's own strongbox and her house as the player's (Never-Sold); a staked shrine at the gate that gives the
drowned tree's blessing on land (Ossu); the Nisswo's skill book of Speech and safe passage through the gate for
anyone, the player's bounty-free standing with the heirs included (Okeeh-Ra). Last, alone with Never-Sold, the
player who knows her secret (`bc03.told`) answers one question of hers, "Will you tell them?": the truth and what is
just come apart here, and the journal does not rule on it. If the answer is yes she lays down her rank and offers
the player her desk, which is the only way the player comes to lead, and it costs the faction its founder twice
over.

## 3. The first three quests

Common to all three.

- **Never-Sold** stands inside her house on Blackrose's north side for the whole line. She must live: her death
  fails any running quest of the line with the faction's message. Nobody is essential. People stand where they are
  placed.
- **How the seat is found.** The keeper of the boardwalk tavern and the canoe-men at the island core's landing (D
  tulmek-washes, Blackrose) each have a line for a stranger who asks after work or after someone held: it names
  Never-Sold, says her house is on the north side, and that its door bears her name.
- **One working stage.** After its briefing each quest sits at a stage at which every way through is open. A way
  ends in a thing held, a person dead, a thing used, or a line that sets the stage which finishes the step. Where a
  way's result has to be remembered (the watchman bought, the door unbarred, the Broker's word), it is a stage of
  its own, numbered below the stages it can still lead to.
- **A line only moves the quest to a stage.** Whatever is given, taken, set, unlocked or switched is done by that
  stage. A stage takes from the player only what the line that reaches it has checked they hold.
- **Every switch is made by a line of Never-Sold's, inside her house,** of people and things that stand outdoors
  somewhere else. So no place in these outlines is promised to lie any distance from another.
- **A fight is started by the player.** Nobody who speaks turns on the player by a line. A person the player may
  choose to kill has a death that moves the quest, marked as the player's choice.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing, and the way is told in words as well. Journal entries are in
  the first person and the past tense and say only what the player has just done. Thresholds are fixed. Each failed
  check has its own refusal and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms; nothing new is modelled.
- "Night" and "dark" in what people say are their words only; no step depends on the hour.
- **What an ending gives.** Each ending's table says what the player gains and loses that they can go and use. The
  outcomes lane's worked example for this line (`story/design/quests/review/outcomes-main.md`) had not landed when
  these were written, so the tables use only what the quest form's results allow today: things and gold given or
  taken, a door unlocked, ownership of a container, a person or thing switched on or off, and a person's regard.
  Regard is used for what Skyrim itself makes of it: a person who is the player's friend lets their bed be slept in
  and their small things be taken without theft.

### BC01. Four Months for a Boat

**What a new player is told, and when.** On first speaking to Never-Sold, before any job, four optional lines, each
two or three sentences: *the Owing* (with nushmeeko glossed: "a month of work nobody thanks you for"); *the grey
ones* ("the Hist is the tree a tribe is hatched under; our colour comes from it, and so does the way home when we
die. Ours lies under the lake, and the island core sells the way down to it"); *the Chainbreakers* (the oath, and
the three legs in one breath); *what it pays and why I might not* (a thief's share, a bed and a door on the lake;
and "caught at a holder's box, you will be owing them yourself"). "Not yet" stays open and free. The job itself is
three sentences, and one thing more: she describes the box, cypress, the hasp on the left, and says "Take hers, and
do not read what lies under it."

**The hidden truth.** Eleven years ago Rows-For-Another, a lake woman of the families the Rose's raiders emptied
out of Sheh-Ara (D tulmek-washes, Sheh-Ara), hired on at Reedfall Farm as a boat-hand, holed the farm's boat and was
assessed four nushmeekos. The farm's keeper wrote her leaf: first the four months, then her food, then a fever.
Every line since is true and the Owing has outgrown her. In eleven years nobody has paid the island core's due for
her, so she has not once been poled out to the drowned tree, and she is going grey in sight of its water (X). The
Tulm-Varo trust no free hand with their count: the keeper is always a debtor hatched on the farm, and whatever any
leaf is short at the season's reckoning is written onto the keeper's own leaf, the last in the box. So a leaf that
vanishes and a debtor who runs both land on Hulm-Tei; and if his own leaf is gone too, the shortfall is spread over
the dozen hands in the far paddies. Never-Sold knows this, because she was that keeper once, and does not say it.
Rows-For-Another's kin are among the two families who keep Lujei Ferry (D tulmek-washes, Lujei Ferry), and the
farm's watchman knows it: the ferry is the first place he would look.

**The ways through** (all open at the working stage, in any order).

- *The count (Pickpocket or Lockpicking, with Sneak).* Lift the key from Hulm-Tei's belt as he stands at the shed's
  mouth, or slip past him and pick the Adept lock, with the household about the house in sight. Opening the box is
  the find, and has its own journal entry. Either leaf can then be taken. It goes wrong as Skyrim's own law does:
  seen, the player is a thief on the family's ground under native law; a bungled pickpocket turns the keeper on the
  player.
- *The word (Speech, the purse, or what the box gave).* Hulm-Tei declares the Owing closed before the household,
  which by custom ends it, in one of three ways, each a line of his that sets its own stage:
  1. *persuaded* (Speech 50, and the first price in hand): he closes it and writes the eleven years onto his own
     leaf with his eyes open. "I have carried worse than a boat-hand." Refused: "A true count is the only thing
     between me and the mud."
  2. *his own leaf put in his hand*: he takes it, closes hers and says the damp had his. He is free of the farm
     tonight, he leaves the count-box open to the player, and the shortfall of both leaves will be shared among the
     dozen hands who stay.
  3. *the long purse*: the whole of what it has grown to, 1,000 gold. The farm is paid and nobody carries anything.
     Refused: "A thousand, to the coin. The Varo do not round down."
- *The hand (Speech, coin or a fight).* Brings-Them-Back agrees to be watching the paddies and not the water for
  Speech 35 or 150 gold, each a stage. The player may attack him instead: he is alone and hard, and his death is
  the player's choice. Bought, he still knows where her kin are. Dead, nobody does, and nobody watches the paddies.
  With her way open, Rows-For-Another is told so, and says where she will go.
- *Meema Tulm-Varo* argues the farm's side to anyone who asks, and adds, to a player sent by Never-Sold: "Ask the
  one who sent you who taught my keeper his letters." Asked, Never-Sold says only: "I was owing on that ground
  once. So were many."

### BC02. One Out of the Smoke

Offered once BC01 has ended completed.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Never-Sold's briefing is short: three people are held in the lock-up of
the Rose's gatehouse, outside its wall, by the families who run it; the Rose is the old prison at the end of the
track that runs south past Reedfall Farm, "and you go no further than its gatehouse: past it the heirs kill
strangers on sight, and they are better at it than you"; one of the three can be got out; she wants the one held
for words. Everything else is Tsaxi's to tell on the spot, in answers the player picks: who she is, what "put under"
means, why only one, and where the Rose's reckoner went. The ferry-keeper at Lujei Ferry tells the rest of that.

**The hidden truth.** Before the player came to Blackrose a fire went through the Rose's upper cells
([40-factions](../40-factions.md) §39). The heirs moved three of their held out to the gatehouse lock-up under one
gate-keeper, Third-Born Tsaxi, who counts heads and cannot read. The leaves that say who is held for what were
singed. The Rose's reckoner, the one heir who reads them, carried them over the channel to Lujei, where a few
families "descended from the old Imperial prison's inmates" still write (D tulmek-washes, Lujei), to have them
copied fair before they crumbled (X). He came back by night on the cable raft with the leaves in a sealed chest (D
tulmek-washes, Lujei: "a night crossing with a passenger's sealed chest"). In mid-channel the cable was cut (D
tulmek-washes, Lujei Ferry: "a raft cable that has been cut") by the poleman who was taking him over, a man of the
Sheh-Ara families the Rose's raiders emptied out (X; D tulmek-washes, Lujei: "a missing ferryman"). The raft swung
down into the reeds below the landing, where the channel's two crocodiles lie (D tulmek-washes, Lujei Ferry). The
reckoner went into the water; his purse is in the reeds (D: "a drowned traveller's purse in the reeds") and the
chest is still on the stranded raft, because nobody at the ferry will go down among the crocodiles for something
that is the Rose's. Until somebody reads those leaves the count is whatever Tsaxi says, and she can say that one of
three died of the smoke. Not two. Of the two younger held, one is there for words and one for a killing, and the
killer will be put under when the leaves are read. So both say they are the one held for words, and each can recite
the words, because the whole yard heard them. The old woman knows which is which and will not say, because the
killing was done for her.

- **Walks-Far** (man) said in the yard that the hatch over the drowned cells should be opened and the Rose left. He
  owes three seasons for it.
- **Mends-Nets** (woman) drowned a prison-born shelter-reckoner, Otheel, in a net in the wash-pool, after he added a
  third winter to an old woman's roof-owing. She nurses that old woman's cough now, and she still has the key she
  took off him.
- **Seven-Floods** (old woman), Rows-For-Another's mother, of Sheh-Ara, owes the heirs for a roof and always will
  (`topics/labour-and-bondage.md:136`). The smoke is in her chest. She wants to die near the lake, so that she gets
  home to the drowned tree (`:119`), and she wants Mends-Nets taken first. The game names her "Old Seven-Floods".

**The ways through.**

- *Who is who* (three ways, any one enough, none required):
  1. *the leaves* (Sneak or a fight; Lockpicking or the key). The raft lies in the reeds below Lujei Ferry's
     landing, among two crocodiles of the Dangerous band, warned of twice (Tsaxi: "the lake took him, and the lake
     has teeth there"; Nuleem-Ei: "nobody goes down below the landing"). A fighter kills them, the quest's hard
     fight, with their hides and the chest's gold for it; a sneak wades in from the bank side and is not seen. The
     chest is sealed: an Expert lock, or the small key from the purse in the same reeds.
  2. *Seven-Floods*: she tells the truth to a player who eases her breathing (Restoration 25), who knows what eases
     a smoke-chest (Alchemy 25), or who presses her gently (Speech 40). Refused: "Ask me which of my daughters I
     would drown."
  3. *thinking*: Tsaxi, asked how the reckoner Otheel died, says he was drowned in a net. One of the two is a
     net-mender by name and by her hands. No skill, and no certainty.
- *Getting one out* (the held stay where they stand; the one chosen goes after the player has left):
  1. *Tsaxi's word*: Speech 35, or the 100 gold, and she will lose one to the smoke and unlocks the lock-up. Each
     is a stage. Refused: "My count is my neck. Give me a reason I can carry."
  2. *the leaves given her*, with the news that nobody is coming: the same word with no check, and she takes the
     leaves. "Then the count is mine, and I say one died."
  3. *without her leave*: her key lifted from her belt (Pickpocket), or the lock-up's Adept lock picked
     (Lockpicking), under her eye. Opening the door is the step. She will swear to one death sooner than own that
     she lost one, and the reckoner who could say otherwise is in the lake.
  4. *Tsaxi dead*: the player's choice, a fight of the Deadly band and far the hardest thing in these three quests.
     Then all three can go.

Whichever one is chosen while Tsaxi lives, the other two refuse to follow: "One is smoke. Two is a hunt, and the
hunt goes to our kin."

**The choice, and each side's honest case.** *Walks-Far* is innocent of anything but words, is the one Never-Sold
asked for, and faces only three seasons if left; what he wants opened could kill everyone in the Rose. *Mends-Nets*
is guilty, will be put under if left, and killed for someone who was being ground down. *Seven-Floods* owes only for
a roof, will die soon either way, and it is her way home that is at stake; she asks not to be chosen. Never-Sold's
order, the law of the Rose and the old woman's wish point three ways. Tsaxi's own case, for leaving all three: "Lose
me one and I can carry it. Lose me three and they send hunters to the kin, and somebody's clutch-brother sits in
that lock-up by the next rains."

where they said; the two left greet the player with what staying means for them, and Tsaxi with what she has decided
to believe about her reckoner.
