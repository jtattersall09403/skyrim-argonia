# The Reed-Sail: the line's story

**Status: to be rewritten.** This story rests on facts of the main quest's earlier plot that [the plot](main-plot.md) no longer holds; it is to be rewritten to the plot and is not yet. What in it is untrue, by section:

- Section 2 (The line's arc): it names Reads-the-White-Water, pilot-elder of Seafalls, as the main quest's person, but the plot has no such person and gives Seafalls no part in the main quest.
- Section 3 (The first three quests): RS02 relies on Reads-the-White-Water as the main quest's pilot-elder in her named house, which the plot does not hold.
- Section 6 (For the other lines): it speaks of the main quest's preacher at Seafalls and leaves the landing's raiders to MQ09, but the plot has no preacher and its ninth quest is The Bells of Meliton, far from Seafalls.
- Section 6 (For the other lines): it makes Fort Swiftmoth a crew that buys and sells nothing, while the plot makes it a keep of the Reed holding dreamers in its cellar.

The story of the Reed-Sail's seven quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the [province plan](province-plan.md) gives the line (its sections 3.8, 4 and 7). A quest writer turns each
outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations. **D** is the digest of the new world: "D althii-archipelago, Uluo" is that place's record
or its hooks in `story/design/world/regions/althii-archipelago.md`; "D province, Peoples" is a section of
[province](../../world/province.md). Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is
`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation from the cited data and lore
(decision 0032). No place of this line is built yet, so a place is named by what its record says it is, and
everything else a quest needs of it is a row of section 4.

**Words used below**, for a reader new to the province. *The Three Waters*: the bayou tribe whose one town is Uluo.
*The knot*: a cord of three knots, the tribe's mark on a sack, a lamp or a person. *The share*: the part of every
raft's freight that goes to the elder of Uluo. *The Wardens* (Archwardens): the thin successor state, heir to the
An-Xileel; its forts are kept by old *Archein* families, Argonians who served the Empire and kept its keys. *The chain*: the toll chain a fort lays
across a channel. *A mark*: a post, a stake or a lamp that a pilot steers by. *Skeith*: the rock island off the
east coast held by two outlaw crews: the half-chartered pirates of *Marque Landing* and the smugglers of *Hollow
Tide Camp* (both names are the map's). *The commission*: a letter of marque, the paper that makes a pirate a
privateer.

## 1. The faction in a page

**What they are.** The Reed-Sail are the people who move everything: the raft-masters of the Three Waters Tribe,
who "run the back-water routes between Archon and the Lilmoth delta that the customs officers do not see" (D
province, Peoples: Three Waters), and the coast pilots of Archon and Seafalls who have taken the tribe's knot (X,
province-plan 3.8: "reed for the rafts, sail for the coast pilots"). Their seat is Uluo, "both neutral ground and
a smuggler's door" (D althii-archipelago, Uluo), in the raft-masters' hall on the landing. They have two sayings.
"The route is shared": a pilot in trouble is every pilot's business. "A pilot's word on the water is law": aboard,
nobody argues with the one who knows the channel.

**The wrong they fight, as they tell it.** The state has no navy and no tax but one: "tolls at crossings and customs
at ports, nothing else" (*state*:704 to :706). So the old Imperial forts sit on the water like weirs. Fort Reedmoth
lays a chain across the strait between Uluo and Archon and "the Three Waters families ... say the chain is theirs
by older right" (D althii-archipelago, Fort Reedmoth: hooks). Fort Gysilla tolls Lake Ulaish and "has begun
stopping Truowa boats" (D lake-ulaish, Truowa: hooks). The customs post stands on Archon's quay (D nushara-bayou,
Archon). The villages that live by the water pay all of them. The Reed-Sail carry past all of them.

**How they work, in the line's own skills (X).** No boat is sailed in this line. A pilot's trade, as the player
practises it, is what happens at the water's edge:

- **the carry**: a cargo got out of a watched place and taken on the player's own back to the hand that should have
  it, past people who would take it. Speech turns a toll-captain, a master or a buyer; Sneak and the player's breath
  under water (Alteration's water-breathing, a pearl-diver's necklace, a Hist's blessing, or an Argonian's own gills)
  open the side of a place that has no eyes on it;
- **the marks**: the lamps, posts and stakes a channel is steered by. A pilot can tell a true mark from a false one,
  and the line's worst deeds are done with a lamp hung a stone's throw from where it should be;
- **the bank**: a pilot fights from where he stands. Archery first, at lookouts on a gallery, robbers on a reed
  bank and bowmen on a goat path; One-Handed when they close.

**Who they are.**

- **Ukka-Tei**, elder of the Three Waters (man), in the raft-masters' hall. He "takes a share of every raft" (D
  althii-archipelago, Uluo) and the share feeds the tribe's hatching families and buys its lamp oil. Patient,
  generous, and never surprised. He gives every job.
- **Sounds-Twice**, the oldest raft-master (woman), on Uluo's landing. Her name is her rule: sound the bottom twice
  before you pole once. She teaches the marks and believes the first saying more than the second.
- **Starless**, the elder's sister's son and the tribe's best night pilot (man). The crews named him for running
  without stars. He is easy company and proud of the work, and he believes the second saying more than the first.
- **Otul-Zan**, a raft-master (man) who stopped paying the share last season, and says a share that buys lamps in
  other people's country is no longer a share.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **The forts.** Captain **Varia Okeel** of Fort Reedmoth (woman; named here, as no line is seated there) keeps
  the chain with ten men who have not been paid from Helstrom in living memory. Her case: the chain's toll keeps
  the only gate between Uluo and Archon that a robbed traveller can run to, and she is the fifth hand of the
  line's ending. At Lake Ulaish the fort's case is made by a toll-boat rower: the chain pays for the one lantern
  on the lake that is lit every night.
- **Marque Landing** (D skeith-isle). Captain **Blind-Side**, one-eyed, "sails on a commission that the
  Archwardens neither honour nor repudiate". The chartered crews are "the state's stand-in for a navy"
  (*state*:403 to :405); they leave Archon's boats alone, charge the coast for protection, and sell the captives
  nobody ransoms. Their case: there is no other law east of the surf.
- **Hollow Tide Camp** (D skeith-isle). **Names-the-Quay**, "a former Archon harbour hand who knows every customs
  man on the quay by name", runs "outlaws in full". They pay no fort, no elder and no captain, they carry what the
  villages want untaxed, and they take boats when they are short of them. Their case: water that pays three tolls
  is nobody's water.
- **The Night-Reed**, over who moves stolen goods (province-plan 3.6), and **the Waykeepers**, whose lights someone
  in the Reed-Sail has been buying (province-plan 4).

**What it pays and what joining costs.** A carrier's fee in coin, the knot (which the fort's own people know and
let by), and pilots' things: a necklace to breathe under water, a good bow, the knowledge of every landing from
Lake Ulaish to Skeith. The cost: a carrier caught in a fort is under the old Imperial law, with a cell and
everything stolen taken back (D province, Power and law: foreign law); and the knot is known on Skeith, where one
camp or the other shoots at it.

**What is unsettled about them.**

1. "The route is shared" and "a share of every raft" are the same words. Mutual aid and a cartel differ only in
   who may leave.
2. The Reed-Sail need the forts. If no water were taxed, nobody would pay a pilot to find the water that is not.
3. A pilot's word is law aboard. Nobody has said whose word is law over a pilot.
4. Ukka-Tei hides what he has done with a lamp (section 2).

**Whose water it is.** Every quest of the line puts the same question in a different place: the forts that tax the
water, the crews that run it, or the villages that live by it. Each time the player's own hands decide, and each
time the other two pay.

**The build leaning.** Speech and Archery first; One-Handed; Alteration for breath under water; Sneak for a store's
water side and a robbers' cache (`profiles.yaml`, `reed-sail`). Lockpicking and Pickpocket open things in this line
as they do anywhere, but no quest needs them: every lock has another side, a key or a word. A talker, an archer
and a swimmer-sneak can each finish every quest. Swimming is how a pilot gets about and wins nothing by itself
(owner-bar, "Action and the player's skills"). Nothing is levelled (decision 0035).

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** Ukka-Tei understood long ago that the back-water routes are worth something only because
the open water is taxed, and that a compact which nobody may leave is stronger than one which anybody may. He has
done three things about it, each worse than the last.

1. *He has an understanding with Fort Reedmoth.* Cargo under the knot passes the chain for a token. Cargo without
   it is the fort's, and word of whose raft carries none comes from Uluo. The fort's own hook is "a smuggler's tip
   about a night landing on the east shingle" (D althii-archipelago, Fort Reedmoth); the tips are his. When Otul-Zan
   stopped paying the share, his saltrice was stopped at the chain within the month. The quarrel over the chain "by
   older right" is real among the raft families, and the elder leads it loudly.
2. *He buys lights.* On Lake Ulaish and the interior waters the night runs steer by lanterns that a keeper was paid
   to show for the knot alone (province-plan 4, "the bought lights"). No pilot of his names a keeper.
3. *He wrecked the Brindle Gull.* The smugglers' fast sloop was carrying more untaxed cargo along the east coast
   than all Uluo's rafts, and paying nobody. She steered at night by two lights on Kornakim headland: the old
   spell-fire on the ruin's tower, which "has never gone out", and the smugglers' own lantern on the headland (D
   hatandros-bight, Kornakim: hooks). One night last season Starless, on the elder's word, hung a second lamp a
   stone's throw seaward of theirs, and sent word to the privateers of the night she would sail. They chased her
   "without lights" (D padomaic-ocean, Wreck of the Brindle Gull). She took the wrong lamp for her own and went
   onto the Kornakim shoals with twelve hands. The privateers boast of it. The smugglers blame the privateers. The
   war between the camps that the data calls "close" (D skeith-isle, Hollow Tide Camp: hooks) is the war the elder
   wants: whichever camp is left will be weak enough to take the knot. Starless's lamp, with his family's red bead
   on its cord, still hangs where he left it, because no living person goes up to Kornakim (D hatandros-bight,
   Kornakim: "a place not to dig"). He has not gone back for it. He does not sleep well.

Nobody else is clean. Blind-Side was mate of the privateer Stormwidow and put her on Skeith's beach in the dark to
take her crew from her captain (X, on D skeith-isle, Wreck of the Stormwidow: "ran onto the beach in the dark and
was abandoned by her crew, who joined the Marque Landing camp"). The commission was the captain's. He hid on the
Grey Tooth with it and died there, so Blind-Side sails on a paper he has never held. The smugglers took Arorha's
ochre boat (D hatandros-bight, Arorha: hooks) and shoot at strangers.

Earlier choices never change a later quest's stages. They change greetings, prices and single lines, read from
stored numbers. Only the finale's choice carries into the world after the line.

**RS04, The Shore Under Marshgate.** The smugglers of Hollow Tide land their cargo for Thorn on the shore below
Marshgate Watch, the Wardens' toll bar where the Thorn road crosses the neck of land (D hatandros-bight, Marshgate
Watch: "smugglers landing by night who fight when asked to pay"), and the post sees nothing, because "a warden has
been letting Skeith smugglers' boats through for pay". Ukka-Tei wants two things: the cache itself ("what they land
without a share is the share they owe"), and to know which warden is blind. The way is the harbour boat from Archon
to Arorha, the clay village whose quay is the nearest boat stop (D hatandros-bight, Arorha), and the shore road
north, where the record keeps mudcrab swarms and giant snakes. The landing is the job: four of the smugglers' hands
at the Wild band's top, two of them lookouts with bows on the two points of the cove, a Khajiit sugar-dealer by the
fire with the cache's key, and the cache chest between them. They are hostile on sight. An archer takes the
lookouts from the scrub before the fire knows (Archery); a sneak lifts the key and empties the chest between two
watch-fires (Sneak, Pickpocket, or the chest's Adept lock); a fighter goes in. Two people warn of the landing
first, the pot-painter elder of Arorha and the sergeant at the bar. In the chest are sealed jars of moon sugar,
Arorha's lost kiln-load of ochre (D hatandros-bight, Arorha: hooks), and a warden's brass toll-token strung on a
cord with Skeith coin, whose name when looked at is "Ilas Teem's Toll-Token". Then the player holds a name three
people want. **Hadrus Neel**, the sergeant, in the blockhouse, "wants the man caught before the blame falls on the
sergeant". The elder wants it, and says what for only if pressed: a crew that pays no share is a crew the forts may
have. **Ilas Teem**, at the toll bar outside, asked, says the post has had no pay in two years and his clutch is at
Ysan. Each takes the token by a line. The quest ends on the report to Ukka-Tei at Uluo, and that line, far from the
post, makes whatever switch the choice calls for: given to the elder or the sergeant, Ilas is gone from the bar
when the player next passes and the sergeant says he is in the cells at Fort Thornmoth; given back to Ilas, he
stands where he stood, the elder pays nothing ("You brought me their sugar and kept me their road"), and Half-Share
greets the player as a friend. The ochre, carried to Arorha's elder, is paid for in the village's glazed ware. This
is the line's misjudgement: the player who does as the patron asks ruins the one decent man in the quest, and
learns what the elder does with what he is told. It costs standing with one side or the other and ends nothing.

**RS05, The Gull's Lamp (the turn).** Both camps claim the strongbox in the Brindle Gull, and "whoever brings it
up has both camps asking after it" (D padomaic-ocean, Wreck of the Brindle Gull: hooks). Ukka-Tei wants what is in
it brought to him first: whoever holds it is the one both captains must come and talk to. The Gull lies in "shallow
green water on the Kornakim shoals", a swim from the shore north of Arorha; the breath for it is the necklace of
RS02, the blessing of Archon's tree, which gives water-breathing (D nushara-bayou, Archon), or a spell. Her drowned
crew are the Deadly band's and two people say so before the player goes. They stand on her deckhouse floor, behind
the dive, and the swim down is passed without a fight (the lead's third ruling). The strongbox, named "Brindle
Gull's Strongbox", holds one thing to take, "The Gull's Purse", and the Deadly band's reward beside it. It is got
by killing her captain (the hardest fight of the line), by Sneak and the box's Expert lock, or by arrows from the
hatch and a long retreat. Taking the purse moves the quest. On the beach below Kornakim stands the smugglers'
lamp-man, apart from his crew and not hostile; he is the one person who speaks first. He does not ask for the
purse. He asks the player to look at the headland, because none of his crew will go up among the Velothi dead (the
ruin's household ghosts, D hatandros-bight, Kornakim): he lit the true lamp that night and the Gull steered wide of
it. His line puts a marker on the seaward post. The ghosts are in the ruin's hall (Dangerous) and need not be met;
the post is outside. On it hangs a raft-lamp whose name is "Raft-Lamp with a Red Bead". Taking it is the turn. The
journal says only what was taken and from where; the player knows what a lamp on the wrong post does (RS03), and
whose bead is red (the rack at Uluo). Then, in any order and none required: Starless, who stands in the hall
beside his uncle from this quest on ("He gave the word. I hung it. I have not been up there since, and I hear her
every night. Whose word did you think was law over a pilot?"); Ukka-Tei, who denies nothing ("Twelve outlaws who
paid no one. Forty families who eat because the route is one route. I would hang that lamp again"); Sounds-Twice,
who did not know ("A false light is the one thing no pilot is forgiven. Keep it. Do not give it to him"). The quest
ends on the report to Ukka-Tei, which is open once the purse has been taken, whatever the player holds by then:
the purse is given to him, was given to the lamp-man on the beach, or is kept, and each is a number; the lamp is
shown or not, and that is a number. A player who took the lamp before the job was given has the same lines.
Everything in RS01 to RS04 reads differently now: the rice that was seized, the warden who was given up, the
robbers who were offered the knot.

**RS06, The Widow's Key.** Ukka-Tei, who is still the elder and still gives the work, says the war can be ended
without a boat lost: Blind-Side has never shown his commission, the old pilots say the Stormwidow's captain kept
it, and "both camps believe the captain of the Stormwidow buried his share under the cairn" on the Grey Tooth (D
skeith-isle, The Grey Tooth: hooks). The commission is the paper, sealed by Archon's harbour authority, that makes
a crew the port's privateers; the customs officers on Archon's quay answer to whoever can show it. He wants it.
The player crosses to Skeith from Arorha's shore, where every place fights a visitor. The Stormwidow lies beached;
"the captain's strongbox is still sealed in the stern" under the sand of the lower hold, with scavengers of both
camps about the hull (Dangerous: a fight, or Sneak). In it are a brass key and a strip of lead scratched with a
knife, a thing to read: his mate put her ashore, and what the key opens is "with the old one's bones, on the north
face". The Tooth is the island's peak; coast Argonians "tell that the stone is a Hist's old bone" (D skeith-isle,
The Grey Tooth: faith), and the pirates tell of "a shaman's bones in a cave on the north face" and do not go there,
which is why two camps have dug at the cairn and "found only stones". The goat-path up the Tooth is held by the
privateers' lookouts with bows (Archery against archers, or Sneak); hackwings nest on the crags. In the cave, beside
the old bones, lies the captain with his sea-chest: his share, which is the hunt's fixed reward, and the
commission. It is the one paper in the line, an outsider's paper in the place the lore puts such things
(province-vibe §9: "Archon's charters"; *state*:403), and nothing is done with it but carry it. It names the master
of the Stormwidow "and whoever holds this letter after him" (X). Taking it ends RS06 and begins RS07.

**RS07, Whose Water.** Two decisions, in this order.

*The lamp.* While the commission is in the player's pack, and only then, the lamp of RS05 can be put into
Sounds-Twice's hand on Uluo's landing. Her line, spoken outdoors, changes the inside of the hall: the raft-masters
of the tribe are standing in it when the player goes in, each with a line of their own on what was done in their
name; Starless is gone from it, to Skeith, they say; and Ukka-Tei is still there, in the place where the youngest
sits, with no share and no word, and says so. Sounds-Twice does not go in. "The hall can come out to me." Or the
lamp is given to the elder, who thanks the player and is never seen with it; or kept, and then it is a lamp.

*The commission.* Five people want it, each in their own place, each with a reason of their own and each saying
what the others would do with it. Nobody is hurried: the war is "close" for as long as the player holds the paper.

- **Blind-Side, the privateers' captain, at Marque Landing.** With the paper he is what he has claimed to be. The
  chartered crews are the coast's law in fact; the raids on Seafalls' landing and Arorha's boats stop, because a
  commission can be taken from a captain who robs the port's own pilots; the coast pays him for protection, and
  the captives nobody ransoms still go "to buyers on the Okuwun coast" (D skeith-isle, Rainbreaker Keep). The
  player "is taken aboard as crew" (D skeith-isle, Marque Landing: hooks).
- **Names-the-Quay, the smugglers' leader, at Hollow Tide Camp.** With the paper the outlaws are the privateers and
  Blind-Side is a pirate with a keep. The coast's trade goes untaxed and unprotected. He means to open Rainbreaker's
  cells, and to find out who hung a second lamp on Kornakim.
- **The hall at Uluo**: Ukka-Tei, or, if the lamp has been before the raft-masters, Sounds-Twice. With the paper
  the knot is the charter. Under him, both camps carry under it or starve and everything that moves between Lake
  Ulaish and Skeith pays one share to one elder. Under her, the pilots hold it in common, and she says plainly that
  she does not know whether a paper can be held in common.
- **Reads-the-White-Water, the pilot-elder of Seafalls** (the main quest's person; if she is dead, Nomu speaks for
  the village). Her people pilot for everyone and belong to nobody (D province, Peoples: Spray-Folk). She asks for
  the paper so that the falls may have it: the sea keeps what is let fall. Then there is no charter, both camps are
  outlaws, the villages are raided by whoever is hungry, and no pilot answers to a captain or an elder. She says
  plainly that her landing will be robbed again.
- **Captain Varia Okeel, at Fort Reedmoth.** The paper is the state's, and she will send it to the customs
  officers at Archon, who will give it to whom they please or to nobody. Her case: a commission that an elder or
  an outlaw can pick up in a cave is the reason the coast has no law. What it costs: the forts get their navy back
  on a leash, every chain from Reedmoth to Gysilla is the stronger for it, and the knot is worth more than ever.

Each takes the commission by a line, in their own place, and that line ends the quest. Both Skeith camps are
hostile on sight (D skeith-isle), so each has one person at its edge who is not: the shore men the player has known
since RS01 and RS02, Half-Share and Vasha-Lo, each with a marker, or, where one of them is dead, a second person of
that camp who was switched on at the same spot when the commission was taken in the cave. Nobody speaks first.
Giving the paper to a camp puts the player in that camp's faction, by the same line, so that its people no longer
attack and its traders will deal (D skeith-isle, Marque Landing and Hollow Tide Camp: gives); the other camp stays
as it was.

*The last fight is offered, never ordered.* After the paper is given to a Skeith camp, the journal keeps one
more objective, with a marker, that the player may leave for ever. For the privateers: Names-the-Quay is still on
his bluff with his crew (D skeith-isle, Hollow Tide Camp: the record's boss fight and cache). For the smugglers:
the cells under Rainbreaker Keep, past "pirate crew in the hall, stair and jetty" and the quartermaster (D
skeith-isle, Rainbreaker Keep); the cell door's key is on him, and a captive behind the door says who is held there
and that they will go when the stair is quiet. They are gone when the player next lands on the island. It is the
line's hard place, and it is the player's own choice to go.

What the choice did is found afterwards and never watched: the shore men's greetings; Seafalls' lighthouse; who
stands in the hall at Uluo; and one line from each fort's people on what the coast's new master means to the chain.
Under the privateers' ending the quartermaster of Rainbreaker Keep, who "knows which wardens took the pirates'
money", tells the player their names, and each of those wardens has a line for a player who knows. No ending
changes who holds Fort Reedmoth, Fort Gysilla or Marshgate Watch. Sounds-Twice, if she holds the hall, names the
player raft-master, the line's last rank; nobody offers the player the hall.

## 3. The first three quests

Common to all three.

- **Ukka-Tei** stands in the raft-masters' hall at Uluo and gives each job. From RS01 to RS06 he must live: his
  death fails any running quest with the faction's message. **Sounds-Twice** stands on Uluo's landing. If she dies
  her favours and her lines are lost and every quest still reads. Nobody is essential. People stand where they
  are placed.
- **One working stage, results as numbers.** After the briefing each quest sits at one working stage. What the
  player achieves there is a stored number or a plain fact (a thing held, a person dead), never a stage of its own,
  so the ways can be done in any order, mixed or abandoned. Every person or thing whose line or taking can move
  the quest from a stage is a target of that stage's objectives.
- **Taking a thing only writes a journal note.** A line that needs a thing checks that it is held, however and
  whenever it was got. A player who holds it before the job is given is offered the job with a line that says so.
- **A death moves no stage** except the failure named. Lines check who is alive.
- **The report is always open.** Each quest ends on a line of Ukka-Tei's that is open from the working stage on
  and reads what is true by then, so no death and no order of doing things leaves a quest with nothing to say.
- **The crime system is Skyrim's and the quest never reads it.** A thing taken from an owner in sight is a theft
  with a bounty. At a fort the law is the old Imperial one: pay, the cell or resist, and what was stolen is taken
  from the player and put with the fort's seized goods (D province, Power and law: foreign law).
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense
  and say only what the player has just done. Skill thresholds are fixed. Each failed Speech check has its own
  written refusal and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms; nothing new is modelled. A dead person
  who is there from the start is a thing that is searched, with a name.
- "Night", "dark" and "dusk" in what people say are their words only; no step depends on the hour. Nobody speaks
  of a tide.
- **How the player knows a place or a thing** is said in each outline and again in section 4: by the name the game
  shows on a door or a thing, by a marker, or by a named person who tells them before they need it.

### RS01. The Knot on the Sack

**What a new player is told, and when.** The player comes to Uluo by raft from Lilmoth, Archon or Truowa (D
althii-archipelago, Uluo: boat hub) and finds the raft-masters' hall by the name on its door. On first speaking
to Ukka-Tei, before any job, four optional lines of two or three sentences each: *the Three Waters* ("the bayou,
the strait and the open coast. One tribe poles all three, and our cord has a knot for each"); *the Reed-Sail*
("reed for our rafts, sail for the pilots of Archon and Seafalls who took our knot. The route is shared. A
pilot's word on the water is law"); *the forts and the chain* ("the Archwardens are what the An-Xileel's heirs
call themselves now: a government with forts and no boats. The forts were the Empire's. The families who kept the
keys still keep them, and one of them has laid a chain across water my grandmothers poled before there was a
fort"); *what it pays, and why you might not* ("a carrier's fee and the knot. And a fort keeps the Empire's law: a
cell, and whatever you were carrying"). "Not yet" stays open and free. The job is four sentences: a load of the
tribe's saltrice was stopped at Reedmoth's chain and lies in the fort's toll store; no raft family can fetch it,
because every man at that gate knows every one of them, and the chain would close on all their rafts the next day;
nobody at the gate knows the player; bring it out and carry it to the widow who keeps the lantern house at Xi-Inou,
the tribe's landing above Archon, and not to Archon's quay, where the customs post stands. "She will show you the
knot. Give it to nobody who cannot."

**The hidden truth.** The saltrice is Otul-Zan's. He stopped paying the share last season. Ukka-Tei sent word to
the fort that a raft without the knot would come down the strait, and the fort stopped it at the chain, fined him
and kept the load and the raft's lamp, which was his clutch-sister Sounds-Twice's. Now the elder is having the
load fetched for the buyer it was always going to, the lantern-house widow, who will pay the elder and not
Otul-Zan. The sacks still carry Otul-Zan's cord, two knots and a shell, the same cord that hangs on his own lamp
on Uluo's rack. Otul-Zan has been told his rice is lost. At Xi-Inou, the smugglers' shore buyer has heard that a
load is coming and means to have it first.

### RS02. What the Pilot Brought In

Offered once RS01 has ended completed.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Ukka-Tei's briefing is short: the knot has a sail side. Seafalls is the
village under the falls on the coast north of Archon, where cargo is carried round the falls and ships are taken
through the surf; its people, the Spray-Folk, are the pilots. Their pilot-elder has sent to Uluo for a hand who is
not of her village. The way is the player's own journey by the world's boats: Uluo's raft to Archon, and the
harbour boat from Archon's wharves to Seafalls (D province, Roads). Everything else is told at Seafalls by the
pilot-elder, Reads-the-White-Water, in the house with her name on its door.

She says three things and no more. A coaster, the Ash Petrel, lies at Seafalls' mooring; she piloted her in nine
days ago; her master says ash-glass and wine out of Tear, the Dunmer port a short sail north, and she rides too low
for glass and wine. The night after, a boat's crew from Skeith took the lighthouse on the point and carried off the
lamp's burner; the keeper was thrown down the stair, and his hatchling is hiding in the keeper's hut. And a man
with a privateer's paper came by the Archon boat the next morning and wants the ship held until his captain comes
for her. "A pilot who holds a ship she brought in is no pilot. A pilot who takes a ship out past a dark light kills
her. So I can do neither, and whichever I do, one crew on that island will call it my choice and burn my landing
for it. I sent for someone whose word it can be instead. Get my burner back, find me what is under that wine, and
then say what is done with her. I will do it."

**The hidden truth.** The Ash Petrel is hired by the smugglers of Hollow Tide Camp. Under her wine are forty
crossbows and their bolts, for the war that is coming on Skeith. Her master, **Dralen Vathri** of Tear, had a
brother aboard the Brindle Gull. The privateers of Marque Landing learned of the cargo in Archon, and their shore
man, **Vasha-Lo**, came to see her kept at the mooring until the captain's boats can take her as a prize. He could
not ask a pilot to hold a ship, so he had three of his own crew put the lighthouse out: no master will go out
through those shoals past a dark light, and no pilot will take him. He tells everyone the men on the point are the
smugglers'. Their leader carries the burner, and a brass token of the Marque Landing crew, as every one of the
captain's men does.

### RS03. The Old Pilot's Passage

Offered once RS02 has ended completed.

**What a new player is told, and when.** Ukka-Tei: Truowa is the small lake city at the south-east edge of Lake
Ulaish, a raft's journey from Uluo (D althii-archipelago, Uluo: boat hub). The Archwardens toll the lake from Fort
Gysilla, on its islet, with a chain; Truowa's council has refused the toll and the garrison has begun stopping its
boats (D lake-ulaish, Truowa: hooks). So the council has hired the knot to carry its goods round the chain, by an
old passage through the reed shoals that only pilots know. The first load, Truowa cloth in a Truowa canoe steering
by the passage's marks, went in and did not come out. Starless is to pilot the passage this season. "Before he
takes a raft into it, somebody walks it. Find what is wrong, and put it right in whatever way leaves the passage
ours." At Truowa's quay Starless tells the rest; **Xal-Meema**, the council merchant who hired the knot (the
record's Speech trainer), wants her cloth.

Starless teaches the marks, and this is where a player learns to read them. A mark is a post with a lamp on it. A
pilot keeps the lamp on his steering hand and does not look down. The passage has three: the post at Shalaia's salt
pans, where the passage leaves the open lake; a lamp-stake on a reed bank south of Shalaia, the middle mark; and a
third light, beyond, "that is shown for us and for nobody else. It is not yours to touch and not yours to ask
about." He names no keeper (province-plan 4, "the bought lights"). If a mark is wrong, the player is to bring him
its lamp and not set it anywhere: "A mark is set by the pilot who will steer on it." He also says that his own lamp
went overboard off the east coast last season, and that the one he carries is borrowed. He is easy company, proud
of the work, and gives the player a quiver of good arrows for the walk.

**The hidden truth.** The middle mark was moved. **Pike**, who rowed Fort Gysilla's toll boat until he deserted,
knows every boat on the lake and what a lamp-stake means. He pulled the stake out of its reed bank and planted it
on the south bank near Yalugo, where his three boat-robbers moor (D lake-ulaish, Yalugo: the record's three on the
south bank, at the Dangerous band, with their cache). The Truowa canoe steered on the lamp and ran in under their
bows. Its two boatmen are dead on that bank; the cloth is in the robbers' keeping. The garrison knows roughly where
Pike is and has been told to leave that bank alone: a robbed passage teaches Truowa to pay at the chain.
