# The Reed-Sail: the line's story

The story of the Reed-Sail's seven quests, written inside the quest ceiling
([quest-form](../../../../docs/design/quest-form.md)) and the owner's bar ([owner-bar](../../owner-bar.md)), on the
ground the [province plan](province-plan.md) gives the line (its sections 3.8, 4 and 7). A quest writer turns each
outline in section 3 into a record without inventing anything.

How to read the citations. **D** is the digest of the new world: "D althii-archipelago, Uluo" is that place's record
or its hooks in `story/design/world/regions/althii-archipelago.md`; "D province, Peoples" is a section of
[province](../../world/province.md). Lore is `file:line` under `story/lore/`; *state* is
`extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md`. **X** marks this document's own extrapolation from the cited data and lore
(decision 0032). No place of this line is built yet, so a place is named by what its record says it is, and
everything else a quest needs of it is a row of section 4.

**Words used below**, for a reader new to the province. *The Three Waters*: the bayou tribe whose one town is Uluo.
*The knot*: a cord of three knots, the tribe's mark on a sack, a lamp or a person. *The share*: the part of every
raft's freight that goes to the elder of Uluo. *The Wardens*: the thin successor state; its forts are kept by old
*Archein* families, Argonians who served the Empire and kept its keys. *The chain*: the toll chain a fort lays
across a channel. *A mark*: a post, a stake or a lamp that a pilot steers by. *Skeith*: the rock island off the
east coast held by two outlaw crews: the half-chartered pirates of *Marque Landing* and the smugglers of *Hollow
Tide Camp* (both names are the map's). *The commission*: a letter of marque, the paper that makes a pirate a
privateer.

## 1. The faction in a page

**What they are.** The Reed-Sail are the people who move everything: the raft-masters of the Three Waters Tribe,
who "run the back-water routes between Archon and the Lilmoth delta that the customs officers do not see" (D
province, Peoples: Three Waters), and the coast pilots of Archon and Seafalls who have taken the tribe's knot (X,
province-plan 3.8: "reed for the rafts, sail for the coast pilots"). Their seat is Uluo, "both neutral ground and
a smuggler's door" (D althii-archipelago, Uluo), in the raft-masters' hall on the landing. They have two sayings.
"The route is shared": a pilot in trouble is every pilot's business. "A pilot's word on the water is law": aboard,
nobody argues with the one who knows the channel.

**The wrong they fight, as they tell it.** The state has no navy and no tax but one: "tolls at crossings and customs
at ports, nothing else" (*state*:704 to :706). So the old Imperial forts sit on the water like weirs. Fort Reedmoth
lays a chain across the strait between Uluo and Archon and "the Three Waters families ... say the chain is theirs
by older right" (D althii-archipelago, Fort Reedmoth: hooks). Fort Gysilla tolls Lake Ulaish and "has begun
stopping Truowa boats" (D lake-ulaish, Truowa: hooks). The customs post stands on Archon's quay (D nushara-bayou,
Archon). The villages that live by the water pay all of them. The Reed-Sail carry past all of them.

**How they work, in the line's own skills (X).** No boat is sailed in this line. A pilot's trade, as the player
practises it, is what happens at the water's edge:

- **the carry**: a cargo got out of a watched place and taken on the player's own back to the hand that should have
  it, past people who would take it. Speech turns a toll-captain, a master or a buyer; Sneak and the player's breath
  under water (Alteration's water-breathing, a pearl-diver's necklace, a Hist's blessing, or an Argonian's own gills)
  open the side of a place that has no eyes on it;
- **the marks**: the lamps, posts and stakes a channel is steered by. A pilot can tell a true mark from a false one,
  and the line's worst deeds are done with a lamp hung a stone's throw from where it should be;
- **the bank**: a pilot fights from where he stands. Archery first, at lookouts on a gallery, robbers on a reed
  bank and bowmen on a goat path; One-Handed when they close.

**Who they are.**

- **Ukka-Tei**, elder of the Three Waters (man), in the raft-masters' hall. He "takes a share of every raft" (D
  althii-archipelago, Uluo) and the share feeds the tribe's hatching families and buys its lamp oil. Patient,
  generous, and never surprised. He gives every job.
- **Sounds-Twice**, the oldest raft-master (woman), on Uluo's landing. Her name is her rule: sound the bottom twice
  before you pole once. She teaches the marks and believes the first saying more than the second.
- **Starless**, the elder's sister's son and the tribe's best night pilot (man). The crews named him for running
  without stars. He believes the second saying more than the first.
- **Otul-Zan**, a raft-master (man) who stopped paying the share last season, and says a share that buys lamps in
  other people's country is no longer a share.

**Who stands against them, and why each is arguable.**

- **The forts.** Captain **Varia Okeel** of Fort Reedmoth (woman; named here, as no line is seated there) keeps
  the chain with ten men who have not been paid from Helstrom in living memory. Her case: the chain's toll keeps
  the only gate between Uluo and Archon that a robbed traveller can run to. At Lake Ulaish the fort's case is made
  by a toll-boat rower: the chain pays for the one lantern on the lake that is lit every night.
- **Marque Landing** (D skeith-isle). Captain **Half-the-Sky**, one-eyed, "sails on a commission that the
  Archwardens neither honour nor repudiate". The chartered crews are "the state's stand-in for a navy"
  (*state*:403 to :405); they leave Archon's boats alone, charge the coast for protection, and sell the captives
  nobody ransoms. Their case: there is no other law east of the surf.
- **Hollow Tide Camp** (D skeith-isle). **Names-the-Quay**, "a former Archon harbour hand who knows every customs
  man on the quay by name", runs "outlaws in full". They pay no fort, no elder and no captain, they carry what the
  villages want untaxed, and they take boats when they are short of them. Their case: water that pays three tolls
  is nobody's water.
- **The Night-Reed**, over who moves stolen goods (province-plan 3.6), and **the Waykeepers**, whose lights someone
  in the Reed-Sail has been buying (province-plan 4).

**What it pays and what joining costs.** A carrier's fee in coin, the knot (which the fort's own people know and
let by), and pilots' things: a necklace to breathe under water, a good bow, the knowledge of every landing from
Lake Ulaish to Skeith. The cost: a carrier caught in a fort is under the old Imperial law, with a cell and
everything stolen taken back (D province, Power and law: foreign law); and the knot is known on Skeith, where one
camp or the other shoots at it.

**What is unsettled about them.**

1. "The route is shared" and "a share of every raft" are the same words. Mutual aid and a cartel differ only in
   who may leave.
2. The Reed-Sail need the forts. If no water were taxed, nobody would pay a pilot to find the water that is not.
3. A pilot's word is law aboard. Nobody has said whose word is law over a pilot.
4. Ukka-Tei hides what he has done with a lamp (section 2).

**Whose water it is.** Every quest of the line puts the same question in a different place: the forts that tax the
water, the crews that run it, or the villages that live by it. Each time the player's own hands decide, and each
time the other two pay.

**The build leaning.** Speech and Archery first; One-Handed; Alteration for breath under water; Sneak for a store's
water side and a robbers' cache (`profiles.yaml`, `reed-sail`). Lockpicking and Pickpocket open things in this line
as they do anywhere, but no quest needs them: every lock has a water side, a key or a word. A talker, an archer
and a swimmer-sneak can each finish every quest. Swimming is how a pilot gets about and wins nothing by itself
(owner-bar, "Action and the player's skills"). Nothing is levelled (decision 0035).

## 2. The line's arc

**The hidden spine (X).** Ukka-Tei understood long ago that the back-water routes are worth something only because
the open water is taxed, and that a compact which nobody may leave is stronger than one which anybody may. He has
done three things about it, each worse than the last.

1. *He has an understanding with Fort Reedmoth.* Cargo under the knot passes the chain for a token. Cargo without
   it is the fort's, and word of whose raft carries none comes from Uluo. The fort's own hook is "a smuggler's tip
   about a night landing on the east shingle" (D althii-archipelago, Fort Reedmoth); the tips are his. When Otul-Zan
   stopped paying the share, his saltrice was stopped at the chain within the month. The quarrel over the chain "by
   older right" is real among the raft families, and the elder leads it loudly.
2. *He buys lights.* On Lake Ulaish and the interior waters the night runs steer by lanterns that a keeper was paid
   to show for the knot alone (province-plan 4, "the bought lights"). No pilot of his names a keeper.
3. *He wrecked the Brindle Gull.* Hollow Tide's fast sloop was carrying more untaxed cargo along the east coast
   than all Uluo's rafts, and paying nobody. She steered at night by two lights on Kornakim headland: the old
   spell-fire on the ruin's tower, which "has never gone out", and the smugglers' own lantern on the headland (D
   hatandros-bight, Kornakim: hooks). One night last season Starless, on the elder's word, hung a second lamp a
   stone's throw seaward of theirs, and sent word to Marque Landing of the night she would sail. The privateers chased her
   "without lights" (D padomaic-ocean, Wreck of the Brindle Gull). She took the wrong lamp for her own and went
   onto the Kornakim shoals with twelve hands. Marque Landing boasts of it. Hollow Tide blames Marque Landing. The
   war between the camps that the data calls "close" (D skeith-isle, Hollow Tide Camp: hooks) is the war the elder
   wants: whichever camp is left will be weak enough to take the knot. Starless's lamp, with his family's red bead
   on its cord, still hangs where he left it, because no living person goes up to Kornakim (D hatandros-bight,
   Kornakim: "a place not to dig").

Nobody else is clean. Half-the-Sky was mate of the privateer Stormwidow and put her on Skeith's beach in the dark
to take her crew from her captain (X, on D skeith-isle, Wreck of the Stormwidow: "ran onto the beach in the dark
and was abandoned by her crew, who joined the Marque Landing camp"). The commission was the captain's. He hid on
the Grey Tooth with it and died there, so Half-the-Sky sails on a paper he has never held. Hollow Tide took
Arorha's ochre boat (D hatandros-bight, Arorha: hooks) and shoots at strangers.

Earlier choices never change a later quest's stages. They change greetings, prices and single lines, read from
stored numbers. Only the finale's choice carries into the world after the line.

**RS04, Past Marshgate.** Hollow Tide's leader "will hire a stranger to carry a cargo past Marshgate Watch" (D
skeith-isle, Hollow Tide Camp: hooks), the Wardens' toll bar where the Thorn road crosses the neck of land (D
hatandros-bight, Marshgate Watch). Ukka-Tei tells the player to take the hire, because the buyer Half-Share
already knows the player's face from RS01, and to bring back one thing: how the outlaws pass the bar. The hire is
taken from Half-Share at Arorha, the clay village whose quay is the nearest boat stop to Skeith (D hatandros-bight,
Arorha). The cargo is jars sealed as Arorha's ochre and too light for ochre: moon sugar, which Half-Share admits
when asked, before the player takes it. The bar can be passed four ways: on the watch of **Ilas Teem**, the warden
who "has been letting Skeith smugglers' boats through for pay", with Half-Share's word for him; round the post by
the shore, where the record keeps swamp-crocodiles in the ditch and giant snakes (Archery, One-Handed); by talking
the sergeant, **Hadrus Neel**, into waving a clay-carrier through (Speech); or past the blockhouse by Sneak. The
jars go to a Dunmer buyer who waits on the Thorn side of the bar. Then the player holds a name three people want.
The sergeant "wants the man caught before the blame falls on the sergeant". The elder wants it, and says what for
only if pressed: a crew that pays no share is a crew the forts may have. Ilas Teem, asked, says the post has had
no pay in two years and his clutch is at Ysan. Told to the elder or the sergeant, Ilas is gone from the bar when
the player next comes (switched off far away) and the sergeant says he is in the cells at Fort Thornmoth;
Half-Share greets the player as the one who shut his road. Told to nobody, or the warden warned, the elder says
only "Then you carried sugar for outlaws and brought me nothing", and pays nothing. This is the line's
misjudgement: the player who does as the patron asks ruins the one decent man in the quest, and learns what the
elder does with what he is told. It costs standing with one side or the other and ends nothing.

**RS05, The Gull's Lamp (the turn).** Both camps claim the strongbox in the Brindle Gull, and "whoever brings it
up has both camps asking after it" (D padomaic-ocean, Wreck of the Brindle Gull: hooks). Ukka-Tei wants it brought
to him first: whoever holds it is the one both captains must come and talk to. The Gull lies in "shallow green
water on the Kornakim shoals", a swim from the shore north of Arorha; the breath for it is the necklace of RS02,
the blessing of Archon's tree, which gives water-breathing (D nushara-bayou, Archon), or a spell. Her drowned crew
are the Deadly band's and two people say so before the player goes. They stand on her deckhouse floor, behind the
dive, and the swim down is passed without a fight (the lead's third ruling). The strongbox is got by killing her
captain (the hardest fight of the line, with the Deadly band's reward in the box), by Sneak and its lock, or by an
arrow from the hatch and a long retreat. Hollow Tide's lamp-man waits on the beach when the player comes up, speaks
first, and does not ask for the box. He asks the player to look at the headland, because none of his crew will go
up among the Velothi dead: he lit the true lamp that night and the Gull steered wide of it. The ruin's ghosts are
in its hall (Dangerous) and need not be met; the lamp-post is outside, on the seaward side. On it hangs a raft-lamp
with a three-knot cord and a red bead. Taking it is the turn. The journal says only what was taken and from where;
the player knows what a lamp on the wrong post does (RS03), whose cord has three knots (RS01) and whose bead is red
(RS03). Then, in any order and none required: Starless ("The elder gave the word. Whose word did you think was
law over a pilot?"); Ukka-Tei, who denies nothing ("Twelve outlaws who paid no one. Forty families who eat because
the route is one route. I would hang that lamp again"); Sounds-Twice, who did not know ("A false light is the one
thing no pilot is forgiven. Keep it. Do not give it to him"). A stored number holds whom the player has
shown it to and where it is. The strongbox goes to the elder, to the lamp-man for Hollow Tide, or stays in the
player's pack, which is a number and changes no stage. Everything in RS01 to RS04 reads differently now: the rice
that was seized, the warden who was given up, the robbers who were offered the knot.

**RS06, The Widow's Key.** Ukka-Tei, who is still the elder and still gives the work, says the war can be ended
without a boat lost: Half-the-Sky has never shown his commission, the old pilots say the Stormwidow's captain kept
it, and "both camps believe the captain of the Stormwidow buried his share under the cairn" on the Grey Tooth (D
skeith-isle, The Grey Tooth: hooks). He wants the paper. Sounds-Twice asks that it be brought to nobody until the
raft-masters have seen the lamp. The player crosses to Skeith from Arorha's shore, where every place fights a
visitor. The Stormwidow lies beached; "the captain's strongbox is still sealed in the stern" under the sand of the
lower hold, with scavengers of both camps about the hull (Dangerous: a fight, or Sneak). In it are a brass key and,
scratched inside the lid, the dead captain's words: his mate put her ashore, and what the key opens is "with the old
one's bones, on the north face". That is the cave the pirates only tell of (D skeith-isle, The Grey Tooth: "a
shaman's bones in a cave on the north face"), which is why two camps have dug at the cairn and "found only
stones". The goat-path up the Tooth is held by Marque Landing's lookouts with bows (Archery against archers, or
Sneak); hackwings nest on the crags. In the cave, beside the old bones, lies the captain with his sea-chest: his
share, which is the hunt's fixed reward, and the commission. It is the one paper in the line, it is an outsider's
paper in the place the lore puts such things (province-vibe §9: "Archon's charters"; *state*:403), and nothing is
done with it but carry it. It names the master of the Stormwidow "and whoever holds this letter after him" (X).

**RS07, Whose Water.** The commission is in the player's pack and four people want it, each in their own place,
each with a reason of their own and each saying what the others would do with it. Nobody is hurried: the war is
"close" for as long as the player holds the paper.

- **Half-the-Sky, at Marque Landing.** With the paper he is what he has claimed to be. The chartered crews are the
  coast's law in fact; the raids on Seafalls' landing and Arorha's boats stop, because a commission can be taken
  away from a captain who robs the port's own pilots; the coast pays him for protection, and the captives nobody
  ransoms still go "to buyers on the Okuwun coast" (D skeith-isle, Rainbreaker Keep). Hollow Tide is broken within
  the season. The player "is taken aboard as crew" (D skeith-isle, Marque Landing: hooks).
- **Names-the-Quay, at Hollow Tide Camp.** With the paper the outlaws are the privateers and Half-the-Sky is a
  pirate with a keep. The coast's trade goes untaxed and unprotected. He means to open Rainbreaker's cells, and to
  find out who hung a second lamp on Kornakim; if the player gives him the lamp as well, he knows.
- **Ukka-Tei, in the hall at Uluo.** With the paper the knot is the charter. Both camps carry under it or starve,
  nobody raids, and everything that moves between Lake Ulaish and Skeith pays one share to one elder, who killed
  twelve people with a lamp to bring it about.
- **Reads-the-White-Water, the pilot-elder of Seafalls** (named by the main quest; if she is dead, her chosen
  successor, Nomu, speaks for the village). Her people pilot for everyone and belong to nobody (D province, Peoples:
  Spray-Folk). She asks for the paper so that the falls may have it: the sea keeps what is let fall. Then there is
  no charter and no chartered crew, both camps are outlaws, the villages are raided by whoever is hungry, and no
  pilot answers to a captain or an elder. She says plainly that her landing will be robbed again.

Each takes the commission by a line, in their own place, and that line ends the quest. Both Skeith camps are
hostile on sight (D skeith-isle), so each has one person at its edge who speaks: the shore men the player has known
since RS01 and RS02, or, if either is dead, a hailer in their place. Giving the paper to a camp puts the player in
that camp's faction, by the same line, so that its people no longer attack and its traders will deal (D skeith-isle,
Marque Landing and Hollow Tide Camp: gives); the other camp stays as it was. What the choice did is found
afterwards and never watched: the losing camp's leader is gone from his place when the player next lands on the
island; the shore men's greetings; Seafalls' lighthouse; who stands in the hall at Uluo.

The lamp is the player's second decision and can be made before or after the first. Put into Sounds-Twice's hand,
it goes before the raft-masters: when the player next comes to Uluo she holds the hall, Ukka-Tei stands on the
landing as a raft-hand with no share, and Starless is gone to Skeith. If the commission went to Ukka-Tei first,
it is hers now, and she holds it for the pilots in common. She names the player raft-master, the line's last rank;
nobody offers the player the hall. Put into the elder's hand, it is never seen again and he says thank you. Kept,
it is a lamp. No ending changes who holds Fort Reedmoth, Fort Gysilla or Marshgate Watch: the forts tax the water
after every ending, and one line of each captain's says what the coast's new master means to the chain.

## 3. The first three quests

Common to all three.

- **Ukka-Tei** stands in the raft-masters' hall at Uluo for the whole line and gives each job. He must live: his
  death fails any running quest of the line with the faction's message. **Sounds-Twice** stands on Uluo's landing.
  If she dies her favours and her lines are lost and every quest still reads. Nobody is essential. People stand
  where they are placed.
- **One working stage, results as numbers.** After the briefing each quest sits at one working stage. What the
  player achieves there is a stored number or a plain fact (a thing held, a person dead), never a stage of its own,
  so the ways can be done in any order, mixed or abandoned. The lines that carry the result to the person waiting
  for it set the stages, and where two results are true at once the outline gives the order in which those lines
  are tried.
- **Taking a thing only writes a journal note** and is open from the quest's first shown stage. A line that needs
  a thing checks that it is held, however and whenever it was got. A player who holds it before the job is given
  is offered the job with a line that says so.
- **A death moves no stage** except the failure named. Lines check who is alive.
- **The crime system is Skyrim's and the quest never reads it.** A thing taken from an owner in sight is a theft
  with a bounty. At a fort the law is the old Imperial one: pay, the cell or resist, and what was stolen is taken
  from the player and put with the fort's seized goods (D province, Power and law: foreign law). The job stays open,
  because every hand-over asks only that the thing is held.
- Every objective has a marker on its person or thing. Journal entries are in the first person and the past tense
  and say only what the player has just done. Skill thresholds are fixed. Each failed Speech check has its own
  written refusal and leaves another way open. Things use existing forms; nothing new is modelled.
- "Night", "dark" and "dusk" in what people say are their words only; no step depends on the hour. Nobody speaks
  of a tide.
- **How the player knows a place or a thing** is said in each outline and again in section 4: by the name the game
  shows on a door or a thing, by a marker, or by a named person who tells them before they need it.

### RS01. The Knot on the Sack

**What a new player is told, and when.** The player comes to Uluo by raft from Lilmoth, Archon or Truowa (D
althii-archipelago, Uluo: boat hub) and finds the raft-masters' hall by the name on its door. On first speaking
to Ukka-Tei, before any job, four optional lines of two or three sentences each: *the Three Waters* ("the bayou,
the strait and the open coast. One tribe poles all three, and our cord has a knot for each"); *the Reed-Sail*
("reed for our rafts, sail for the pilots of Archon and Seafalls who took our knot. The route is shared. A
pilot's word on the water is law"); *the forts and the chain* ("the Wardens are what the old government calls
itself now. Their forts were the Empire's. The families who kept the keys still keep them, and one of them has
laid a chain across water my grandmothers poled before there was a fort"); *what it pays, and why you might not*
("a carrier's fee and the knot. And a fort keeps the Empire's law: a cell, and whatever you were carrying"). "Not
yet" stays open and free. The job is four sentences: a load of the tribe's saltrice was stopped at Reedmoth's
chain and lies in the fort's toll store; no raft family can fetch it, because every man at that gate knows every
one of them, and the chain would close on all their rafts the next day; nobody at the gate knows the player; bring
it out and carry it to the widow who keeps the lantern house at Xi-Inou, the tribe's landing above Archon, and
not to Archon's quay, where the customs post stands. "She will show you the knot. Give it to nobody who cannot."

**The hidden truth.** The saltrice is Otul-Zan's. He stopped paying the share last season. Ukka-Tei sent word to
the fort that a raft without the knot would come down the strait, and the fort stopped it at the chain, fined him
and kept the load and the raft's lamp, which was his clutch-sister Sounds-Twice's. Now the elder is having the
load fetched for the buyer it was always going to, the lantern-house widow, who will pay the elder and not
Otul-Zan. The sacks still carry Otul-Zan's cord, two knots and a shell. Otul-Zan has been told his rice is lost.
At Xi-Inou, Hollow Tide's shore buyer has heard that a load is coming and means to have it first.

Pike is and has not gone after him: a robbed passage teaches Truowa to pay at the chain.

**The choice, and each side's honest case.** *Clear the bank:* the two boatmen are owed it, and the lake's
fishers at Marzai and the reed camp are robbed by nobody; it is four hard men, and Ukka-Tei says another Pike will
be on that bank by next season. *Give him the knot:* the passage is safe for the Reed-Sail the same day at the
price of a word, Truowa's goods pass, and every boat on the lake without a knot is steered onto his bank by a lamp
the player let him keep. It is the elder's way, and he says so warmly. *Tell the fort:* the robbers are finished
by people whose work it is, nobody else is robbed, and the passage is known to the garrison for good, so that
Truowa pays at the chain after all and the Reed-Sail lose the lake. *Leave them:* a lamp taken is a lamp he will
cut again.

elder, less in two cases. *Cleared:* the bank is empty; Xal-Meema is grim and grateful; Sounds-Twice says, "That

collectors"); Ukka-Tei pays half: "You have given Gysilla a road it did not know it had." *Left:* he pays half and

easily done. One stake, a stone's throw. Anyone who knows what a lamp means can do it."
